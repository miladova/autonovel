# A Woman Made of Glass

A reception room dressed for an Easter that no one celebrates held the hour in a particular way. Sunlight filtered through blackout cloth into a dirty gold pressed against its edges, and a crystal bowl filled with sugared violets seemed to have been left there by someone who understood that objects could outlive the hands that used them. Krystyna entered carrying the public story of the moment: Marion entertains officers while a pianist plays a waltz too softly. She wore it as carefully as the dress, the coat, or the uniformed man's confidence required. The story made room for her, then closed its door.

Beneath it ran the other account. She identifies which guest carries the cipher book for the western rail command. She did not name that account, even inside her own head. Names were handles, and every handle offered the enemy a way to lift what it touched. Instead she noticed the floor, the hinges, the nearest window, the distance to the street. She noticed who looked away and who looked twice. A room announced its loyalties through such small movements.

Somewhere beyond the walls, Warsaw kept its own time. The city had learned to breathe through rubble, to make a market of a courtyard and a message of a loaf of bread. Krystyna felt that hidden breathing now, not as comfort but as an instruction. She had come to this place to change one line in a ledger. The line had changed her instead.

“A woman made of glass is still a woman. She can cut you.”

The words did what words always did in an occupied room: they offered one meaning to the person who heard them and another to the person who had learned how to survive their echo. The danger waited in the shape of an SS intelligence clerk who smiles whenever Marion turns her back. Krystyna measured the silence first. Silence was not absence. It was a space in which a decision could be placed without being seen.

“You make everything sound like a riddle,” the other voice said.

“Only the things that have more than one answer.”

“You are afraid,” the voice said.
