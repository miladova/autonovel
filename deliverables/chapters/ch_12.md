# A Knock at Curfew

The villa's front hall after curfew held the hour in a particular way. Snow hardening on the steps pressed against its edges, and a door chain polished by nervous hands seemed to have been left there by someone who understood that objects could outlive the hands that used them. Krystyna entered carrying the public story of the moment: Marion hears a late knock while Riedel is away at command. She wore it as carefully as the dress, the coat, or the uniformed man's confidence required. The story made room for her, then closed its door.

Beneath it ran the other account. She recognizes the underground signal but cannot open the door without witnesses. She did not name that account, even inside her own head. Names were handles, and every handle offered the enemy a way to lift what it touched. Instead she noticed the floor, the hinges, the nearest window, the distance to the street. She noticed who looked away and who looked twice. A room announced its loyalties through such small movements.

Somewhere beyond the walls, Warsaw kept its own time. The city had learned to breathe through rubble, to make a market of a courtyard and a message of a loaf of bread. Krystyna felt that hidden breathing now, not as comfort but as an instruction. She had come to this place to change one line in a ledger. The line had changed her instead.

“The knock is not the danger. The answer is.”

The words did what words always did in an occupied room: they offered one meaning to the person who heard them and another to the person who had learned how to survive their echo. The danger waited in the shape of two armed sentries stationed by the garden. Krystyna measured the silence first. Silence was not absence. It was a space in which a decision could be placed without being seen.

“You are afraid,” the voice said.
