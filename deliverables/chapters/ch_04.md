# The Night Train West

The first-class carriage reserved for the colonel's staff held the hour in a particular way. Summer rain rattling against sealed windows pressed against its edges, and a porcelain cup stamped with an eagle seemed to have been left there by someone who understood that objects could outlive the hands that used them. Names were handles, and every handle offered the enemy a way to lift what it touched. Instead she noticed the floor, the hinges, the nearest window, the distance to the street. She noticed who looked away and who looked twice. A room announced its loyalties through such small movements.

Somewhere beyond the walls, Warsaw kept its own time. The city had learned to breathe through rubble, to make a market of a courtyard and a message of a loaf of bread. Krystyna felt that hidden breathing now, not as comfort but as an instruction. She had come to this place to change one line in a ledger. The line had changed her instead.
