# The Colonel's Ledger

The townhouse's dining room beneath a chandelier with half its bulbs removed held the hour in a particular way. Heat trapped beneath the high ceiling pressed against its edges, and a ledger bound in cracked brown leather seemed to have been left there by someone who understood that objects could outlive the hands that used them. Krystyna entered carrying the public story of the moment: Riedel hosts a supper for officers who speak of victory as a household expense. She wore it as carefully as the dress, the coat, or the uniformed man's confidence required. The story made room for her, then closed its door.

Beneath it ran the other account. Marion tracks the ledger's movement from hand to hand. She did not name that account, even inside her own head. Names were handles, and every handle offered the enemy a way to lift what it touched. Instead she noticed the floor, the hinges, the nearest window, the distance to the street. She noticed who looked away and who looked twice. A room announced its loyalties through such small movements.

Somewhere beyond the walls, Warsaw kept its own time. The city had learned to breathe through rubble, to make a market of a courtyard and a message of a loaf of bread. Krystyna felt that hidden breathing now, not as comfort but as an instruction. She had come to this place to change one line in a ledger. The line had changed her instead.

“The hungry man watches the plate. The clever man watches the hand that serves it.”
