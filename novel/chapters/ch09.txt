An Inquiry

A death in a dining house belongs, by the city's old and flexible anatomy, to several jurisdictions at once, which means it belongs to whoever moves first with the best Latin. By the second hour of the morning the dead man's uncle the senator has written to the urban prefect; by the third, Antonia's physician has deposited, at her instruction, a sealed account with a notary she has paid for years precisely against the day someone's heart would table a motion. By noon the baths have decided that the man was poisoned by a rival for a singer's favours, cuckolded by a tax farmer and carried out singing, three deaths, all false, all immortal. Rome does not mind an inquiry. Rome minds having nothing to say while it proceeds.

The uncle's letter is the first to move because grief, in senatorial families, keeps office hours. He writes to the prefect before the body is cold in its own house — the body having been conveyed home at dawn under Barbatus' direction, in a procession so discreet it was indistinguishable, from any window that mattered, from a delivery of wine. The letter does not accuse. Accusation is for men who can afford to be wrong in public. The letter *deplores, *in the washed vocabulary, requests, in view of certain committees currently sitting, that the prefect satisfy himself, and invites the city, by implication read in every atrium by the second hour, to watch the prefect satisfying himself at a particular address on the Caelian.

The prefect, who is a man of the new school — which in AD 112 means a man who has realised that Trajan's Rome runs on paperwork the way the old Rome ran on omens, and with the same priesthood — reads the letter twice and sends, not men, but a note, to three addresses. To the aediles: has the house a history? To the tax office: is the house in order? To his own senior clerk, a freedman with a memory like Diomedes' third book: who ate there? The answers arrive before the uncle's second letter, which is how a prefect demonstrates that he is not influenced by committees. He is influenced by filing. It is a more durable vice.

Meanwhile, on the Caelian, Antonia is conducting the morning after a death in the house the way she conducts everything: by sections, lit from the back. The great dining room is cleaned by her own instruction *twice*, once for decency and once for the record, so that any man inspecting it shall find, boringly, a room. The couches are re-covered. The hanging — Prima's border marching over last night's geography — she orders left exactly as it was.

“Take it down,” Eirene says, “and it looks like evidence. Leave it up—”

“Leave it up and it looks like Tuesday. A house that redecorates over a death has written its own evidence. We are a dining house, Eirene. Dining houses are allowed Tuesdays.”

The staff are briefed in the kitchen, standing, with Ammonius' fires lit behind them and the boy who cried in the night given, by Antonia personally, a task involving water and distance from the great room. She tells them the order of the evening — she used the phrase before she received it; the house will never know which of them coined it, and the not knowing is the closest the phrase will ever come to a wedding present — and she tells them the rule beneath it: answer what is asked, add nothing that gleams, and if a question arrives wearing tomorrow's clothes, walk it back to the fish. Barbatus, who has given statements to arena officials in two reigns, nods like a man recognising a formation.

“Fish first,” he says to the boy, kindly, as to a recruit. “Then the song. Then the story. Then the laugh. Anything asks you different, it's not a question, it's a door. Don't go through doors other people hold open, in inquiries or in life.”

Philomela is the one Antonia keeps back. The singer has been awake since the fifth hour with her hair still dressed for last night, a state in which, Diomedes will record, she resembles a temple after a festival: garlanded, exhausted and smelling of oil. She has Nikias' Greek copy of the night's statement in her lap and is reading it with a singer's lips, learning it the way she learns a programme.

“Don't learn it,” Antonia says.

“I was at the couch. I had his shoulders.” Philomela looks up. The famous self-possession is on her face like makeup applied by another hand. “If I don't learn it, I'll *sing* it, Antonia. It'll come out arranged. Everything comes out of me arranged. It is the whole disease.”

“Then arrange it in the order it happened. That is all learning is.” Antonia takes the copy, not unkindly, and puts it under a bowl, where paper in this house goes to become less important. “You sang. He spoke about metre. Serapion told a story with a crocodile for a witness. The man laughed. You held him up. Nikias came at a run and was a physician for four minutes against a heart that had filed its resignation last year. That is the whole of your evidence, Philomela. It has the great merit of being true at the speed it occurred.”

“And if they ask whether he was — in love with me, let's say it in their Latin — or I with his uncle's money, or whether the song was the Greek one, for people who know?”

“Then the question has arrived from the wrong end, and you walk it back to the fish.” Antonia meets her eyes, freedwoman to freeborn Corinthian, across every column Rome keeps. “You are registered, your papers are boring, your money is with a Greek who would adopt a tax return before he'd open your strongbox to a committee. You are the cleanest witness in Rome today, Philomela. Behave like a tax receipt and no one can make a song of you without showing their own handwriting.”

The singer laughs — once, unwillingly, and the laugh does what laughs do in this house's mornings, which is recruit the kitchen to normality. Below the talk, the day assembles: Diomedes' tablets in three hands, Nikias' sealed account gone at dawn to a notary who has been being paid for years against precisely this light, Ammonius baking, out of defiance and almonds, at a rate the morning cannot justify. By the third hour the baths have their three deaths, all false, all immortal. The house has one, true, and written down while it was still warm. Antonia, who knows Rome, settles herself to watch which of the four arrives at the prefect's office with a seal on it.

Lentulus receives the news at his clients' hour, in the mouth of a freedman who brings petitions and relishes disaster the way pastry cooks relish saffron: professionally, and more than he admits. He dismisses the man mid-flourish. He stands in his own atrium among the masks with the information arranging itself in him like troops: his committee, her house, a corpse, the city's tongue. He is aware — he will be able to date the awareness later, to this minute, to the quality of light through the compluvium — of a feeling he cannot at first name because it has arrived wearing the wrong uniform. It is not satisfaction. It has the shape of its opposite, hurrying.

Servilia, who has also heard — Servilia hears everything at the speed other people take to decide whether to repeat it — says from the doorway, “Do not go there.”

“I have no intention of going there.”

“You have every intention of being the man Rome watches not going there. It amounts.” She comes into the room. “If you press this now, you will look like a creditor applauding a fire. If you defend her, you will look like—” She stops, not for want of the word.

“Like a man who inspects a house at dawn and eulogises it by noon.”

“Like its patron,” Servilia says, with surgical calm. “The Avidii will read it. The emperor's secretaries will read it. Your daughter, Lentulus, will read it, in the faces at dinner for the rest of her unmarried life.”

“So I am to let the prefect's men establish whether a man died of his heart or of her fish, and my committee is to master the antiquarian skill of silence.”

“You are to let the truth be established by men who do not love or hate in that street. It is a short list. The physician is on it, I understand, because *she* had the foresight to employ him before she needed him. There is a lesson in that for families who arrange their credit after the vintage.”

Marcus comes in then, as his family would say, *by the garden side*, which is the household's phrase for entering a conversation after every door for it has been formally closed. He has clearly been at the baths — the other baths, the early, functional ones where news is beaten like laundry — and he has the first, unwashed version of the story all over his face.

“They say he was poisoned,” he says. “In the fish. By the Alexandrians, because of the fleet contract. There's also one where Daphne poisoned him for a necklace.” He gets the last word out and hears, as young men do a beat late, whose necklace that is in every sense that matters. “Those are the baths' versions. I thought the house should — I thought somebody here should know what the—”

“The house knows what the baths will say, Marcus,” his mother says. “The house is presently deciding what its head will say in the Senate, which is a related but more expensive plumbing system.” She looks at her son the way she looks at a room being set for a dinner that will now require reseating. “You know this singer they're decorating with poison. Will she hold?”

The question lands on Marcus like a hand on a shoulder in the baths. He has been managed by his mother since before he could decline the noun, but he has never been *briefed* by her — asked, as an asset rather than scolded as a liability — and the novelty makes him, for once, precise. “She returned a necklace someone gave her because it was glass,” he says, to the room, missing nobody. “She keeps her accounts quarterly. She asked me once whether my father really believed registries protect women or only families, and then sang the Greek one, so I wouldn't have to answer in that order. She'll hold, Mother. She's cleaner than the committee.”

“Then we do nothing, loudly,” Servilia says, before her husband can promote the observation into a family injury. “Lentulus. Attend to the optics as you would attend to a harbour due. The committee does not meet this week. A meeting postponed for the prefect's convenience is statesmanship. A meeting held tomorrow is a prosecution of a corpse's dinner.”

“Postumus will say—”

“Postumus will say whatever he says to you in porticoes, and then he will say the opposite to Acilius in a warmer room, and both will be called opinion. Let him. The Avidii are the readership that matters this season, and Avidius was *there*, husband. He ate the fish. He watched your inspected house produce a physician, two sealed statements and a widow's errand run with empty hands before the lamps were trimmed. Do you imagine a man who unloaded grain does not price seamanship in a squall? He will be telling his son by Thursday that whatever else the Cornelii are entangled with on the Caelian, it is competently crewed. You cannot buy that paragraph, Lentulus, and if you press the inquiry you will personally set fire to it at the harbour mouth.”

It is a general's briefing, and like all her briefings it leaves Lentulus in command of exactly the ground she has chosen. He looks at Marcus — at twenty being briefed by his mother in front of his father, standing very straight the way boys do when a household suddenly shows them the machinery — and does an unfamiliar thing. He includes him.

“You heard this where Daphne sings, or where Rome bathes?”

“Both, Father. The baths had the poison before the water was hot. The house — the Caelian had the physician's version at dawn and nobody was selling it. That's what made the baths angry, I think. A story nobody's selling.”

“An unpriced commodity,” Servilia says, “in a city of auctioneers. Yes. That would anger the baths.” She rises; the room rearranges itself around her standing the way rooms do around Antonia's on the other hill, which neither woman would thank a narrator for observing in company. “Write to Postumus cancelling Thursday with a compliment to the prefect's industry. Write to nobody about the house. If Acilius asks at the baths what the committee intends, the committee intends to be general, eventual and dull. And you, Marcus, will not be seen within two streets of the Caelian until the prefect has filed his dullness, not because you are guilty of anything except a necklace, but because grief in this city is a theatre with a long run and I will not have this family heckling from the cheap seats.”

Marcus bows — correctly, uniquely, without the slam his family uses instead — and goes out by the garden side of his life. Lentulus watches him go and says, to the masks, to the prepared speech of a face his wife retains for household earthquakes, “He has started to answer in the order of the evening.”

“Has he.”

“The baths first. Then the house. Then what it means. No boy of this family has ever arrived at meaning last. We breed for the peroration.”

Servilia, at the door, considers her husband with the attention she gave, long ago, to a sentence about Baiae dropped into a conversation about oysters. “Then somebody is educating your son, Lentulus,” she says, “in a street where you and I do not love or hate within the prefect's hearing. Let us be grateful the tuition has so far been in manners. It is more than the tutors achieved at the same rates.”

She goes. The masks remain. Lentulus looks up at his grandfather — Gnaeus, naturally, the dented-mirror Gnaeus, under whose cupboard a sealed tablet has been ripening for nine years — and has the clearest thought of his morning, the kind a man buries standing up: *If I open it, it is because a man has died at her table. Everything in this family requires a death to become sayable.*

He does not open it. He sends, instead, an unsigned note by a slave who is not his usual messenger, which is the most signature-like act available to a Cornelius: *Keep the physician's account sealed until the prefect asks. Answer in the order of the evening, not the order of the questions.*

It is good advice. It is also, and he knows it before the slave has left the street, the first unpaid thing he has done for Antonia Cassia in twenty-one years, and it sits in him for the rest of the day like a coin swallowed in childhood: unnoticed by the world, impossible to forget the swallowing of.

The prefect's men are correct, which Antonia prefers; malice she can price, correctness she can plan around. They view the dining room. They view the kitchen, where the pastry cook, misunderstanding the visit in a way she chooses to believe is genuine, offers them almond cakes of a quality that will enter the record nowhere and influence it everywhere. They take the physician's account. They take Philomela's statement, and Philomela — who has sung for these men's superiors and remembers the programme — gives it in the order of the evening, as a certain unsigned advice has suggested, though Antonia would saw her own lyre in half before admitting that the order was not her own idea an hour before the note arrived.

There are three of them besides the clerk, and they have clearly drawn, among themselves, the morning's choreography: the senior man asks, the second man looks, the third man exists to be the person nothing is said in front of, which in Roman administration is a load-bearing function. They view the dining room. The room — washed twice, hung as it was, set for nobody — gives them back the reflection they have come with: a good room, expensive, unremarkable in the way of places that are professionally discreet about being remarkable. The second man runs a finger along a couch frame and looks at it, disappointed by the absence of metaphor. The senior man notes the hanging, the way the inspector did before him — officials in this house always pause at Prima's border, two hundred instances of the same flower insisting on their differences, as if the room kept a register of its own. He does not write it down. There is no column for it. It enters the record anyway, in the margin where Rome files the things that actually persuade people.

Philomela's statement takes eleven minutes. Antonia has counted them against a water-clock the house does not possess, by Diomedes' breathing, which in the presence of paperwork keeps better time. The singer stands — she declines the chair, correctly, as a freeborn woman giving evidence in her employer's house — and gives them the fish, the metre, the crocodile, the laugh. When the third man, whose function is silence, unexpectedly asks whether the deceased had ever visited her rooms after the music, Antonia watches her walk it back like a bucket up a stair.

“Officer, after the music that evening there was no afterwards in this house for Gaius Calpurnius. Before the music there was fish.” She folds her hands over the place where, last night, a dead man's weight registered. “I can go back to the fish again, if the record is hungry.”

The senior man closes that door himself, with the air of a person shutting a draught on a colleague's behalf. Daphne follows, three minutes, pert as a tax receipt: she was resting her voice in the small room, she heard the story through the door, she came out when the laugh changed sound — “a laugh finishing the man instead of the story, officer; it is a singer's distinction and I ask the record's pardon for its accuracy.” Barbatus follows, mountainous, and gives them the street: watch passed, guests departing in order, the widow's house reached by a route he names with a soldier's nostalgia for distances properly walked. He is asked whether anyone left *early*. “The man left at the hour he was carried, officer,” he says. “Before that, nobody left who hadn't paid to stay. It is a dining house.”

In the kitchen Ammonius has arranged, out of whatever instinct makes Alexandrians lay ambushes with hospitality, a complete reconstructive exhibit of the fish course: the sauce (rebuilt), the fish (today's cousin), the little salted lake fish in a dish like a formation of silver boats. He invites the prefect's men to inspect it as men inspect a treaty. The second man, whose function is looking, looks. The clerk, whose function is writing, writes *kitchen in order* with the hopeless fluency of a man who has written it before in this house in a previous inquiry and will keep the phrase in his hand like a lover's lock of hair.

“You serve dormice,” the senior man says to Antonia. It is in his commission somewhere, probably under headings.

“I serve what rich men will pay to feel wicked about at a price that includes the absolution. Dormice are a tax on guilt, officer. The meat is incidental.”

“The deceased ate—”

“One. At the fourth course, with honey, while telling Philomela that elegiacs were a disease of his class. Nikias' account has the count, and the count of his wine, which I caused to be watered at his end of the table by house policy, because his physician wrote to mine last year and physicians, unlike committees, share their minutes.” This is new information and she gives it the way she gives everything dangerous: as paperwork, over a counter, with the seal already on it. “Diomedes has the letter. It is uninteresting as prayer. That is how the house files its foresight.”

The letter comes. The senior man reads it standing in the kitchen doorway with cakes cooling beside him like small pieces of contrary evidence against the whole theory of poisoners running pastry sections. His face does the thing her rooms specialise in: it tells the truth under the arrangement, for exactly one second, before he files it under conduct unbecoming. What it says, for that second, is that he has been sent by a competent prefect to audit a competently run house for the benefit of a senatorial uncle's grief and a committee's convenience, and that the paperwork — physician retained, wine watered by instruction, accounts deposited before the cock was thinking about it — is going to make the correct thing also the easy thing, a conjunction Roman officialdom experiences so rarely that it annotates it in the margins.

“The ring,” he says, because the second man, looking as assigned, has been looking at the box in Diomedes' office where it sits among the forgotten. “Serapion's. For the singer. It is in the Alexandrian's statement.”

“It is in the box,” Antonia says, “where it will remain until your inquiry has concluded, after which she may file it with her Greek like any other audience. If it were the price of a statement, officer, it would be on her hand and in the baths by now. I run a house, not a mint.”

He looks at her then — not the almost-smile; something drier, the respect officials pay to systems that will outlast their masters' instructions. “Madam,” he says, “I shall write that the house cooperated.”

“Write that the house was boring,” Antonia says. “Cooperation is a word with gleams on it. Walk it back to the fish.”

“You keep a physician on retainer,” the senior man says.

“I keep a house full of rich men eating dormice in a heat they complain of and pay extra to sit in. A physician on retainer is not foresight. It is tableware.”

“And the dead man owed you money.”

“Diomedes.” The tablet comes, the upright hand, the small sum, a dinner in December and a loan against a racehorse, stamped, witnessed, uninteresting as prayer. “He owed me less than his uncle spends on mourning. If I poisoned debtors of this size, officer, Rome would need a second Tiber.”

The man almost smiles, files the smile under conduct unbecoming, and closes the tablet. At the door, with the correctness that has carried them both through the day like a shared umbrella, he says, “The prefect will find a death by misadventure, madam, if misadventure is what the account supports. The prefect is not influenced by committees.”

“Tell the prefect the house appreciates inhabiting the same city as his principles.”

When they have gone, Eirene says, “The note helped.”

“The physician helped. The cakes helped. Notes are just handwriting that has been for a walk.” Antonia puts the note in the box with the tax receipts, beside its predecessor, between the quarters: a small, growing archive of a language neither of them writes in anymore and both of them still read fluently. “Barbatus said the widow asked for me.”

“She asked whether he suffered and whether he was — alone with anyone, at the end. Barbatus said he died laughing at a story about a tax farmer, in a room full of witnesses, with a singer holding his shoulders. She said, 'He would have liked the witnesses.' Then she cried, he said, like a person opening shutters.”

“Send her the December bill,” Antonia says. “Marked settled. And flowers that cost less than the bill, so it doesn't look like arithmetic.”

The verdict, when it comes three days later, comes in the form of things not happening. No second visit. No seal on the dining room. A line in the prefect's morning list, read out among market frauds and a collapsed stair in the Subura: *death by misadventure, the heart; no further interest*. Diomedes enters it in the first book under *officials, concluding*, and the entry is so dull that Antonia, reading it over his shoulder, experiences the professional pleasure she takes in good drainage: the dangerous thing has been conducted underground, by paperwork, to a place where it can rot usefully as precedent.

By noon the baths have amended the record. The poison dies of a heart complaint; the tax farmer is unmarried from the singer; the carrying-out singing survives, as it will survive for thirty years, because it is the only version with music in it. Philomela hears it from a client that same evening — for the house reopens on the fourth day, *in the order of the evening*, with the hanging up and the little salted fish unannounced and Gaius Calpurnius' couch given, by Antonia's instruction, to the elderly advocate, a man whose heart has been reported to *his* wife, by the same Nikias, as *administratively immortal*.

“I am told I carried him out singing,” Philomela says.

“You held his shoulders. The city has scored it for full chorus.” Antonia hands her, across Diomedes' counter, the box with Serapion's ring in it. “The inquiry has concluded. It is now merely a present from a man who watched you work. File it with the Greek.”

Philomela looks at the ring in its bed of forgotten things — beside it, somebody's stylus has been claimed and gone, leaving a stylus-shaped improvement in the cedar dust — and does not put it on. “In Corinth,” she says, “my mother would have called this a down payment on being believed.”

“In Rome it is a receipt for it. The direction of travel matters.” Antonia closes the box on the rest of the city's lowered guard. “Sing the fast version tonight, for the first set. The room will want its characters back.”

The widow comes on the fifth day, in the dead middle of the afternoon, veiled in the grey that costs more than colour, with the sister who started lists. Eirene meets them — Barbatus, who announced them by no name at all, having learned his trade in this house's school of weather — and brings them not to the great room but to the small dining room, sized to nobody's self-regard, where Antonia keeps the April-weather conversations. There is water. There are, inevitably, the almond cakes.

“I wanted to see the room,” the widow says, “and then I got to the street and I didn't.” She is younger than Antonia expected from Barbatus' report — thirty, perhaps; Roma has filed her under mourning so completely that the youth comes as a kind of clerical error. “My sister says a visit is an admission.”

“Of appetite,” Antonia says. “Take a cake. Women's visits are admitted here for any reason they choose to file.”

The sister, at the widow's shoulder, performs the family office of looking for traps in the pastry. The widow takes a cake and eats it — actually eats it, grief being a country where the customs forms do not always arrive — and says, “He wrote elegiacs. Did he tell you? It seems important that the room know. The versions—” Her mouth does the thing Antonia has watched all week in mirrors and at tables: it composes itself at cost. “The versions have him cuckolded and poisoned and carried out singing. None of them have him breathable.”

“Philomela has his measure,” Antonia says. “And the house has his evening in the order it happened, sealed, with two physicians on it like a treaty. Neither will be published. That is a form of mourning Rome is competent at: the unfiled kind.” She does not offer to show the account. Showing accounts is for tax offices and bankers; what she offers, by Diomedes' prior placement on the low table between them, is the December bill marked *settled*, and the flowers Eirene already sent, now three days past flowers and into evidence of having been meant.

The widow looks at the bill a long time. “He owed for a racehorse too,” she says at last, with a sound her sister will later report, inaccurately, as composure giving way. “The horse is still alive. It owes *us*, on form. I have kept the horse, madam. I have given away his library to the uncle, his seal to the household gods, and his elegiacs —” the mouth again, the cost again “— I have kept those too. Somebody in this city is going to have to be the archive of the true ones. The versions.”

“Then keep them where the light is,” Antonia says, “and let the baths keep theirs. Baths evaporate.” It is more advice than she gives widows as a class; but it has been a week of unsigned advice arriving by hand and proving good, and a house, like a singer, develops in the direction of its exercises. The widow rises; the sister's list closes over the visit like water over a stone; at the door the widow turns, in her costly grey, with the December bill folded into her sleeve like a letter.

“He liked your fish,” she says. “He came home in December and said the fish had stopped being ambition. I have thought about that all week, whether it is a fit thing to put on a tomb.”

“Put *he died laughing*,” Antonia says. “Diomedes already has. In the second book, in the hand for facts that must survive fashion. The first book is the one the committee reads.”

When they have gone, Eirene clears the cakes in silence, and the house does a thing it has not done in four days: it simply becomes a house again, being prepared for an evening, with nobody rehearsing fire procedures against the prefect, the committee, or the sound of a laugh changing key. Ammonius is rebuilding a sauce. Daphne is practising scales at a volume calculated to annoy nobody who matters. In the courtyard the garden roses, overwatered as nations, open their January account with the sun. The inquiry is over. What it has cost, and whom, is itemised nowhere the first book keeps.

Eirene goes to arrange the flowers that are not arithmetic. Antonia sits a moment in the emptied dining room under the hanging with the red border, listening to her house's heart resume its ordinary rhythm, and allows herself — the room is empty, the accounts are straight, a man is dead who died laughing and there are worse inscriptions — one unfiled thought, arrived in a familiar hand: *In the order of the evening, not the order of the questions.* It is how you tell any story if the telling is to save you. It is how she has never yet told hers.
