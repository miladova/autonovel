# The Judge's Desk

*Warsaw, 14 May 1943*

At four-fifteen on Friday afternoon Sylwia rode a rattling Number Four tram down Krakowskie Przedmieście toward Powiśle without getting off, standing wedged behind two nuns on the rear platform while peering down the steep cobbled drop of ulica Wiślana as the car ground past the corner.

Forty yards down Wiślana, opposite the high mossy brick garden wall of the Ursuline convent where Sister Teresa supposedly kept a clean Lublin *Kennkarte* waiting under her blotter for five-forty-five, a young man in a belted grey leather motorcyclist's coat leaned casually over the saddle of a parked BMW sidecar combination, pretending to clean a spark-plug with emery cloth ninety minutes early while his companion smoked inside the baker's doorway across the street.

*Ost-44* painted white across the mudguard curved plate.

Sylwia didn't even blink as the tram lurched southward round the bend; she stayed aboard two stops to Nowy Świat, stepped down calmly at Świętokrzyska, and walked west toward Królewska with forty milligrams of anhydrous aconitine clinking softly inside its cardboard tube deep inside her right jacket pocket where her bare fingers rolled the smooth cylinder round and round as she walked.

Had she obeyed Marek's order blindly today:
At five-fifteen she slid forty milligrams of monkshood alkaloid under Ludwig Schoeler's scarred skin.
At five-twenty-four Ludwig died convulsing in silent ventricular fibrillation on Maurycy Silberstein's parquet while Mekelburg shouted down the stairs.
At five-forty-five Sylwia walked dutifully down Wiślana expecting convent sanctuary—straight into that BMW sidecar crew from Aleja Szucha, tipped off in advance by *"Zegar"* inside Section II so Reinhart Kroll could arrest the "rogue Polish assassin" red-handed with a Diamond-scored syringe still wet in her bag, hang her inside Pawiak before dawn Saturday, and close *both* ends of the leak forever without a single witness left breathing!

And if she simply failed to inject Ludwig today and told Marek tomorrow *"I couldn't do it"*? Within forty-eight hours *"Zegar"* either sent a three-man liquidation squad to shoot Sylwia on Koszykowa stairs for disobeying a Special Court execution order, or tipped Kroll anyway to clear the board.

To walk out of Friday evening alive, she needed two things inside the next forty minutes: an ironclad physical alibi recorded on German sentry paper proving *nobody* could have given an injection at Królewska 16 at five-fifteen today—and whatever scrap of paper inside Ludwig Schoeler's flat pointed toward *"Zegar"*'s real Polish throat.

At four minutes to five Bruno Mekelburg opened the cream-painted pantry door on Service Stair B.

Today the boy wore his field-grey tunic buttoned neat, belt buckled, boots gleaming—clearly waiting to drive somewhere at six. "Afternoon, *Frau Doktor*. He's in the study, though Berlin trunk-line's been ringing back every ten minutes trying to clear static through Breslau."

Inside the study chestnut leaves pressed thick green shadow against open casements; warm May air smelled of dust, lilac, and beeswax. Ludwig stood behind Judge Silberstein's broad mahogany desk in uniform trousers and grey wool service shirt with collar open, sorting three sheaves of pale-pink and grey onion-skin flimsies into two piles: one going into the iron wire burn-basket by the porcelain stove, the other sliding into a thin buff folder inside the wide shallow center drawer of the desk.

When Sylwia crossed the threshold, he looked up—catching something razor-taut in the set of her shoulders before she said good afternoon—closed the shallow center drawer until its brass lock tongue clicked lightly, and turned the small flat Chubb key half a turn, leaving the key-ring lying momentarily beside his inkstand only because out in the front vestibule the heavy brass field-line extension bell suddenly jangled three long, urgent rings!

Mekelburg poked his head in from the hall: "*Amt Tirpitzufer* through Königsberg repeater, *Herr Oberstleutnant*—scrambler line's clean for three minutes only."

Scrambler equipment sat bolted inside the locked front cloakroom closet where German transport colonels had wired direct trunk pairs from Saski Palace. Ludwig glanced once from her scuffed Gladstone bag on the wooden chair to the Chubb key-ring beside his blotter—picked up the *key-ring* naturally out of lifelong intelligence habit and dropped it into his trouser pocket!—and paused passing her shoulder.

"Wait four minutes," he said crisp and low. "Don't leave."

Double frosted-glass hall doors clicked shut behind him; thirty feet down the corridor a second oak door muffled his voice completely. Out in the pantry Mekelburg was whistling tunelessly between his teeth over running tap water rinsing spinach grit out of a colander for supper.

Four minutes. Maybe two and a half if Königsberg cut off early.

Sylwia did not waste three seconds staring at doors. Moving silent on thin leather soles across bare oak parquet, she stepped behind Judge Silberstein's desk while pulling from her open Gladstone bag not a clumsy hairpin—locks laughed at hairpins—her stiff stainless-steel Kocher grooved surgical director and a narrow pair of curved Hartmann ear-forceps.

Cabinetmakers in nineteen-twelve fitted Biedermeier mahogany drawers with flat two-lever brass mortise locks, not bank-vault Chubb cylinders—Ludwig had turned a small flat warded brass key on his ring, not a pin-tumbler!

Sliding the flat two-millimeter steel tongue of the grooved director up under the upper brass strike-plate first to test spring tension, she felt the bolt nose—not dead-bolted twice, single-throw spring-latched!—hooked the stiff curved jaws of the Hartmann ear-forceps inside the keyhole below the ward post, pressed upward against the single brass tumbler leaf until grease yielded with a tiny oiled *tick*, and eased the center drawer open four inches without making wood creak.

Right on top lay the thin buff folder stamped across its diagonal corner in red purple-pad block letters: **SONDERMAPPE: UHRZEIGER / KROLL-ABFLUSS (NUR EIGENHÄNDIG)**.

Sylwia flipped the cover back with dry fingertips, eyes photographing German type faster than breath:

Top sheet: carbon transcript of Danzig *Commerzbank* safe-deposit transfers converted into twenty-franc Swiss bank-notes—*14.XI.42, 18.XII.42, 22.I.43, 08.II.43*—drawn by *SS-Sturmbannführer* Hahn's special slush fund and signed out on Warsaw receipt slips by *Hstuf. R. Kroll* for informant **IV N / 119 ("Uhrzeiger" / poln. "Zegar")**.

Second sheet: Ludwig's own slantless vertical fountain-pen ink notes reconstructed after the February ninth Ochota ambush from what his dead railway cut-out had glimpsed before Kroll's squad opened fire:

*1. Quelle "Uhrzeiger" sitzt unmittelbar im Stab Kedyw / Abt. II Bez. Warschau (Zugriff auf Tagesmeldungen binnen 12–24 Std.).*
*2. Anwerbung Okt. 42 über Pawiak-Erpressung: Neffe (Medizin- od. Polytechnikum-Student, ca. 20 J., verhaftet Sept. 42 bei Funkpeilung Żoliborz) heimlich nach Dachau überstellt statt erschossen; Lebenszeichen monatlich gegen Lieferungen.*
*3. Warnung: am 8.II. meldete "Uhrzeiger" verdächtige chirurgische Kellerstelle ul. Pańska (Bahnarbeiter-Schusswunde)—Zugriff durch SD wegen Ochota-Gefecht am 9.II. unterblieben!*

Blood roared cold inside Sylwia's ears.

*1) Recruited October 42 via Pawiak blackmail over a twenty-year-old nephew (medical or Polytechnic student arrested Sept 42 in Żoliborz radio-direction raid) transferred secretly to Dachau instead of shot, monthly proof-of-life postcards traded for underground deliveries!*
*2) On EIGHT FEBRUARY—twenty-four hours BEFORE Sylwia ever dug that copper-jacketed bullet out of the nineteen-year-old railwayman's hip on ulica Pańska!—"Uhrzeiger" had ALREADY reported the cobbler's surgical cellar on Pańska to Kroll's desk, and the ONLY reason Gestapo hadn't raided Pańska at seven p.m. on February ninth and caught Sylwia and Marek together over the bloody bench was that Kroll pulled every squad across town to Ochota at midnight to ambush Ludwig Schoeler!*

Wait: *who on God's earth knew on February eighth that Sylwia was going to ulica Pańska on February ninth?!* Only three human beings: the cobbler's wife, **Marek Wolski**, and whoever upstairs in Section II handed Marek medical dispatch chits—OR somebody closer to medical/Polytechnic circles with a twenty-year-old student nephew arrested in Żoliborz last September! Wait—did Marek have a twenty-year-old nephew?! No, Marek was an only child from Kielce—Wait: *who had a twenty-year-old Polytechnic/medical student nephew arrested in Żoliborz in September forty-two?!*

Before Sylwia's brain could pull the name out of memory, zero floorboard creaked behind her—yet cold shadow fell across green blotter leather, and a bare right hand slammed flat down onto the open mahogany drawer edge an inch from her knuckles, pinning folder and drawer immobile while hard grey wool brushed her shoulder blade and the cold blued steel muzzle of a Walther PPK pressed quiet and unmistakable below her right ribs!

"Take both hands clear of the wood, Sylwia," Ludwig said six inches behind her right ear, voice twenty degrees colder than winter Spree water. "Slowly. Leave the ear-forceps on the blotter."

Sylwia laid the curved steel forceps gently onto green blotting paper and straightened up without turning fast, feeling the seven-sixty-five-millimeter muzzle track steady against her ribs as she pivoted slow inside the narrow kneehole gap until her spine touched Judge Silberstein's mahogany edge and she looked straight up into his face.

For the first time since forty-point-four delirium she saw what Ludwig Schoeler looked like when *Abwehr* instinct took the wheel pure, stripped of Berlin history or hospital debt: eyes pale ice-slate without a millimeter of pupil softness, jaw locked white, pistol held low against his hip where nobody looking through Saski Garden trees could see steel, finger resting directly inside the trigger guard with the safety off.

"Königsberg cut off after ninety seconds," Ludwig said softly. "Evidently two minutes too early for Warsaw Section Two's photography schedule. Who sent you into my desk—the same leak who handed Kroll *Chłodna twenty-two*?"

"Put the pistol away before you ruin Silberstein's wainscot," Sylwia said without raising her voice half a note, staring straight back into ice-green murder from ten inches away while her heart hammered cold slow strokes against the gun muzzle. "I don't carry cameras, and I didn't come here to steal for Section Two. Keep your gun on my ribs while my right hand goes two inches into my outer jacket pocket—two fingers only—and look at what Section Two *did* send me here with at two o'clock Wednesday."

His eyes didn't blink—measuring angle, pocket depth, weight of her jacket hem where clearly no firearm sagged—and gave one hairline nod without shifting the Walther muzzle a millimeter off her seventh rib.

With thumb and index finger only, slow as lifting glass out of pus, Sylwia drew the small grey cardboard pill-cylinder out of her pocket, slipped its cap off one-handed against her hip, and stood the unlabelled two-milliliter amber glass ampoule upright on the green blotter squarely between her curved forceps and his left wrist.

"Two-percent procaine hydrochloride laced with forty milligrams anhydrous aconitine and strychnine," Sylwia said cleanly. "Issued verbally Wednesday afternoon in Towarowa sawmill for subcutaneous injection into your lateral pectoral scar at five-fifteen today—four minutes from now. Six to nine minutes to ventricular fibrillation after I walk down your back stairs; autopsied at Ujazdowski as idiosyncratic Novocain shock. Followed at five-forty-five by a promised Lublin escape *Kennkarte* at the Ursuline convent gate on Wiślana—outside which Kroll's *Ost-44* BMW sidecar crew has been parked cleaning spark-plugs since four-fifteen waiting to hang your murderer before she talks."

Dead, absolute silence dropped inside the study.

Out in the kitchen pantry tap water shut off with a faint brass knock; distant tram bells chimed cheerful through open chestnut leaves from Marszałkowska.

Ludwig's eyes dropped for one single second to the diamond-scratched neck of the amber ampoule glinting brown in afternoon light—surgeon's eye recognizing hand-flamed clandestine laboratory glass instantly—then came straight back up to Sylwia's calm, bloodless face.

Slowly his right thumb clicked the red-dot safety lever up on the Walther PPK and slipped the flat black pistol silently inside his trouser waistband behind his hip.

"You rode past Wiślana at four-fifteen before coming here," he said quietly.

"Number Four tram. Two *Referat IV* plainclothes men and a sidecar." She tilted her chin half an inch toward the open buff folder still pinned beneath his left palm. "Which meant *Zegar* inside Section Two pushed through a fake liquidation order to kill you before Monday—they claimed you were flying to Tempelhof Monday with Warsaw files—and dispose of me thirty minutes later on Wiślana so neither of us stayed breathing after London's Chłodna audit started rattling Section Two's floorboards. I opened your drawer because if I walk out that door tonight without knowing how *Zegar* sits wired, I'm dead from *both* sides of Marszałkowska before Tuesday."

Ludwig looked down at the typed sheet lying exposed in the open drawer—seeing instantly where her eye had reached before he came back down the hall.

"Did you read point two and point three?" he asked low.

"Twenty-year-old student nephew arrested last September in Żoliborz radio sweep, held alive in Dachau against monthly proof-of-life postcards," Sylwia recited verbatim without glancing down, brain already racing frantically down every name she knew while point three burned white-hot behind her forehead. "And the Pańska cobbler's cellar reported on *eight February*—the day before you were shot, the night Marek and I pulled a bullet out of a railwayman's hip there!"

Wait—stop—hold the scalpel still on *twenty-year-old nephew arrested September forty-two in Żoliborz*!

Marek had no nephew—Marek's single sister died of scarlet fever at twelve in Kielce. Inżynier Stach? She didn't know Stach's family, maybe Stach had three nephews—WAIT: in February forty-three in Pavilion Three's dispensary, when Docent Mirecki had poured rectified spirit with trembling fingers and talked about Kroll's white cotton gloves, Mirecki had spoken about his cousin Bronisław from Radom... AND last October, who else in Warsaw medical/Polytechnic circles had lost a twenty-year-old nephew in the big September Żoliborz *Funkpeilung* roundup?! Half of Żoliborz intelligentsia! No—don't guess blind names without proof or you frame an innocent throat; follow the *access*: only someone inside **Section II / Kedyw dispatch** knew on February eighth that *"Szew"* (Sylwia's medical code-name, never spoken at hospital!) was booked for ulica Pańska at six-thirty p.m. on February ninth! Nobody at *Dzieciątka Jezus*—not Mirecki, not Bronka, not Halina—had *ever* heard of the Pańska cobbler's shop! Only **Section II's dispatch room** knew Pańska on February eighth!

"Only Section Two's own dispatch desk knew Pańska on the eighth of February," Sylwia breathed, confirming Ludwig's point one to the hilt. "Nobody at Lindleya ever heard that street name."

Ludwig closed the buff folder, slid it back inside the drawer, locked the brass latch with his pocket key—giving her curved Hartmann ear-forceps a single dry, sideways look that said *remind me to change nineteenth-century locks tonight*—and leaned both palms against the desk edge, thinking at combat speed.

"What happens tomorrow morning when I'm still eating breakfast upright and you haven't reported to Wiślana?" he asked sharply.

"If I have zero excuse for not injecting you between five and five-twenty today," Sylwia said, "Marek reports an aborted Special Court execution by nine a.m., and whoever sits above him sends pistols to Lindleya gate before noon—while pulling Kroll's sidecar off Wiślana to hunt me at Koszykowa."

"And what excuse satisfies an engineer without burning your pass?"

"Witnesses in the room," Sylwia answered inside two heartbeats. "Nobody injects strychnine-aconitine with six minutes' onset when three armed Wehrmacht officers are sitting round the patient's table with briefcases open and an orderly pouring tea at his elbow! If your courtyard gate-sentry logbook and building register show two or three officers from Saski Square arrived here at *four-fifty-five* for an unscheduled cipher-briefing and stayed until *five-forty*, so Frau Doktor did five minutes' dry pulse check in the anteroom under three pairs of German eyes without opening her syringe case—"

Ludwig was already moving toward the hall telephone extension before she finished the word *case*.

"Bruno!" he called once toward the pantry. When Mekelburg appeared wiping wet hands on a dish-towel: "Run across Saski Garden—don't telephone—tell Hauptmann von Berg and Leutnant Rademacher I want them upstairs here with the Minsk transport maps inside seven minutes, signing the downstairs sentry sheet *four-fifty* in back-dated pencil—give Corporal Weber at the gate twenty cigarettes to smudge the four-fifty line above Frau Doktor's five-o'clock entry. Move!"

Mekelburg grabbed his field cap off the peg and took the back stairs four steps at a bound.

Alone again inside the quiet room, green chestnut shadow seemed suddenly twice as thick now that steel and poison sat naked on green blotter leather between them.

Ludwig walked slowly back around the corner of Judge Silberstein's desk until he stood two feet in front of Sylwia, picking up the tiny amber glass ampoule between thumb and forefinger and holding its clear, innocent-looking colorless fluid against the window glare. Forty milligrams—enough to stop four grown men's hearts in hideous opisthotonic convulsions while awake.

"You didn't hesitate long at the tram window," he remarked quietly, eyes dropping from the deadly glass cylinder straight into hers. "Suppose you'd ridden Number Four down Wiślana at four-fifteen and seen *nobody* parked outside the convent wall—just nuns raking gravel and clean Lublin travel papers waiting on Sister Teresa's table?"

There sat the ugly, unvarnished question neither saint nor patriot wanted asked aloud in daylight.

Had Wiślana looked completely clean at four-fifteen—had Section II's order been genuine Polish underground justice rather than a traitor's double murder—would Sylwia Kotarska have walked up Judge Silberstein's stairs at five o'clock today, drawn two milliliters of colorless monkshood poison into her boiled glass syringe while looking into winter-green eyes across scarred muscle *she* had rebuilt stitch by stitch out of pride and blood, slid the needle home above his collarbone, and walked down to Lublin?

Sylwia looked straight back at him without dropping her chin half a millimeter—giving him the merciless truth he always stripped out of her anyway.

"I rode past Wiślana to check *who* sent me," she said, voice low and steady as rock, "not to decide whether I was going to put strychnine into my own Carrel repair. Had I wanted you dead on a table, Ludwig, February ninth cost zero ampoules and zero nuns."

For five long seconds neither of them breathed.

Something dark, fierce, and shockingly unguarded flared deep behind the grey-green slate of Ludwig's irises—closer than April's splint room, stripped now even of surgical excuse because four inches to his left lay a loaded pistol in his waistband and right between his fingers sat the poison she had carried across Warsaw straight onto his blotter rather than into his veins.

Slowly he set the unbroken amber ampoule back inside its grey cotton-lined cardboard cylinder, capped it, and pressed it back into *her* palm—closing his warm bare fingers hard around her closed fist for three silent beats before letting go.

"Dump the liquid into a sewer grate three streets away—not here—and keep the broken glass neck if your handler demands to see why you aborted," Ludwig instructed quietly, brain already guarding her flank two moves further down the board than she had calculated herself! "Tell him when von Berg and Rademacher walked in at five, your bag got bumped off the pantry chair onto tile and cracked the ampoule neck inside its cotton tube—so even if he searches your bag tomorrow he smells wet procaine cotton and sees broken diamond-scratched amber glass instead of refusal."

Sylwia stared at the small cardboard tube in her hand—stunned for half a beat by the sheer effortless perfection of *cracked inside its cotton tube when bumped onto pantry tile*, which spared even having to explain why she hadn't left the intact ampoule ready for Tuesday!

Down in the front vestibule two minutes later, heavy boots echoed up the main marble stairs followed by the front bell ringing twice: Hauptmann von Berg and young Leutnant Rademacher arriving winded from Saski Square with rolled map-cases under their arms and four-fifty inked neatly onto Corporal Weber's gate sheet downstairs.

Sylwia had her charcoal jacket already buttoned and her stethoscope coiled inside her bag when Ludwig opened the double hall doors to admit his officers—pausing deliberately in the open foyer under both Germans' eyes to say in crisp, irritated official tones:

"Forget the nerve-block infiltration today, *Frau Doktor*, my staff will be an hour on Minsk rail returns—leave your ointment jar and come Tuesday at five when my room isn't a railway station. Mind that narrow pantry chair, Bruno knocked your bag off it earlier."

"Tuesday at five, *Herr Oberstleutnant*," Sylwia answered stiffly in full view of Hauptmann von Berg's monocle, picking up her bag and walking out down Service Stair B past three witnesses whose sworn German signatures would confirm to any spy on earth—including Kroll's clerk if *Zegar* checked!—that nobody could have stuck a poisoned needle into Ludwig Schoeler inside *Wohnung 4* on Friday the fourteenth of May.

* * *

At ten-forty-five that night, under damp starless cloud cover smelling of lilac and pine-tar, a horse cart heaped high with dry peat-turf slabs backed silently up to the contagious mortuary lane behind *Dzieciątka Jezus*.

Four days of silver-nitrate tannic eschar, massive oral sulfathiazole, and six liters of sweet salted fluid slipped down by Halina at midnight had pulled twenty-four-year-old Dawid Różański back across the cliff-edge: burn back dry black parchment without green *Pseudomonas* pus, fever down to thirty-seven nine, right eye clear enough now to count fingers at ten feet while the left stayed taped sterile under atropine pad. Buttoned into coarse peasant hemp smock with forged *Kreishauptmannschaft Grójec* woodcutter's papers Bronka's cousin had stamped for six hundred złoty, he gripped Sylwia's raw right hand once with his unburned hand in total silence before climbing down beneath the peat-turf arch toward Konstancin pine woods.

Halina stood beside Sylwia in the dark cinder lane for sixty seconds after the iron-tired wheels faded down Koszykowa without making a sound—then let out one long, shuddering breath that seemed to come all the way up from her heels, wiped her nose roughly on her cardigan sleeve like a street urchin, and walked straight back down into her cellar to feed lice without saying thank you, because *thank you* between them would have insulted sixteen-year-old Rachela buried Tuesday morning in Bródno lime.

Twenty minutes later, walking home alone down empty dark Nowogrodzka under her night surgical pass, Sylwia paused beside a cast-iron storm-sewer grate thirty yards from Emilii Plater.

Checking fifty yards forward and back along blacked-out walls—nobody awake, only a stray dog trotting silent across tram rails—she took the cardboard pill-tube out of her pocket, slipped the amber ampoule out onto the iron grate bar, wrapped it inside one layer of rag, and stepped down hard with her steel-tipped boot heel.

*Crunch.*

Two milliliters of colorless aconitine-strychnine procaine dripped harmless down into black rushing storm-water toward the Vistula; carefully with gloved fingertips she tucked the jagged diamond-scratched glass neck shards back inside damp procaine-smelling cotton wool inside the crushed cardboard cylinder, ready to throw onto Marek Wolski's sawmill plank tomorrow noon with cold disgust at clumsy German orderlies knocking medical bags off pantry chairs.

Looking down through the iron bars into pitch-black water rushing seaward beneath Warsaw's streets, Sylwia felt the ground shift irrevocably under her soles.

Four months ago she had belonged to a side—exhausted, half-starved, unsentimental, yet cleanly bounded by language, blood, and uniform. Tonight an unknown Pole sitting upstairs in her own underground directorate had tried to murder her at a convent wall to protect his Gestapo Swiss francs, and the single human hand standing between her throat and two gallows belonged to a Wehrmacht *Oberstleutnant* sleeping behind stolen law-books on Królewska.
