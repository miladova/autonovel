# Spiska Street and Blackout

*Warsaw, 30 June 1943*

Nobody fired a single pistol shot on ulica Spiska that Wednesday morning, which was how people along Grójecka knew inside twenty minutes that something catastrophic had been taken alive.

When *Kedyw* boys got cornered in safe-flats, staircases exploded with Filipinka grenades, roof tiles rained into gutters, and ambulances clanged for two hours afterward hauling dead gendarmes under canvas. At nine-forty on the thirtieth of June—six hundred meters southwest of Pavilion Three's laundry gate, close enough that Sylwia walking between Wards A and B with morning pus charts heard the sudden three-street cordon of police horns seal Niemcewicza, Grójecka, and Aleje Jerozolimskie simultaneously—six grey Gestapo *Sturmbanne* lorries and three passenger sedans slid around Number Fourteen Spiska like pike closing on a tench in still water, walked upstairs to the second floor without breaking a lock, and drove off twelve minutes later toward Aleja Szucha with sirens screaming triumph clear down Ujazdowskie.

By noon Pavilion Three's corridors smelled of scorched paper despite thirty-one degrees of suffocating June mugginess outside.

Nobody spoke the name *Grot* above breath-level, yet even nineteen-year-old probationer nurses who didn't belong to a platoon knew within two hours when Warsaw's ground lost gravity: pale couriers arrived at kitchen gates whispering three words to porters and vanishing; upstairs in the surgical dressing room Docent Mirecki locked the door for ten minutes to burn six forged *Arbeitskarte* blanks in an enamel kidney basin, fanning grey ash down the drain with shaking pince-nez misted white by sweat.

*Spiska.*

Standing at the open third-floor landing window where hot dusty wind off Ochota carried zero oxygen and distant thunder grumbled behind yellow haze over Wola, Sylwia gripped the warm iron sill until her knuckles turned chalk-white.

*Chłodna zweiundzwanzig—nicht vor dem Zehnten zum Optiker—wenn Kroll den Uhrzeiger hat, brennt das ganze Netz bis zur Spiska.*

Four months ago to the day—twenty-eighth of February at two-fifteen in the morning, shaking at forty degrees point four under vinegar cloths—Ludwig Schoeler had spoken *Spiska* straight into her palm before passing out.
