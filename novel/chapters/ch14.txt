The Committee Reports

The Senate in the morning is a theatre that has agreed to call itself a constitution. Sun through high windows, the benches filling in order of a precedence everyone affects not to calculate, the emperor's letter read by a secretary with the flat delivery of a man who knows the words will outlive everyone in the room except the building. Lentulus sits where a former consul sits, in the sightlines of power, and listens to a debate about harbour dues with the portion of his mind the Avidii have under option, and with the rest composes, for the last time, the speech he is no longer going to give.

He wrote the clause at dawn, with Felix waiting and the razor cooling in its water, because the day's official beginning had not yet made his hand anyone else's. That is how the household will remember it afterwards: not as legislation, but as an interruption in shaving. The committee's draftsman had sent up, the evening before, a fair copy in the washed Latin his father issued to a generation of Cornelian paperwork: *order, decency, precedent*, twenty-three categories of establishment arranged like furniture in a room nobody proposed to enter. Lentulus read it after dinner, commended its *general* dullness to Servilia, who was re-roping a household in another register entirely, and put it under the bust in his study like a man filing a letter he intends to be found by.

At dawn he sent for it again. His secretary, arriving with his face still arranged for sleep, found the master of the house standing at the window in the hour when clients are a rumour and ancestors are at their least upholstered, saying: “Read me the penalties.”

The penalties were read. They were good penalties, Roman penalties: fines scaled to visibility, closure for recidivists, a paragraph on unregistered persons that treated the persons themselves as the irregularity, the way his family's paperwork has always treated girls who arrived in households in other people's columns. Lentulus listened with his whole back.

“Strike that sentence,” he said.

“Which, dominus?”

“The one in which the person is the offence. Write instead—” He dictated it slowly, because a sentence that has been dictated to a man in a banker's back room by the woman it was designed to inspect deserves to go down at the speed it occurred. *No house to profit by a person whose refusal has no legal existence.* The secretary's stylus paused, a toll-gate, at *refusal*. Felix, who has no Latin he will admit to in that room, set the razor down like a man making way for furniture. “Again,” Lentulus said, and dictated it again, and then a third time, tightening the middle of it the way a man tightens a strap he expects to be hauled on in courts he will not live to attend.

The secretary, who has written harbour dues in upholstery and resignations in poetry, said at last, “The committee, dominus, did not minute this clause.”

“The committee will discover it has always intended it. Committees are how my family thinks. It is time one of them thought in public.”

Servilia, informed over the morning offering — told as an aside, at oyster-depth, which in this marriage is the register of state papers — poured, spoke the words, and set the cup exactly in the ring its predecessors had left on the household shrine. Only when the slaves had withdrawn did she say: “You will lose Syria on a subordinate clause.”

“I was losing Syria on the main clause, apparently. At least let the epitaph decline correctly.”

She straightened a fold of his dress with hands expert and, this morning, undisguised. “Then see it is read clearly. If Rome is to misquote you for a generation, Lentulus, don't let it be the clerk's fault.”

That is the family he carries into the Curia: a sentence shaved around at dawn, a wife who schedules everybody's acts including her own support, a daughter in the music room inserting unlicensed turns into old songs, a son out being invisible at considerable expense, and behind them all, in a cupboard under a cleaned mask, a dead man's instructions to choose better, which Lentulus is presently obeying in the only dialect his class will accept as tender of payment: he is about to be dull in the Senate, generally, eventually, and at ruinous personal cost, and in the middle of the dullness, like a stair inside a drum of stone, put one sentence where the future will have to climb through it to get to the view.

He had drafted it the way his family drafts everything: thoroughly, in periods that could support an aqueduct. *That the city which taxes a trade owes that trade its regulations; that young men of family are mortgaging their grandfathers to freedmen's bankers; that what the aediles cannot find, the Senate has declined to see.* It is a good speech. It would have been quoted in baths. It names no house and aims at one, which is rhetoric's oldest laundry service.

What he gives, when called, is the report. It is four minutes long. A general registry, quarterly fees, inspections at stated hours by appointment of the aediles, twenty-three categories of establishment, penalties for unregistered minors and unfree persons held without manumission papers — a clause he inserted at dawn in his own hand, with Felix the barber waiting, in language tighter than anything the committee's draftsmen produced: *no house to profit by a person whose* no *has no legal existence*. He hears Acilius shift on the bench at the clause, hears the room decide it is a fine sentiment and will be no trouble, hears, under the shuffle of assent, the small, specific silence of men recalculating which of their private arrangements the clause has just described in public Latin.

Debate follows because debate is what the theatre does after it has decided. An ex-consul of ninety, carried in by grandsons with faces like junior partners, rises to commend the report in a speech about the Rome of his youth, in which, to judge by the oration, nobody under sixty had ever wanted anything. He sits to applause, having demonstrated once again that longevity, in the Senate, is a form of evidence. A young wit of the new style, whose family made its money in bolt-cloth and its name in one consulship, wonders whether registries of houses might not benefit from public posting, like tax lists — a joke with a client inside it, since half the chamber has eaten the posting — and is applauded for the nerve and then, very smoothly, ignored for the substance. The Senate is skilled at this two-stroke reception. It has used it on invading armies, with mixed results.

Acilius speaks third, which in his arithmetic is where outcomes are parked. He supports the report as a model of *general* administration — the word lands exactly where Lentulus put it, between the quarters, and the men who understand the filing smile into their beards and documents. He praises the penalty structure. He passes over the clause at a height of perhaps four feet, as a man steps over a stair in the dark he has himself placed there, neither tripping nor calling attention to its useful location. On the bench beside Lentulus, Postumus murmurs, “He's walking it back to the fish,” in a voice so low it can only be heard by history and Onesimus, who is not present but will forget it by evening anyway as a matter of professional solidarity.

The only trouble arrives, as trouble in constitutions does, from a man who believes the paperwork is the point. He is a praetor with a future in provincial audits and a voice built for reading lists, and he wants the committee to define *establishment* for the twenty-fourth time in case houses should, through wit or cookery, migrate between categories overnight. Lentulus rises to him with the patience of a man who has decided to become an aqueduct in public: water arriving, anonymously, where the maps claimed. The definition is given. It is accepted. The praetor, defused by being minuted, sits with the satisfaction of a man who has been given the middle-sized purse at a patron's morning — *encouragement*, not *danger* — and the chamber shuffles itself toward the vote the way crowds shuffle toward an emperor: in a lane that opens before the reason is visible.

It passes the way his wife said it would when it finally became uninteresting enough to govern: without division, because a division would have required several hundred men to register, by name, how they felt about registering houses where women are paid to hear them tell the truth after dinner. The clerk reads the text into the record in the flat delivery of a man who knows the words will outlive the room. At the clause, his voice does not change. That is the whole art of it. *No house to profit by a person whose refusal has no legal existence.* It goes into the record like a stair goes into a column drum: set in stone, invisible from the ground, load-bearing for anyone who ever climbs.

Lentulus does not look at Acilius. Acilius does not look at the clerk. Postumus looks at his hands, where an old ring of his grandfather's has been telling him the weather for forty years. Somewhere in the benches, a senator whose private arrangements have just been described in public Latin begins, with great statesmanship, to revise them in his head: a girl in a lodging near the Carinae whose manumission money his steward is holding against breakages; a music master whose contract would not survive being read aloud by anybody's daughter. Multiply him by the chamber, subtract the men whose arrangements are already clean and the men too old for arrangement, and you have the exact size of the silence under the shuffle of assent: not repentance, nothing so expensive. Administration, arriving at last in the rooms Rome had kept locked and called honest.

The emperor's secretary makes no note that anyone can see. That, too, is entered everywhere that matters by noon.

Postumus, afterwards, in the portico: “That was either statesmanship or surrender. Your family does both in the same accent. Which is the Senate to put in the minutes?”

“The registry will pass.”

“The registry would have passed in December, before you sent a man to measure one woman's doorways at dawn. Now it passes *over* you, old friend. Syria—” Postumus, who loves him, and has that licence, does the un-Roman thing: he lowers his voice for a truth instead of raising it for a crowd. “Acilius tells me the secretaries have begun to say *Hadrian's people for the east*. Athens has been writing letters. Your name is — spoken, Lentulus. In baths. As steam.”

Lentulus watches the Forum performing its noon: the changers, the litigants, the column stone baking in its yard. “I piloted a morals registry through the Senate and put the freedom of the women in it in words they cannot unwrite without reading them aloud. If Syria is the price, Syria was always going to be paid by someone in some currency. My father—” He stops, surprised by the route. “My father would call that a provincial sentiment.”

“Your father,” Postumus says, with feeling, “has been dead nine years and still schedules the household. Come to dinner. My wife has a singer who does Greek, and she sings it for people who know, which tonight, I fear, means us.”

Avidius is waiting in the portico, which is not a thing fathers of prospective sons-in-law are supposed to do outside the Curia, and is therefore the thing Avidius does supremely well: hugely, patiently, in a cloak that costs less than his son's opinions and will outlast them. He has come, officially, about the harbour dues, which were debated before Lentulus's report in the same padded Latin; unofficially, he has come the way cargo comes, to inspect the receiving arrangements.

“Your clause,” he says, without preamble, because preamble is for men who schedule.

“It was the committee's.”

“Harbours are committees too, senator. The clauses that govern them are the ones nobody reads at the wedding feast.” He looks out over the noon Forum, where his world is going up and down in bales under other men's marble. “I employ freedwomen in the counting houses at Ostia. Three of them can read a manifest better than my second son-in-law can read a will. One of them asked me last year — me, Avidius, who came up by the dock ropes — whether her savings in the company's chest were hers *in law* or only in her name. I told her to ask the lawyers. She told me the lawyers were what she was saving against.” He produces, from this parable, the harbourman's shrug that has ratified most of the century's sensible arrangements. “Your sentence will travel, Lentulus. Cargo does.”

“It is a registry clause about lodging houses.”

“So was grain, once. A registry clause about breakfast.” Avidius inclines his great head the smallest degree a rich man can manage without spraining anything. “Tell Servilia the fleet money is comforted. My boy still listens with his face. The figures, when your brother-in-law is ready to stop calling them a climate, are ready to be weather.”

He goes down the steps like a ship leaving a festival: slowly, correctly, with everyone's calculations quietly re-ballasted around him. Postumus watches him go.

“There,” Postumus says. “That is the vote that mattered, and it wasn't in the chamber. Your daughter will be married out of a sentence about brothels, old friend. Rome will call it an alliance. I intend to be insufferable about it at the feast.”

“You intend to be insufferable about everything at the feast. It's your family's one export.”

They walk, because a senator who has just lost a province in the baths' version and gained a son-in-law in the portico's needs to arrive at his own house at human speed, the way people do who are carrying news home in their own hands for a change. The Forum performs its noon around them: changers changing, litigants litigating for practice, the column stone in its yard taking the sun like a beached argument. A boy trots beside them for a whole block selling the day's posted lists — taxes, notices, the text of the registry already being copied by a scribe who will charge a freedwoman half for the clause alone, though nobody in the street knows it yet, least of all the freedwoman, who at present is in a lodging house near the Carinae having her manumission money held against breakages by a man who considers the phrase a form of care.

At the Palatine house, Servilia hears the result the way she hears everything — at the speed other people decide whether to repeat it — which is to say she has already converted it, by the time Lentulus reaches the atrium, into three actions: a letter, a dinner, and a change in the standing instructions about Marcus. The letter to her brother has gone from draft to sealed in the interval. The dinner is Thursday. Marcus is discovered, in the garden, being invisible in full view of the household, with his accounts and his philosophy.

“The committee has reported,” Lentulus tells his wife.

“I know. Acilia's steward's wife dyes my wool.” She hands him the household's version of the text, copied fair by the family clerk, in which the clause sits in its paragraph like a newel post. “Read it,” she says, and it is not a request any more than ministries make them.

He reads it. It reads, on his own paper, in his clerk's hand, like a letter from somebody else's better self, which, in the banking sense, it is. “It will be quoted against me in Athens,” he says. “By secretaries.”

“Then Athens will finally be quoting you for something you wrote after you were twenty-four.” Servilia takes the copy back and puts it where the family's real epic lives, in the chest with the day-book and the Arpinum gratitude and the formulae for refusing people in excellent Latin. “Dinner on Thursday, husband. Plotina's freedwoman. Bring the clause, by all means. Leave Syria at home in its locked room; we are feeding other pets now.”

Somewhere in the house Cornelia is at her lesson. The lyre is corrected, note by note. Then it begins again, and this time nobody corrects it at all.

He goes to Postumus's dinner out of the same instinct that made him walk home at noon: a need to arrive at the evening at human speed, among people who will tell him the baths' version before the fish. Postumus's wife keeps a table at which new money, old malice and extremely good music are seated in proportions Servilia describes, in her letters, as *a harbour in a gale*, and means as praise. The singer is a freedwoman from Antioch with a voice like a road going east, and she sings the Greek ones for people who'd know, and Postumus, meeting Lentulus's eye across a sauce his cook has clearly stolen from a better house and improved with money, raises his cup a fraction: *I told you I feared this*.

Acilius is there, being spontaneous in his rehearsed way among the second-course conversations. With him is a secretary of the emperor's household, short-sighted and vain about it, the same man Antonia once catalogued from the margin of a dedication crowd among contractors' wives, and he is retailing the day in the flat tones secretaries keep for matters that have been decided elsewhere. The registry: approved as dull; dullness being, at that altitude, a recommendation. The clause: *noted*. The word comes across the table with the boar and is caught, as words are at Postumus's dinners, by three conversations at once.

“Noted by whom?” asks a senator whose nephew died in the spring of very little that could be registered, and whose interest in houses is therefore presumed philosophical.

“By the people who note things, in Athens as in Rome,” says the secretary, without looking up from the operation of his knife. “Hadrian writes that an empire which cannot say what a refusal *is* will spend a great deal of money finding out province by province. He says it in Greek, which makes it sound less like an administrative warning.”

The table laughs in the way tables do when a future has just been served among the courses and everyone is deciding how much of it to eat in public. Lentulus, with the portion of his mind that is no longer under option to anyone, hears his dawn sentence arrive back at him translated, framed, and lightly garnished with Athens. Syria, he understands, is leaving the room by the same door the clause came in. It goes quietly. It has been, in his life, a sentence accepted too, in a way — a place his family's kind of man was for, the way marble is for columns — and he has never till tonight watched himself not grieve an inheritance in public and call the not-grieving statesmanship too. Perhaps, his father's kitchen voice says out of nine years of brine, choosing better is also a province, but you govern it from inside the house.

Postumus's singer begins again for the last set — Greek, lower, travelled. A young man at the low end of the table, some connection's son being shown the capital like a prospectus, asks loudly whether songs like that were the sort the committee meant to register, and the room does the thing rooms have been doing all year in Lentulus's hearing: it laughs, and stops, and looks, not at the singer, but at him.

“No,” Lentulus says, into the small, specific silence. “The committee registered the houses. The songs were always going to be above our competence.”

It gets the laugh back, with interest, and Acilius inclines his head by the half degree a consul can manage without spraining the future. The secretary does not write it down. It will be in Athens by the new moon anyway, improved, like everything else the plumbing carries uphill: *a Cornelius with a freedwoman's conscience, who has stopped applying for the east and started drafting for it.* Lentulus drinks to nobody in particular, and thinks of a sealed tablet being smoothed by a dead man's hand, and of a girl across two rivers of paperwork holding her own copy this time, and lets the Greek song travel, lower down, all the way to where the keeping happens.

The language of a report is a public room with all its furniture turned to the wall. Postumus prefers it that way. He reads the draft aloud in Lentulus’s study, giving every cautious phrase the dignity of a man reading his own future inscription and finding the lettering tasteful.

“‘Prejudicial to established custom,’” he says. “That is good.”

“It is not good,” Lentulus says. “It is roomy.”

“In reports, roominess is a virtue.”

“In houses, it is called a corridor.”

A Secretary at the side table is making three copies because the original may have to survive being understood by men with appointments. Diomedes is not present—Diomedes is never present in rooms where the Senate writes about people who pay his house’s fees—but his habits have colonised Lentulus’s vocabulary to an extent the senator is only beginning to invoice. Every clause now asks, at the back of Lentulus’s mind, where the copy is kept and whose hand is allowed to be honest.

Postumus objects to a sentence about “conditions of service” on the grounds that it implies conditions might differ.

“They do differ,” Lentulus says.

“Not in the report’s preferred universe.”

“Then let the report apply for citizenship in ours.”

The Secretary pauses with his pen in the air, a young man learning that history sometimes turns up as an argument over adjectives before lunch. Postumus sighs with the profundity of a Roman whose principles have encountered paperwork. “You have become troublesome in a minor key.”

“I am told it improves audibility.”

“By whom, the water board?”

“By women with copies.”

That, finally, is too much even for a room arranged to prevent meaning. Postumus looks up. Lentulus looks level. The sentence has entered the study in the wrong hand, so to speak, and can no longer be removed without smudging. The Secretary does not write it. It goes instead into the air of the committee, where many true sentences spend a season before anyone invents a heading that can survive them.

When the draft is done, Lentulus keeps no special copy. That is new. The Greek will have one eventually; the house on the Caelian will have one sooner; the Senate will deny needing one. Rome, as always, is being governed by distribution.

The news reaches the Caelian, as all news does, in three strengths: the door-book version by noon, the baths version by dinner, and Diomedes's version, which is the facts with their shoes on, by the time the evening guests are at the fish. Antonia hears them all. Her house is to be registered quarterly, like the tax, like the licence, like the weather. The clause — Diomedes reads it from the posted text with the neutrality he uses for death notices — about persons whose refusal must have legal existence, she hears standing still in her own kitchen with a tasting spoon struck dumb in her hand.

The first version arrives before noon on a human back, as most of the house's essentials do. It is carried by a wine carter who once tried to smuggle Campanian past Barbatus in the dark and now overpays his debt to the house in news, delivered at the yard door with the amphorae: *the committee's done; it's general; you're all to be registered and nobody's to be closed before you've been apologised to by appointment.* He is tipped in soup. In the door-book, Diomedes enters him under *intelligence, vintage of*, with a note to adjust his cartage at the next quarter in recognition of services to the cellar's morale.

The second version arrives with the afternoon's first caller, a grain man from Puteoli who cancelled three bookings in the inspection weather and now wishes, with a merchant's memory for tides, to uncancel all three and add a fourth as ballast. “A house that's survived the Senate,” he says, settling into Antonia's presence like cargo into a hold, “is a house a man can do business in without his wife's brother writing letters. No offence to the committee.”

“None taken on its behalf. I file offences quarterly, and the committee has had its quarter.” She moves him from apology to menu in two sentences, because apology unconverted is a cost a house carries twice. Behind her, Diomedes enters the rebookings in the second hand, the one for facts that must survive fashion, and the house feels, along its corridors, the small arterial change of a calendar filling up.

The third version — the facts with their shoes on — Diomedes fetches himself from the posting place, having sent nobody who might improve it in transit. He has the text copied entire. This costs money. Money, in this house, is spent on paper the way other houses spend it on priests: early, regularly, and in the belief that disasters prefer unregistered premises. He lays the copy out on the kitchen's least floury table, because Antonia, who is tasting sauces against the return of the Alexandrians, conducting the foreign policy of the evening from beside a stove, will not leave the kitchen in the middle of a sauce for anything less than an emperor, a fire, or a clause.

“The registry is as madam was advised,” he says, reading as a man reads a bill he has already paid. “General. Quarterly. By appointment. Inspections at stated hours, by officials named in advance, in the public rooms.” He says *public rooms* in a way that costs Ammonius, at the great stove, an audible snort into the saffron. “Penalties for unregistered persons are struck out. In their place, a provision concerning house-keepers.” The stylus behind his ear stays where it is. In eleven years it has only once been taken down for emotion, and the kitchen was informed of it for a week. “Shall I read the provision, madam?”

“Read the provision, Diomedes.”

He reads it in his death-notice neutrality, standing in a kitchen in a house built out of stairs, and the women shelling peas for the Alexandrians stop shelling at *refusal* the way a room once stopped laughing between courses. Philomela, who is resting her voice upside down on a stool with her hair in a towel — a professional posture nobody feels entitled to question — sits up slowly, like an audience in one body. Daphne, who has been mending at the table's far end because in this house even the singers mend, puts the needle down carefully, so that history shall not later claim she dropped it.

“Again,” Antonia says, in the voice she uses for menus and mercy.

He reads it again.

Outside the kitchen window the courtyard performs its winter afternoon: the damp map of a country drying off the wall at last, Barbatus supervising a delivery of lamp oil with thumbs the size of small hammers, a girl with a basket walking the long way past the music room. Inside, nobody does anything with the sentence for a moment except keep it. Keeping, in this house's book and a fuller's across the river, is a complete action. Then Daphne says, “That is the sentence you gave him,” not as a question, because Daphne has the singer's disease and believes every room has a song under it, and today the song and the sentence have arrived in the same post.

“I gave nobody anything.” Antonia takes the tasting spoon back from wherever her hand has parked it in the air and returns it to the sauce, which has been waiting with a patience the Senate might study. “I described, in a banker's office, what a registry would have to contain if it was to be a registry and not a raid. A senator drafted. Men who draft should be encouraged with soup, Daphne, not attribution. Attribution is a tax.”

Philomela, upright now, with the towel slipping, says, “In Corinth we had a saying about generals who win battles other people planned: they get the processions, we get—”

“—the contracts,” Antonia says. “Yes. At the new year. Every woman in this house, with that provision cited in her own hand at the foot of her terms. Diomedes will prepare the copies. Yours, Philomela, may be in Greek, for people who'd know. Daphne's will be in a hand large enough for auditors.” She says it as housekeeping. The kitchen hears it as something else and files it as housekeeping anyway — that is how revolutions are entered in the first book, upright and dull, so that they may survive to be interesting in the second.

The pastry cook, who has strong views on saffron and none that he voices on politics, says, “Mistress?”

“More salt than that, in the sauce for the Alexandrians. They will be back now. Men come back when a house has survived the Senate; it is like a recommendation from a temple.” She gives him the spoon. “Diomedes. Read the clause again.”

He reads it again. Latin, exact, unlovely — the most beautiful letter she has ever not received.

That evening the house fills in its ordinary, extraordinary way, except that ordinariness — for three months a commodity under inspection — now commands a premium the day-book records under *room hire, post-senatorial*. The Alexandrians' agent sends to book the great room for the fleet signing, with Prima's hanging to be left up for the occasion, a compliment itemised in the message among the courses as if cloth were a sauce. Serapion himself is still at sea, but Heron dines in the small room with the tax farmers, celebrating a partnership at its third resurrection, in a house whose legal shape has this morning been re-drawn by a committee into something a Greek can bank against. The elderly advocate holds his court. The equestrian widow laughs twice, the second time without checking the door. Rome, having inspected the premises and found a kitchen, goes back to eating in it as if it had never stopped, which is the highest form of acquittal the city issues.

Eirene does the late round with Antonia, lamps going down front to back, the theatre unlit in reverse. At the linen room door she says, “Well. We are a registered scandal. It is entered. What does madam propose for an encore? I ask as junior partner in catastrophe; the position is otherwise underoccupied this month.”

“Prima,” Antonia says. It is the first time she has put the word at the head of a day's agenda rather than in its margins, under thread. “Tomorrow is Thursday. The hanging comes home from the Alexandrians. Her father is no worse, which across the river means he is worse at a statelier pace. The guild papers from the Greek should be ready by the new moon.” She stops at the office door, where Diomedes has set out, in a row, the evening's small dignities: the rebookings, the posted text, a bowl of olives with one missing because Eirene has celebrated the survival of the house in her own way, standing up. “I am not going to spend her on this, Eirene. Not on a clause, not on a committee, not on being right in a banker's window room. The telling comes when it comes, in the order of the evening, with the fish a long way behind us.”

“And the senator?”

“The senator has had his clause read into the record by a clerk with no inflection. It is the closest his family gets to a love letter, and he arranged it himself, at dawn, unshaven, in his own hand. Let Athens quote him.” She looks at the posted text, flat under Diomedes' paperweight — a worn bronze of a river god with an expression of a man who has read the small print since Romulus. “Tomorrow, send to Transtiberim that the house will want the spring work again. By the piece. Thread separate. And Eirene—”

“Love.”

“The next time I say I am not counting catastrophes yet either, charge me the widow's rate for self-deception. We are on the third by any book that matters, and it is not yet the vintage.”

“File it,” she says, “with the tax receipts.”

“Which quarter?”

“Between the quarters. With the rest of his correspondence.”
