# Under Bonifraterska

*Warsaw, 10–12 May 1943*

Two nights after German sappers pumped gas into the command bunker under Miła Eighteen, the municipal sewer inspector knocked on Halina's attic window on Tamka at nine-fifteen and took her four thousand złoty without counting the fifties.

By eleven-forty Sylwia was lying flat on her stomach alongside Halina inside the tarred plank false-bottom of a two-horse municipal sanitary dray creaking north up Marszałkowska under full curfew *Nachtfahrt* lantern permits. Six inches above the backs of their necks lay twelve heavy empty pine hospital coffins being hauled nominally from *Dzieciątka Jezus* to the Powązki municipal carpenter's yard for zinc-lining repairs; through cracks between the planks smelled chloride of lime, horse sweat, and sweetish warm May night air tainted thick as grease with smoke blowing off the dying fires of Nalewki.

Nobody spoke inside the nineteen-inch crawl space. Every time iron-rimmed wheels bounced across tram-switches at Królewska—two hundred yards west of Judge Silberstein's dark windows where Ludwig Schoeler was doubtless sleeping or burning blue flimsies—Sylwia's shoulder bumped Halina's rigid arm, and Halina's teeth clicked once together in the pitch dark without a breath escaping.

Past Senatorska the driver turned east off Theatre Square into a narrow cobbled service cul-de-sac behind a bombed-out printing works off **ulica Długa**, four hundred meters south of the Bonifraterska wall where orange searchlight beams and Magnesium parachute flares still hung white and theatrical every three minutes above blackened brick chimneys.

Wood scraped iron; coffins slid six inches rearward as the drayman unlatched the tailboard.

"Eight minutes," whispered a hoarse voice stinking of raw spirit and sewer methane—Kazik, the fifty-year-old water-board ganger, already prying up the round cast-iron inspection cover tucked inside the ruined printer's archway with a greased crowbar so iron didn't ring on stone. "Ukrainian patrol walks past the outer corner every quarter-hour. Two of my boys went down with ropes twenty minutes ago to guide them south from the storm-junction under Krasiński. Only four made it past the iron weir—Stroop's engineers rigged hand-grenades on tripwires across the main four-foot brick collector at noon."

Up came the hundred-pound iron lid onto folded burlap sacks; out of the black twenty-four-inch hole in the cobbles belched a hot, suffocating wave of fermented human excrement, illuminating-gas condensate, dead rat, and bitter almond-chlorine residue from German tear-gas candles dropped up-line that afternoon.

Sylwia didn't gag—four winters of ruptured gangrenous appendices cured gagging—tying a vinegar-soaked gauze pad tight over her nose and mouth, strapping her flat canvas burn-pouch crosswise over her chest, and swinging rubber-booted legs straight down the slimy iron wall-rungs ahead of Halina because Halina weighed forty-eight kilos and couldn't haul drowned deadweight vertical up iron rungs alone.

Ten feet down, her boots hit two feet of rushing black sludge moving swift toward the Vistula outfall.

Down inside the four-foot ovoid brick vault, sound behaved insanely: street patrols twenty feet above vanished altogether, replaced by hollow roaring water-echoes, dripping lime stalactites grazing her kerchiefed hair when she bent double, and—forty yards north up the pitch-black tunnel—faint rhythmic splashing coupled with torn, whistling human breaths that sounded like broken bellows pumped under water.

One tiny shielded bicycle-dynamo red lens blinked twice out of darkness. Two water-board men in thigh-waders materialized bent nearly horizontal under the low brick crown, dragging between them three figures who could no longer walk upright because forty-eight hours crouching knee-deep in pitch-black freezing sewage without food or drinking water locked quadriceps and hamstring tendons into stone cramps. Behind them stumbled a fourth—taller, bony, bareheaded, supporting a tiny girl no bigger than twelve-year-old bone structure hooked over his burned left shoulder while feeling along slimy brick with an empty oxidized Belgian Browning pistol welded shut into his right fist by dried blood and rust!

Halina shoved past Sylwia's hip in two feet of rushing filth, biting clean through her vinegar gauze mask so not one echo carried up the iron shaft as her hands hit the tall boy's wet, soot-blackened face.

"*Dawid—*"

Barely a breath—soundless tear of air—as the twenty-four-year-old boy flinched violently backward against brick at human touch before recognizing his sister's voice through sewer roar.

Up close under Sylwia's hooded red surgical pencil-torch, Dawid Różański looked eighty years old: eyebrows and front hair singed clean off down to blistered scalp, right eye swollen half-shut around soot-bleeding conjunctiva, left cornea milky opaque white where burning white-phosphorus or lime splatter had seared epithelium two days ago on Świętojerska, shirt burned completely off his left scapular blade leaving raw charred dermis weeping yellow plasma mixed with black sewer water.

"Take Rachela first," Dawid croaked—voice burned down to raw vocal-cord whisper from inhaling superheated bunker smoke, shoving the tiny girl off his raw shoulder straight into Sylwia's arms before letting Halina touch him. "Swallowed smoke when they blew the ventilation pipe at Franciszkańska—she's drowning sitting up—"

Sylwia caught the girl—sixteen, maybe seventeen, weighing less than Staś's bicycle, wet wool dress hanging heavy with forty pounds of sewage—and heard without a stethoscope the coarse bubbling pink foam boiling straight up out of trachea and nostrils with every agonized gasping heave: acute phosgene-or-smoke pulmonary edema flooding both lungs solid!

"Up the rungs—fast!" Sylwia hissed, hoisting the girl upward under wet armpits while Kazik leaned down through the manhole collar above to grab wrists and drag her clear into the dark courtyard archway, followed inside ninety seconds by two silent boys from Hashomer Hatzair with shrapnel-chewed calves and last of all Dawid himself—having his cramped legs pushed rung by rung from beneath by Sylwia's shoulder while Halina pulled desperately from above until all four lay hidden flat inside the false plank coffin-bed!

Down went the cast-iron manhole cover onto greased burlap without a clink; twelve empty pine coffins slid booming back over the trapdoor; whip cracked once over the horses' rumps as Ukrainian *Trawniki* auxiliary boots clicked loud round Długa corner eighty yards east!

Inside the pitch-black nineteen-inch crawl space rolling south down Marszałkowska toward Lindleya, packed six human bodies abreast stinking of excrement and burnt flesh so close ribs ground against ribs, sixteen-year-old Rachela died against Sylwia's collarbone before the dray passed Saski Garden.

Nothing heroic or clean happened in nineteen inches of dark plank: Sylwia tried clearing pink froth out of the girl's throat with two bare fingers hooked behind the tongue base, injected half a milligram of atropine and two milliliters of camphor-in-oil straight through wet dress cloth into pectoral muscle by blind touch, even tried turning her prone so fluid drained downward—and at Królewska corner (fifty yards from chestnut trees and white café awnings!) the small wet ribcage gave two convulsive shudders against Sylwia's breast, blew warm bloody foam across Sylwia's neck, and stopped moving forever.

Beside Sylwia in the dark, Dawid felt the stillness through his sister's arm without asking. Nobody wept; cry aloud inside a sanitary cart passing Marszałkowska night gendarmes and six more people died before Towarowa. Only Halina's freezing fingers locked around Sylwia's wrist for ten crushing seconds in pitch blackness, and let go when the wheels turned hollow through *Dzieciątka Jezus*'s back mortuary lane.

* * *

From two a.m. until quarter past five in the brick contagious-dead-house annex behind Pavilion One—windowless, freezing cold, protected by four genuine typhus corpses lying tagged in open pine boxes along the outer vestibule slab so even night porters crossed themselves and stayed twenty yards clear—Sylwia, Bronka, and Halina worked without pause under two hooded paraffin surgery lamps.

Sixteen-year-old Rachela needed no forged papers now: Bronka washed sewer filth gently off the thin child-face with warm water, wrapped her in a clean hospital shroud under yesterday's unclaimed female typhus charity-ward slip (*NN — femina circa 18, ex via Krochmalna*), and nailed the pine lid down herself with muffled hammer-strokes padded through folded felt so tomorrow's municipal burial lorry carried her uninspected to Bródno pit with a priest's Latin instead of Gestapo photographs.

The two Hashomer boys had clean shrapnel leg wounds without major bone smash; dressed, fed hot sweet barley broth with forty drops of rectified spirit, injected with three thousand units each of horse tetanus antitoxin, and buttoned into dirty municipal coal-heaver overalls Bronka kept ready, they went out at four-forty in a baker's flour wagon toward a railway safe-house at Włochy before first light.

Dawid could not travel twenty yards without dying of burn shock or septicemia.

Thirty percent body-surface second- and patchy third-degree flame-and-phosphorus burns stretched from left trapezius across scapula down to tenth rib, crusted black with sewer silt and brick dust. Give general ether to a smoke-damaged lung and pneumonia killed him inside thirty-six hours; clean thirty percent raw burns soaked in human sewage *without* anesthesia killed by neurogenic shock.

Sylwia gave twenty milligrams intravenous morphine slow into his right forearm vein, waited eight minutes until his single seeing right pupil contracted down to a black pinhead and his breathing slowed to fourteen deep draws, then spent ninety merciless minutes with sterile forceps, warm physiological saline, and neutral soap lifting every single black shred of blistered epidermis and sewer-contaminated slough off living dermis before painting the entire raw crimson back black-violet with five-percent silver nitrate and warm sterile tannic-acid jelly to seal weeping plasma loss under dry coagulum.

Halina held his forehead steady on folded towels across her own knees the whole ninety minutes without dropping one tear onto sterile linen, murmuring low Polish nursery nonsense from Warsaw Nalewki courtyard rhymes into his blistered ear whenever forceps hit unburned nerve edge.

Last came the left eye.

Under magnifying loupe with lid-retractors gently separating swollen palpebral fissures after four drops of precious cocaine-hydrochloride surface solution salvaged out of Mirecki's prewar ophthalmic box, Sylwia irrigated three tiny embedded white-phosphorus ash specks out of the inferior conjunctival fornix before they burned deeper through sclera. Central cornea over the pupil was cooked dense porcelain-white—permanent leukoma; he would never read print through that left eye again unless somebody thirty years hence grafted donor cornea—yet the globe hadn't perforated, zero hypopyon pus sat in the anterior chamber, and a crescent of clear peripheral iris survived temporally enough to catch light and shadow if infection stayed out! Atropine sulfate one-percent ointment in, sterile petrolatum eye-pad taped smooth.

At five-fifteen they slid Dawid—sleeping deep under morphine on dry rubberized ticking—into the brick recessed cooling-alcove behind the towering stoneware formaldehyde carboys, screened off by stacked coffin-lids painted red with *FLECKFIEBER — NICHT BERÜHREN!* Until seventy-two-hour burn plasma shift stabilized and Bronka's brother-in-law brought a turf-cutter's wagon from Konstancin woods on Friday night, Halina's brother lived six feet behind dead typhus paupers fifty yards across slushy gravel from Pavilion Three.

Out in the grey courtyard dawn smelled suddenly clean of wet lilac and bakery smoke—Muranów's wind had shifted east toward Praga.

Sylwia stood behind the boiler-house coal pile stripping her sewer-soaked wool skirt, stockings, and blouse straight off into a galvanized tub of boiling cresol water Bronka had waiting, scrubbing her bare skin from neck to ankles with coarse yellow lye-soap in ten-degree morning draft until her ribs and breasts turned bright lobster-pink and the phantom stickiness of sixteen-year-old Rachela's bloody lung-foam finally washed off her collarbone.

Pulling on clean rough ward linen under a fresh white smock, drinking black barley *plujka* scalding out of an enamel mug while sparrows chirped insanely cheerful along the eaves, she felt something hard and irreducible settle behind her breastbone: no exhaustion left room for fear anymore.

Staying awake through Tuesday's eight-o'clock surgical list took black coffee and cold tap-water splashed behind both ears every forty minutes, because ten o'clock brought the municipal burial lorry backing its iron-rimmed tailgate right up against the contagious dead-house threshold fifty yards across the yard.

Standing by Ward Two's tall landing window with post-operative charts clamped tight against her ribs while pretending to dictate dressing changes to Dr. Jankowski, Sylwia watched two municipal grave-carters in carbolic-soaked canvas aprons sling hooks into the five nailed pine boxes lined along the outer vestibule slab—four genuine charity-ward typhus dead plus sixteen-year-old Rachela sleeping weightless under *NN — femina circa 18, ex via Krochmalna*. Six feet behind that inner brick partition, separated only by stacked red-stenciled *FLECKFIEBER* coffin lids and stoneware carboys of forty-percent formalin, Dawid Różański lay flat on rubberized ticking with silver-nitrate coagulum stiffening across thirty percent of his back. If burn delirium broke through fading midnight morphine while those two municipal carters heaved pine boxes across stone—if one cracked, smoke-burned Hebrew or Yiddish syllable rasped out from behind the formaldehyde carboys—both carters ran straight to Walenty's gatehouse shouting ghosts or Jews, where two *Schutzpolizei* troopers stood smoking cigarettes beside a flat bicycle tire!

Nothing moved behind the carboys. Rachela's light pine box slid *thump* onto the lorry bed third from left; canvas straps buckled tight; horses leaned into leather collars and hauled Nalewki's daughter out through iron gates toward consecrated pauper sand without a German boot lifting off the kerb.

Sylwia had barely exhaled half a breath when downstairs through Pavilion Three's rear garden door stepped **Docent Tadeusz Mirecki** himself—hatless in May sunshine, linen laboratory duster flapping round narrow trousers, carrying an empty two-liter ground-glass jar straight across the gravel path toward the contagious dead-house to draw concentrated formalin off the reserve carboys for a resected gastric-carcinoma specimen!

Sylwia took the stone ward stairs three treads at a jump without seeming to run—skirting the lilac hedge fast enough to step across the dead-house threshold four seconds behind Mirecki just as the old surgeon set his glass jar down on the zinc draining board and paused, nostrils flaring sharp behind gold-rimmed pince-nez.

Forty-percent formaldehyde stung nose and conjunctiva fiercely inside the windowless brick vault; yet underneath formaldehyde—unmistakable to a man who had spent forty years walking surgical burn-wards from Vienna to Warsaw—hung the faint, sweetish-astringent hospital tang of **fresh warm tannic-acid jelly, silver-nitrate protein crust, and living human sweat**.

Mirecki did not touch the brass spigot on the stoneware carboy.

Very slowly his watery blue eyes shifted left past the carboy necks toward the dark recessed cooling-alcove blocked off by three propped pine coffin-lids painted *FLECKFIEBER — NICHT BERÜHREN!* Down along the damp brick mortar-joint two inches above floor level trailed one dried black drop of five-percent silver nitrate splashed off Sylwia's forceps at four a.m.

"Since when," Mirecki asked in a voice barely louder than mouse-claw on slate, without turning his grey head from the coffin-lids, "do dead typhus paupers in cold brick alcoves smell of fresh hospital tannic-acid paste and twenty-milligram morphine ampoules, Pani Doktor?"

Sylwia closed the heavy oak dead-house door quietly behind her own spine—dropping the iron latch noiselessly into its slot so zero sunlight or yard shadow crossed the threshold—and stood three paces behind her chief with both raw hands hanging open at her sides.

Tell him a lie, and forty years of clinical authority felt insulted into panic; tell him a name, and if Aleja Szucha ever twisted Mirecki's thin sixty-two-year-old wrists downstairs, he carried fatal freight in his skull.

"Nobody is in that alcove, *Panie Docencie*, after eleven o'clock tonight when turf-carts load behind Koszykowa," Sylwia said quietly in Polish. "Until eleven o'clock tonight, if anybody opens those three coffin-lids, neither you nor I nor sixty beds upstairs see Saturday."

Mirecki stood motionless for six full seconds staring at the red *FLECKFIEBER* stencil six feet ahead of his shoes. Through the thin linen shoulders of his coat Sylwia saw the slight senile tremor that always took his left scapula after two hours standing at the operating table—an honorable prewar Warsaw professor who hated loud noises, kept his German tax receipts filed in three neat drawers, crossed the street when gendarmes shouted, and paid five hundred złoty every month out of his shrunken stipend into All Saints' poor-box without signing his name.

Behind the stacked pine lids, six feet away in pitch shadow, somebody drew one slow, ragged, whistling breath through smoke-scorched vocal cords—and held it dead still.

Mirecki's chin flinched half an inch; he did not step forward, and he did not step back.

Instead he bent stiffly over the stoneware formaldehyde carboy, turned the ground-glass spigot open until two liters of pungent clear preservative hissed up to the neck of his specimen jar, stoppered it tight, and straightened up facing Sylwia in dim grey door-crack gloom.

"Our night dispensary ledger," Mirecki remarked dryly, looking six inches past her left ear at the whitewashed brick wall, "shows thirty grams of tannic-acid burn jelly and four ampoules of two-centigram morphine unaccounted for since Saturday's foundry scalding on Wola. Kindly enter them on Bed Twelve's foundry chart before municipal auditors check narcotics on Friday, Pani Doktor—sloppy pharmacy arithmetic gives Stabsarzt Vesper indigestion."

He reached into his side coat-pocket, drew out his own spare brass mortuary padlock key—the duplicate second key nobody save the surgical chief carried!—and laid it silently on the cold zinc draining board beside Sylwia's hip so no third porter could open the dead-house before midnight.

"And give five hundred milliliters of warm subcutaneous Ringer's solution before moving extensive second-degree burns by cart," Mirecki added softly with one hand on the oak latch. "Secondary plasma collapse hits hardest round hour twenty-four, even in robust young subjects."

Then the old man slipped out into May lilac sun without looking back once, carrying his glass carcinoma jar upright across the gravel as fussy and unremarkable as any sixty-two-year-old museum curator in Europe.

At ten-forty-five that Tuesday night, after eight hundred milliliters of warm subcutaneous saline under both clavicles steadied Dawid's radial pulse down to a firm ninety-two and fresh sterile vaseline gauze cushioned his black tannic-acid carapace against jolting boards, Sylwia and Bronka carried him slung low inside a clean canvas laundry-sheet out the boiler-lane postern straight into the hollowed peat-turf bed of Bronka's brother-in-law's country cart. Halina didn't even come out past the cellar shadow lest some night window spot her pale face—she simply pressed one tiny folded square of greased paper holding her dead mother's gold wedding band into Sylwia's palm to slide inside Dawid's knotted shirt-hem before dry brown bog-peat stacked loosely over breathing slats hid him clean under country stars rolling south toward Konstancin pines.

* * *

Fifteen hours later, at two o'clock on Wednesday afternoon the twelfth of May, circular rip-saws screamed through wet pine logs inside Brzeziński's timber yard on ulica Towarowa—*zeeeeeeee-krrrrk!*—throwing yellow resin-smelling sawdust ankle-deep across the yard and drowning human voices at four paces so thoroughly that Marek didn't even lower his chin when he stepped beside Sylwia behind stacked scaffolding planks.

Sylwia hadn't seen Marek alone since Saski Garden four weeks earlier—having answered two intermediary notes with polite hospital excuses about Vesper's typhus spot-checks while feeding one harmless typed note on Wehrmacht frostbite train numbers up the drop so nobody smelled sudden silence.

Looking at him today in hard May sunlight between pine stacks, seeing the familiar grey eyes, crooked nose-bridge, and dog-wool jacket of the man who had lain between her thighs on Koszykowa in February while knowing *Elektoralna fourteen* had gone from his ear straight into Reinhart Kroll's Gestapo log inside forty-six hours, Sylwia marveled coldly at how seamlessly her own face produced calm collegial attention.

"Vesper didn't cancel your Friday five-o'clock pass to Królewska?" Marek asked over the scream of the thirty-inch steel blade twenty yards left.

"Signed through the end of May," Sylwia said, brushing pine sawdust off her sleeve. "Twice weekly—Tuesdays and Fridays. Why?"

"Because Friday the fourteenth is the last afternoon *Oberstleutnant* Schoeler spends in Warsaw before flying to Berlin-Tempelhof Monday morning with six months of *Abwehr* Eastern counter-espionage summaries in his briefcase," Marek said cleanly.

Sylwia's pulse didn't jump—simply noted the lie or half-truth immediately: yesterday afternoon at five o'clock on Królewska, Ludwig had mentioned casually while she stretched his shoulder that *Hauptmann von Berg* was flying routine quarterly returns to Tirpitzufer next Monday while Ludwig himself remained in Warsaw! Why did Marek's Section II desk think—or *claim*—Ludwig was carrying files to Berlin Monday?

Marek reached inside his waistcoat ticket-pocket and pressed a tiny cylindrical cardboard pill-tube no longer than Sylwia's thumb directly into her gloved hand.

Inside, cushioned in grey cotton wool, sat one flame-sealed amber glass two-milliliter ampoule without factory label, bearing a single scratch of a diamond glass-cutter on its neck.

"Two-percent procaine hydrochloride solution containing forty milligrams anhydrous aconitine nitrate and strychnine base," Marek said, voice flat as bridge-girder arithmetic beneath the howling rip-saw. "Clear as tap water, pH adjusted so it doesn't sting sharper than ordinary Novocain on needle entry. You still infiltrate two milliliters locally around his lateral pectoral scar cord on Fridays for nerve-traction pain?"

She hadn't injected procaine into Ludwig's scar since the seventeenth of April—she used plain lanolin and bare thumbs—yet three weeks ago on her dummy report slip she *had* written *"weekly procaine scar-infiltration continuing"* to make her continued green *Sonderausweis* sound unimpeachably clinical to Section II!

"Two milliliters subcutaneously above the coracoid," Sylwia lied without blinking.

"Inject this instead at five-fifteen Friday," Marek ordered. "Subcutaneous absorption gives you six to nine clean minutes before paresthesia and ventricular fibrillation hit—plenty of time to snap your bag shut, walk down the back pantry stairs past his corporal, and clear the Królewska barrier before he drops off his chair. Autopsy at *Lazarett Ujazdowski* finds needle-puncture matching four weeks' logged procaine blocks, acute pulmonary congestion, zero gross poison lesions—classic delayed procaine idiosyncratic syncope, two cases in every surgical textbook. Special Court liquidation warrant was signed at ten this morning by *Góra*."

Saw-teeth hit a pine knot with an ear-splitting shriek that made sawdust jump on plank ends.

Sylwia held the tiny cardboard cylinder steady in her palm, looking straight into Marek's grey eyes from eighteen inches away.

Every piece of the February-to-May puzzle locked together with sickening mechanical beauty:

1. On February ninth in Ochota, Ludwig met someone bringing proof of *"Zegar"* inside AK Section II—and Kroll's squad ambushed both cars to protect Kroll's star source.
2. On April fifteenth, Ludwig slipped warning of *"Zegar" in Section II* through St. Charles Borromeo Church on Chłodna to London's independent audit channel!
3. Now London's audit inquiry had started rattling cages high up inside Warsaw District headquarters—terrifying *"Zegar"* inside Section II that if Ludwig Schoeler stayed alive in Warsaw another fortnight comparing notes with Berlin or Western cut-outs, *"Zegar"* got unmasked and shot in a cellar!
4. So *"Zegar"* forged or pushed through an emergency Section II liquidation order handing Sylwia Kotarska four centigrams of aconitine to murder the single man in Warsaw who knew *"Zegar"* existed—knowing full well that if German toxicologists in Berlin *did* run alkaloid tests on an *Abwehr* lieutenant-colonel dropping dead ten minutes after a Polish doctor's needle, Reinhart Kroll immediately arrested and hanged Sylwia Kotarska too, burying *both* ends of the *Chłodna 22 / Elektoralna 14* trail in one afternoon!

"Did you see the signed Special Court warrant yourself, Marek?" Sylwia asked quietly as the saw-blade spun down whining between logs.

For one micro-second—barely the blink of a shutter—something defensive tightened round Marek's mouth. *No,* that flicker said plain as print: *Inżynier Stach handed him the ampoule verbally over table-cloths!* Marek wasn't *Zegar*; Marek was *Zegar*'s loyal, blind, lethally obedient hammer, handing his own former lover a suicide needle without asking twice because engineers obeyed blueprints!

"Written court warrants aren't carried through street checks in May," Marek said stiffly. "Verbal confirmation from Section Chief direct. After Friday five-fifteen you don't go back to Koszykowa or Lindleya—walk straight to the Ursuline convent garden gate on Wiślana with your bag; clean *Kennkarte* and railway travel papers to Lublin district surgical inspectorate are waiting under Sister Teresa's blotter."

*Bet Sister Teresa's garden gate has a dark-grey Adler Trumpf Ost-44189 parked across the street at five-forty-five,* Sylwia thought with icy, murderous calm.

"Understood," she said evenly, sliding the cardboard tube containing forty milligrams of aconitine deep into her jacket pocket. "Friday at five-fifteen."

Marek looked almost relieved—reaching out briefly as in winter to touch two dry fingers toward the side of her cheek.

Sylwia didn't flinch away this time; she stood rock-still and let dry paper-warm skin brush her cheekbone for half a second, feeling nothing at all except clinical pity for a dead man walking who didn't even know whose shovel he was carrying, before turning cleanly out through the timber-yard gate into Towarowa dust.
