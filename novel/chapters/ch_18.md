# Luisenstraße, 1933

*Warsaw, 14 July 1943 — Berlin, February–March 1933*

Stabsarzt Doktor Kurt Vesper always smelled heaviest of Yardley lavender water on mornings when somebody from Aleja Szucha had frightened his bowels loose before lunch.

At twelve-twenty on Wednesday the fourteenth of July, Vesper sat sweating inside Docent Mirecki's ground-floor consulting room—commandeered whenever Vesper visited Lindleya because Mirecki's leather armchair had softer springs than the dispensary stool—pouring himself two fingers of Martell cognac out of a flat pocket-flask into Mirecki's water tumbler while Sylwia stood across the blotter waiting for his signature on forty typhus-vaccine requisitions for Wola.

Spread open to the left of Vesper's cognac glass lay a thick, buff-colored Reich archival registry folder bound with red-white-black string and stamped across its top cover in purple Gothic block letters:

*REICHSÄRZTEKAMMER BERLIN — ZENTRALARCHIV DER FRIEDRICH-WILHELMS-UNIVERSITÄT (AUSZUG FÜR K.D.S. WARSCHAU / REF. IV A).*

*K.d.S. Warschau / Referat IV A.* Reinhart Kroll's desk on Aleja Szucha.

Sylwia kept her eyes politely fixed on Vesper's fountain pen without letting her gaze stray two millimeters leftward toward the buff folder, though upside-down from three feet away she had already read her own maiden matriculation line inside half a heartbeat: *Kotarska, Sylwia Helena — geb. 14.XI.1910 Posen — Matr.-Nr. Med. 28/412.*

"Routine credential harmonization across all municipal epidemic establishments serving Wehrmacht billets," Vesper muttered pompously, blotting his violet signature crooked across the Wola vaccine sheet while his stomach gave a loud, sour cognac rumble. "Berlin is gratifyingly thorough even in wartime, Frau Doktor—every foreign-trained physician working under Generalgouvernement license verified back to semester rolls! Excuse me one moment—something in that casino mayonnaise last night was decidedly off."

Clutching his soft pigskin gloves out of habit even on a trip to the doctors' lavatory down the hall, Vesper hurried out and shut the door—leaving the red-white-black stringed Berlin archive folder lying open six inches from Sylwia's hip!

Lavatory cistern down the passage took ninety seconds to fill after flushing, plus thirty seconds if Vesper checked his moustache in the mirror.

Sylwia didn't step behind the desk—stepping behind the desk trapped you if the handle turned early—simply leaned six inches over the front edge on silent leather soles and turned two stiff photostat leaves with the dry tip of her little finger.

Page One: her own nineteen-twenty-eight Charité matriculation card, signed at the bottom in faded brown iron-gall ink by *Geheimrat Prof. Dr. V. von Halden* confirming state surgical examination passed *mit Auszeichnung*, 24 February 1933; certificate noted at the bottom margin in red clerk's ink: *Nicht abgeholt — Kandidatin abgereist nach Polen 9.III.33.*

Page Two: **Schoeler, Ludwig Heinrich** — *geb. 3.IV.1909 Kiel — Matr.-Nr. Med. 27/889.* Identical faculty advisers, identical surgical clinic rotations under von Halden 1930–33, identical Virchow Medal notation, followed by transfer stamp dated *8.III.1933*: *Übertritt Sanitäts-Sonderdienst Reichswehrministerium (Abt. Abwehr), Berlin.*

Reinhart Kroll wasn't harmonizing municipal epidemic credentials; Reinhart Kroll had smelled something uncanny inside Room Fourteen back in February and March—two people speaking the exact clipped Berlin clinic cadence over smashed watches and coughed blood—and had waited patiently four months for Berlin's bombed-around university registries to ship photostats proving *Oberstleutnant* Ludwig Schoeler and *Dr. Sylwia Kotarska* had spent five years side by side under the same roof on Luisenstraße!

Wait: why had Kroll sent the folder across to *Stabsarzt Vesper* today instead of keeping it locked on Aleja Szucha? Attached to the inside front cover by a paperclip sat Kroll's polite Saxon routing slip: *Herrn Stabsarzt Dr. Vesper zur vertraulichen Kenntnisnahme und Rückäußerung betr. dienstliche Notwendigkeit weiterer ambulanter Behandlung Królewska 16 — Rückgabe bis 15.VII. mittags.*

Kroll was inviting Vesper to confirm on paper whether Schoeler's shoulder *really* needed twice-weekly house calls from an old Berlin classmate five months after February!

And tucked into the vellum pocket pasted inside the back cover—marked in pencil *Anlage B: Polizeipräsidium Berlin Abt. IA (Politische Polizei) — Alt-Akte März 1933, Mikrofilm-Duplikat*—protruded two identical strips of thirty-five-millimeter safety microfilm six frames long! Why two identical strips? Archive camera operators in Berlin routinely snipped duplicate six-frame negative test-strips into pockets when copying old Political Police ledgers in case one scratched in transit!

Down the corridor the cast-iron lavatory cistern roared loud with rushing water.

Moving faster than suturing silk, Sylwia pinched *one* of the two identical six-frame thirty-five-millimeter microfilm strips out of the back vellum sleeve—leaving the first strip untouched in place so Vesper (who didn't even own a microfilm reader!) and Kroll's return clerk counting one attachment per pocket wouldn't notice a duplicate missing—slid the celluloid ribbon flat down inside her starched cuff, and stepped two paces back to the window just as Vesper bustled back in smelling freshly of lavender water and buttoning his tunic pocket.

"Where were we—ah, Wola vaccine receipts," Vesper puffed, snapping the Berlin archive folder shut without glancing inside the back flap and locking it pompously into his pigskin briefcase. "By the way, Frau Doktor—speaking purely collegially—*Oberstleutnant* Schoeler's clavicular scar must surely be soundly consolidated by mid-July, wouldn't you say? Five months!"

Drop the pass meekly right now on Vesper's cue, and Kroll smiled into his white cotton gloves knowing she dropped it the afternoon Berlin's file arrived; cling too fiercely, and Vesper smelled personal attachment.

"Consolidated in bone callus since May, *Herr Stabsarzt*," Sylwia answered in her driest, most bored clinical tone, handing him his fountain pen cap off the blotter. "As I told *Oberstleutnant* Schoeler himself in May, once-monthly neurological review for brachial-plexus scar causalgia at *Lazarett Ujazdowski* would be wholly sufficient—if *Abwehrstelle* headquarters cared to release me from wasting two afternoons a week crossing your sentries."

Vesper beamed instantly from ear to ear: *she disliked going!* That saved Vesper having to cross *Abwehr* colonels while letting him write Kroll a splendidly pompous medical opinion recommending termination of outpatient visits by August first!

Yet walking upstairs five minutes later with that cold strip of nineteen-thirty-three Berlin Political Police celluloid burning flat against her radial pulse inside her white cuff, Sylwia smelled neither July heat nor hospital lysol—she smelled wet charred paper blowing across snow off the Spree on the morning of the twenty-eighth of February nineteen-thirty-three.

* * *

*Berlin, 28 February – 9 March 1933*

When the Reichstag dome burned orange against low winter clouds on the night of Monday the twenty-seventh of February, Sylwia had been sitting fully dressed in her coat at Widow Pohl's kitchen table off Savignyplatz drinking weak linden tea to celebrate passing her final oral surgical state board (*Staatsexamen*) seventy-two hours earlier under Geheimrat von Halden.

At seven-thirty Tuesday morning, walking east from Friedrichstraße station along the freezing canal quay toward Luisenstraße, she found fine black flakes of burned parliamentary shorthand paper still drifting onto dirty snowbanks, and two truckloads of young men in brown SA shirts wearing brand-new white linen *Hilfspolizei*—Auxiliary Police—armbands parked directly inside the main iron gates of the *Charité*.

Overnight Berlin's skin had turned inside out like a gutted rabbit.

Inside Surgical Pavilion Two nobody looked anybody else straight in the eye at eight-o'clock rounds. Fat Kleist—who had scraped through state boards with a miserable *genügend* three weeks behind Sylwia and Ludwig—marched down the tiled corridor wearing shiny brown riding-boots underneath his white clinic smock, whispering beside the radiator with two orderlies who wore Nazi party pins openly on their lapels now instead of hidden behind collars.

Only upstairs in the red-brick *Neuroanatomisches Laboratorium* annex at the rear of the courtyard did sixty-two-year-old **Professor Doktor Jakob Mandelbaum** put on his ink-stained black alpaca laboratory coat at nine sharp as he had done every weekday morning since nineteen-oh-eight, adjust his round gold-wire spectacles over gentle, near-sighted brown eyes, and set thirty serial celloidin brain-stem sections staining in Weigert's hematoxylin.

Sylwia loved Jakob Mandelbaum the way unsentimental fatherless girls loved the single adult in a hard city who never asked favors or told lies.

When Kazimierz Kotarski's diplomatic remittance stalled for three months during the thirty-one banking crash and twenty-one-year-old Sylwia lived on two boiled potatoes a day rather than beg Mommsenstraße charity, Mandelbaum had invented twelve hours a week of paid "histological slide-indexing" at two Reichsmarks an hour out of his own private textbook royalties—pretending furiously that nobody in Prussia except a stubborn Polish girl could read faded Russian labels on his St. Petersburg myelin slides. Even Ludwig—who respected roughly four living brains in Berlin—never sharpened his teeth on Mandelbaum, spending Tuesday evenings after surgical clinic arguing spinal-tract degeneration across Mandelbaum's Bunsen burner while eating Frau Mandelbaum's caraway seed-cake.

One foolish thing—foolish only once wolves took the police stations—sat locked inside the bottom drawer of Mandelbaum's roll-top oak desk: since autumn thirty-one, two evenings a month down in Wedding behind Nettelbeckplatz, Mandelbaum had run an unpaid night dispensary stitching split scalps and setting broken ribs on unemployed metalworkers and Communist *Rote Hilfe* street casualties whom municipal hospitals reported straight to detectives. Twice in thirty-two, when Mandelbaum had influenza, Sylwia had gone down to Wedding in an old shawl to suture torn ears herself—signing two narcotics-and-tetanus stock receipts at the Wedding pharmacy in her own neat hand: *i.V. S. Kotarska, cand. med.*

On Monday the sixth of March—the morning after Hitler's Reichstag election ballots were counted—Sylwia met Ludwig on the icy gravel path between Surgical Clinic Two and Pathology.

They hadn't exchanged five civil words since New Year's; twenty months after that July thunderstorm in the dog-house, their rivalry had hardened into cold adult steel—tied neck-and-neck for the single salaried Junior Assistantship (*planmäßige Assistentenstelle*) opening on von Halden's surgical service on April first. Everyone on Luisenstraße knew von Halden preferred Ludwig's vascular audacity on Mondays and Sylwia's diagnostic ice on Thursdays; only one contract sat in the Ministry budget.

Except that Monday morning Ludwig wasn't wearing a clinic smock. He came down the steps of the Administrative Deanery walking fast beside a tall, monocled Reichswehr *Major* in field-grey wool from Bendlerstraße, Ludwig's jaw locked white and sleepless in winter glare. When he spotted Sylwia ten yards off by the leafless linden tree, he broke step abruptly from the Major, intercepted her elbow out of earshot, and spoke without preface:

"Has Mandelbaum burned his Wedding dispensary receipts yet?"

Sylwia stiffened instantly, yanking her elbow clear of his glove. "What business is Professor Mandelbaum's private charity of yours—or of Reichswehr majors?"

"Don't play proud idiot with me this week, Sylwia!" His voice cut low and furious through freezing wind, winter-green eyes bloodshot at the rims as if he hadn't slept since Friday. "Göring's *Abteilung IA* Political Police copied the Wedding pharmacy narcotics-book on Saturday night—Kleist's brother-in-law works clerk on Alexanderplatz and boasted in the doctors' mess twenty minutes ago that detention lists for Charité 'Marxist medical helpers' are being typed for signature tomorrow! Tell Jakob tonight to pack two bags with his wife, leave his damned myelin jars behind, and get on the morning express from Anhalter Bahnhof to Prague before Wednesday noon!"

Cold fear went straight down Sylwia's spine—half believing him, half poisoned by five years of watching Ludwig calculate every chessboard three moves deep. Wait: if Mandelbaum fled to Prague and Sylwia's own Wedding pharmacy signature (*S. Kotarska*) forced *her* across the Polish border before April first, who inherited von Halden's solitary salaried Surgical Assistantship unopposed without lifting a finger?!

Even so, she ran straight up to the Neuroanatomy Annex at five p.m. and begged Jakob Mandelbaum for forty minutes over his cooling Bunsen burner to take the Tuesday morning Prague train.

Mandelbaum only smiled sadly behind thick round lenses, patting her chapped hand with fingers stained purple by hematoxylin. "Run away like a pickpocket at sixty-two, child, because noisy beer-hall hooligans wave flags for three months? I held an Iron Cross First Class at Verdun digging shrapnel out of Prussian skulls under shellfire. Geheimrat von Halden assured me personally at noon that Prussian university professors stand under Ministry protection. As for those two Wedding tetanus receipts with your name—don't fret, Sylwia, the ledger sits locked in my private roll-top drawer here, not on Alexanderplatz."

Forty-eight hours later—Wednesday the eighth of March at twelve-fifteen noon—snow began falling wet and grey across Charité courtyard gravel.

Sylwia was crossing from Surgical Clinic One carrying blood-agar plates when two open green police lorries packed with SA *Hilfspolizei* in brown shirts and chin-strapped shakos screeched to a halt directly outside the red-brick Neuroanatomy Annex.

She dropped her wooden slide-tray onto snow and ran toward the annex steps until old porter Krause grabbed her white smock from behind a brick buttress, clamping one calloused hand hard over her mouth: *"Still, Fräulein Kotarska, um Gottes willen—sie haben Listen!"*

Through falling snow twenty yards away, the oak annex doors banged wide.

Two twenty-year-old storm-troopers who smelled of wet leather from across the yard dragged Professor Jakob Mandelbaum out by both black alpaca elbows without letting him put on his winter overcoat. Somebody had already struck him across the bridge of the nose upstairs; blood trickled thin scarlet down into his trimmed white moustache, and as his rubber galoshes slipped on the icy top marble step, his round gold-wire spectacles flew off his face onto the stone landing.

"Meine Brille—bitte, meine Herren, ohne Brille sehe ich die Stufen nicht—" Mandelbaum stammered mildly, groping blind with hematoxylin-stained fingers toward the wet marble.

The taller SA man—laughing over his shoulder at fat Kleist standing just inside the vestibule doorway—stepped deliberately sideways onto the gold-wire frames with his hobnailed boot-heel, grinding both thick convex lenses into white glass powder against the stone before hauling the half-blind old man down into the lorry tailboard!

And forty feet higher up—standing behind the tall second-floor arched corridor window of Geheimrat von Halden's Surgical Pavilion directly overlooking the annex steps, flanked on the left by von Halden's white beard and on the right by that identical monocled Reichswehr *Major* from Monday morning—stood **Ludwig Schoeler**.

Sylwia saw his pale, immobile face framed crystal-clear behind wet window-glass looking straight down onto Jakob Mandelbaum's smashed spectacles in the snow—not shouting, not running down the stairs, turning three seconds later back into von Halden's corridor alongside the Reichswehr major before the police lorry gears ground away toward Alexanderplatz!

By nine o'clock that evening, behind bolted shutters in Widow Pohl's cold fourth-floor back room off Savignyplatz, Sylwia had two battered leather suitcases open on Widow Pohl's iron cot, stuffing anatomy atlases and winter stockings inside with numb hands because Polish Legation counselor Lipski's clerk had telephoned at six advising Polish nationals connected with University clinics to clear Berlin temporarily until street "excesses" subsided.

At nine-twenty someone hammered frantic knuckles on her landing door: Lotte Bergmann tumbled inside wearing a snow-soaked green velvet cloak over an evening slip, pupils pinprick-small with morphine, mascara running black rivers down hollow white cheeks.

"They took Jakob to Columbia-Haus or Sonnenburg cellar—Frau Mandelbaum's sitting at my flat right now staring at walls!" Lotte gasped, gripping Sylwia's arms with icy claw-fingers. "And do you know who came out of Jakob's locked annex office at *eleven-thirty* this morning—forty-five minutes before the brown trucks pulled up—carrying Jakob's private black oilcloth dispensary ledger under his coat straight over to the Reichswehr car waiting behind Pathology?! *Schoeler!* I saw him with my own two eyes from the library stair-window, Sylwia—Kleist unlocked the annex door after him, and by four o'clock this afternoon Fräulein Weber typed Ludwig's transfer commission straight into Bendlerstraße!"

Every word hit Sylwia's stomach like molten lead poured down a funnel.

*Eleven-thirty a.m.* Forty-five minutes before the SA lorries arrived, Ludwig had unlocked Mandelbaum's roll-top desk, walked out carrying the Wedding dispensary ledger, stood watching behind upstairs glass at twelve-fifteen while storm-troopers ground Jakob's spectacles to powder, and collected his Reichswehr officer's commission by four o'clock!

Lotte slipped down the back stairs at ten when heavy single boots sounded coming fast up Widow Pohl's front staircase.

Sylwia stood alone beside her open trunk with her pawnshop ebony Collin scalpel box gripped unopened in both hands when two crisp raps struck the pine panel.

She pulled the door open without chain.

Ludwig stood under the dim landing gas-jet wearing a brand-new, uncreased field-grey Reichswehr officer's greatcoat—silver buttons gleaming cold, dark blond hair wet with sleet, gaunt face carved out of grey granite without a flicker of expression around his mouth. Under his right cheekbone, pale against wind-chapped skin, shone the three-centimeter crescent scar she had stitched with juniper schnapps in Schöneberg snow.

"You're packed," he said quietly, stepping one pace across the threshold without taking off his gloves and laying a long green-and-buff stamped cardboard *Mitropa* ticket envelope onto the lid of her closed leather suitcase. "Good. Twenty-three fourteen express from Friedrichstraße—not Zoologischer Garten, SA auxiliary patrols are checking foreign passports at Zoo tonight. Lipski's attaché car is downstairs at the corner of Kantstraße right now to take your trunks through diplomatic lane. Don't go back to Luisenstraße tomorrow morning for your state diploma—leave it."

Sylwia could barely hear his words through the black roaring blood behind her eardrums, staring at that spotless field-grey German uniform coat smelling fresh from the military tailor's iron while Jakob Mandelbaum bled blind without spectacles on wet concrete three miles east.

"Lotte saw you come out of Jakob's annex at half-past eleven this morning," Sylwia whispered, voice shaking so violently she barely recognized her own German, "carrying his black Wedding ledger forty-five minutes before the storm-troopers dragged him down the steps."

Something flickered behind Ludwig's bloodshot winter-green eyes—one split-second spasm as if she had driven steel between his ribs, followed instantaneously by shutters slamming down twice as cold and impenetrable as iron plate.

He didn't explain. Didn't say *Anhalter Bahnhof*, didn't say *warrant*, didn't utter one syllable of defense.

"Lotte Bergmann puts three grains of morphine up her arm before noon and talks through every keyhole in Berlin," Ludwig said in a voice dead flat as coffin-zinc. "If you're still inside Prussia at seven o'clock tomorrow morning when Alexanderplatz opens Thursday's University supplement list, Lipski's passport won't save you five minutes. Pick up your bag and walk downstairs, Sylwia."

"How much did Bendlerstraße pay for the ledger, *Herr Leutnant*?" she breathed, stepping six inches from his new silver buttons, shaking from head to heel with pure twenty-three-year-old agony and loathing. "Or did you hand Kleist the names free so nobody Polish stood between you and von Halden's table?!"

His jaw locked white as chalk under the gas-lamp; not one word came past his teeth.

Sylwia spat square across his right cheekbone—right across the white ladder-marks her own needle had put into his skin—snatched the *Mitropa* ticket off her trunk, tore it once across, threw both halves at his polished new boots, and hauled both heavy suitcases out onto the landing herself, walking down four flights past him into Berlin sleet to catch the midnight fourth-class wooden bench train from Friedrichstraße on her own last four Reichsmarks sixty without once looking back across ten years of fire.

* * *

*Warsaw, 14 July 1943*

At eleven-thirty at night inside the stifling slant-roofed night-interns' dormer cubby on the third floor of Pavilion Three—forty degrees of baked slate heat still radiating six inches above her hair while moths batted dry wings against the blue blackout paper—Sylwia locked the pine door, wedged a wooden chair-back tight under the brass knob, and screwed Halina's borrowed twenty-power dissecting hand-loupe into its wire eye-frame.

Behind a tin biscuit box shielding light from the keyhole burned one bare sixty-watt hospital bulb.

With fine anatomical thumb-forceps she lifted the stolen duplicate six-frame strip of thirty-five-millimeter Berlin archival microfilm out of her starched cuff and held Frame One two centimeters in front of the frosted glass bulb while bringing the twenty-power lens close to her right eye.

White negative print reversed black-on-clear across tiny photographed police foolscap—too cramped on body paragraphs without a darkroom projector enlarger to decipher blurred carbon third-copies, yet the bold stamped letterhead and top three inked lines of Frame One leaped into crystal-sharp focus through twenty-power optical glass:

**POLIZEIPRÄSIDIUM BERLIN — ABTEILUNG IA (POLITISCHE POLIZEI)**
*Vorläufiges Schutzhaft-Verzeichnis — Charité / Wedding ("Rote Hilfe" Unfallstelle)*
*Ausgefertigt: Berlin, den **7. März 1933**, 18:00 Uhr.*

Sylwia's forceps trembled half a millimeter against warm glass.

*Seventh of March, nineteen-thirty-three, six p.m.* Twenty-eight hours *before* Jakob Mandelbaum was arrested—eighteen hours *before* Lotte Bergmann saw Ludwig carry that black oilcloth ledger out of the Neuroanatomy Annex at eleven-thirty on the eighth! Alexanderplatz *already* had its arrest list typed at six p.m. on Tuesday the seventh—just as Ludwig had warned her on the gravel path Monday morning!

Heart hammering hard against her ribs in the airless attic, she shifted the celluloid strip four millimeters left to center the numbered arrest roster on **Frame Two**—where somebody's heavy fountain pen had drawn a thick black deletion cross and a marginal Reichswehr counter-stamp across Entry Number Two:

*1. Dr. med. Jakob Mandelbaum, a.o. Prof., geb. 1871 (Jude) — festgenommen 8.III. 12:15.*
*2. [GESTRICHEN LT. VERF. RWM / ABW. II VOM 8.III.33, 11:45 UHR — AKTE ENTFERNT]* — and faintly legible *underneath* the heavy black deletion cross-hatching, typed in Alexanderplatz's original seventh-of-March purple ribbon before someone scrubbed the ledger and stamped Reichswehr preemption over it fifteen minutes before noon on the eighth:

**Kotarska, Sylwia Helena, cand. med. (poln. Staatsang.) — Wedding-Rezepte Nr. 114, 119.**

The tin biscuit box rattled loud as Sylwia's elbow hit the deal table.

*8.III.33, 11:45 Uhr.*

At eleven-thirty on the morning of March eighth, Ludwig hadn't stolen Jakob Mandelbaum's Wedding ledger to sell names to the Gestapo! Ludwig had broken into Mandelbaum's office forty-five minutes before the SA trucks rolled—after stubborn old Jakob ran back from Anhalter Bahnhof at ten a.m. refusing to leave his brain-stem jars for Prague!—ripped Sylwia's signed Wedding pharmacy receipts *out* of the black ledger before Kleist's storm-troopers seized the desk, carried them straight across the courtyard to that monocled Reichswehr intelligence major at eleven-forty-five, and traded **his own surgical career—signing a seven-year Reichswehr Abwehr commission on the spot!**—as the price for Bendlerstraße slapping an official military-intelligence preemption deletion over *Kotarska, Sylwia Helena* on Alexanderplatz's arrest sheet fifteen minutes before noon!

And ten hours later, when twenty-three-year-old Sylwia stood in Savignyplatz spitting across his cheekbone scar and calling him Judas who sold Jakob for silver buttons, Ludwig Schoeler had stood stone-still in his two-hour-old uniform coat—unable to tell her he had just sold his own life to Bendlerstraße to strike her name off a concentration-camp warrant without making proud, penniless Sylwia Kotarska refuse to get on the midnight train out of misplaced honor!—so he had taken her spit clean across his face in silence to drive her safely across the Polish border alive!

Sylwia sat down hard on the narrow iron attic cot in the dark, thirty-five-millimeter celluloid ribbon clenched lightly inside forceps while sweat ran cold down her spine despite suffocating July slate-heat.

Wait—don't faint like a Victorian schoolgirl; read **Frame Six** before hiding the strip! Why had Reinhart Kroll sent this film across Warsaw today?

She held Frame Six against the bulb: Kroll's own typed *Referat IV A* request note dated *10.VII.1943* querying Prinz-Albrecht-Straße Central Registry why *Abwehr* deletion stamp *8.III.33* hid Polish national *S. Kotarska*—marked at the bottom: *Original-Belegmappe März 33 folgt per Kurier aus Berlin Ende August.*

*Original voucher-file arriving by Berlin courier end of August.* Meaning Kroll didn't have the full unredacted text under the ink cross-out *yet*—only the blurry microfilm copy until Berlin's dusty basement clerk dug out the physical 1933 Wedding receipt slip in six weeks!

Ten years of pure, distilled, righteous hatred—the iron spine Sylwia had leaned against whenever loneliness or hunger or Tuesday afternoons on Królewska terrified her—had just cracked clean in half in a third-floor hospital attic.

And she couldn't even run across town tonight after curfew to scream at him for stealing ten years of her life with a lie, because downstairs in Pavilion Three's scrub corridor frantic night bells were suddenly clanging twice—*emergency laparotomy*—pulling her back into white linen before her hands stopped shaking.
