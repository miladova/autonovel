# Gypsum Mouths

*Warsaw, 16 October 1943*

Pink paper changed colour when flour-paste soaked through from damp October brick behind it—turning the exact livid salmon-wet of sliced raw lung laid on zinc.

On Friday morning the fifteenth of October the first of Brigadeführer Franz Kutschera's new yard-wide *Bekanntmachungen* had gone up wet on every kiosk, tram-stop shelter, and chipped tenement corner from Wola to Mokotów: zero judicial pretense left, zero black borders—just screaming bold black German and Polish type announcing that because unidentified assassins had shot an SS trooper on Wspólna, twenty Polish "terrorists and hostages" had been publicly executed that morning, and **one hundred further named hostages** sat numbered one to a hundred in Pawiak cells ready to be shot on open city pavements at ten-to-one ratio the next hour any German hand bled in Warsaw.

On Saturday afternoon the sixteenth of October at half-past two, Sylwia climbed the dark terrazzo stairs of Number Sixty-Eight Filtrowa carrying two fresh ten-milliliter vials of Danish Novo insulin packed in wet sawdust alongside five grams of Benedict's copper-sulfate reagent.

Inside the cramped third-floor flat, forty days of autumn terror had rearranged every piece of emotional furniture between Wanda and Stefan without breaking a teacup.

Gone were Stefan's pompous *Ostbahn* suspenders and loud evening opinions on German punctuality: sitting quietly by the kitchen window in a darned grey cardigan two sizes too loose round shrunken collarbones—sclerae faded now from bright August picric-acid canary down to pale tallow-parchment—Stefan spent Saturday afternoons pasting Ration Office fuel coupons neat into cardboard ledgers for his harmless new clerk's stool at Praga's municipal coke-weighing shed, where zero human worker's name ever crossed his ink-pad. Across at the iron coal-range Wanda stirred black roasted-acorn coffee beside a simmering pot of horse-bone marrow soup she had queued three hours before dawn at Hala Mirowska to buy for Staś—hair streaked visibly greyer above both temples since August nineteenth, thin hands chapped raw red from taking in neighborhood darning by night to make up Stefan's lost Wola overtime pay, yet steadier in the eye than Sylwia had ever seen her older sister in thirty years.

Out at the scrubbed pine table nine-year-old **Staś** held the Pyrex test-tube straight in brass wooden-handled tongs above the tiny spirit-flame himself while counting seconds off Stefan's pocket-watch:

"Ninety—one hundred—one hundred and twenty! Look, Ciociu—clear blue-green, not one speck of brick-orange sediment at the bottom!"

"Cleanest kidneys in Ochota," Sylwia agreed gravely, ruffling his thick dark hair and checking his upper thighs where Wanda rotated subcutaneous needle sites down millimeter-ruled grids neat enough for Charité wards. "And what passes beneath the middle third of the clavicle where I tapped your collar?"

Staś grinned gap-toothed, puffing skinny ribs proud under his darned sweater: "*Arteria subclavia, vena subclavia, plexus brachialis!* Learned down to the radial nerve off that torn German pocket book in your coat!"

While Staś ran into the bedroom to put away his glass syringe in boiled linen, Stefan stood up awkwardly beside the window—thin lemon-tinted fingers twisting once round a wooden matchbox—and spoke low before Wanda turned from the range:

"Saw Kubiak's brother-in-law passing Narutowicz Square tram-stop on Thursday morning, Sylwia." Stefan didn't meet her eyes at first, staring painfully down at his own faded yellow thumbnails before forcing his chin up like a man paying debt across counters. "Looked right through me onto the cobbles and kept walking with both hands inside his pockets. Whatever sat inside those two bitter yellow capsules you made me swallow in August... thank you. For the boy."

Sylwia didn't soften it with pretty lies and didn't twist the knife either; she touched one brief hand to Stefan's thin cardigan sleeve—"Keep counting coke-sacks in Praga and stay indoors after dusk, Stefan"—kissed Wanda's warm flour-dusted cheek at twenty minutes past three, and walked east toward Marszałkowska carrying two round loaves of dark ration bread Bronka had asked her to pick up from the cooperative bakery on Koszykowa.

Cold drizzle had stopped at noon, leaving low leaden clouds dragging across chimney-pots and wet black asphalt gleaming like gunmetal along **Aleja Niepodległości** where it crossed Piusa XI.

Halfway up the block toward the bombed Ministry gardens, Sylwia heard heavy engines accelerating fast from *both* north and south simultaneously.

Every pedestrian within a hundred yards stiffened in mid-stride—feet pivoting instinctively toward courtyard gates for *łapanka* cover—until twenty steel-helmeted *Schutzpolizei* troopers leaping off two open half-tracks didn't herd people *into* trucks at all: instead they swung cocked submachine guns **outward toward the pavements**, driving pedestrians backward tight against shop shutters between Koszykowa and Piusa XI while hammering every wooden courtyard gate bolted from the street side so nobody could duck indoors!

"Stehenbleiben! Auf dem Bürgersteig bleiben—Hände aus den Taschen! Wer sich umdreht oder ins Haus läuft, wird erschossen!"

*Stand still on the pavement—hands out of pockets—whoever turns away or runs indoors gets shot!*

Sylwia stood wedged flat-backed against the cold iron rolling-shutter of a closed stationer's shop directly across the forty-foot roadway from a long windowless stretch of pockmarked red-brick garden wall. To her left a grey-haired postal clerk held his nine-year-old daughter's face crushed hard inward against his wet wool coat-front so the child couldn't see; to her right an old woman carrying cabbage leaves began whispering *Wieczny odpoczynek racz im dać, Panie* before a single vehicle came round the corner—because since yesterday noon Warsaw already knew what outward-facing rifles opposite a blank brick wall meant.

Down the emptied wet asphalt from the north rolled two vehicles: a grey passenger Mercedes bearing Security Police pennants, and behind it a heavy closed tarpaulin lorry out of ulica Dzielna—Pawiak prison transport.

Zero siren, zero megaphone, zero drum-roll.

Iron tailboard chains clanked down onto wet cobbles. Two Latvian or Ukrainian *Trawniki* guards in black greatcoats jumped down first, yanking back the green canvas flap and prodding inside with rifle barrels.

Out onto the freezing wet October stones dropped **twenty men**—landing hard on *bare, purple-bruised feet* because Pawiak's cellars kept leather boots for winter salvage before trucks loaded.

None wore coats, jackets, or trousers: over bare shivering legs bruised black across shins and thighs from forty-eight hours of cellar truncheons, every prisoner wore a grotesque sleeveless knee-length tunic fashioned out of **stiff brown-white three-ply Portland cement paper sacks** with holes torn rough for head and arms, rustling dry like dead leaves whenever cold wind hit them! Their wrists were twisted behind their backs with thin galvanized baling wire cut deep into swollen flesh; two young boys in front had black dried blood crusted across broken noses and split scalps so they swayed sideways against neighbors to stay upright on bare soles.

And across the lower half of every single face from ear to ear—packed solid between forced-open jaws and smoothed wet across lips, moustache, and chin thirty minutes ago in Pawiak's courtyard before hardening rock-stiff—gleamed a thick, gagging white muzzle of **set surgical plaster of Paris**!

Sylwia's fingernails cut clean through her woollen gloves into her own palms round the bread string.

Yesterday morning at Kutschera's first street execution on Madalińskiego, before firing squads pulled triggers, five bound hostages had roared *"Jeszcze Polska nie zginęła!"* loud enough to ring four blocks down Mokotów windows—so overnight some tidy hygienist on Aleja Szucha had requisitioned fifty-kilo hospital drums of **gypsum splint-plaster**, mixed grey-white paste in buckets, and plugged twenty living human mouths solid to the back molars so men died in Warsaw gutters quieter than slaughtered sheep!

Guards shoved the first five cement-sack figures clumsily shoulder-to-shoulder facing the red-brick garden wall forty feet opposite Sylwia's stationer's shutter.

Wait: the remaining fifteen stood huddled waiting their turn ten paces left beside the lorry tailboard—and as the third man from the left in the second five shifted bare bleeding heels on icy asphalt to prop up a sixteen-year-old boy sagging faint beside him, he turned his head slowly across the street toward the silent Polish crowd pinned along the kerb.

Above the hardened white gypsum mask crusted across chin and jaw, beneath a four-centimeter pink bullet-graze scar high on the left temple and matted brown hair clotted dark over one eyebrow, two narrow grey engineer's eyes looked straight across forty feet of October air and found Sylwia standing against iron shutters.

**Marek Wolski.**

Sylwia's heart stopped dead—not jumping, freezing solid inside ice while every instinct screamed *don't move an eyelash!*

Twenty yards down the kerb to Sylwia's left, walking unhurriedly behind the steel-helmeted *Schupo* line in a dry greenish loden coat with hands in pockets and a miniature Leica camera hanging openly at chest-button level—snapping discreet wide-angle frames of Polish faces pressed against the shutters whenever five men stepped to the bricks!—strolled one of Reinhart Kroll's *Referat IV* street spotters!

Catch one woman gasping, lunging forward, or staring fixed in horror at Prisoner Number Eight, and inside ten minutes that loden-coated spotter pulled her *Kennkarte* right off the pavement to match against Prisoner Eight's pocket list!

Across forty feet of cold grey street, Marek saw the Leica spotter at the exact tenth of a second Sylwia did.

Instantly—with the same compact, unsentimental bridge-engineer's discipline with which he had counted tram-bells in Pańska's cellar and handed unloaded pistols across smoke—Marek pulled his grey eyes *away* from Sylwia's face without blinking twice, shifting his gaze three yards past her shoulder onto the stationer's painted tin sign overhead as if scanning sky before rain!

First five stood facing wet red brick.

An SS *Hauptscharführer* in field-grey coat didn't even read sentences—simply raised two black leather-gloved fingers and dropped them.

Five Schmeisser submachine guns chattered one three-second burst together—*brrrrrrrap!*—shockingly flat and dry against brick, kicking brick-chips red into the air while five paper-sack backs jerked stiff against stone and crumpled sideways like empty meal-bags dropped off a wagon. Before boots stopped twitching, the *Hauptscharführer* stepped calmly along the row firing five single muffled *pop... pop... pop* Walther shots behind ears, stepped back two paces, and pointed at the second five.

Marek walked barefoot across wet cobbles into the fresh dark crimson pool already snaking toward the kerbstone—supporting the half-conscious sixteen-year-old boy beside his elbow with his own wired hip so the child didn't fall face-down into another man's brains before bullets came, turning his back square to the rifles to face red brick without once looking back across the street.

*Don't shut your eyes,* Sylwia commanded every muscle in her skull as the loden-coated Leica spotter raised his lens ten yards left. *Look straight ahead at brick—surgeons watch blood every morning, don't flinch, don't swallow, owe him stillness!*

*Brrrrrrrrap!*

Cement paper split across Marek's left shoulder-blade in four grey puffs of dust and wet red mist; white gypsum cracked red against wet cobbles as his knees gave clean and quiet beside the sixteen-year-old boy, still before the single Walther *pop* ever reached him.

Third five.
Fourth five.

Total elapsed clock time from tailboard drop to twentieth pistol shot: **four minutes and ten seconds**.

Before the blue cordite smoke even drifted clear above linden branches, four prison orderlies hauled all twenty limp paper-wrapped bodies back over the lorry tailboard by wrists and ankles like cordwood; canvas laced shut; engines roared off north toward Powązki or Warsaw Ghetto brick-kilns where October bodies burned without graves; and right behind the departing grey Mercedes rolled a municipal street-flushing tank-wagon requisitioned ten minutes earlier on Koszykowa, two terrified Polish water-men ordered at gunpoint to open brass two-inch hydrants wide across the red-brick wall!

Cold river water hissed white across stone and asphalt, driving thick dark clots, grey brains, and crumbled white chunks of blood-soaked gypsum plaster swirling pink down the cast-iron gutter grate while *Schutzpolizei* half-tracks climbed roaring back onto axles and pulled out.

* * *

For thirty seconds after the last German exhaust pipe vanished round Piusa corner, nobody along two hundred yards of Aleja Niepodległości moved a shoe.

Then silence broke not into shouting—into a low, rustling whisper of wool coats and wooden soles crossing wet asphalt toward the washed wall.

Nobody cared whether loden-coated watchers still lingered inside corner cafés. Old women knelt right down on soaked cobbles where hydrant water hadn't scrubbed deep mortar cracks clean, opening worn handbags to press clean white linen handkerchiefs—and when handkerchiefs ran out, tearing white crumbs out of ration bread loaves—straight into bright red crevices between bricks so Polish blood wouldn't wash down sewers into rat-water uncollected! Within two minutes somebody produced six stubs of blessed Candlemas wax candles out of a coat pocket, shielded matches inside cupped caps against October wind, and wedged six tiny yellow flames flickering straight along the wet red-splashed brick ledge beside three bunches of crushed Michaelmas daisies!

Sylwia did not cross to the wall.

Keep thirty paces clear of the wall if you carried seventy hospital beds and hidden files on your shoulders—every underground cell rule Marek himself had drilled into her in November forty-two sat dry and iron-clear behind her eyes while cold numbness gripped her legs from hip to ankle so hard each step down Koszykowa felt walked on wooden prostheses.

Only as she passed four yards downwind of the iron storm-sewer grate at the corner did her shoe-sole crunch once across a washed-down white pebble the size of a walnut that hadn't slipped through the iron bars: not stone, a jagged piece of hardened white dental-grade **gypsum plaster** knocked off somebody's jaw by nine-millimeter impact, stained faint pink along its inner tooth-impression ridge.

Sylwia didn't stoop to touch it; she walked straight through *Szpital Dzieciątka Jezus*'s gatehouse arch at four-ten with two cold loaves of ration rye under her arm, handed the bread to Bronka in Pavilion Three's kitchen without speaking a word, and walked straight upstairs toward Theater One.

Out on the first-floor surgical landing **Antoni Majewski**, a forty-two-year-old Wola drop-forge smith whose right hand had been caught obliquely under a slipping two-hundred-kilo steel die at noon, sat hunched white-lipped on the wooden bench nursing blood-soaked rags while young Doktor Jankowski finished plating an open tibia inside.

"Take ten minutes' hot tea first, Pani Doktor," Bronka muttered low behind Sylwia's shoulder at the kitchen stair-head after one sharp squint at the dead chalk-white ring pinched tight round Sylwia's nostrils and mouth. "You look like somebody walked over your grave on Koszykowa. Jankowski can stitch finger tendons at four-thirty."

"Jankowski shakes after tibial bone-drills, and drop-forge smiths without index and middle extensors starve by Christmas," Sylwia answered in a voice that sounded six miles away even inside her own skull—already unbuttoning her street wool coat and turning both cold-water taps wide open over the porcelain scrub-trough!

For seventy uninterrupted minutes beneath Theater One's bright afternoon glass—having infiltrated twenty milliliters of two-percent procaine without adrenaline round Majewski's wrist median, radial, and ulnar trunks so the burly ironworker lay wide awake behind a green linen towel-screen talking hoarsely about coal prices to keep from thinking about crippled fingers—Sylwia sat on a low revolving iron stool stitching severed white ribbon-ends of *extensor indicis*, *extensor digitorum communis* slips two and three, and frayed dorsal joint capsules together with hair-fine five-zero waxed arterial silk on curved atraumatic eye-needles!

Tendon surgery tolerated zero rage and zero tremor: pull one Bunnell criss-cross stitch half a pound too tight and tendon fibers bunched ischemic like wet string; leave a two-millimeter gap and scar adhesions welded finger-joints stiff forever; let attention drift one tenth of a second—

Every time Sylwia blinked sweat or dry air off her corneas behind her magnifying loupe, forty feet of wet gunmetal asphalt flashed crimson behind her eyelids: bare purple-bruised heels propping up a swaying sixteen-year-old boy on icy cobbles; white plaster of Paris packed hard across bruised lips and chin; narrow grey bridge-engineer's eyes catching hers across forty feet of October light and shifting deliberately three yards sideways onto a painted tin stationer's sign so Reinhart Kroll's Leica missed her face!

*In December forty-two on Koszykowa, when coal ran out altogether for three nights before Christmas,* memory cut unbidden and razor-clean across the shiny white tendon ribbon between her forceps, *Marek had sawed up his own oak Polytechnic drawing-trestle into stove-lengths without asking, boiled two stolen potatoes in salted snow-water on her spirit-ring, and warmed both her ice-cold feet inside his wool shirt against his ribs without even trying to reach higher because she'd lost a twelve-year-old appendicitis boy that afternoon.*

Then June came, and lies came, and Stach's poison ampoule came, and she had held eight inches of Krupp steel at his throat and sent him down stairs hating or mourning her—and sixteen months of human breath, warmth, stubborn blind courage, and bad judgment ended inside four minutes and ten seconds wearing a torn Portland cement paper sack with wet surgical plaster gagging his teeth so Warsaw gutters stayed quiet!

Sylwia's needle-holder did not slip half a hairbreadth.

*Snip.* Five-zero black silk knot laid flat and pearl-smooth inside paratenon sheath. Passive wrist extension tested: index, middle, and ring fingers glided smooth and anatomical across the metacarpophalangeal knuckles without catching a single fiber! Skin closed neat with interrupted horsehair; palmar plaster volar slab molded warm in wet **gypsum bandage**—*sweet Christ in iron heaven, wet white gypsum paste stiffening warm between her own rubber-gloved palms five inches from her eyes!*—smoothed rock-steady from palm-crease to forearm without her jaw unclenching once until Majewski walked out grateful at five-thirty-five!

Only at quarter past six, when day nurses cleared downstairs and frail, papery Docent Tadeusz Mirecki shuffled slowly into the locked ground-floor dispensary wrapped in two cardigans—permitted downstairs two hours daily since October first so long as he climbed zero stairs fast—did Sylwia lock the frosted glass door behind him and speak quietly beside the poison cupboard:

"Marek Wolski was in the second five on Aleja Niepodległości at three-thirty today."

Mirecki—who had never known Marek's real surname, knowing only from winter glances that Sylwia had an engineer friend outside who smelled of wet dog-wool and brought railway bullet inquiries—stopped short beside the zinc sink, watery blue eyes closing behind thick pince-nez for three long seconds before he sat heavily down on the dispensary stool.

"Did he enter here under his own *Kennkarte* at any date?" Mirecki asked in a whisper, old coward's survival instinct and decent colleague's loyalty firing together out of habit.

"Never once," Sylwia said, pulling down the emergency out-patient suture register from January and February anyway to check every single line round February ninth with cold merciless eyes—nothing, Pańska had been sewn in a cobbler's cellar, All Saints' vestry on September twenty-ninth (when she had handed Marek Page Twenty-Two's fake Mińsk clay-pit sheet and parted cleanly without knowing it was the last five minutes of his life!) carried zero paper. "Question is what name Pawiak booked him under when they caught him in Praga—because if Szucha's clerks booked *Marek Wolski, civil engineer*, and cross-matched dead Stach's surviving July scraps..."

They had to wait three hours until nine-fifteen p.m., when old Walenty came shivering in from bolting the night gate carrying a damp, flour-paste-smeared **salmon-pink broadsheet** peeled furtively off an unguarded hoarding behind Lindleya lane before paste dried hard.

Spread flat beneath the single shaded dispensary bulb, wet pink paper smelled sour of rye paste and kerosene ink:

*BEKANNTMACHUNG / OBWIESZCZENIE — Der SS- und Polizeiführer im Distrikt Warschau.*
Twenty numbered names executed at 15:30 hours on sixteen October in reprisal for attacks on German police personnel.

Sylwia's raw index finger ran straight down Numbers One through Twenty without stopping on *Wolski*:

*6. Kowalczyk, Janusz, geb. 14.III.1909 Kielce (Heizer).*

*Janusz Kowalczyk, born 14 March 1909 Kielce — Stoker.* Marek's exact birth-day and town attached to the forged railway-stoker's *Kennkarte* matching that greasy peaked cloth cap and quilted black *fufajka* jacket he had worn in Powązki's rain twenty days ago!

Meaning for forty-eight or seventy-two hours down inside Pawiak's concrete vaults—where men got beaten across iron trestles with bull-whips and iron rods until kidneys ruptured—Marek Wolski had held onto his forged stoker's cover without coughing up his real Polytechnic name, his Praga platoon, Koszykowa Thirty-Three, or *Dr. Sylwia Kotarska*—going barefoot onto Aleja Niepodległości cobbles with wet plaster of Paris setting hard around his bruised teeth as *Janusz Kowalczyk, railway stoker*, taking five rounds into his chest without giving Aleja Szucha one syllable!

* * *

Mirecki shuffled quietly out at nine-thirty leaving her the dispensary key without embarrassing either of them with speeches.

Alone behind frosted glass in dead hospital silence, Sylwia uncorked the five-liter glass pharmacy carboy of ninety-six-percent *Spiritus vini rectificatus*, poured thirty milliliters into a porcelain measure, carried it not to her mouth—to the corner floor drain—and tipped half slowly onto cold tiles the way country carters in Poznań poured first vodka drops onto earth on All Souls' Night for men buried without coffins.

She drank the remaining fifteen milliliters raw in one burning swallow that tore tears into the corners of her eyes purely from chemical vapor, rinsed the porcelain measure clean at the tap, and stood gripping cold zinc with both hands until the floor stopped tilting.

Eighteen days since she had burned her green *Sonderausweis* and walked down Service Stair B on Królewska in September rain.

Zero optician's postcard had come through Walenty's pigeonhole—only silence from Saski Square while autumn tightened round Warsaw like galvanized wire round bare wrists: rumours whispered by ambulance drivers that Berlin's Reich Security Main Office was gutting Canaris's Eastern *Abwehr* stations desk by desk, rumours that three German officers from Königsberg had been flown west under arrest on Tuesday, pink posters blooming fresh every forty-eight hours across wet brick walls.

She did not pray for Ludwig tonight—praying belonged to people who still believed somebody upstairs counted sparrows—she merely walked up three dark flights to her freezing slant-roofed dormer cubby, wrapped two hospital blankets tight over her coat, and lay listening to boots patrol Nowogrodzka until at seven-ten on Wednesday morning the twentieth of October old Walenty climbed three flights grey-faced to hand her a stiff white envelope bearing the black runic return stamp of **Der Kommandeur der Sicherheitspolizei und des SD für den Distrikt Warschau — Aleja Szucha 25, Zimmer 214**:

*Vorladung.* Summons for **Dr. med. Sylwia Kotarska** to appear in person today at **11:00 Uhr** before *SS-Hauptsturmführer* Kroll for "supplementary municipal epidemic-record deposition."
