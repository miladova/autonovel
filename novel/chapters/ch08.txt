The Alexandrians

The banquet of Alexandrians has been booked since December, and like all Antonia's profitable evenings it rests on a lattice of small, unglamorous decisions: who is placed where the kitchen noise reaches least, which singer does Greek without making the Romans feel examined, what to serve men from Egypt who consider Roman cooking a promising provincial attempt. The great dining room has its new hanging, Prima's red flowers marching the border like a well-drilled cohort, and the lamps have been filled by a boy who has been threatened, kindly and specifically, about the consequences of a smoky wick.

The preparations began, as Ammonius insists all true history begins, with an insult delivered by a supplier. The Alexandrians' agent sent word at dawn that his principals preferred their fish *in the Egyptian manner*, a phrase that arrived in the kitchen like a declaration of war from a country Ammonius left thirty years ago and has been improving in exile ever since. He read the message twice, aloud, in the accents of a man defusing a siege engine.

“Egyptian manner,” he said to the kitchen at large. “I am an Egyptian. The manner is at home. Send back: the fish will be in the manner of this house, which has the advantage that this house is where they are eating it.”

“Send back also,” Antonia said, tasting the morning stock, “that the house has acquired, at some trouble, the little salted fish from the lake boats they praise in their letters home, and will serve it before the courses, without announcing it, so that they can discover home like a compliment and not like a curriculum.”

Ammonius bowed to the stockpot. Statecraft had occurred. This is the part of her empire nobody carves: a cook's honour managed at dawn so that a fleet contract can be managed at the third course. Rome is the mistress of the known world, says the inscription programme, and the known world, in Antonia's experience, is a set of small governances conducted over fish by people the inscriptions will not name.

By the sixth hour Diomedes has the seating in wax, and wax, as she told a young equestrian in another context, has carried Roman law for a thousand years. The head of the Alexandrian syndicate — Serapion, sixty, with a beard his Roman partners regard as a philosophy and he regards as a face — must not sit within negotiating distance of the tax farmer who foreclosed, two winters ago, on a cousin's warehouse in Ostia. The foreclosing tax farmer, Felix by name (the useful men again; it is a guild), must sit where he can see Serapion being civilised by other people's manners, because a man who watches his enemy fed at Antonia's prices tends to recalculate empires. The senator's nephew, who is decorative, connected, and — Diomedes has it in the second book, in a column headed in all but name *cardiovascular* — under doctor's orders that he treats as literature, is to be placed nearest the musicians and furthest from the undiluted wine, a geography Antonia designs without anyone's permission, out of a principle her house does not advertise: she does not let men kill themselves expensively in her rooms if the seating can help it. Free will is a guest's own affair. Geography is hers.

Prima's hanging went up at noon. She came herself to hang it, which is not in the commission and which Antonia permitted as she permits Daphne's second set: on principle, at a price, with the corners watched. Barbatus held the ladder. Prima, at the top of it among the red flowers, held pins in her mouth and directed, through them, a staff of serving boys with the unconscious authority of a girl whose whole life has been work nobody applauds. The boys obeyed her the way the kitchen obeys Ammonius: instantly, and with commentary reserved for later.

“Left,” Prima said, round the pins. “Your left. The one the goddess keeps her thumb on.” She caught Antonia watching from the doorway with a tradeswoman's gaze and did not startle, which Antonia noted. Girls who startle at being watched by power have been taught that the watching is the point. Prima has been taught that the work is. The hanging settled; the border marched; the room acquired, as rooms in this house do by design, the air of having been inevitable since Romulus.

“You'll spoil the effect standing there,” Eirene said, arriving with lamp oil on her hands and the day's intelligence in her face. “The room is supposed to look like nobody made it.”

“The room is supposed to look like *Rome*,” Antonia said. “Which is the same instruction. Let them sit down inside the work of women from three provinces and call it the natural order. We will charge the natural order by the course.”

The guests arrive as Alexandrians abroad always arrive: punctually, in numbers, with their own weather around them. There are interpreters who are not needed and secretaries who are, a physician travelling with Serapion who looks at the dining room the way Diomedes looks at accounts — professionally, for the places where trouble files itself — and a young man, Serapion's nephew and heir in every sense except the will, whose Latin is better than the Romans' Greek and whose manners, consequently, must do twice the work. He bows to Antonia low enough to honour the house and not low enough to insult his uncle. She returns the exact exchange rate.

“Your uncle's health,” she says. “His letter said the sea.”

“His letter says the sea every year, madam. It is his way of informing the sea that the arrangement continues.” The young man — Heron is his name; she files it with a harbour's usefulness — looks at the hanging the way his uncle will shortly look at the fish: as evidence. “The border is local work?”

“Transtiberim. The best light in Rome, and the women who sew in it can count.” She watches him take that in: a Roman hostess praising the far bank to an Alexandrian, both of them provincials in the capital's filing system, conducting diplomacy at the level where it actually happens. “Counting is underrated as a decorative art, Heron. Empires run on it and call the runners clerks.”

By the time the salted fish goes round — unannounced, discovered, received with the small, startled sound men make when a city they are visiting pronounces their childhood correctly — the room is hers in the way she likes rooms to be: not awed, not hers at all to look at, simply functioning at a temperature where agreement becomes possible. Serapion eats two of the little fish and does something with his face his beard was installed to prevent. “The lake at Canopus,” he says to nobody, and then, recovering the harbour wall of himself, to Avidius across the table: “Your hostess smuggles, Avidius.”

“Quality,” Avidius says, in the voice of a man reading his own best sentence back to himself, “is the only bribe that keeps giving evidence.” And looks at Antonia, and she at him, customs officer to cargo, while the courses begin to move like a fleet in line ahead.

The guests are merchants of the grain fleet and their Roman partners, and among the partners, inevitable as a harbour due, is Avidius the father. He eats in Antonia's house the way he buys cargo space, with an evaluator's love. “Your cook has improved the fish,” he tells her, which from Avidius is a love letter. “Last year it was ambition. This year it is fish.”

“I shall tell him. He will pretend to be insulted and raise his price.”

“You will pay it. Quality is the only bribe that keeps giving evidence in your favour.” He looks around the room; at the Senator's nephew holding a conversation with the singer Philomela about metre, at two tax farmers burying a partnership in the third course and resurrecting it by the fifth. “I am to marry my son into the Cornelii,” he says, in the tone of a man discussing the weather at sea. “If the Lentulus does not drown the match first, wrestling with your paperwork. Tell me, Antonia — you may; everyone else here is paid not to — will he?”

“He will do what his family has always done. He will discover, at the last moment, a principle that coincides with solvency.”

Avidius laughs, the deep harbour laugh. “You know him.”

“I know the type. Rome has a mould.” She moves to go, because a hostess who lingers is a hostess auditioning, but Avidius, who has spent forty years reading the moment cargo shifts, shifts his couch-weight almost imperceptibly into her path.

“Between ourselves,” he says. “I have made my money not frightening. I unloaded grain, my son unloads nothing heavier than a lyre, my grandson will unload provinces. It requires that nothing with my name on it be laughed at in the baths. If your house and his committee are going to make my family's wedding into a mime, I would rather buy a different old name. There are several for sale, though they call the shop something else.”

“Then tell him so.”

“I have. In a letter about figures. Men of his mould only read figures. It is their poetry.” Avidius takes an olive. “You hold paper, I am told, in several directions at once. It is a talent. Paper is the only freedman who never forgets his place, because his place is wherever you file him.”

She looks at him then, really looks, the customs officer at the cargo, and Avidius — who respects competence the way priests respect omens — inclines his head the smallest degree a rich man can manage without spraining anything. Nothing has been said. Everything has been invoiced.

Philomela sings after the second course, Greek first, as commissioned — a drinking song from Lesbos that has survived three centuries of symposia by being about exactly what is happening in the room, viz. men making treaties with their cups raised. She sings it straight, without the vocal embroidery Romans pay extra for and Greeks apologise for, and Serapion beats time once on the table edge with a knuckle, a harbour signal of approval, and then remembers his beard and stops.

It is in the pause — musicians retuning, plates moving like weather systems, the wine doing the slow work wine does on geography, softening the borders Antonia drew in wax at the sixth hour — that the senator's nephew gets Philomela into the conversation about metre. He is called Gaius Calpurnius; he is thirty; he has, besides the heart reported to his wife and the doctor's literature, a love of verse that his family treats as a minor and picturesque disease, like an allergy to shellfish. He writes elegiacs. Rome is full of this condition. He wants to know whether Philomela finds the hexameter *breathable*, a word he has brought to the dinner like a new knife.

“Everything is breathable,” Philomela says, “if the singer is allowed to breathe. Your poets write for the eye now, Gaius Calpurnius. The eye doesn't have lungs. It is why their epics need buckets.”

This goes through the room's musically literate like a wire through cheese. Somewhere a poet of Antonia's acquaintance — present tonight as garnish; she hires them by the evening in the slack season, for conversation, at rates that would make the door-book blush — decides to have heard it as a compliment to the bucket and drinks. Calpurnius laughs, genuinely, a big, unguarded sound that reorganises his face into the one his wife presumably married. He is enjoying himself. This is the fact about the evening the versions will later drop, the way versions drop ballast: the man was happy, in a room full of people exercising their best professional selves, arguing about breathing with a singer from Corinth who treated his hobby like a trade.

“You should set something of mine,” he says. “A short thing. For a real voice. My uncle says it would be founding a dynasty in the wrong art.”

“Bring the short thing to the house on a morning,” Philomela says, “and I will tell you if it breathes. Singers are the customs officers of poetry, Gaius Calpurnius. Most of it is smuggled. The rest is ballast.”

Across the room the tax farmers are on their fifth course and their second partnership. The first one died over the fish (a spoilage clause; Felix the tax farmer believes in writing spoilage down until it confesses; his partner, a lean man from Brundisium with a smile like a contract's fine print, believes spoilage, like gratitude, is improved by leaving it unminuted). Now Heron joins them with the fleet's winter figures in his head and no paper anywhere about his person — Alexandrian style, which Diomedes, observing from the serving door with a colleague's respect, describes later as *carrying the books behind the eyes, like madam* — and the partnership resurrects itself between the boar and the little cakes, in language so approximate that only the men who will profit by it understand a word, which in Rome is the definition of a binding agreement.

Antonia, doing her circuit, hears Serapion tell Avidius a story about a harbour tax in Alexandria and a priest of Serapis who attempted to collect it twice, once for the city and once for the god, on the grounds that the god's share had been omitted since the Ptolemies. It is a story with a long, dishonest preamble, and Avidius is enjoying it the way harbour men enjoy weather they are not currently in. The story's architecture requires — Serapion is an artist; the beard is a curtain, and it rises — that the priest produce from the temple archives a receipt, forged by his grandfather, witnessed by a crocodile. The room starts to gather the way rooms gather around warmth. The musicians have stopped tuning. Even Ammonius, presenting in person a sauce he has rebuilt from a first version Antonia returned with the single word *again*, pauses in the doorway with the dish like an offering left too long at a shrine, unwilling to break the story's surface.

“And the collector,” Serapion says, arriving at the part he has clearly been sailing toward since the fish, “examines the receipt. He examines the witness. The witness —” the beard performs a small, grave undulation, “— examines *him*.”

The laugh goes up where he put it: Apicius would have timed a sauce by it. Avidius laughs like a harbour in a gale. The tax farmers laugh in their newly resurrected partnership, at the same rate, as a clause. And Gaius Calpurnius, who has been waiting all evening, it appears, for a story in which the paperwork is witnessed by a crocodile — a man who writes elegiacs about breath, whose doctor has written literature about his heart, whose wife two streets away is at that moment telling her sister that these Alexandrian dinners run late and his colour has been better at home all week — Gaius Calpurnius laughs last and longest, with his whole unguarded face, in the interval between courses, while Philomela beside his couch is still smiling, and Antonia at the far end of the room is turning toward the sound with the expression of a woman whose seating geography has done everything it can.

The fleet comes up with the boar, as it was always going to. All evening the grain ships of Alexandria have been sailing in the room's ballast — tonnage under the talk of fish, winter weather under the music — and with the third course Serapion brings them formally to anchor.

“The prefect of Egypt will want the sailings early this year,” he says, in the tone of a man discussing the weather at sea, which in his family *is* the weather at sea. “There is talk in the Canopic quarter of stores for an eastern movement. Grain follows talk the way gulls follow offal. It does not ask the talk's credentials.”

“Eastern movement,” Avidius says, tasting the phrase the way he tasted Ammonius' rebuilt sauce: carefully, and with the intention of having it again at his own prices. “The emperor's east is a rumour with a staff.”

“Most staffs begin as rumours. It is how they avoid the winter sailings.” Heron, who has the figures behind his eyes, lays them out on the table between the cups without paper: hulls available, hulls promised, the harbour dues at Ostia sitting in the middle of the numbers like a reef everyone at the table has hit in a different season. Antonia, on her circuit, hears the reef get its name — the same dues her landlord's agent, her husband-that-never-was, her whole triangle of paper and committee, have lately been navigating — and hears Avidius say, with the mildness of a man reading figures, which are his poetry, “A senator of my acquaintance will speak to the dues this year. Family reasons.”

The room does not pause. Rooms in her house are trained. But Diomedes, at the serving door, makes a note in the darkness behind his eyes to match Heron's, and Antonia files Avidius' sentence where she files all sentences that arrive with their price already worked out: between the quarters. A wedding for a harbour due. A committee concluded for a wedding. Paper in several directions at once. She has seldom been prouder of the seating: Serapion on one flank, the tax farmer watching his enemy civilised, the senator's nephew breathing hexameters by the musicians, and in the middle distance her own hostess's body, circulating like good coin, while the empire's breakfast next winter is arranged over boar.

“Your boar is from Hispania,” Serapion says to her, as she passes. It is not a question. In Alexandria men identify pork the way Romans identify accents: as intelligence.

“From a man outside Tarraco who owes the Greek at the Basilica an apology and expresses it quarterly in meat. Quality is the only apology that keeps giving evidence.”

Avidius laughs before he can file it. “She steals, Serapion. In front of witnesses. It is why the house is full.”

“I recycle,” Antonia says. “In this city everything has been somewhere else first. That is the dedication we're all performing.”

She says it lightly, for the table, and moves on; but Serapion's eyes follow her the length of the room with the expression of a customs officer who has just watched cargo volunteer its own manifest. Later — much later, in the baths version, where the evening will be misfiled under a death like a letter under a stone — men will remember the food and the singing and the terrible minute in the interval. Nobody will remember that the winter fleet was priced over the boar, and the harbour dues traded for a wedding, and a widow-maker of a story loaded by a bearded man from the Canopic quarter, all in the same twenty minutes, in a room hung with two hundred red flowers sewn by a girl across the river whose name the register will now never spell. That is not because the minute swallowed the evening. It is because the evening, like all Antonia's profitable ones, was a lattice of small, unglamorous decisions, and Rome has never known how to invoice a lattice. It can only carve the part that falls down.

Serapion has brought with him from Alexandria a small climate of his own: brighter cloth, slower vowels, and a way of discussing money as if it were weather with responsibilities. He is in the office with the Greek’s subordinate, going over freight for a cargo that includes wine, linen and—because Alexandria cannot resist sending Rome a metaphor—papyrus in quantities that would keep committees from silence for a decade.

“Your city imports arguments,” Serapion says, “and exports instructions for holding them.”

Antonia, who is checking the wine before it acquires a reputation in her cellar, says, “Your city exports both. We merely add taxes in transit.”

Philomela has been summoned not to sing but to listen, a professional refinement that startles some of the younger men. An Alexandrian musician in Serapion’s party has lately taken to setting Greek verses with a pause before the final noun, “so the room can appreciate the expense,” he explains. Philomela hears him through once. “The expense is stealing the noun’s entrance.”

“You disapprove?”

“I harmonise. It is more dangerous.”

She gives him the line back with the pause shortened and the last word allowed to arrive like a person whose fee has been paid in advance. Serapion looks towards Antonia as if a cargo had shifted into better trim.

“She is expensive,” Antonia says helpfully.

“Most accuracy is.”

Diomedes is copying figures at a rate that suggests the figures fear him. A young Alexandrian clerk, keen to show metropolitan technique, has used a term for demurrage that Diomedes renders into Latin as *the price of keeping ships waiting while men find adjectives*.

“That is not the term,” the clerk says.

“It is the meaning. The term may join it when it is sober.”

There is business in all this, but there is also the slower import Rome rarely declares: a different idea of women’s money. In Alexandria, Serapion tells them, his sister owns shares in a warehouse and litigates in her own voice through a Secretary who has learned to take dictation without making it sound like pregnancy. The room receives this as it receives sauces from abroad, with interest and a suspicion that Rome could do it more importantly.

“Would she sing?” Philomela asks.

“She would invoice.”

“Then we are colleagues by another harbour.”

Antonia makes no speech. She enters the wine, appreciates the papyrus and watches Philomela correct the pause in a stranger’s song. Empire, when it works at all, is often no grander than this: somebody from elsewhere showing a room where its own drains might run clearer.

It is the senator's nephew who dies. Later there will be versions — the house will accumulate versions the way the Tiber accumulates offerings — but the version Antonia keeps, the housekeeping version, is this: a man of thirty, overweight, over-wined, with a heart his physician had already described to his wife in the words physicians use for a roof in a bad street, laughing at a story in the interval between courses; a choke that was not a choke; Philomela, who has presence of mind the way other singers have jewellery, getting him upright and calling for the physician who lives two streets away and dines, on Antonia's standing arrangement, with one ear open; the physician arriving fast and working competently and the man's colour deciding, as colour does, to leave anyway, all of it over in less time than the kitchen needs to ruin a sauce.

A death in a house like hers is not a tragedy first. It is a legal shape first, and a rumour immediately after, and a tragedy — for a wife two streets away is receiving the news from Barbatus, who has taken off his door-keeper's face and put on the human one he keeps for such errands — in its proper place, which is everywhere and therefore nowhere the aediles can measure.

Antonia does the things. She has rehearsed the things the way ships rehearse fire. The room is cleared without panic. The other guests are given cloaks and discretion and the back street in ones and twos, with the bill forgotten by policy and remembered by gratitude. The body is laid in the small courtyard room, decently, with a coin and clean linen, and no one, *no one*, is permitted to say the word poison within the walls, because words are seeds and this house will not garden its own ruin. Diomedes writes the evening down while it is still warm: who was present, what was served, the hour, the physician's name, the sequence, in the upright dull hand of the tax book, because truth, to survive in Rome, must learn to look boring on paper.

Avidius is among the last to leave. At the door he says, “You run a tight ship in a storm.”

“It was not a storm. It was a man laughing, with a bad heart, in a city that feeds men like him for thirty years and then blames the room he dies in.”

“All the same.” He puts on his cloak. “The Lentulus committee will not call it that. Mind your paperwork, Antonia. And mind who profited from a death in your house this quarter. Sometimes a thing is an accident with excellent timing, and sometimes—” he shrugs, a harbourman's shrug, philosophical and entirely sincere “—sometimes the timing is the accident's employer.”

Barbatus goes to the widow the way he once went into arenas: without anything in his hands. It is a policy he and Antonia arrived at years ago, after a death in another house, in the two-table years — a drowned boatman, fished out by the house's people on a winter morning, with the news to be carried to a wife in a street off the cattle market. Hands empty, face his own, the facts in the order of the evening. The widow of Gaius Calpurnius keeps him standing — senators' families keep everyone standing; it is a kind of architecture — and asks the questions wives of her order are not supposed to know how to ask, in the order her grief supplies rather than her rank: did he suffer, was he alone, who held him, what had he eaten, had he laughed. Barbatus answers them all, including the one inside the others, which he has heard before in other houses and other registers, *was he with a woman*, without the words ever being put into the street between them.

“A room of witnesses, domina. Twenty men and the musicians. The singer had his shoulders. There wasn't time to be alone. That's the house's word and it'll be the physician's, in better Latin.”

She looks at the big freedman doorkeeper delivering better Latin's advance guard in her hall at midnight, and what her face does then Barbatus will report to the night porter, much later, as a fight only in the technical sense: something taken on the chin, absorbed, no falling. “He would have liked the witnesses,” she says, and cries, after, with the efficiency of a person opening shutters, while her household deploys around her and her sister starts, already, the list that Rome starts: the uncle to be told first, the will, the mourning that must not look bought. Barbatus withdraws backwards a pace, the way men leave shrines, and is given at the door — by a steward in a panic of good management — a purse for his trouble, which he refuses with a formula Antonia taught him for these errands: *the house doesn't charge for the carrying, dominus; only for the dinners.*

In the small courtyard room, meanwhile, the physician writes. He is Antonia's tableware physician — Nikias, Greek, fifty, with a practice built out of rich men's dinners and poor women's childbeds, which he maintains in that order *so that the first pays for the second and neither knows it is being governed*, a man after Diomedes' own heart — and he writes the evening in the order of the evening before anyone in this story will coin the phrase: the hour, the course, the laughter, the colour going like a tide going out where a child has dug a channel, the measures taken, the measures irrelevant. He signs it. He seals it in front of Diomedes and the Alexandrians' travelling physician, who had been at the table with his own counsel's eye on the room and who now adds, unasked and at cost of some professional weather across his face, a second signature to the Greek copy.

“Two physicians,” Nikias says to Antonia, in the corridor, washing his hands in a basin a boy holds as if it were evidence, “agreeing in writing. It happens in Rome perhaps once a reign. Frame the phenomenon.”

“You'll deposit it at dawn.”

“I shall deposit it at dawn, buy fish, and tell three dinners tonight's story with the names filed off, so that by noon the truth has a head start in a false beard. It is the immunisation we have.” He dries his hands on a cloth of Prima's work without knowing it — everything in this house is somebody's work, being used as itself — and looks at her with the directness men of his trade are licensed for. “It was his heart, Antonia. It was his heart last year and it would have been his heart at a temple feast, with the omens excellent and the priests all looking the other way. Your seating did what seating can.”

“Seating,” she says, “is not among the causes the committee will list.”

“No,” Nikias says. “For that you have paper, and witnesses, and a singer who held his shoulders instead of the note. Hold your paper the way you hold everything, madam: quarterly, and in more hands than yours.”

The Alexandrians are the last to go besides Avidius. Serapion at the door stops and looks back at the emptied dining room — couches stripped, the hanging's red border marching over a field suddenly gone quiet, serving boys moving like a tide going out — and does the thing his Roman partners will retell at baths until it becomes, like all their truths, a kind of cargo. He takes off a ring. Not an heirloom: a working ring, harbour gold, worn smooth. “For the singer,” he says, “who held the man. Not payment. In Alexandria we have a word for money that is not payment and it takes a philosopher to spend it. Give it to her with my house's name filed off.”

Antonia puts it — she does not entrust it to the night's confusion of trays — in the box in Diomedes' office, *Guests' Property, Forgotten*, on top of the evening's other arrested tides: a cloak clasp, a Greek secretary's stylus, someone's luck-piece on a cord. The box is getting to be a museum of the city's lowered guard. Tomorrow claimants will come at dusk in panics of discretion. The ring will wait a day, by her decision, so that when Philomela receives it the giving has cooled into record, and no one, in any inquiry anyone is composing, can file it under the price of a woman's statement.

Philomela is in the musicians' room with her lyre across her knees like a child she is not holding for comfort. “He asked if the hexameter breathes,” she says, to Antonia, to the room, to the colleague tuning in the corner who knows better than to be in this conversation. “I should have given him a duller answer.”

“You gave him the true one. It is the house speciality. We charge by the course.” Antonia sits — in this room, at this hour, sitting is a message the staff read correctly: the emergency is over; the night is now merely terrible — and puts her hands flat on her own knees the way she tests walls. “Tomorrow men will ask you questions in an order designed to make the evening into other evenings. Tell it as it happened. The fish, the metre, the story, the laugh. In that order. If a question arrives from the wrong end, walk it back to the fish.”

“Is that a technique?”

“It is a life,” Antonia says. “The technique is just its paperwork.” She rises; the room reassembles; somewhere a boy is crying in the kitchen because death has been in the building and he is eleven and his mother apprenticed him for arithmetic, and Ammonius is finding out loud, in Alexandrian Greek of the first quality, whether pastry is spoken anywhere in the theology of the moment.

She stands in the doorway after he has gone, in the smell of lamp oil and cooling food, and listens to her house performing its grief procedures: low voices, water, linen. Eirene comes to stand beside her without touching her, which is how they touch.

Diomedes finishes the evening's writing in the small hours, in a house that has gone the particular quiet of buildings where somebody has died between courses. He enters the Alexandrians' booking as fulfilled, the wine as served, the staff as paid for a short night at the long rate, because Antonia, in the corridor an hour ago, said *full rate* in the tone of a woman settling with the gods in a currency she controls. He enters the ring, temporarily, under guests' property, forgotten, which is not yet true, and will be by morning, and is the truest description of most things entrusted to paper. Then, in the second book, in the hand for facts that must survive fashion, he writes the name of Gaius Calpurnius, the hour, and — after a pause during which the stylus does not move, a toll-gate in the night — the words *died laughing*, which no tax office requires and no committee will know how to file.

“Second catastrophe,” Antonia says. “In its proper order.”

“What was the first?”

“I am not counting those yet either.”
