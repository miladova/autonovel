# The Tarpaulin Truck

*Warsaw, 4 June 1943*

June came down on Warsaw like a brass lid dropped onto a boiling pot: thirty degrees in the shade by noon, tar bubbling soft black beads along Marszałkowska tram-setts, horseflies thick as currants round droshky blinkers, and every unwashed courtyard smelling of sour cabbage-brine and overflowing dustbins.

At twelve minutes past one on Friday afternoon Sylwia stepped out of Jabłoński's antiquarian medical bookshop on Nowy Świat carrying six glass tubes of prewar sterile Brno catgut Jabłoński had kept hidden behind *Anatomia Człowieka* since thirty-nine, squinting against blinding white sunlight bouncing off church stucco.

Halfway to Świętokrzyska corner she noticed every pigeon along the cornice of the Ministerstwo Skarbu ledge take wing at once before human ears caught why.

Two seconds later three shrill tin police whistles blew simultaneously—north at Świętokrzyska, south at Foksal, west down Warecka—and out of side streets roared four olive-green *Ordnungspolizei* open-topped troop carriers and six high canvas-hooded Mercedes *Buda* lorries, tires screaming black rubber across hot cobbles as iron tailboards crashed down on chains!

*Łapanka.*

Instantly Nowy Świat turned into a kicked anthill inside a sealed glass jar. Men in linen jackets dropped briefcases and sprinted into courtyard archways only to meet grey-green *Schupo* riflemen and black-uniformed Ukrainian *Schutzmannschaft* auxiliaries already pouring out of rear passages to hammer oak courtyard gates shut with iron bars; a red Number Nine tram ground to a spark-showering halt fifty feet from Sylwia with policemen boarding both front and back platforms simultaneously, yanking screaming shopgirls and bareheaded clerks down onto the asphalt by their coat collars.

Sylwia did not run.

Running in a *łapanka* got a Mauser round through your kidneys before you made ten yards, and inside her handbag sat triple armor: municipal *Szpital Dzieciątka Jezus* epidemic-surgeon *Arbeitskarte*, grey *Kennkarte* stamped by *Amtsarzt* Warschau, and Ludwig Schoeler's pale-green Wehrmacht *Sonderausweis* valid across every cordon in the district—today being Friday, three hours and forty-five minutes before her standing five-o'clock attendance at Królewska Sixteen!

Standing flat-footed beside a cast-iron lamppost out of the stampede trample, clutching her catgut parcel inside her handbag against her ribs, she already had both folded cards open in her right hand when a heavy, sweat-purpled *Scharführer* in field-grey police wool—Sudeten or Austrian borderlander by his thick *k.u.k.* barrack vowels, tunic dark under both armpits, breath reeking of warm plum schnapps from ten paces—shoved an old piano-tuner past him toward the nearest canvas lorry and swung his slung Karabiner rifle sideways across Sylwia's chest.

"Links in den Wagen—schnell, weiter!"

"Chirurgin im Seucheneinsatz," Sylwia said clearly, holding the pale-green Wehrmacht *Sonderausweis* and her hospital card steady at eye level without flinching from the hot gun barrel brushing her blouse. "Städtisches Krankenhaus Kindlein Jesu und *Ärztlicher Außendienst* der Wehrmachtkommandantur. Prüfen Sie den Stempel, *Herr Scharführer*."

Had he been sober, or Prussian, or alone, violet *Abwehrstelle* ink would have ended the conversation inside five seconds.

Instead two junior Ukrainian auxiliaries stood grinning behind his elbow watching to see whether a sharp-voiced Polish woman in clean grey linen could talk a German sergeant out of street quota—and across the sergeant's left cheek sat three fresh strips of white sticking-plaster from whatever brick or partisan glass-splinter had put four *Schupo* patrols in foul murder-temper since dawn on Praga bridge.

His bloodshot little eyes dropped to *Kotarska, Sylwia — Polin* on her grey *Kennkarte*, slid across to the pale-green Wehrmacht pass authorizing five-o'clock visits to an *Oberstleutnant*'s private apartment on Królewska, and twisted into an ugly wet sneer.

"Außendienst um fünf Uhr beim Herrn Oberstleutnant," he mimicked loudly so both auxiliaries sniggered, snatching the pale-green card out of her fingers before she could pull back. "Für polnische Offiziersmatratzen gibt's heute Sonderzug nach Osten—da kannst du Feldwebeln die Stiefel wischen!"

With two fat, sweaty thumbs he ripped Stabsarzt Vesper's and Hauptmann von Berg's stamped green Wehrmacht *Sonderausweis* straight down the middle, tore the halves across a second time, and tossed all four pale-green scraps fluttering into horse-piss and gutter dust beside the kerb!

Fury—pure, blinding, suicidal Berlin reflex before survival sense caught her wrist—made Sylwia lunge half a step down toward the torn green quarters in the gutter: "Call *Ortskommandantur* Saski Square before you tear Wehrmacht stamps, you drunken idiot—!"

The flat walnut butt-plate of his Mauser rifle swung short and vicious into her left shoulder blade—not full bone-cracking swing, half-arm cattle-prod blow heavy enough to knock every cubic inch of breath roaring out of her lungs and pitch her to one knee on burning cobbles, scraping skin raw through her left stocking while one glass tube of Brno catgut snapped sharp inside her handbag!

Before she dragged half a breath back through dusty teeth, two pairs of rough hands grabbed both her upper arms from behind, hoisted her bodily off the asphalt, and flung her sprawling over the iron-bound oak tailboard onto the splintered wooden floor of the third *Buda* lorry.

Up slammed the tailboard; iron pins dropped *clang-clang* through staples; heavy green tarpaulin flaps laced shut over the rear hoop, plunging thirty-eight jammed human bodies into ninety-degree canvas twilight smelling of hot axle-grease, unwashed sweat, crushed garlic, and terror.

With a grinding lurch the Mercedes diesel roared away eastward down Ordynacka plunge toward Poniatowski Bridge.

Sylwia hauled herself upright onto bruised knees between a grey-haired notary clutching a crushed straw panama hat to his chest and a terrified girl no older than sixteen in a blue school gym-tunic whose bronchial tubes whistled dry asthmatic wheezes like reeds with every breath in the airless canvas oven. Through her torn left stocking blood trickled warm down Sylwia's shin; behind her left scapula a palm-sized hematoma was already stiffening muscle hot and dull where the rifle-butt had landed.

Nobody inside a *Buda* lorry talked loud because two armed guards rode perched on the rear step outside the laced canvas. Women prayed *Pod Twoją obronę* under their breath; a twenty-year-old boy in baker's white trousers tried slicing a ventilation slit through the tarpaulin with a pocket razor-blade until three men dragged his wrist down in panic lest the rear guards fire blind through the canvas into packed backs.

Ten minutes across the river; rattle over Targowa tram-tracks; sharp right turn through tall brick schoolyard gates ringed triple-deep with barbed wire along **ulica Skaryszewska**.

Every soul in Warsaw knew *Skaryszewska 8*. Prewar municipal high school turned *Arbeitsamt* transit pen—where street *łapanka* catches were sorted like slaughterhouse calves inside twenty-four hours: healthy young hands shoved onto night freight cars west to Essen Krupp works or Peenemünde marshes; anyone coughing, crippled, Jewish-featured, or carrying "suspicious" papers culled left into closed cattle-wagons bound south-east for Majdanek or Oswiecim.

By half-past two Sylwia stood penned with two hundred and ninety other women inside the former third-floor gymnasium under tall wire-meshed windows baked white by afternoon sun.

Zero water taps worked on the third floor; one overflowing zinc slop-tub sat in the corner behind a torn blanket for three hundred bladders; chalk dust and chloride of lime rose off dry parquet with every shifting shoe until throats burned raw and the sixteen-year-old schoolgirl in the blue gym-tunic—*Zosia*, daughter of a tram-conductor on Solec, caught buying thread after school—sank down onto the floorboards beside Sylwia's ankles, blue-lipped and clawing at her own collar in full status asthmaticus panic.

Sylwia knelt immediately beside her, snapping open her battered handbag—thank Christ *Schupo* street sweeps didn't confiscate handbags until downstairs sorting tables!—checking past the one broken catgut tube for her emergency ampoule pocket. One solitary milliliter ampoule of one-in-a-thousand *Adrenalinum Parke-Davis* sat wedged beside her boiled metal-cased pocket syringe!

No alcohol swab, no boiled water—spit on thumb or dry skin beat suffocation inside ten minutes. Sylwia snapped the glass ampoule neck inside her skirt hem, drew three-tenths of a milliliter into her pocket needle, pushed Zosia's damp blue sleeve above the deltoid, and slid subcutaneous adrenalin clean into the shaking arm while holding the girl's chin up out of floor lime.

"In through the nose, four counts—out through pursed lips, six counts," Sylwia ordered quietly in her calmest ward voice, pressing two cool fingers gently against Zosia's racing carotid until five minutes later smooth muscle relaxed along bronchioles, whistling eased from stridor to dry wheeze, and pink crept back into the child's lips.

"If they hear me wheezing downstairs at the doctor's table," Zosia whispered against Sylwia's bruised shoulder, trembling like a wet rabbit, "they mark *Krank* in blue pencil—my cousin got marked blue in January from Skaryszewska and went to Lublin instead of Saxony, and her mother got ashes notice in March—"

"Nobody marks you blue while you hold my bag," Sylwia said grimly, glancing up at the round electric Siemens wall-clock bolted high above the gymnasium basketball backboard—dead unplugged since thirty-nine, so she checked her own pinned lapel watch instead.

Four-forty-five.

Fifteen minutes until five o'clock.

Walk through the mechanics cold, without fairy tales:
At five-zero-zero, Sylwia did not ring Service Stair B at Królewska Sixteen.
At five-fifteen—Ludwig tolerated ten minutes' Warsaw tram delay, never fifteen—Bruno Mekelburg walked down to the courtyard gatehouse telephone and rang *Szpital Dzieciątka Jezus* gate-lodge on Lindleya.
Old Walenty or Bronka answered: *Pani doktor went out at twelve-thirty to Jabłoński's bookshop on Nowy Świat for catgut and was due back for three-o'clock rounds—she never came back, and word came in at two that Nowy Świat between Foksal and Świętokrzyska was swept clean by Buda trucks!*

Wait: what if Ludwig wasn't even inside *Wohnung 4* today?! What if Tirpitzufer called him to Minsk this morning, or an afternoon briefing at Palais Brühl ran till eight p.m.—and by eight p.m. Friday's six-thirty Lublin/Reich freight train at Dworzec Wschodni was already forty kilometers out of Warsaw with wooden doors wired shut from outside?!

Five-ten. Downstairs in the school courtyard lorry engines revved; iron whistles blew twice; heavy boots tramped up the stone stairs and flung the double gymnasium doors wide.

"Alle aufstehen! Zu zweien antreten—Kennkarten in die linke Hand, Taschen öffnen!"

Two *Arbeitsamt* civilian clerks in brown linen dust-coats flanked by four Latvian or Ukrainian guards began driving the line down two flights to the long trestle sorting-tables set up across the sunlit asphalt courtyard under horse-chestnut trees, where a fat balding German camp physician in shirt-sleeves with a stethoscope dangling untouched round his neck glanced two seconds per woman—teeth, hands, ankles—chalking *A-1* (Reich heavy agriculture/munitions) on backs of coats in white chalk or jerking his chin left toward the closed brick shed (*Sonderbehandlung / Polizeiüberprüfung*) whenever papers looked irregular or lungs rattled.

Five-thirty-two. Twenty women ahead of Sylwia and Zosia in the shuffling double column down the hot stone stairs.

Five-forty-one. Six women ahead. Out in the glaring courtyard beyond the open vestibule doors, Sylwia could already see two long covered lorries idling to ferry the *A-1* chalked batch eight hundred meters across to Dworzec Wschodni freight ramps for the six-thirty departure. Without her torn green Wehrmacht pass, carrying five unbroken surgical catgut tubes, a hypodermic syringe, and three scalpels inside her handbag—what happened the second the *Sonderdienst* table guard dumped three surgical scalpels out onto the sorting board in front of Security Police clerks?! Straight left into the brick shed as an underground partisan medic!

"Drop three paces behind me if they open my bag," Sylwia whispered barely moving her lips to Zosia clinging white-knuckled to her sleeve. "Say you never saw me before the truck."

"Nächste—vortreten!" barked the sorting clerk.

Sylwia stepped out into blinding five-forty-six June sun before Trestle Two, laying her grey *Kennkarte* and municipal hospital *Arbeitskarte* flat on the scarred pine planks just as the Ukrainian guard grabbed her scuffed Gladstone bag upside-down and dumped scalpels, arterial forceps, stethoscope, and catgut tubes clattering loud across the wood!

The fat camp doctor blinked over sweaty spectacles: "Was zum Teufel—Messer im Transportlager?!"

"Municipal surgeon on epidemic typhus duty at *Dzieciątka Jezus*, swept unlawfully off Nowy Świat while carrying theater suture stock," Sylwia said clearly in icy Berlin German—gambling everything left in her lungs on buying three minutes' bureaucratic hesitation—"and scheduled at seventeen hundred hours under *Wehrmachtkommandantur* order at *Abwehrstelle Warschau*—"

"Every third Polack caught with knives works personally for Field Marshal Keitel," the brown-coated *Arbeitsamt* supervisor sneered, picking up his red-lead police pencil to mark her *Kennkarte* straight for the Aleja Szucha van waiting by the shed. "Left into the brick—"

Out at the main Skaryszewska iron gatehouse fifty yards away, tires screamed across hot cobbles hard enough to make every guard in the yard whirl round toward the street!

A heavy black-and-grey Wehrmacht staff Mercedes-Benz 260 crashed straight up to the lowered red-and-white wooden pole without waiting for the gate-guard's whistle; before the pole finished rising halfway, Bruno Mekelburg leaped out of the driver's seat one-handed to yank the barrier pin clear himself while the right rear door swung open and **Oberstleutnant Ludwig Schoeler** stepped out onto Skaryszewska asphalt.

Sylwia had never seen him walk inside German ranks before.

Buttoned to the throat in immaculate field-grey gabardine tunic with silver braided *Oberstleutnant* shoulder-boards blazing white in low June sun, Iron Cross First Class gleaming cold over his left pocket, peaked cap visor pulled low and lethal above winter-water eyes, gloves slapped flat in his left fist—he didn't hurry, didn't shout, didn't waste one syllable on Ukrainian riflemen. Walking straight across fifty yards of sorting yard as if three hundred prisoners and ten *Arbeitsamt* clerks were weeds growing through gravel, he stopped directly in front of Trestle Two while the fat camp physician scrambled hastily off his kitchen chair trying to button his shirt-collar!

"Who commands this cattle-pen?" Ludwig asked—voice quiet, clipped, carrying thirty yards across dead-silent asphalt like a steel ruler snapped over marble.

Out of the ground-floor office hurried Camp *Lagerführer* Hanke—fifty, bloated belly inside SA-reserve brown breeches, wiping beer foam off his lip and puffing up until he got close enough to read silver general-staff rank *plus* the small silver-chained Wehrmacht high-command warrant credentials Mekelburg stepped forward two paces to hold open six inches from Hanke's nose!

"*Herr Oberstleutnant*—Lagerführer Hanke, *Arbeitsamt Warschau*—"

"At thirteen-fifteen hours today on Nowy Świat," Ludwig said without acknowledging Hanke's name or raised arm by half a blink, "one of your drunken street-sweepers tore up a stamped *Wehrmachtkommandantur* and *Abwehrstelle* specialist pass issued over *Sanitätsabteilung* seal, struck an epidemic-ward vascular surgeon under direct Wehrmacht requisition with a rifle stock, and dumped her into this yard while six typhus-isolation beds and a senior staff medical inspection waited." His eyes dropped once to Sylwia's surgical scalpels scattered across dirty trestle planks, then lifted cold as arctic brine back into Hanke's sweating red face. "Give me the name and company number of the *Schutzpolizei Scharführer* who commanded the Nowy Świat sweep at thirteen-fifteen. Right now, on that pad, before I telephone General Stahel's military-court duty judge from your own desk."

Every drop of beer-pink drained gray out of Hanke's jowls. *Arbeitsamt* civilian bullies ruled Poles like gods; cross Wehrmacht counter-intelligence and military-governor epidemic statutes in the same afternoon, and forty-eight-year-old *Lagerführer* found themselves transferred to clearing partisan swamps behind Pripyat!

"A regrettable street-confusion by *Ordnungspolizei* auxiliary detachments not under my direct table, *Herr Oberstleutnant*!" Hanke stammered furiously, snatching Sylwia's *Kennkarte* out from under his terrified clerk's red pencil before a mark touched it and shoving her scalpels and catgut tubes back into her Gladstone bag with his own plump hands! "Naturally municipal epidemic surgeons enjoy full Reich exemption—Frau Doktor is released immediately with apologies of the Directorate!"

Ludwig took the bag out of Hanke's hands himself—checking calmly inside that nothing steel was missing—and handed it to Sylwia without touching her bruised arm in front of ten German eyes.

Sylwia took the bag, did not move half an inch toward the waiting Mercedes, and hooked her left hand firmly round sixteen-year-old Zosia's trembling blue gym-tunic elbow right behind her.

"And my typhus-laboratory technician," Sylwia said coldly in German straight at Hanke's nose. "Carrying four boxes of slide cover-slips in my pocket when your patrol split us on the kerb—without her washing infected glass tomorrow morning, my basement diagnostic bench closes."

For one fraction of a second Hanke's little pig-eyes narrowed at sixteen-year-old Zosia's schoolgirl collar and book-satchel—any blind idiot saw she was a high-school pupil caught on Solec, not a typhus technician!—and glanced nervously toward Ludwig to see whether the *Oberstleutnant* knew the Polish doctor was lying through her teeth right under his visor.

Ludwig didn't even turn his head to look at the girl.

"Did I stutter on *full epidemic staff*, Hanke?" Ludwig asked softly. "Stamp the girl's release slip before my driver loses his temper."

Purple rubber stamp hit paper twice—*BUM, BUM*—faster than machine-gun bolts.

Ninety seconds later the black-and-grey Mercedes rolled smoothly back out Skaryszewska's iron gates—leaving three hundred white faces staring silent after them through wire mesh in burning dust, a sight that stayed jammed like ragged tin down Sylwia's throat—turned three corners south to a quiet tram-island off Targowa out of sight of camp guards, and paused five seconds while trembling Zosia scrambled out whispering broken sobs over Sylwia's knuckles with two hundred złoty Sylwia shoved into her school pocket to get home before dark.

* * *

Mekelburg didn't drive straight across Poniatowski Bridge into town.

Down the cobbled riverside ramp beneath the towering stone viaduct arches along Wybrzeże Szczecińskie—where willow scrub screened the dusty embankment from bridge traffic overhead and dirty green June river-water lapped sluggishly against sunken nineteen-thirty-nine barge ribs—Mekelburg pulled the Mercedes into deep stone shadow, murmured something wooden about checking the left front brake-drum for dragging shoes, stepped out with a wrench, and walked forty paces upwind round the masonry pier out of sight and earshot.

Inside the spacious grey wool-upholstered back seat of the staff car, heat sat trapped and motionless, smelling of sun-baked German leather, warm motor oil, and the sour Skaryszewska chloride-of-lime dust clinging to Sylwia's hair and torn left stocking.

All afternoon—under rifle-butts, inside stifling tarpaulin, kneeling in lime dust beside choking girls, facing red pencils at Trestle Two—adrenalin and iron self-command had kept her hands steady as slate. Now, sitting three feet from Ludwig Schoeler on plush Wehrmacht cushions with safety suddenly locked round her like a velvet collar, every humiliated, furious, raw nerve in her body detonated at once.

Ludwig took off his peaked cap, tossed it onto the jump-seat ahead, and turned sideways toward her—grey-green eyes raking rapidly, clinically down from the loosened dark hair straggling dusty across her cheek, past the grey boot-print bruise darkening the left shoulder of her linen jacket where the Mauser butt had hit her, down to the dried crimson crust where cobbles had torn skin open below her left kneecap through ripped lisle stocking.

Without asking, he reached his ungloved right hand across the seat to ease the dusty lapel of her jacket half an inch back off her bruised left shoulder-joint to feel whether bone creaked.

"Did that pig crack your scapula or—"

Before his fingers touched linen, Sylwia swung her open right palm—not pushing him off, full whip-crack shoulder-turn backhand—square across his mouth!

Bone and raw mercury-roughened knuckles struck his jaw and lower lip with a sharp crack loud as a pistol cap inside the closed car, splitting the pale February scar-ridge on his lower lip open instantly in a bright crimson bead while knocking his head twenty degrees sideways!

Sylwia tried grabbing the chrome door-handle behind her hip with shaking fingers to get out onto river stones where she could breathe air that didn't belong to his army—and never got the latch half-way up.

Ludwig didn't strike back, and he didn't shout. Faster than Schöneberg ropes, his right hand caught both her wrists together in one iron ring before her second swing cleared her lap, twisting her clean round off the door-latch until her back hit the angled grey corner-upholstery hard and his six-foot frame came straight over her across the seat—left knee wedged onto leather beside her thigh, left hand bracing high against the window pillar despite the missing collarbone strut while his right fist pinned both her trembling wrists flat against the wool cushion beside her ear!

Ten inches above her face, a thin bright thread of scarlet ran from his split lower lip down toward his chin; his pupils were black flint-chips inside blazing green ice, breath coming hot and ragged straight into her face.

"Go ahead and scream at me if it washes the lime out of your throat, *Kotarska*!" he snarled low in Berlin German, stripped bone-raw of every cool officer mask—voice shaking for the first time in ten years with genuine, white-hot terror that had been eating his own stomach since five-fifteen when Lindleya's porter said *Nowy Świat, Buda trucks*! "Fifteen minutes later onto that siding and Hanke's cattle-cars wired shut for Majdanek—where forty stamped passes from Tirpitzufer wouldn't pull your ashes out of a pit! You think I enjoyed walking in there wearing their silver cord so three hundred Polish women watched you climb into a German car?!"

"Let go of my wrists!" Sylwia gasped, arching upward off the seat cushions so furiously her breasts crushed hard against the silver-buttoned wool of his tunic chest and her torn knee scraped rough grey breeches between his legs, hating him—hating the blood she had just drawn on his mouth, hating that when those iron gates swung open at five-forty-six and she saw his tall grey coat walking across sunlit asphalt toward her trestle, something traitorous and animal deep inside her belly had leaped alive before her brain could strangle it! "I didn't ask you to come across the river—!"

"No—you'd have gone upright into a Lublin gas-shed biting your tongue rather than owe Ludwig Schoeler twenty minutes' breath!"

He shoved her pinned wrists half an inch deeper into hot wool upholstery, leaning down six inches until the blood-bead on his lower lip hovered two finger-breadths above her parted mouth and she smelled copper blood, hot gabardine wool, sun-warmed skin, and forty minutes of pure panic sweating off him under iron control.

Neither of them moved for eight pounding heartbeats while overhead on Poniatowski Viaduct a Number Seven tram thundered past shaking fine dust off the stone arch onto the car roof.

Sylwia's chest heaved tight against his; every breath dragged her thin linen blouse across his Iron Cross pin until metal bit skin right above her left breast. Looking straight into his bleeding mouth two inches away, she felt the terrifying physical pull between them—uglier than gratitude, older than war, sharpened to razor wire by four months of blood and near-death—coiling so tight low in her groin that one more second pinned under his weight on hot leather meant pulling his bleeding mouth straight down onto hers with her teeth!

Ludwig felt the exact shift in her breathing—saw her dark pupils swallow her irises black, felt her thighs stop kicking against his knee and go dangerously, burningly still—and froze rigid as stone two inches above her lips.

Not out here—not in broad six-o'clock daylight under a bridge arch with a bruised shoulder, Skaryszewska lime in her hair, a twenty-two-year-old corporal knocking stones forty yards away, and gratitude muddying the water between them so tomorrow morning she could tell herself shock made her do it!

With a violent, jaw-locked wrench of willpower, Ludwig let go both her wrists at once and shoved himself upright back into his own corner of the seat, pulling a folded grey service handkerchief out of his cuff to press hard against his split lower lip without taking his blazing eyes off her.

"Button your collar," he said raw through bloodstained linen, chest still rising fast beneath grey gabardine. "Mekelburg's driving you as far as Emilii Plater alley—not Koszykowa, not Lindleya gate. I'll have a duplicate green *Sonderausweis* stamped over General Stahel's own garrison block delivered to Docent Mirecki's safe tomorrow morning—let's see a drunken *Schupo* tear Stahel's signature without losing both thumbs."

Sylwia sat bolt upright in the opposite corner, pulling her torn jacket lapel closed with fingers that still shook slightly and refusing to wipe the smudge of Skaryszewska white chalk off her skirt until she reached her own room.

All the way back across the sunlit Vistula behind Mekelburg's silent neck, neither of them spoke another word, sitting thirty inches apart on grey German wool while Ludwig held red linen to his lip and Sylwia stared straight out through bulletproof glass at her conquered city—both of them knowing now, without a shred of illusion left to hide behind, that the next door that locked behind the two of them was not going to open clean.
