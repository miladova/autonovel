# The Tramway Salon

*Warsaw, 20 October 1943*

Before walking up Aleja Szucha a sensible surgeon emptied her pockets the way dying men emptied bladders—leaving behind every scrap of metal or paper that could hang a second person when fingernails came off.

At quarter to ten inside Pavilion Three's locked dispensary, Sylwia laid her gold-cased lapel watch, her hospital master-key ring, her pawnshop ebony Collin scalpel box, and the four remaining blank Vesper movement-vouchers on the marble slab in front of Docent Mirecki and Siostra Bronisława. Into her plain black handbag went nothing except her grey *Kennkarte*, municipal surgical *Arbeitskarte*, one clean linen handkerchief, two hundred złoty in small notes, and today's white *Vorladung* envelope stamped *Zimmer 214*.

She had zero hollow cyanide capsule inside her molar crowns, having refused Section II's potassium-cyanide glass bead back in forty-two because surgeons who carried suicide pills under their tongues bit down twenty minutes too early when bluffing still had legs.

"Four o'clock," Sylwia told Mirecki calmly, buttoning her heavy dark-grey winter wool coat because basement cells in October froze unheated through November. "If I haven't walked back through Walenty's arch by four, Jurek moves Halina and her brother from Grójec to Kielce parish before dawn tomorrow, and Bronka burns Vesper's four blanks in Boiler Two. Don't telephone Stabsarzt Vesper asking after me—Vesper will wet his breeches and tell Szucha everything he doesn't even know."

Mirecki couldn't speak past the grey tremor in his throat; he took her bare right hand between both his papery, blue-veined palms and pressed his dry lips once to her mercury-scarred knuckles in prewar Warsaw drawing-room salute—as if seeing a colleague off onto an ocean liner rather than twenty minutes' walk south down Ujazdowskie. Bronka didn't kiss anything; Bronka shoved half a chunk of cold salted lard-bread wrapped in greaseproof paper deep into Sylwia's coat pocket (*"Eat before eleven—empty stomachs faint faster on stone"*) and turned furiously back to bang bedpans into boiling water so nobody saw her eyes wet.

October sun shone pale lemon-yellow along **Aleje Ujazdowskie**, lighting drifted carpets of golden-brown Norway maple leaves inside Łazienki Park's iron railings until you reached Plac Na Rozdrożu and turned southwest past white-painted steel dragon's-teeth tank barriers into **Aleja Szucha**.

No trams ran down Szucha anymore—rails rusted orange between clean-swept asphalt kerbs—and no Polish pedestrian walked there unless marched between rifles or carrying a white summons slip in wet fingers. German secretaries in smart Loden capes tripped briskly up and down wide granite steps carrying shorthand pads toward café lunch; motorcycles purred polite by curbstones; Number Twenty-Five itself—built in nineteen-thirty by Warsaw's proud young Ministry of Public Education out of sleek functionalist grey limestone, tall brass-framed modernist windows, and polished travertine marble halls—looked far cleaner and brighter from outside than any hospital in Poland.

Inside the main glass rotunda, an SS *Rottenführer* behind plate glass stamped *10:51* across Sylwia's white summons, confiscated her handbag against a numbered brass cloakroom token, and pointed not up the wide travertine grand staircase toward Second Floor Room 214—down a narrow concrete side-staircase descending beneath courtyard level.

"Warteraum unten links bis Aufruf Zimmer zweihundertvierzehn." *Waiting room downstairs left until called.*

Nobody called that basement corridor a waiting room in Polish. Warsaw called it **the Tramway** (*tramwaj*).

Down twelve concrete steps hit the smell: sour boiled turnip-cabbage from guard kitchens braided thick with wet floor-lysol, unwashed terrified human sweat, stale urine, and underneath the lysol that unmistakable iron-copper butcher-shop sweetness Sylwia scrubbed off theater tiles every day of her life.

Along a whitewashed concrete passage forty feet long sat two parallel double rows of plain varnished wooden bentwood chairs arranged back-to-center like seats inside a streetcar—except turned outward so every seated prisoner faced **three inches straight into bare whitewashed brick wall**, forbidden on pain of immediate rubber-truncheon blows across the kidneys to turn chin left, right, or rearward!

Fourteen men and three women sat motionless along the wooden rows when the guard pointed Sylwia onto Chair Nine, Left Row: three inches ahead of her nose sat bumpy white limewash stippled with one tiny dried brown pin-head fleck at eye-level; behind her spine paced slow, unhurried nailed jackboots—*clack... pause... clack*—an SS *Sturmmann* slapping eighteen inches of lead-weighted black rubber cable rhythmically against his palm.

Eighteen inches to Sylwia's left on Chair Eight—close enough inside peripheral vision to read grey hair-pins and soaked wool collar without moving an eyeball—sat an elderly Polish woman past sixty in a neat, mended plum-coloured winter coat whose right velvet lapel was stiffened black-brown with dried blood from a split zygomatic arch and swollen purple ear. Every four seconds the old woman's swollen lips moved half a millimeter without emitting sound—not praying Polish litanies, shaping silent classical quantitative beats that Sylwia's Poznań gymnasium ear recognized after thirty seconds by pure lip-rhythm alone:

*Forsan et haec olim meminisse iuvabit... Durate, et vosmet rebus servate secundis...*

Virgil's *Aeneid*, Book One: *Perhaps one day it will help to remember even these things; endure, and keep yourselves alive for better fortunes.* Some retired girls'-lyceum Latin mistress hauled off Żoliborz beds at five a.m. for mimeographing underground news-bulletins, holding her sixty-year-old skull together between two cellar beatings by scanning hexameters three inches from Gestapo limewash! Down beside the woman's scuffed black lace-up shoe lay one snapped wooden bead off a pocket rosary that had rolled when guards shoved her down; neither woman dared twitch a toe toward it while nailed heels paused three seconds right behind their shoulder-blades—smelling sour of wet boot-grease, cheap peppermint drops, and pipe shag—before *clack... pause... clack* resumed toward the wash-sink.

Ten minutes ticked past in dead tomb-silence. Twenty.

Then, forty feet down the passage beyond a heavy iron-reinforced oak door bearing numbered tin tags, bolts scraped back; two guards dragged someone limp by both armpits—bare bruised heels scraping *scuff-scuff-scuff* across wet concrete behind them without lifting—and dumped him onto Chair Fourteen behind Sylwia's row while down by the slop-sink an orderly wrung a mop out into a galvanized bucket with a loud wet *slosh*. Nobody coughed; nobody turned an eyeball half a millimeter off three inches of white brick.

Sylwia kept her spine straight, hands loose in her wool lap, breathing slow four-second surgical counts through her nose while chewing nothing save cold arithmetic inside her skull—refusing to let twenty-eight minutes in *the Tramway* soften her knees by half an inch before upstairs began.

At eleven-twenty-four boots stopped behind Chair Nine.

"Kotarska. Zweiter Stock, Zimmer zweihundertvierzehn."

* * *

Upstairs on the second floor, autumn sunlight poured cheerful and warm through tall brass-framed Ministry windows across polished oak parquet and thriving green potted *Monstera* plants along the corridor sills.

Room 214 had once belonged to a Polish Under-Secretary for Secondary Schools: generous proportions, cream walls hung with a framed colour portrait of the Führer above prewar Warsaw oak wainscoting, a long conference table covered in spotless dark-green baize cloth, and over by the radiator a pale-blond German stenographer in a neat grey jumper sitting poised behind an Adler Continental typewriter without looking up.

**SS-Hauptsturmführer Reinhart Kroll** stood beside the tall window watering a small terracotta pot of flowering purple cyclamen from a glass carafe.

He wore freshly pressed field-grey wool, silver *SD* diamond gleaming above his left cuff, sandy hair combed damp-neat—and on both hands thin snowy-white boiled cotton gloves. Even from twelve feet away as Sylwia crossed the threshold, she noticed the cotton over the inner aspect of his right wrist looked slightly thicker today—layered over gauze underneath—and when he set the glass carafe down, his left thumb went straight to stroking that covered ridge.

"Good morning, Frau Doktor Kotarska," Kroll said gently, gesturing toward the upright oak chair centered facing green baize and bright window glare while seating himself unhurriedly opposite her with light behind his sandy head. "Forgive the twenty-minute delay downstairs; Wednesday transport clearances crowd the rotunda. May Fräulein Hinz pour you hot herbal tea?"

"Nothing, thank you, *Herr Hauptsturmführer*," Sylwia said calmly, folding raw ungloved hands quietly in her lap so zero tension showed in shoulders or jaw. "I have two strangulated hernias scheduled after two o'clock."

"Optimistic scheduling," Kroll murmured without malice—not threatening, stating calendar weather as a notary might—and opened a clean grey manila binder three inches thick centered squarely on green baize.

He did not shout, did not pace, did not flourish whips. One by one, with delicate white-cotton fingertips, he laid three items side by side across green felt facing her right side up:

First: an eight-by-ten glossy street photograph enlarged sharp from thirty-five-millimeter Leica grain—showing Sylwia herself standing flat-backed in dark wool coat holding two round rye loaves against a stationer's iron rolling-shutter at **three-thirty-two p.m. on Saturday the sixteenth of October**, forty feet opposite bound barefoot men in cement-paper sacks!

Second: a Pawiak prison post-mortem mugshot and ten-print fingerprint card dated *16.X.43*, showing Marek Wolski's bruised dead face before gypsum was packed into his jaw—matched by red ink arrows to a nineteen-thirty-four Warsaw Polytechnic civil-engineering diploma registration card in the name of **Inżynier Marek Wolski, geb. 14.III.1909 Kielce**!

Third: a signed Polish police deposition taken yesterday afternoon from **Helena Walczak, aged fifty-eight, concierge's wife at ulica Koszykowa 33**, testifying that between December forty-two and thirty June forty-three a lean grey-eyed gentleman matching Photograph Two visited Dr. Sylwia Kotarska's fourth-floor room once or twice fortnightly before curfew—until a noisy quarrel with stove-smoke on the afternoon of **thirty June**, after which neither gentleman nor doctor ever slept there again!

Sylwia looked down at all three documents for five measured seconds without changing breath depth by a hair.

Thank God—and thank Marek's silent teeth in Pawiak! Kroll hadn't got Sylwia's name out of Marek under torture; Kroll's own loden-coated Leica spotter on Aleja Niepodległości had recognized *Dr. Kotarska* standing accidentally in the Saturday execution crowd, cross-checked every dead prisoner's prewar Warsaw acquaintanceship circle after matching Marek's Polytechnic thumbprints on Monday, sent two detectives round to Koszykowa Thirty-Three's concierge yesterday, and struck gold on winter visits!

"You recognize Prisoner Six from Saturday's reprisal action on Aleja Niepodległości, Frau Doktor?" Kroll asked mildly while Fräulein Hinz's typewriter keys clicked soft crisp German headers in the corner.

Deny what fifty-eight-year-old Pani Walczak had already sworn on paper, and you walked back down to *the Tramway* inside sixty seconds as a proven liar.

"Certainly, *Herr Hauptsturmführer*," Sylwia answered in cool, level Berlin German, meeting Kroll's pale eyes without blinking against window light. "Inżynier Marek Wolski. His uncle knew my father slightly before thirty-seven through Ministry railway patent arbitrations; we met again casually in autumn forty-two queuing for coal briquettes on Polna."

"He visited your fourth-floor room at Koszykowa thirty-three roughly fourteen evenings between November forty-two and thirty June forty-three," Kroll read pleasantly off Walczak's sheet.

Sylwia allowed one dry, wintry flicker of adult female disdain to touch her mouth—the exact expression no male police officer on earth ever doubted when an unmarried thirty-three-year-old woman doctor explained a man climbing back stairs before eight-o'clock curfew.

"Warsaw winters are cold, *Herr Hauptsturmführer*, and unmarried surgeons of thirty-three are neither nuns nor blind—until one discovers a man is lying about where he spends his afternoons."

Kroll's sandy eyebrows rose one polite millimeter. "And what caused your sudden rupture on the afternoon of thirty June—the exact morning, by curious calendar chance, that Security Police arrested Polish terrorist commander Stefan Rowecki six blocks away on Spiska?"

"Inżynier Wolski walked into my room uninvited at one o'clock using an unauthorized wax-impression key while I was off rounds, burning papers in my stove that smelled considerably less innocent than Ostbahn bridge repairs," Sylwia said—handing Kroll ninety percent of the **physical hallway truth** Pani Walczak had smelled through the door so every brick held ten tons of weight while cutting away the underground tissue underneath! "When I demanded to know what political lunatic rubbish he was bringing under my hospital license—and ordered him down my stairs at once—he lost his temper, accused me of cozying up to German medical authorities because *Oberstleutnant* Schoeler had pulled me out of Skaryszewska three weeks earlier, and left his key on my table. Being disinclined to wait around an unguarded flat in case hotheads took offense, I moved into Pavilion Three's third-floor interns' quarters that same afternoon."

Click-click-click went Fräulein Hinz's Adler keys; then stopped as Kroll raised one white cotton finger.

For ten seconds Kroll sat very still, pale colourless eyes boring straight into Sylwia's across four feet of green baize while *scritch-scritch* went left cotton across covered right wrist.

He slid Document Four out of his grey binder.

Typed surveillance log from German Quarter gate-wardens at **Królewska Sixteen**: recording *twenty-seven Tuesday-and-Friday entries* by *Dr. med. S. Kotarska* into *Wohnung 4* between twenty-seven April and twenty-eight September—plus an eight-August motor-trip log to Świder—stopping **dead cold** after 19:30 hours on Tuesday the twenty-eighth of September, ninety minutes after an unidentified *Abwehr* corporal shot his way out of Kroll's trap outside St. Charles Borromeo Church on Chłodna leaving Group O blood on Mirów bricks!

"Twenty-seven specialist consultations of thirty to forty-five minutes each—plus a Sunday water-inspection excursion to Otwock pines—for a healed clavicular scar, Frau Doktor," Kroll said softly, every trace of velvet small-talk thinning down to bare Saxon piano wire. "And terminating abruptly on the precise evening of twenty-eight September, when *Oberstleutnant* Schoeler's East Prussian driver vanished off Warsaw rosters toward Königsberg after firing upon Security Police officers at a dead-letter drop on Chłodna originally betrayed during Schoeler's February stay inside your Pavilion Three!"

Every muscle inside Sylwia's ribs locked stone-calm; discreetly behind closed lips she bit down hard onto the scarred inside of her own left cheek—old trick learned from Ludwig himself in Room Fourteen—letting sharp copper pain anchor her pulse flat at seventy-two so zero flush climbed her neck!

"My attendance at Królewska Sixteen stood under compulsory written order of *Stabsarzt* Doktor Vesper and *Wehrmachtkommandantur Warschau*, renewed expressly by *Herr Stabsarzt* Vesper on twenty-six July over my own stated preference to discontinue," Sylwia replied crisply—reminding Kroll right across the baize that dragging Kurt Vesper onto a witness stand meant hauling out Vesper's terrified July twenty-fourth hospital night too! "On twenty-eight September *Oberstleutnant* Schoeler informed me brusquely that Berlin was closing his Warsaw outpatient file prior to reassignment and canceled future passes. Where his driver went before five o'clock—while two staff officers or kitchen pots occupied his back hall—he naturally did not confide to a Polish municipal doctor."

Kroll leaned two inches forward over green baize.

"Let me save you three unpleasant weeks downstairs, Frau Doktor," he said almost gently, pulling three freshly typed sheets of heavy Reich-eagle deposition bond out of his folder and uncapping a black fountain pen beside her right wrist. "I bear zero personal animus toward Polish surgeons who survive winter how they must—whether in cold rooms on Koszykowa or warm sofas on Królewska. *My* sole administrative interest is **Oberstleutnant Doktor Ludwig Schoeler**."

Sylwia did not look down at the uncapped fountain pen.

"Hauptmann von Berg has already confirmed in Prinz-Albrecht-Straße," Kroll continued softly, "that since spring *Oberstleutnant* Schoeler used unauthorized channels to obstruct Security Police operations in Warsaw—including registering a fictitious counter-espionage shield over your hospital on eighteen August under the charming title *Vorgang Naht*. Unfortunately von Berg lacked firsthand witness proof of *whom* Schoeler passed warnings through in Warsaw. Sign these three pages attesting that under threat of revoking your hospital protection, *Oberstleutnant* Schoeler coerced you between April and September into carrying verbal and written intelligence messages between Królewska Sixteen and Polish underground operative Marek Wolski—now conveniently dead and beyond further harm!—and at twelve-thirty today you collect your handbag downstairs, walk back out into October sunshine to *Kindlein Jesu*, and never see this building again."

Look at the clean, lethal beauty of Kroll's offer:

Marek Wolski was already burned ash; signing three pages blaming a dead Polish engineer and handing Reinhart Kroll high-treason hanging proof against *Oberstleutnant Ludwig Schoeler* before the *Volksgerichtshof* in Berlin bought Sylwia Kotarska instant daylight freedom, seventy safe hospital beds, and Staś's peaceful winter!

Refuse—and right behind Kroll's elbow sat the red internal *Schutzhaft* detention slip sending *Kotarska, Sylwia* down into *the Tramway*'s cellar vaults where rubber cables and iron hooks broke wrists before supper!

Sylwia looked straight across sunlit green baize into Reinhart Kroll's pale, lashless Saxon eyes.

"Were I to sign one word stating *Oberstleutnant* Schoeler passed messages through me to Marek Wolski, *Herr Hauptsturmführer*," Sylwia said in crystal-clear Berlin German without a tremor in syllable or breath, "I would be inventing perjury to buy my lunch. Herr Schoeler never spoke Marek Wolski's name in my presence, never handed me a scrap of intelligence paper, and if he registered my hospital on some bureaucratic turf-file in August without telling me, it matches every underhanded trick I watched him pull on Luisenstraße twelve years ago. I have nothing else to depose."

* * *

For ninety minutes after twelve-thirty Kroll stopped offering tea.

He didn't touch her himself—jurists from Dresden kept clean cuffs—simply kept her standing upright without chair or water two feet in front of blazing south window glare while reading aloud twenty-four separate Polish hospital death-entries, Wola typhus-quarantine dates, Anna Nowak's September seventh admission versus Irena Kwiatkowska's death-hour, hunting one single contradicted date or cracked verb across three solid hours of microscopic cross-examination.

Sylwia stood straight on aching ankles in her heavy winter wool coat—eating nothing of Bronka's pocket bread, tasting salt blood inside her bitten left cheek whenever dizziness flickered behind her eyes—repeating identical timestamps down to the minute (*00:51 clamp, 21:40 exitus Kwiatkowska, 17:00 Tuesday Królewska per Vesper's order*) like an iron metronome forged in Poznań and tempered in liquid nitrogen.

At two-fifteen Kroll suddenly switched tracks without changing pitch—sliding **SS-Untersturmführer Mahler's eighth-September inspection protocol** from Pavilion Three's basement typhus mortuary out from beneath his blotter!

"One technical curiosity before we conclude upstairs, Frau Doktor," Kroll murmured, holding Mahler's typed sheet delicately between white-cotton thumb and forefinger. "Your deceased basement laboratory technician, *Irena Kwiatkowska*, expired at twenty-one-forty hours on seven September—some twelve hours after accidentally spilling eighty-eight-percent liquefied carbolic acid across the palmar ridges of her right thumb, index, and middle fingers. Remarkably awkward clumsiness for an experienced Weigl-feeder on the precise night railway cordons sealed Warsaw after Bürkl's shooting—especially when Charity Ward Admission Number Seven, *Anna Nowak, seamstress, aged twenty-nine*, died of epidemic typhus on the same floor four hours earlier."

Not a lash moved round Sylwia's dark eyes, though inside her heavy wool coat cold sweat slid one thin bead straight down between her shoulder-blades.

"Had *Herr Untersturmführer* Mahler doubted which pauper lay in Box One and which laboratory assistant lay in Box Four when he inspected both open coffins personally at eight-twenty-six a.m., *Herr Hauptsturmführer*," Sylwia answered in bone-dry clinical German, "he stood six inches from both faces with magnifying lens and caliper rule for twelve minutes. Forty-degree *Fleckfieber* rigor shakes glass Pasteur pipettes out of far steadier fingers than a dying twenty-eight-year-old girl's; had I wished to fake carbolic burns on a corpse's hand post-mortem, any first-year forensic student in Dresden knows dead skin lacks vital erythematous blister-margin reaction—which your own Untersturmführer verified around the eschar before signing his seal. Should *Referat Vier* wish to exhume Municipal Typhus Pit Twelve at Bródno after six weeks' autumn rain to re-examine six hundred unlimed charity corpses, Municipal Sanitation will gladly lend picks and shovels."

Fräulein Hinz's pale fingers hovered frozen above her Adler keys; across green baize a faint, ugly flush crept up Kroll's smooth cheekbones at hearing Dresden forensic textbooks thrown neatly back into his teeth.

At quarter to three Kroll finally closed his grey manila binder with a soft flat slap.

All courtroom pleasantness vanished off his thin mouth; pulling a pad of salmon-red internal remand orders (*Einlieferungsschein — Hausgefängnis Zelle*) across green baize, he dipped his pen to fill in *Kotarska, Sylwia Helena — Verdacht der Feindbegünstigung u. Spionagebeihilfe*:

"Admirable loyalty to Luisenstraße classmates, Frau Doktor," Kroll said dryly, scratching hard through white cotton at his covered right wrist. "Let us see whether seventy-two hours standing below stairs improves dates before we bring your Docent Mirecki and that fat scrub-nurse down to compare calendars—"

On the small side-table behind Kroll's left elbow, the black direct-line command telephone—not room switchboard, red-striped Reich Security / Military District priority trunk—rang one long, sharp peal.

Kroll paused with nib poised three millimeters above salmon paper, frowned slightly, picked up the heavy bakelite receiver: "Kroll, *Referat Vier A*."

Sylwia stood motionless two feet before the desk watching Kroll's face.

Whoever spoke on the other end—crisp, clipped, high-ranking Prussian military-judicial brass coming straight down from Cracow Generalgouvernement High Command or Berlin RSHA-OKW liaison—did not pause for Kroll's interruptions. Ten seconds in, Kroll stood slowly upright behind his chair; thirty seconds in, every drop of pale pink drained chalk-white out of Kroll's smooth cheeks; sixty seconds in, while uttering only *"Jawohl, Herr Oberstkriegsgerichtsrat... Nein, noch keine Verhaftung vollzogen... Jawohl, Akte wird sofort abgegeben,"* Kroll's left white-cotton index finger dug so savagely and unconsciously back and forth across the gauze-wrapped inner aspect of his right wrist that raw weeping eczema split wide open underneath—soaking a bright five-pfennig circle of **crimson blood** straight through snowy white cotton cuff!

Kroll set the black bakelite receiver slowly back onto its cradle without looking down at his bleeding glove.

For eight seconds dead silence hung inside Room 214 save for autumn flies buzzing sleepy against warm window-glass.

When Kroll looked back up at Sylwia standing motionless before his green baize table, pure cold arsenic sat behind his colourless eyes—the look of a hunter watching quarry yanked bodily out of steel jaws two tenths of a second before teeth met bone.

"At twelve hundred hours today before the Chief Military Prosecutor of *Oberkommando der Wehrmacht* at Cracow," Kroll said in a voice so tight and soft it barely carried six feet across green felt, "*Oberstleutnant* Doktor Schoeler formally dissolved *Sondervorgang Naht* upon signing voluntary relinquishment of *Abwehr* staff immunity and immediate punitive front-line combat transfer—*Frontbewährung*, commanding a divisional reconnaissance-medical combat group on the lower Dnieper bend east of Kremenchug."

Sylwia's knees did not move half a millimeter under grey wool; only the whole sunlit room behind Kroll's sandy head seemed suddenly to lose sound, like film running silent when projector sprockets tear.

*Lower Dnieper bend east of Kremenchug, late October nineteen-forty-three.*

Even Polish hospital orderlies reading crumpled German communiqués upside-down knew what *lower Dnieper bend* meant this week: thirty Soviet tank armies smashing through burning steppe mud across broken German bridgeheads at five-to-one artillery odds, where staff colonels sent out of disgraced *Abwehr* offices under *Frontbewährung* lasted eleven days on average before Russian Katyushas turned grey wool to red rags!

"As condition of OKW countersigning closure of *Abwehrstelle Warschau*'s autonomous files without public inter-service tribunal," Kroll continued through stiff pale lips, tearing the half-written salmon remand slip once across and dropping it into his wire basket before picking up Sylwia's white morning *Vorladung* summons to stamp **ENTLASSEN — 15:08 UHR** viciously across the bottom in purple ink, "Wehrmacht High Command places all listed municipal medical installations under closed military-epidemic seal retroactive to February. Collect your bag at the rotunda, Frau Doktor. Should one single unregistered bandage cross Lindleya's threshold this winter after your patron freezes in Ukrainian mud, no colonel in Cracow will pick up a telephone a second time."

Sylwia reached across green baize with steady, ungloved fingers, took her stamped white release slip three inches from Kroll's blood-spotted white cotton wrist without glancing down at his stain, inclined her chin two polite millimeters—"Good afternoon, *Herr Hauptsturmführer*"—and walked unhurriedly out down polished travertine stairs, collected her black handbag on Token Twelve, and stepped past steel tank-barriers out onto sunlit Aleje Ujazdowskie at sixteen minutes past three.

Only fifty yards inside Łazienki Park's deserted side-walk behind thick yellowing hornbeam hedges—where zero Leica lens watched—did Sylwia stop walking, lean one wool shoulder hard against cold iron park railings, spit a mouthful of bright red salt blood from her bitten cheek into dead yellow leaves, and press both raw hands flat across her ribs to hold them together while October wind roared hollow through bare branches overhead.

At six minutes to four—four minutes before her own deadline sent Jurek galloping toward Grójec and Vesper's blank passes into coal-flames!—Sylwia pushed open Walenty's arched postern gate on Lindleya.

Old Walenty took one look at her tall grey-coated figure stepping alive out of afternoon shadow, dropped his broom clattering across cobbles, and crossed himself three times from brow to breastbone before running bow-legged toward the boiler cellar steps shouting *"Jest! Wróciła!"* Down at the foot of Boiler Two's iron stair Siostra Bronisława stood squarely in front of the open roaring furnace door holding Vesper's four green *Sanitätsabteilung* passes pinched ready above red-hot clinkers in iron fire-tongs while Docent Mirecki leaned grey as chalk against the whitewashed wall checking his gold pocket-watch at *3:57*!

At sight of Sylwia walking down the stone steps intact on her own two ankles, Bronka slammed the cast-iron firebox door shut with an ear-splitting *clang*, shoved the four green passes straight back into Sylwia's coat pocket without trusting her voice to speak one word, marched furiously into the scullery, and broke a chipped earthenware soup-plate clean in half into the sink just to have something solid to curse at!

"No cellar?" Mirecki whispered beside the heating pipe, gripping his ebony cane handle hard enough to turn thin knuckles ivory.

"Closed file—military epidemic seal countersigned from Cracow," Sylwia answered evenly, letting zero syllable of *Frontbewährung* or *Kremenchug* cross her raw-bitten mouth because speak his name aloud right now under yellow cellar bulbs and her voice cracked down the middle in front of witnesses. "Tell Jurek Grójec stays quiet through winter. Give me twenty minutes to wash Szucha off my hands and I'll take evening dressings."
