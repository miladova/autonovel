# The Curette

*Warsaw, 24 July 1943*

Even in late July the zinc post-mortem table down in Pavilion Three's basement morgue stayed cold enough to numb bare elbows inside five minutes.

Around the single green-shaded porcelain pendant lamp stood six nineteen-year-old Polish boys and girls wearing white linen aprons tied over shabby summer shirts—nominally enrolled upstairs by day as orderlies and sterilizer clerks under Docent Zaorski's German-permitted *Private Trade School for Auxiliary Sanitary Personnel*, actually third-year clandestine medical students of the Underground University of Warsaw cramming topographical surgical anatomy between ten p.m. and midnight on unclaimed charity-ward cadavers before morning lime carts rolled to Bródno.

"Show me Hunter's canal again without tearing the saphenous nerve, Panna Zofia," Sylwia murmured, tapping the edge of the retracted sartorius muscle with the blunt butt of her scalpel. "On a street barricade you won't have electric retractors or Corning's atlas open beside the stretcher. Cut two centimeters too medial looking for a severed superficial femoral artery and you slice the long saphenous vein longitudinally in dark mud—which bleeds black like tar and blinds your field inside ten seconds."

Slim, spectacles-wearing Zofia—no relation to little asthmatic Zosia from Skaryszewska, an architect's daughter from Żoliborz whose older brother had died at Auschwitz in forty-one—slid blunt curved scissors neatly beneath the aponeurotic roof of the adductor canal and lifted the tough white fibrous band clean off the underlying artery without nicking a twig.

"Good," Sylwia said, allowing one millimeter of rare approval that made all six starved nineteen-year-old faces glow brighter than roast beef. "Memorize the thumb-breadth behind the medial condyle before—"

Up at the head of the basement stone ramp, Bronka's heavy iron key rapped four urgent staccato beats against the heating pipe—*scatter!*

Zero panic, zero chatter: six months' drill moved like greased clockwork. In forty-five seconds flat Zofia draped the dissected thigh smoothly beneath its sewn linen burial shroud as post-mortem typhus spleen-inspection required; five boys swept notebooks and pencils inside their bootlegs, blew out the side spirit-lamp, and vanished noiselessly down the dark boiler coal-tunnel toward Koszykowa's broken fence-post while Sylwia rinsed her hands in carbolic and walked calmly up the basement steps drying her wrists on a towel.

Out in the ground-floor night vestibule nobody from Aleja Szucha stood waiting.

Instead, leaning half-collapsed against Walenty's oak admissions counter with his uniform cap askew, grey summer tunic unbuttoned over a sweat-drenched silk shirt smeared bright scarlet across the belly, stood **Stabsarzt Doktor Kurt Vesper**.

And sagged limp across two hurriedly pushed-together wheeled stretchers beside him—fair Bavarian braids trailing undone down toward the black-and-white tiles, bare white legs streaked dark crimson from thighs to ankles beneath a twisted, blood-sodden linen bedsheet stamped in blue laundry thread *HOTEL EUROPEJSKI WARSCHAU*—lay a girl no older than Sylwia's medical students downstairs.

"Lock the outer courtyard gate, Walenty," Vesper choked out in a shrill, wet Munich falsetto that smelled from eight feet off of fifty-percent Martell cognac and sheer animal terror. Hands shaking so violently he dropped his pigskin gloves twice trying to pocket them, he grabbed Sylwia's clean white sleeve with blood-sticky fingers. "No clerk entries—nobody telephones *Lazarett Ujazdowski*! Theater One immediately, Frau Doktor—wake Docent Mirecki himself, nobody else on the floor!"

Sylwia didn't waste half a second shaking Vesper's filthy fingers off her cuff; one two-second sweep of eyes and fingertips over the girl on the stretcher told the entire sordid Saturday-night story louder than confessionals.

Eighteen or nineteen; grey Wehrmacht *Nachrichtenhelferin*—Signals Corps telephone switchboard auxiliary—skirt and grey rayon blouse stuffed crumpled at the foot of the stretcher; skin dead tallow-white down to blue nailbeds; radial pulse barely palpable at one-forty-six and thready as cobweb; lower abdomen above the pubis no longer soft—doughy, distended, fiercely rigid to lightest touch even through pre-terminal hemorrhagic stupor, while dark venous blood mixed with unmistakable watery brownish-green small-bowel content leaked slow out between cold thighs onto the Hotel Europejski sheet!

*Small-bowel content out a vagina.*

Even first-semester midwives in Wola knew how small-bowel content reached a nineteen-year-old vagina at eleven-twenty on a Saturday night: some drunken panicked gynecologist working blind without speculum light on a soft hotel mattress at twelve weeks' gestation had shoved a sharp steel Hegar dilator or Sims curette straight through the boggy posterior wall of a retroverted pregnant uterus into the peritoneal cavity—felt something soft yield, pulled down with ovum-forceps thinking he held placental tissue, and *dragged a loop of living maternal ileum down through the torn uterine rent to avulse and lacerate it!*

"Bronka—Theater One lamps full on, four bricks under foot-end, boil laparotomy set and two vascular clamps in three minutes!" Sylwia snapped, already wheeling the stretcher straight toward the glass operating-room doors herself while Walenty ran upstairs to bang Mirecki's quarters. She turned cold black eyes onto Vesper stumbling behind her. "What blood group is her *Soldbuch*?"

"A—Group A—I think—her bag's still in Room Two-Twelve at the Europejski—" Vesper babbled, wiping grey sweat off his upper lip and leaving a streak of the girl's blood across his own toothbrush moustache. "It was an incomplete spontaneous miscarriage—she telephoned my quarters at nine bleeding heavily, begged me as a compatriot from Munich—"

"Save your bedtime fairy tales until she has a blood pressure, *Herr Stabsarzt*," Sylwia cut him off like an axe dropping through fat. "How many minutes ago did you pull bowel through the fundus?"

Vesper's fat pink knees literally buckled two inches against the porcelain scrub-trough rim; a dry whimper came out of his throat. "Thirty—maybe thirty-five minutes—the lamp beside the bed fell over when she jerked, I thought it was thickened decidua until—"

"Stay away from my sterile table and go scrub if you want to stay out of chains."

Docent Mirecki arrived four minutes later wearing grey flannel trousers pulled hurriedly over striped pajama bottoms, bare ankles stuck inside leather bedroom slippers, pince-nez crooked over grey sunken cheeks that had looked pinched all week in July's wet heat. One look past the ether screen at nineteen-year-old Ilse Lindner's wax-white German face above the Hotel Europejski sheet—and Vesper retching dry cognac bile over the corner slop-sink—made Mirecki cross himself invisibly behind his apron bib before plunging bony hands into sublimate beside Sylwia.

"If a Reichsdeutsche switchboard girl dies on Polish municipal iron at midnight without admission papers," Mirecki whispered grayly under running tap water, "Vesper tells Aleja Szucha by seven a.m. that *he* brought her in after a Polish street abortionist botched her—and hangs both of us to save his Bavarian wife's allowance."

"Which is why she isn't going to die on my iron," Sylwia whispered back grimly, holding dripping arms up for Bronka to tie sterile tape behind her waist. "Put light open-drop ether plus full oxygen cylinder flow on the mask once I prep—zero chloroform on a shocked heart—and run six hundred milliliters warm gum-acacia saline plus Bronka's universal Type O citrate bottle wide open into an ankle cut-down!"

At eleven-thirty-eight Sylwia drew a single swift subumbilical midline stroke from umbilicus to pubic symphysis with a #20 belly blade, parted linea alba almost bloodlessly—hypotension below sixty systolic barely bled skin—and opened the peritoneum.

Dark purple liquid blood and black clots the size of fists surged up over wound towels onto rubber floor-mats: easily sixteen hundred milliliters free inside the peritoneal cavity!

Sylwia scooped four double handfuls of warm jelly-clot straight out into Bronka's basin to reach the pelvis—and across the table Docent Mirecki gave a sudden sharp, whistling intake of breath that had nothing to do with surgical pathology.

"Tadeusz?" Sylwia glanced up one tenth of a second over her gauze mask.

Mirecki's face above his mask had turned the exact blue-grey of wet slate; cold sweat stood out in huge round beads from brow to hairline, and his right hand holding the broad Doyen bladder-retractor unlocked helplessly, dropping half a pound of stainless steel clattering off the table rim onto tiles as his left hand clawed inward at his own sternum through his sterile gown!

"Sylwia—vise—down left arm—" he gasped, knees folding slowly sideways behind the ether screen before Bronka caught his bony armpit with one massive matron's arm and lowered him flat onto the tiled floor away from blood!

Acute coronary thrombosis. Right in the middle of an open, exsanguinating German abdomen with a lacerated uterine artery pumping scarlet deep in the broad ligament and torn ileum leaking green chyme across pelvic peritoneum!

"Bronka—crush two amyl nitrite pearls under his nose right on the floor, give fifteen milligrams morphine subcutaneous, don't lift his head!" Sylwia barked, shoving two wide packs down against the girl's bleeding right parametrium with her own left fist to hold pressure solo while turning murderous dark eyes straight across Theater One onto Stabsarzt Kurt Vesper shivering green-faced in shirt-sleeves by the door! "Vesper! Get two pairs of sterile gloves or raw alcohol over those fat hands inside *twenty seconds* and stand across this table holding my Doyen retractor—or watch Munich's military prosecutor read your hotel room towel numbers over a dead nineteen-year-old girl!"

Terror moved forty-one-year-old Party gynecologists faster than duty ever did.

Slopping raw spirit frantically up to his elbows without stopping to brush nails, Vesper stumbled across wet floor-clots into Mirecki's empty spot—hands trembling like aspen leaves in wind as Sylwia shoved the spare sterile Doyen retractor handle into his right grip and the ether drop-bottle into his left!

"Pull suprapubic wall straight toward her knees—don't wobble into my silk or I stick this needle through your thumb!" Sylwia snarled in Berlin German ferocious enough to freeze his tremors down to half an inch. "Ten drops ether a minute on the gauze whenever her eyelids twitch, oxygen valve three liters!"

For forty-eight minutes inside midnight heat where moths fried snapping against the surgical skylight glass and Docent Mirecki lay grey and silent under an oxygen mask on a floor mattress ten feet behind her heels with Bronka counting his irregular radial pulse between passing Sylwia boiled clamps, Sylwia operated four-handed with two hands of her own.

First, life: deep in the right side of the twelve-week boggy uterus yawned a ragged three-centimeter perforation extending laterally straight through the ascending branch of the right uterine artery inside the broad ligament! Trying to stitch ragged, friable pregnant myometrium torn open by an unsterile hotel curette guaranteed fatal puerperal peritonitis by Wednesday even if bleeding stopped. One road kept a nineteen-year-old girl breathing: swift subtotal supravaginal hysterectomy.

*Kocher clamp across right round ligament and tube, snip; clamp left, snip; push bladder peritoneum cleanly downward off cervix with wet gauze sponge so ureters drop two centimeters safe out of harm's way—never clamp a ureter blind in blood, even von Halden's panic rules held sacred!—double heavy #2 chromic catgut ligatures driven tight through both uterine artery pedicles at the internal os.* Instantly the deadly bright arterial welling from the pelvic floor stopped dead! Across the cervical stump she whipped six clean interrupted mattress stitches, burying raw mucosa smooth under bladder peritoneum.

Second, filth: twelve centimeters proximal to the ileocecal valve hung the five-centimeter loop of small intestine Vesper's curette had dragged halfway into the womb and ripped open across its mesenteric border, bruising three mesenteric arcade vessels blue-black. Leave bruised devascularized bowel inside a belly and it sloughed open on day four.

"Intestinal Doyen clamps—soft rubber-shod!" Sylwia snapped over her shoulder to Bronka rising briefly from Mirecki's side.

Eight centimeters of ruined ileum came cleanly out between soft clamps; two-layer end-to-end anastomosis—inner continuous Connell inverting stitch in fine catgut, outer interrupted Lembert seromuscular bites in black silk that tucked peritoneal edges neat and watertight as a purse seam—mesenteric window closed, pelvic cavity washed thrice with two full liters of warm sterile saline before pouring ten grams of crystalline sterile sulfanilamide suspension straight across the cul-de-sac and closing fascia with rapid figure-of-eight stainless wire so distension couldn't burst her belly open if ileus hit!

At twenty-six minutes past midnight Bronka's universal Type O donor blood finished dripping; at zero-zero-forty Ilse Lindner's radial pulse under Vesper's terrified thumb climbed down from one-forty-six to one-sixteen, systolic pressure reading eighty-eight over fifty-four on the mercury cuff, warm pink returning faintly to small Bavarian earlobes.

Only Room Fourteen down the empty east annex still had clean single-bed privacy away from Polish ward ears; there orderlies wheeled nineteen-year-old Ilse under hot bricks while upstairs on the third floor Sylwia and Bronka settled Docent Mirecki—chest pain easing slowly from crushing iron down to dull lead under morphine and oxygen, pulse irregular with occasional ventricular extrasystoles yet holding seventy-six—into his private corner room with strict absolute bed-rest orders for six weeks.

* * *

At three-fifteen on Sunday morning, when birds were just beginning to stir sleepy notes inside courtyard lilacs before light greyed the sky, Sylwia walked back down into Mirecki's ground-floor consulting room without washing two dried rust streaks of Ilse Lindner's blood off her white apron front.

Stabsarzt Kurt Vesper sat slumped at Mirecki's mahogany desk in the exact chair where Reinhart Kroll had sat scratching white cotton wrists in February. Vesper's face looked deflated like a pig's bladder pricked with a pin; his hair stood tufted damp where he had dragged fingers through brilliantine, and the pocket cognac flask lay dry upside-down on the blotter.

Without speaking a word, Sylwia laid two sheets of paper side by side under the desk lamp directly beneath Vesper's red-rimmed eyes.

On the left lay a blank municipal-and-Wehrmacht emergency operative report sheet already filled out in Sylwia's crisp black script:

*Patientin: Lindner, Ilse, Nachr.-Helf., 19 J. Diagnose: Graviditas interstitialis rupta dextra cum haemorrhagia intraabdominali gravissima; laesio secundaria serosae ilei per adhaesiones antiquas. Therapie: Laparotomia mediana, amputatio uteri supravaginalis, resectio ilei (8 cm), transfusio sanguinis.*

*Ruptured right interstitial ectopic cornual pregnancy with massive intra-abdominal hemorrhage and secondary ileal tear from old appendicular adhesions.* Zero criminal abortion, zero curette perforation, zero Paragraph 218 Reich penal felony carrying eight years' Zuchthaus and military cashiering to the Eastern Front! Even if another German physician examined Ilse's scar next month, interstitial cornual rupture clinically mimicked uterine-horn tear down to requiring emergency subtotal hysterectomy, and the excised specimen had already gone straight into Bronka's boiler firebox ten minutes ago!

On the right lay four blank grey-green *Sanitätsabteilung Warschau* movement-and-requisition vouchers bearing official eagle watermarks from Vesper's own unlocked briefcase, plus Kroll's July fourteenth routing query asking whether *Dr. S. Kotarska*'s outpatient attendance on Królewska remained medically indicated and whether Berlin's 1933 university registry file raised "any administrative reservation."

Vesper stared from the left-hand salvation sheet to the right-hand stack for ten silent seconds while sweat trickled slow down his fat neck into his wilted collar.

"Interstitial ectopic rupture," Vesper whispered hoarsely, wetting dry grey lips with his tongue. "The girl herself—when she wakes—"

"She's nineteen, terrified to death of losing her Signals badge and breaking her Catholic mother's heart in Rosenheim, and grateful to still own bowel and breath," Sylwia said coldly, standing straight above him smelling of blood and ether. "When I wake her at eight this morning alone in Room Fourteen, I will inform her calmly that an unruptured ectopic horn blew open spontaneously in her hotel room, that *Herr Stabsarzt* Vesper heroically drove her here inside twenty minutes saving her life, and that unfortunately scarred tubes mean she won't carry babies—which beats ten years in Aichach women's penitentiary for wartime self-abortion. She'll kiss your boots till Judgment Day."

Vesper closed his eyes for two seconds with a wet, shuddering exhalation that nearly turned into a sob. Reaching blind for his fountain pen, he uncapped it with trembling fingers.

"Before your pen touches paper, *Herr Stabsarzt*," Sylwia said very softly, placing two raw fingers squarely on top of the right-hand sheets so they couldn't move, "let us make sure Munich and Warsaw speak clear German. First: while Docent Mirecki recovers upstairs over the next eight weeks, I hold full acting chief surgical authority in Pavilion Three—zero outside Reichsdeutscher or Volksdeutscher locum brought into my wards or basement laboratories."

"Agreed—naturally, you know the pavilion routines—"

"Second: those four *Sanitätsabteilung* drug-and-movement vouchers get your signature and official round rubber stamp *blank* on lines two through five—for epidemic insulin, sulfonamide, and rural sanitary transport as municipal need arises."

Vesper swallowed hard once—four blank Wehrmacht medical movement vouchers could move three typhus patients or forty kilos of contraband clean past county gendarmes to Kielce!—glanced once at the dried blood of nineteen-year-old Ilse Lindner on Sylwia's apron front, and signed *Dr. K. Vesper, Stabsarzt* four times in rapid violet ink, thumping his round eagle pocket-stamp beside each signature.

"Third," Sylwia finished, sliding Kroll's Aleja Szucha inquiry slip last beneath his nib: "You reply to *SS-Hauptsturmführer* Kroll Monday morning that Berlin's nineteen-thirty-three Charité registry confirms purely orthodox academic surgical training under Geheimrat von Halden, zero political notations attached, and that *Oberstleutnant* Schoeler's post-traumatic brachial-plexus causalgia continues to require specialist neurological-vascular review under *Sanitätsabteilung* oversight through autumn."

Vesper wrote six cramped, obsequious bureaucratic lines certifying every syllable, blotted them dry, and looked up at her with watery, bloodshot eyes in which craven relief was already curdling into lifelong terror of the tall dark Polish woman locking his blank stamped passes inside Docent Mirecki's steel safe.

"The excised tissue from Theater One—?" he breathed.

"Ash in Boiler Two since one-fifteen," Sylwia said calmly, handing him his signed ectopic copy and opening the office door into grey dawn light. "Go wash your shirt before your orderly sees Hotel Europejski linen in your car, *Herr Stabsarzt*. I'll let you know at noon when Fräulein Lindner can drink tea."

* * *

At seven-thirty on Monday morning the twenty-sixth of July, nineteen-year-old Ilse Lindner lay awake against two flat pillows in Room Fourteen—hair re-braided neat across grey hospital ticking by Bronka, skin still translucent porcelain-white round blue-shadowed eyes, staring terrified at the high cracked ceiling plaster where five months ago Ludwig Schoeler had counted hairline water-stains.

Sylwia locked the door quietly from inside, checked the dressing—bone-dry, abdomen flat, soft laterally with faint returning bowel clicks already audible below the left costal margin, radial pulse steady at ninety-two—and gave the girl two teaspoons of lukewarm weak chamomile tea off a porcelain spoon before speaking in calm, unhurried Berlin German:

"Your ectopic pregnancy ruptured spontaneously through the right uterine horn on Saturday evening, Fräulein Lindner. Had *Stabsarzt* Vesper arrived fifteen minutes later, nothing could have saved your life. Because hemorrhage tore deep into the uterine wall and old appendicular adhesions nicked an adjacent bowel loop, removing the damaged uterine body was unavoidable; your ovaries remain completely intact, so your body and face will age normally, though you will not carry pregnancies."

Watching nineteen-year-old Bavarian eyes widen first with stunned disbelief—waiting for police handcuffs, military disgrace, Rosenheim shame!—and then fill abruptly to overflowing with hot, silent, trembling relief that spilled straight sideways into her fair braids, Sylwia felt zero satisfaction over blackmail, only dry professional mercy for girls caught underneath men's boots in every tongue.

"Does—does my unit matron see—" Ilse whispered, voice cracking smaller than Zosia's.

"Your unit matron sees *Graviditas interstitialis rupta*—a natural anatomical misfortune that happens to married countesses and convent novices alike," Sylwia said evenly, laying two cool fingers once across the girl's wrist to stop her trying to lift her head. "Keep your knees bent flat until Wednesday so your stitches hold, say nothing about Hotel Europejski room numbers to inquisitive roommates when you transfer to *Ujazdowski* convalescent ward on Friday, and next time a forty-year-old married officer offers Martell cognac after switchboard shift, walk home in the rain."

Ten minutes later, promptly on the stroke of eight, Sylwia walked upstairs into Pavilion Three's sunlit doctors' conference room and sat down—not on her accustomed second bench beside the window, squarely in **Docent Mirecki's high-backed leather armchair** at the head of the scarred oak table.

Four male Polish surgeons stood round the cold samovar in starched white coats: gentle fifty-year-old **Doktor Jankowski**, two tired junior assistants from Pavilion One covering summer leave, and fifty-two-year-old **Doktor Stanisław Zawadzki**—a portly prewar reserve medical major from Kalisz with waxed grey moustaches who operated slow as cold molasses, hated women holding scalpels above minor outpatient boils, and had spent six months muttering over spirit-lamps whenever Sylwia's green German pass took her out early on Tuesdays.

Conversation died dead when Sylwia pulled the heavy leatherbound Operating Theater Schedule book across the oak table toward her blotter.

"Docent Mirecki's electrocardiogram shows anterior myocardial infarction," Sylwia stated cleanly without preamble. "He stays flat upstairs six weeks minimum. Municipal Health Directorate and *Sanitätsabteilung Warschau* confirmed my acting service authority through September; morning trauma triage stays mine, elective hernia and gall-bladder lists divide equally between Doktor Zawadzki and Doktor Jankowski. Let's walk Men's Ward B before Theater One heats."

Zawadzki didn't sit—he puffed his starched chest two inches wider above his grey moustache, hooking two stubby thumbs inside his white belt with an indulgent barracks smile aimed sideways at the two junior men.

"Delighted *Sanitätsabteilung Warschau* trusts your administrative versatility so warmly, Pani Koleżanko," Zawadzki drawled, letting *Sanitätsabteilung* hang greasy in the sunlit air for half a beat so nobody missed the dig. "Though before rewriting major abdominal lists, perhaps junior colleagues recall municipal statutes require ten years' post-habilitation seniority for independent laparotomy sign-off. In Ward B just now I examined Bed Nine—the eighteen-year-old brewery dray-boy kicked below the ribs by a shire horse at five a.m.—simple right flank abdominal-wall contusion without peritoneal rigidity; I ordered twenty drops tincture of opium and ice-bags until tomorrow."

Every junior eye flicked straight from Zawadzki's waxed moustache to Sylwia's white-coated shoulders: yield Bed Nine's opium order on Minute Three of Day One to keep polite peace with a fifty-two-year-old man's vanity, and for eight weeks Pavilion Three had no chief—just squabbling committees while underground boys bled in the annex.

Sylwia stood up smoothly without raising her voice half a decibel. "Show me Bed Nine before a single drop of opium crosses his teeth, *Panie Doktorze*."

Out in Men's Ward B, eighteen-year-old Franek lay curled slightly onto his right side beneath coarse grey blankets, sweating lightly round the nostrils. True enough, when Zawadzki pressed four pompous fingers flat across the boy's anterior belly—*"Observe, gentlemen: soft left iliac fossa, zero board-like defense, pulse eighty-four!"*—the anterior rectus wall yielded soft enough to fool lazy thumbs.

Sylwia didn't poke the anterior belly first.

Warming her palm two seconds against her own sleeve, she slipped left fingertips gently behind the boy's right twelfth rib into the costovertebral angle while tapping three light percussive flicks over the upper right flank above the iliac crest, leaned low with Mirecki's ivory monaural stethoscope pressed over the right para-umbilical quadrant for fifteen silent seconds, and finally smelled the enamel kidney-dish beside the pillow where the boy had spat two tablespoons of frothy yellow-green mucus at six a.m.

Then she took her skin-marking indelible pencil and drew a neat cross over the right transpyloric plane.

"Did you percuss his right retroperitoneal flank or palpate rectally for pelvic crepitus before ordering opium to mask his pain, Doktor Zawadzki?" Sylwia asked cleanly across the iron bedstead.

Zawadzki reddened up into his bald crown. "With pulse eighty-four and a soft anterior wall—"

"Pulse sits low at hour three because retroperitoneal bile irritation stimulates vagal tone before bacterial peritonitis floods the general sac at hour ten," Sylwia said, voice crisp and surgical as Luisenstraße lecture-slate so the two young assistants drank every syllable wide-eyed. "A shod brewery horse kicking upward against the right costal cartilage drives the fixed second portion of the duodenum straight backward against the second lumbar vertebral body and splits its posterior extraperitoneal wall wide open! Right psoas sign is fiercely positive, obliteration of right kidney dullness shows retroperitoneal gas-bubbles crepitating already under my fingers, and give him twenty drops of opium till tomorrow morning and you wheel him down to Bródno black-bellied by Thursday! Bronka—cancel the nine-o'clock varicose veins; Bed Nine goes onto Table One inside twenty minutes, and Doktor Zawadzki scrubs first assistant so we inspect his 'simple wall contusion' together."

Thirty-two minutes later under Theater One's blazing morning skylight, when Sylwia incised posterior peritoneum lateral to the ascending colon—performing a textbook Kocher mobilization that flipped the bruised duodenum smoothly medialward before four pairs of masked eyes—out hissed foul, greenish-brown bile-foam mixed with trapped retroperitoneal air-bubbles pouring straight out of a ragged **two-and-a-half-centimeter blowout laceration across the posterior duodenal wall** invisible from the front of the belly!

Above his gauze mask Doktor Stanisław Zawadzki went dead silent—ears purple, holding Sylwia's broad liver-retractor steady with both fat hands without uttering one single pompous syllable for the remaining forty-five minutes while she closed the duodenal rent transversely in two neat watertight layers so the lumen wouldn't stricture, buttressed omentum clean over the repair, and laid a soft rubber cigarette-drain out the right flank stab-wound!

When Sylwia stripped off her wet rubber gloves at quarter past ten and wrote her neat initials **S.K. (p.o. Ordynatora)**—*Acting Chief*—at the bottom of the operative register, Zawadzki bowed two stiff inches from the waist before shuffling meekly off to lance outpatient carbuncles, and Jankowski hid a quiet, delighted grin behind his hand-towel. Pavilion Three belonged to Sylwia's two hands from cellar coal-chute to third-floor rafters—leaving her clear deck for Sunday's drive into the pines.
