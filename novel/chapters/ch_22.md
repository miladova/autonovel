# White Gloves at the Brühl

*Warsaw, 29 August 1943*

Helena Kotarska had worn the black Lyons crêpe-de-Chine once—playing Brahms's D-minor concerto in Poznań's Aula Nova in November nineteen-twenty-eight, four months before pneumonia and thirty grains a week of hospital morphine stopped her heart—and for fourteen years since, folded between sheets of blue tissue paper and camphor blocks at the bottom of Sylwia's tin trunk, the silk had waited while everything else got sold for coal or cut up into bandages.

At quarter to eight on Sunday evening, inside Pavilion Three's slant-roofed dormer cubby under the eaves, Siostra Bronisława held a threaded darning needle between her broad thumb and forefinger, taking three rapid invisible tucks inside each side-seam while Sylwia stood motionless on a spread towel in worn cotton slip and black lisle stockings.

"Hold your breath in half an inch less or you won't swallow soup," Bronka grunted around two brass pins clamped beside her gold eyetooth. "Your mother had wider hips than you—or else people in twenty-eight ate butter on Tuesdays. Lift your arms."

Down slithered cool, heavy prewar French silk—real mulberry weight that whispered like dry water over bare shoulders—cut high boat-necked across the collarbones in front (mercifully covering every ghost of July) and dipping into a narrow V halfway between her shoulder-blades in back, where every thoracic vertebra showed sharp ivory ridges beneath pale hospital skin.

In the cracked wardrobe mirror propped against the dormer knee-wall, the stranger staring back startled even Sylwia: black silk turned her eyes coal-dark above gaunt, aristocratic Poznań cheekbones, stripping away ward calico until she looked neither like an exhausted municipal surgeon nor like a conquered clerk—she looked like some dangerous nineteen-thirty Berlin cabaret portrait Otto Dix might have painted on wood just before the police closed the gallery.

Only her hands betrayed four winters of Lindleya: red-cracked around every nailbed from mercuric sublimate, calloused along the right thumb-web from heavy Liston bone-forceps, two tiny yellow pin-dots of Stefan's picric acid still stubbornly clinging beside the right index cuticle.

"Give them here," Bronka ordered, uncorking a small porcelain jar of pure mutton-tallow melted with glycerine and three drops of hospital lemon-spirit she had stolen from Vesper's shelf, rubbing grease fiercely into Sylwia's raw knuckles for two minutes before wiping excess shine off on clean linen. "Keep them off white tablecloths under strong bulbs if that white-gloved viper looks down. And don't drink two glasses of German wine on an empty belly—eat half this cold potato before you go downstairs so grease doesn't turn your guts inside out."

Sylwia ate the cold salted potato standing up, slipped barefoot into her sole surviving pair of black glacé kid pumps resoled twice with bicycle-tire rubber, draped Helena's threadbare black wool shawl over her bare back, and went down to Stabsarzt Vesper's waiting open Adler at the gate.

Twenty minutes' drive northeast across sultry August twilight carried them across planets.

Along Nowogrodzka and Marszałkowska dark crowds hurried home ahead of nine-o'clock curfew past boarded shopfronts and pink hostage-posters peeling in dry wind; turn left past the iron tank-traps guarding **Wierzbowa and Saski Square**, where floodlights blazed white now that no Soviet planes showed on radar, and suddenly manicured flowerbeds of red canna lilies surrounded the cream rococo wings of **Palais Brühl**, shiny black Mercedes limousines purred three deep along raked gravel, and SS sentries in spotless white summer parade belts checked embossed pasteboard cards under gilded lanterns!

Inside the Casino banquet annex behind the main palace courtyard, four Venetian crystal chandeliers blazed with two hundred unshaded candles—not candles, real sixty-watt clear bulbs burning full voltage!—above a sixty-foot horseshoe table laid in snow-white Silesian damask for forty guests: Wehrmacht sanitary colonels, civilian German *Amtsärzte* from Cracow and Radom, three nervous senior Polish hospital directors dragged along like tame bears to demonstrate "hygienic cooperation in the Vistula space" (old Professor Orłowski from Wolski Hospital looking carved out of grey wood at the far end), and six sleek officers from Aleja Szucha in field-grey SS tunics with silver *SD* cuff-titles.

Before Sylwia was ten paces inside the double salon doors behind puffing, bowing Kurt Vesper, the physical shock of the buffet sideboard hit her solar plexus harder than Skaryszewska's rifle-butt.

Silver chafing-dishes six feet long steamed with roast Pomeranian fat goose swimming in golden crackling and apples; platters of pink Westphalian ham sliced thick around white fat rims; bowls of real yellow Mayonnaise piled over cold Vistula salmon and hard-boiled eggs; pyramids of butter—ten kilos of real yellow creamery butter carved into swans on crushed ice!—beside white wheat rolls crusted with poppyseed.

After forty-six months of grey sawdust bread, rutabaga water, and two hundred grams of rancid horse-tallow a month, merely inhaling hot roast goose-fat vapor in eighty-degree candlelit air made Sylwia's shrunken stomach contract in a violent, painful cramp that flooded her mouth with cold sour saliva—half ravenous wolf-hunger, half immediate nausea so sharp she had to lock her teeth together not to gag on the carpet.

Across the room beside the tall open French doors leading onto the dark garden loggia stood Ludwig.

He was talking quietly with grey-haired Professor Orłowski—holding a stemmed glass of pale Mosel untouched by its foot, immaculate in tailored evening dress-tunic with high silver-braided collar and silver *Oberstleutnant* shoulder-boards catching chandelier crystal. As Vesper steered Sylwia loudly toward the place-cards, Ludwig's eyes flicked once across thirty feet of uniformed shoulders—taking in Helena's black Lyons silk, the bare V of spine glimpsed as Vesper lifted her wool shawl for the cloakroom orderly, the fierce white pallor of her cheekbones—and slid smoothly back to Orłowski without changing rhythm by half a syllable, as cool and distant as if they had met twice over pus-basins in Lent.

Then Sylwia looked down at the gilt-edged place-cards beside the middle curve of the horseshoe table, and saw Reinhart Kroll's neat Dresden law-school geometry laid out across white linen:

To Sylwia's right sat terrified Kurt Vesper.
Two seats down to her left—close enough that nobody could pretend deafness, far enough that every word crossed three pairs of German ears—sat **Ludwig Schoeler**.
And directly *opposite* Sylwia across four feet of low red roses and crystal wine-glasses sat **SS-Hauptsturmführer Reinhart Kroll**, white cotton gloves gleaming snowy beneath grey wool cuffs even at dinner!

* * *

For forty minutes through clear turtle consommé and cold salmon—Sylwia touching one forkful of fish dry without mayonnaise, crumbling half a white roll onto her bread-plate so fingers looked occupied while sipping plain Selters mineral water—conversation round their curve of the table stayed impeccably sanitary: Wola louse-delousing figures, autumn dysentery bacteriophage trials from Lemberg, Cracow serum production.

Only when white-jacketed waiters cleared fish plates and set slice after glistening brown slice of roast goose down before forty guests—Kroll leaving his own rich goose untouched save two spears of tinned asparagus, eating as sparingly as a seminary dyspeptic while his gloved left index finger whispered *scritch-scritch* inside his right shirt-cuff—did the Saxon lawyer smile gently across the red roses at Sylwia.

"Stabsarzt Vesper informs me, Frau Doktor Kotarska, that since Docent Mirecki's unfortunate cardiac indisposition last month you carry sole surgical command of seventy Polish beds at *Kindlein Jesu*," Kroll remarked in his soft, pleasant courtroom voice that somehow silenced two neighboring colonels' chatter inside five words. "Remarkable administrative burden—and yet Berlin's academic registries scarcely surprise one. In fact, clearing old Ministry library crates last week, one of our Berlin liaison clerks stumbled across quite a charming souvenir from Luisenstraße."

Without haste Kroll reached down beside his chair-leg into a slim black leather document portfolio, drew out a glossy octavo sheet torn or photographed from the 1932 *Klinisches Jahrbuch der Chirurgischen Universitätsklinik der Charité*, and laid it face-up on clean damask right beside the salt-cellars between them.

Sylwia didn't lean back and didn't lean greedily forward—tilted her chin four calm degrees downward to look at the twelve-year-old silver-bromide group photograph taken on sunny hospital steps in May nineteen-thirty-two:

Front center in winged starched collar sat white-bearded Geheimrat Professor von Halden looking like Jehovah after successful gallstones. Standing immediately behind his right shoulder—twenty-three, lean-jawed, unsmiling, arms folded across a dazzling white clinic coat—stood **Ludwig Schoeler**. Standing symmetrical behind von Halden's left shoulder—twenty-one, dark hair coiled severe above fierce dark eyes and a chin lifted two inches higher than any girl on scholarship had business lifting it—stood **Sylwia Kotarska**. And half a step behind the pair of them on the second marble riser, blinking benignly through round gold-wire spectacles in his ink-stained black alpaca coat, stood **Professor Doktor Jakob Mandelbaum**.

Beside Sylwia, Kurt Vesper choked slightly on a mouthful of goose skin, reaching hastily for his Mosel glass.

Two seats left, Ludwig leaned slightly forward over his untouched wine-stem to glance sideways at the glossy print—one eyebrow rising two millimeters in cool, bored recognition.

"May thirty-two, after the German Surgical Congress," Ludwig observed dryly before Sylwia even needed to speak, tapping one bare knuckle lightly near the corner of the print where fat Kleist's ear protruded behind three taller assistants. "Before Kleist bought riding-boots. God, von Halden made us stand forty minutes in bright sun waiting for the photographer's magnesium pan while two strangulated hernias waited upstairs. Where on earth did your clerk dig up clinic yearbooks, Kroll—looking for uncollected library fines?"

Kroll smiled faintly, sandy lashes unblinking as he watched *Sylwia's* face rather than Ludwig's.

"Routine background cross-referencing on Generalgouvernement epidemic license holders, *Herr Oberstleutnant*," Kroll said silkily. "Though one admits coincidence possesses a certain literary charm. Out of forty surgical clinics in the Reich and nine municipal hospitals in Warsaw, a midnight ambush in Ochota deposits *Oberstleutnant* Schoeler onto the operating table of the precise classmate standing three feet left of him in May nineteen-thirty-two—followed four months later by *Oberstleutnant* Schoeler personally extracting the same classmate from *Arbeitsamt* transit barracks at Skaryszewska."

Every knife and fork within four seats either side had stopped clicking against porcelain. Even old Professor Orłowski six places down sat motionless behind white whiskers, listening to steel ice crack across thin river water.

Run from it—mumble excuses about blind chance—and Kroll smelled blood inside five seconds.

Sylwia picked up her Selters water glass with fingers steady as granite—Bronka's tallow-and-lemon rub hiding half the sublimate cracks under candle-glow—took one small cool sip, set crystal noiselessly back onto linen, and looked straight into Reinhart Kroll's pale eyes with a dry, wintry curl of her lip that echoed Geheimrat von Halden's nastiest Thursday rounds.

"Had you sat through five Berlin winters beside Herr Schoeler on Luisenstraße, *Herr Hauptsturmführer*," Sylwia said clearly in crisp Charlottenburg German pitched so all six neighboring officers caught every iced syllable, "you would call February ninth divine malice rather than literary charm. In December nineteen-thirty Herr Schoeler lifted three weeks of my venous-manometer figures uncredited onto a blackboard to beat me by one vote for the Virchow Medal; in forty-three he arrived uninvited at one in the morning to dump two liters of blood across my cleanest operating floor and argue suturing gauge awake."

A genuine bark of relieved, tipsy laughter burst out of Stabsarzt Vesper beside her, seconded by two Wehrmacht sanitary colonels grinning into their Mosel—men understood lifelong academic venom instantly where friendship would have smelled treasonous!

"And as for Skaryszewska on the fourth of June," Ludwig cut in smoothly across his wine-stem, voice edged with just enough cold Prussian arrogance toward Polish subordinates to make every German ear at the table nod—*"Do you imagine I rode over to Praga out of sentiment for Fräulein Kotarska's manometer notebooks, Kroll? Only three surgeons east of Breslau have successfully anastomosed a lacerated subclavian-axillary trunk without late aneurysmal thrombosis since forty-one, and Stabsarzt Vesper needed her typhus-ward signatures clean before Wola's workshops shut forty tram-lines down. Leave it to Ordnungspolizei drunks to throw scarce vascular technicians into potato trains."*

"Quite—utterly chaotic street-policing that afternoon!" Vesper chimed in eagerly, wiping goose grease off his chin, desperate to earn Tuesday's peace from Sylwia's safe. "General Stahel's own adjutant reprimanded the *Schutzpolizei* company commander personally on my recommendation!"

Across the roses, Reinhart Kroll's gentle smile did not vanish—yet underneath the table edge Sylwia heard the unmistakable dry *scritch-scritch-scritch* of white cotton rubbing faster across weeping wrist skin.

Kroll laid one gloved fingertip delicately right onto the tiny photographic head of **Professor Jakob Mandelbaum** standing behind their twenty-two-year-old shoulders on marble steps.

"And poor Professor Mandelbaum behind you both," Kroll murmured almost wistfully, tilting his sandy head three degrees. "Died of heart failure at Columbia-Haus detention center in April thirty-three after running an illegal Marxist dispensary in Wedding, if our dusty Alexanderplatz cards read straight. Curiously enough, *Herr Oberstleutnant*, the original March thirty-three arrest-ledger folder from *Abteilung IA*—where one clinic assistant's name was struck out in ink fifteen minutes before Mandelbaum's arrest under an old Reichswehr stamp—arrived in my Warsaw registry pouch yesterday morning instead of next week."

*Yesterday morning instead of next week.*

Not one muscle in Sylwia's face twitched; she did not blink, did not swallow, did not glance left toward Ludwig by half a degree of arc—even as ice-water flooded every vein behind her ribs! Berlin's courier had arrived *five days early*, on Saturday morning! Right now, locked inside Reinhart Kroll's green safe on Aleja Szucha Twenty-Five, lay the physical paper ledger from March seventh nineteen-thirty-three!

Wait—if Kroll had read *Kotarska, Sylwia Helena* legible under the black ink cross-hatching yesterday morning, why wasn't Sylwia sitting in chains down in Aleja Szucha's basement tonight instead of eating salmon off Silesian damask?!

Watch Kroll's pale eyes probing between the two of them like a blind needle fishing for bone: *because physical ink deletion + "[AKTE ENTFERNT — FILE REMOVED]" meant whoever at Bendlerstraße pulled those Wedding pharmacy receipts out of Mandelbaum's folder at 11:45 a.m. on March 8, 1933 had BURNED the physical receipts and scrubbed the paper fibre deeper with pumice-eraser than high-contrast microfilm ultraviolet scatter showed!* Wait—on microfilm camera copy, oblique UV/arc-lamp illumination sometimes brought faint indented typewriter strike-impressions up out of erased paper slightly clearer than naked eye saw on yellowed foolscap—OR Kroll suspected *Kotarska* under the blot without yet holding signed Wedding pharmacy slips to prove *subversive Polish-Communist activity* past Wehrmacht epidemic protection!

"Half the professors on Luisenstraße had assistants whose uncles voted Social Democrat before thirty-three, Kroll," Ludwig said in a voice bored enough to cut glass, picking up his Mosel at last to take one tiny sip and set it down. "If *Abteilung Vier* has so few Polish bandits left to catch in Warsaw after losing your prize source in Mokotów last month that your clerks spend August reading ten-year-old Reichswehr ink-blots, General Stahel will wonder what Aleja Szucha burns petrol for."

Direct hit square into Kroll's teeth—*losing your prize source "Uhrzeiger" in Mokotów last month*!

For one half-second Kroll's pale sandy lashes dropped completely still over colourless eyes; white cotton fingers stopped moving on his wrist; politely he slid the glossy 1932 Charité photograph back inside his black leather portfolio without another word as liveried waiters wheeled in silver bowls of iced peaches and a four-man German string quartet tuned violins on the small gilt dais at the end of the hall.

* * *

Ten minutes into Schubert's *Death and the Maiden* quartet—chairs turned slightly sideways toward the musicians while blue cigar smoke curled lazily up into crystal chandeliers and half the German doctors loosened tight tunic hooks in eighty-degree air—Sylwia felt the postponed rebellion of a four-year-starved stomach hit bottom against two bites of rich salmon and hot goose-fat vapor.

Cold sweat broke fine along her nape; stay seated five minutes longer and she disgraced Poznań brains across Silesian damask.

Touching two steady fingers lightly to Vesper's sleeve with a murmur about terrace air, she rose noiselessly during the *Andante con moto*, slipped through the tall side drapery behind marble pillars—not out onto the crowded garden loggia where officers smoked, left down the cool dim vaulted marble service-gallery connecting banquet annex to main palace cloakrooms.

Inside the empty tiled ladies' retiring room she locked the stall, knelt cleanly on her handkerchief so Helena's silk touched zero dust, brought up four mouthfuls of sour Selters water without making a sound, rinsed her mouth thrice at the porcelain tap with cold water until color settled back cold and clear in her cheeks, and stepped back out into the shadowed marble gallery to return before the movement ended.

Halfway past the deep, heavy crimson-velvet-curtained telephone alcove recessed six feet into three-foot-thick eighteenth-century palace masonry—dark because nobody lit corridor wall-sconces behind blackout portières, thirty paces round the corner from where two SS *Wachmannschaft* sentries stood immobile beneath gilt mirrors by the lobby arch—a bare right hand reached silently out between velvet folds, caught Sylwia's bare elbow, and drew her straight inside behind heavy damask curtains!

Pitch blackness smelling of old velvet dust, faint cigar smoke from down the hall, and crisp gabardine wool.

Before she drew breath to hiss fury at lunatic recklessness inside Palais Brühl itself, Ludwig's back braced against the recessed oak telephone-booth door while both his hands framed her bare shoulder-blades inside the deep V of Helena's black silk—skin burning hot against cool palms—and his mouth came down hard and silent onto hers.

Not Schubert—pure post-combat adrenaline shaking like live high-tension wire through both of them after forty minutes smiling across roses into Reinhart Kroll's white cotton gloves while Schubert violins sawed passionate minor chords thirty yards away through open doors!

"Are you out of your mind—sentries twenty yards down—" Sylwia breathed fiercely into his mouth even as her own hands locked tight onto his silver-braided collar-tabs, pulling him down instead of pushing him away.

"Change of guard takes four minutes at eleven, and everyone else is watching fiddles," Ludwig murmured raw against her throat, sliding both hands down the cool French mulberry silk over her starved ribs to bunch thin black crêpe smoothly up her thighs in pitch velvet dark until bare palms hit hot skin above garter-straps!

Five minutes. Less than five minutes—reckless, mad, utterly unforgivable thirty yards from Reinhart Kroll's back, driven by the sudden blinding certainty in both their bones that Saturday's early Berlin courier meant clocks across all Warsaw had just jumped forward six weeks!

Sylwia didn't wear knickers under bias-cut nineteen-twenty-eight concert silk because thick wartime cotton bunched ugly seams across starved hips—when Ludwig's fingers slid past garter clasps expecting linen and met bare, slick, burning-wet heat directly in the dark, a low sharp breath tore through his teeth against her ear! Unbuttoning grey dress-trousers one-handed in two silent jerks without unbuckling sword-belt loops, he lifted her by both bare thighs four inches up against smooth cold Carrara marble wainscot hidden deep behind three folds of crimson palace velvet, guiding himself straight inside her in one deep, suffocatingly silent upward thrust while clapping his left hand lightly near her cheek as she bit clean into the shoulder wool of his *Oberstleutnant*'s tunic right beside the silver braid so not one gasp escaped into the corridor!

Cold eighteenth-century palace marble froze her bare upper back while six feet of wool-clad German officer burned white-hot between her thighs—violins rising shrill and tragic down the gallery in Schubert's *allegro*, booted heels clicking once past the outer marble arch twenty-five feet away and fading down garden steps without pausing! Every nerve in Sylwia's body screamed raw terror braided indistinguishable from blinding carnal voltage: wrapping both legs tight round his hips under falling black silk folds, grinding down furiously onto quick, shallow, ruthless upward strokes while his thumb worked slick lightning between them until heat snapped blinding behind her eyes—clenching silent and convulsive round him with her teeth locked hard in German gabardine wool five seconds before he buried his mouth against her bare neck and came deep inside her without making velvet stir by half an inch!

Sixty heartbeats later silk whispered smooth back down to her calves; trousers buttoned neat in darkness; Sylwia wiped damp thighs swiftly with his clean linen pocket-handkerchief before stuffing the square deep inside *his* own uniform pocket so he carried the evidence out past Brühl guards himself!

Before parting the crimson velvet crack half an inch to check empty marble corridor, Ludwig held her chin still in the dark for five seconds, breathing quiet now, cold tactical ice returning instant to his whisper against her ear:

"Listen carefully before you go back to your chair: first, Kroll couldn't read your full surname under that nineteen-thirty-three Reichswehr pumice-erasure on original foolscap—only *poln. cand. med.* and half a *K*—because Major von Bredow burned your signed Wedding pharmacy slips in an ashtray in front of me on March eighth before stamping the blot."

Sylwia's heart stopped once in pitch dark—hearing him confirm March eighth nineteen-thirty-three aloud out of his own mouth for the first time in ten years without giving her three seconds to answer before survival cut across it!

"Second," Ludwig whispered rapid and lethal: "because Kroll hit brick on nineteen-thirty-three today, his deputy signed an order at six p.m. scheduling a surprise *Erkennungsdienst* fingerprint-and-birth-parish audit of *all auxiliary laboratory staff* at *Szpital Dzieciątka Jezus* for the morning of **Wednesday the eighth of September**—looking for forged *Kennkarten* under your basement roof to break Pavilion Three open from underneath. Get your Jewish girl out of that cellar before midnight on the seventh."

Violins down the hall hit the final crashing minor chords of the movement; polite German applause pattered crisp across parquet.

Ludwig released her chin, stepped out first through a side smoking-door toward the gentlemen's terrace without looking back, and forty seconds later Sylwia glided serenely back into candlelit salon glare from the opposite arch in black Lyons silk—sitting down beside Kurt Vesper with cool pink cheeks and steady hands to watch Reinhart Kroll clap white cotton gloves softly together for Schubert.
