The Vintage

The vintage comes to Campania like a debt falling due: men in the rows before light, the presses, the year's whole borrowed weather turned suddenly, crushingly, into fact. Antonia goes south in the autumn on the Greek's business that is her business — an investor is entitled to inspect an olive grove, indeed to taste its grievances — travelling with Diomedes and a mule-driver whose opinions on emperors would constitute treason in any province not hardened by long exposure to mule-drivers. She has not been back to Baiae in twenty-one years. The road insists on reminders the way roads do: a hillside town climbed by steps; a well-dressed girl at a fountain holding, steady as a standard, a tray that is absolutely a mirror.

Diomedes has arranged the journey with his usual respect for the fact that bodies age less forgivingly than reputations. They travel in stages, by carriage where the road permits dignity and by mule cart where it permits progress. The driver, who introduces every emperor by the defects of the road built under him, regards Antonia’s tailored cloak with suspicion until she correctly identifies an approaching change in weather by the behaviour of smoke from a hillside kiln. After that he addresses her as an authority on the south and reserves his sedition for national topics.

“Trajan spends like a man putting his name where rain can read it,” he says. “Hadrian will travel like a man checking whether the ink dried.”

“Keep both hands on the reins,” Diomedes says. “Treason is easier to survive at a walk.”

Antonia watches Campania unroll with the business end of her mind doing sentry duty. There are villages white as knucklebones, black soil in patterns older than Rome’s paperwork, vines trained up trees in green marriages of convenience. There are also stairs: town stairs, shrine stairs, a flight beside a cistern down which girls carry amphorae with the economy of long sentencing. At one inn a servant brings a tray of cups up from a cellar and the past, having no manners, puts its foot on every step beside her.

She sleeps badly in a clean room. It offends her efficiency. She has slept soundly through taxation, death threats delivered by committee and a kitchen fire extinguished mostly in Greek. Here, southward weather gets into the shutters. She dreams not of Baiae exactly but of being counted by somebody else: coins, stairs, girls at a muster, all declining to stay in their columns.

Diomedes at breakfast says nothing and pours the watered wine himself. It is one of his liturgies. “The grove is two hours if the driver does not stop to assassinate geography.”

“And Baiae?”

“Visible from the upper terraces, madam. If required.”

“I am here to inspect olives, Diomedes.”

“Yes. And an institution is entitled to a view.”

She has decided en route what she will not do. She will not seek out the dealer’s successor to make an old office perform horror. She will not climb any staircase out of defiance, like a woman in a recitation. She will not, merely because her money now has a southern accent, ask the landscape for a signed confession. Ground is ground. People put the paperwork in.

And yet, at a fountain where they stop for the mules, she sees a girl of perhaps sixteen carrying a polished tray for a mistress in a litter. The girl’s face is shut like a well-kept shop. For one second Antonia’s own reflection crosses the tray’s bright metal, forty years old and on her way to inspect property, superimposed on a younger trade.

She looks away first. The girl does not need an ancestress appearing in the equipment.

“Drive on,” Antonia says.

The driver, who has been telling Diomedes about a cousin unofficially related to a man who once saw the emperor’s horse, climbs up with the air of a philosopher recalled to public life. “The coast makes everybody thoughtful, lady. It’s the salt. Preserves grievances past their season.”

“Then we shall invoice by weight,” Antonia says, and the cart goes on into the autumn light, which has the honesty of a debt finally presented in the correct office.

He is there because the vintage requires owners as well as creditors, and because the Greek, who has survived forty years by arranging rooms, has arranged for once a grove. Lentulus among his olives looks less like a senator than like a man auditing trees that have read his family's accounts for three centuries and remain, professionally, unimpressed. The agent does the talking. The Greek does the not-talking, in two languages. Antonia walks the terraces with the business end of her mind uppermost — drainage (poor, old the Roman way), trees (good), press (a disgrace she is already pricing) — and understands, with the part of her the business is protecting, that she has brought herself to this ground on purpose. The eyes do it, eventually. Cities can be filed. Ground cannot. This is her money's ground now, a useful share of it. It is also, in a book no Greek keeps, the ground she was sold south *from*: the muster, the contract, the south itself like a direction on a coffin.

The estate manager is a narrow man with a broad education in excuses. He begins, as managers do, with what cannot be controlled: rain at the wrong grandfather’s hour, a caterpillar with provincial ambitions, a south wind that has been reading Greek and become changeable. Antonia listens at the level of drainage. The Greek listens at the level of debt. Diomedes listens at the level of what has not been said about labour.

An older worker is summoned because nobody else can explain why one terrace yields like a psalm and the next like a tax demand. He is introduced as Soter, though whether this is the name his mother gave him or the estate settled on for record purposes is left for another reformation. He takes them to a retaining wall and shows where water has been going quietly wrong for ten years.

“Gentlemen station trees,” he says. “Water enlists where it likes.”

Lentulus, arriving along the path with dust on his shoes, hears this and does not correct the politics. It is a measure of his year. “Can it be cut back without losing the wall?”

“Can be persuaded. More hands before the rain. Fewer stones after.”

“Cost?”

The manager has a tablet ready with a figure that has been dieting. Antonia points to a column. “That assumes your tenants lend tools and call it loyalty.”

“They have always—”

“Then they have always been charged twice.”

The Greek’s silence becomes floral. Diomedes opens a page like a drawbridge. For the next hour the grove is not a symbol but an organisation: who eats during harvest; which press house roof leaks; whose son is being promised seasonal work in a dialect that differs from his contract; what the olives will fetch if the Greek’s ships take them at the October price rather than the November quarrel.

Lentulus follows better than his manager expects. Water board habits travel: ask where the pressure goes, ask who says so, ask what happens at the lower end of the system when the upper villas take their full share. Soter, having delivered his diagnosis, watches a senator being tutored by a freedman in estate Greek and a woman whose name is on the new paper. His expression suggests that the republic may yet become educational.

At the oldest tree on the terrace—trunk like twisted rope, branches seasoned by catastrophe—they stop. Somebody’s ancestor is said to have planted it before the convenient centuries. Antonia puts her hand to the bark briefly, not sentimentally. Living things are indifferent to her bookkeeping. That is one of their recommendations.

“It should be left out of the highest yield estimate,” she says. “Old trees object to optimism.”

“It has been estimated hopefully for twenty years,” Lentulus says. “A family policy.”

“Then we are diversifying.”

The joke lands in the open air, where nobody makes it into a myth. Below, the presses begin again, a rhythm like Rome clearing its throat. The manager takes his corrected tablet away with the fragile expression of a man who has just learned that debt may come with women attached and questions in their own handwriting.

“The press wants replacing before next year,” she says, because she came to say things that could be minuted. “Whoever holds the paper after this vintage should insist on it, or the yield will file its own report.”

“The marriage has paid the Greek's first significance.” Lentulus picks an olive, not ripe, and puts it in his mouth anyway, either from habit or penance; the bitterness crosses his face like weather. “The remainder — you are being paid, Antonia. At the full rate. I wanted you to hear the estate say so with its mouth full.”

“I hear quarterly. It is my civic religion; you may have read of it.” She looks down the terraces to where the sea does its old indifferent glitter, Baiae-ward. “I climbed stairs down there for two years with water that weighed more than I did, by the end of the summers. I own paper on your trees now. If a poet of my acquaintance wrote it he'd be sent back to the kitchen for saffron abuse.”

They eat at the press house rather than in the villa, because Diomedes has understood that Antonia would rather inspect soup where it is made than genealogy where it is polished. The meal is harvest food: bread with density, greens bitter enough to tell the truth, oil from a pressing good enough to make the Greek’s agent briefly close his eyes in a manner not permitted in his contract.

Workers come and go at the lower table. Nobody pretends the proprietors are not in the room; nobody performs a festival about it either. Campania in vintage has no leisure for Roman theatre. A woman with juice to the elbows tells the manager that the old press has begun to sing in its beam, which nobody at the table translates into optimism.

“Sing how?” Lentulus asks.

“Like a tooth about to give evidence,” she says.

“Cost of replacement?”

She looks at the manager. The manager looks at the Greek. The Greek looks, in two languages, at his hands.

Antonia says, “We have discussed the press. It is being replaced. Talk about the beam as if it were machinery and not a relative.”

It gets a laugh from the lower table because she has not tried to buy one. Released from metaphor, the beam’s defects are enumerated with satisfying brutality. A carpenter is named. A timber source is argued. Soter suggests the old beam can be cut for vineyard props, which gives the object a future in humility.

After the meal, while Diomedes is annexing information under the guise of admiring storage jars, Antonia walks with the Greek between the ranks of amphorae. The banker’s sandals make almost no sound.

“You have arranged more than a grove,” she says.

“I have arranged an inspection. What the inspected parties make of the weather belongs to their accounts.”

“Diomedes says your small room has enlarged itself into landscape.”

“Diomedes is a poet with a filing system.”

She stops by an amphora sealed and marked for Rome. Her name is not on it. Her money, through channels respectable enough to be boring, is. “When I first came south,” she says, “I was cargo.”

The Greek does not give her a proverb. It is why she trusts him. “Paper improves with the bearer, madam.”

“Paper burns.”

“Then we keep copies.” He inclines his head towards the terraces where Lentulus is standing with the manager over a drain. “He pays on the day.”

“I know.”

“He has stopped asking for the statements to be made smaller.”

That is, from the Greek, a character reference with artillery. Antonia accepts it at face value and no more. A man paying on the day is not a moonrise. Still, a calendar in this family has changed its phases.

“Do not bring Prima into the estate paper,” she says.

“I could not without instructions in her own hand.”

“And if she ever gives them?”

“Then a new room will be required.” The banker's face does not move, but something around his eyes can be filed as approval. “Rooms are a specialism.”

A whistle sounds below. Doves change trees as if disgusted by economics. The Greek returns to silence, leaving the landscape to conduct its own negotiations in the open.

Before the afternoon inspection resumes, Antonia does the thing she decided en route she would not do, but she does it under house rules. She walks with Diomedes to the upper path where Baiae can be seen—not entered, not indicted, seen. The town lies along its curve in a glitter of roofs and steam and famous idleness. It is smaller than memory, as capitals of suffering usually are when approached with account books.

Diomedes stops one pace behind. He has made a profession of distances.

“There was a house with blue tiles,” Antonia says. “Behind the dealer’s rooms. Stairs down to water. A woman with henna on her nails who could make soup out of threats.”

“Would you like the house located, madam?”

“No.”

“Would you like it purchased and put to some humiliating civic use?”

“Diomedes.”

“I ask for operational clarity. Revenge has procurement implications.”

She looks at the coast for longer than a view can be held responsible for. Somewhere down there a girl learned that standing up was compulsory because the arithmetic of lying down included disappearance. Somewhere down there a report was coined and spent upward into better houses. The girl is not down there any longer. She is here, with a Greek’s strongbox behind her and a daughter across a river who charges by the piece. The knowledge does not make the town innocent. It removes its right to be the final address.

“I would like our oil taken by the easier road,” she says. “Not through Baiae. It adds two days and saves the jars.”

Diomedes accepts the instruction as if no one has been standing at a border of the underworld discussing logistics. “The inland route is less scenic and more solvent.”

“Then it is the family route.”

They walk back to the grove. Whatever has been laid at the coast remains there, unsentimental as ballast. Antonia does not feel healed, a word she regards as a cosmetic for ledgers. She feels oriented, which is rarer and can be used in weather.

The vintage does not pause for the moral improvements of ownership. By the third day the press house is running at a tempo that makes conversation sound like a hobby of the underemployed. Baskets come in from first light: grapes for wine from the lower parcels, olives still early but judged by men who can hear ripeness in a branch. Antonia takes a turn with the tally because Diomedes is needed at the store tally and because nobody, including Antonia, can be trusted to stand in a workplace merely radiating investment.

She counts aloud in her Caelian office voice and then, when a young worker corrects her by a basket, corrects the total without the ownership grimace. It wins her more in the press house than six amphorae of fine feeling. The woman who diagnosed the beam says, “You count like someone has counted you.”

“It gives the numbers manners,” Antonia says.

Soter is everywhere at once, as older men are during harvests, mostly by having convinced the young that he sees around corners. He tells Lentulus which terrace to leave unpicked until the moon changes the sugar, tells the manager not to shout at the mules in Latin they despise, tells a boy that a ladder is not a relative and should not be leaned on for emotional support.

Lentulus watches his estate being run by people whose surnames, if they have them, are not on his maps. “I have owned this since I was twenty,” he says to Antonia during a lull no longer than a swallow.

“You have received reports since you were twenty.”

“Is that distinction also plumbing?”

“It is drainage. Authority should visit the lower ground before it signs.”

He goes down to the lower terrace to look at Soter’s proposed cut and returns with mud on his calves and the news that the drainage crew should start after the press beam is taken out, not during peak movement of baskets. “Otherwise the path becomes an argument,” he explains.

“You are improving the estate in the order of the evening. We may make a manager of you at slave rates.”

“Madam,” the Greek says from the amphorae, without appearing to have arrived, “the paper has a phrase for that.”

“Do not say it here,” Diomedes tells him. “The beam has suffered enough.”

On the way between grove and posting inn there is a town she has not named in her accounts for years. Diomedes has given the driver no instruction to stop, which is how Antonia knows he expects her to give one. The cart passes a laundry court where steam rises in disciplined weather. A woman at the gate is haranguing a dyer with the fluency of long financial intimacy. The voice reaches the road before the face does.

“Stop,” Antonia says.

The woman turns. Time has worked with its usual mixture of cruelty and editorial restraint: the hands are dyed permanently at the fingertips, the hair is iron, the eye is the same one that once asked for a loan to redeem tools from a brother-in-law and paid in wool and gossip. She looks at Antonia, then at the good cloak, then at the Greek’s not quite visible influence around the cart like good weather for banks.

“You,” she says. “You kept growing.”

“So did the business.” Antonia gets down carefully, because the gesture must not become a descent from a monument. “Your tools?”

“Mine. His yard too, since the fever. Heaven operates at retail.” She calls into the court, where two younger women are working the rinse, “This is the lady who thinks a loan is a sentence with the interest where you can see it. Attend, and don’t borrow from priests.”

There is wine in a back room with accounts on the wall in chalk. The old borrower—whose name in Antonia’s oldest informal book was followed, she remembers, by *pays in colour*—has become a lender herself, in sums so small official Rome would trip over them. She shows the chalk without showing off. “Mostly tools. One wedding, which I regret as a category. A widow’s stall. No sermons. I took the wording off you.”

“Did you?” Antonia says, and finds the afternoon rearranging itself with more than estate drains in it.

“I took the part where you wrote what I owed where I could read it in daylight. It ruined me for men with folders.” The woman wipes a cup dry and gives her the better one without noticing, or perhaps noticing exactly. “Your Senate clause came through here in the summer with a copyist. Half price. We put it over the gate. The priests said it weathered badly.”

“Paper burns,” Antonia says.

“We keep the ash in a jar as a warning and copies in the strongbox.” She grins, dyed fingers around her cup. “You own olives now, they say.”

“Paper on olives.”

“Paper grows if you don’t give it to brothers-in-law.”

They do not embrace. South of Rome, among women who have made it through, sentiment often inspects the premises instead: drainage (good), younger women (learning), away to her cart by a girl with indigo up to the wrist and a directness Antonia respects at once.

“Is the lady coming into the yard?” the girl asks.

“The lady,” says the proprietress, “is going back to Rome to register the world. It takes it out of a person.”

It is not a blessing. It is better: a former debtor regarding her as a colleague in an occupation no committee has yet learned to name.

“I have thought—” he begins, and stops, because what he has thought has taken him nine months to come down to this terrace to say without a committee to hold it. The presses groan below. A overseer's whistle, somebody's laugh, doves. “I have thought a man should be able to apologise in the order of the evening, too. Not the questions. Yours would be unanswerable at my rates.”

“Then in the order of the evening,” Antonia says, and folds her hands, and lets him.

He tells it. The muster. The study smelling of wax. The question asked once; the statesman's sentence accepted; twenty years marching with a stone in the shoe and calling the limp a style. He tells it without the Senate in it, which for Lentulus is nakedness beyond any baths. He does not say the word *love* because they are forty and the word has been taxed out of their bracket; he says *I let my father choose the currency*, which in Cornelian is the same confession with the ancestry left in.

He begins badly, because men of his training do. “When my father—” he says, and hears the opening arrange three centuries in front of him like furniture. He stops. Looks down at his hands, which have olive dust in the creases and are not, for once, holding a document up to the light. “No. When *I* came into that study. He was there. I keep putting him in the sentence so that I can stand behind the name.”

Antonia does not help him. Help, at this stage, would be another form of service.

“I asked whether there was a child,” he says. “Not whether she lived. Not where. Not what your name was on the contract, or who held the paper, or what Baiae meant in money and stairs. I asked a question that could be answered by grammar. He gave me one, and I paid for my whole life with the savings. That is the muster, Antonia. I never left it. I just learned to march indoors.”

A press groans lower down. Somebody laughs at a dropped tool. It is indecent how intact the afternoon remains.

“I received a world in which you had been filed. I improved my application to it. I married—Servilia deserves her own sentence and will, I hope, one day write it—into the arrangement his settlement bought. When Marcus was born I thought: the account balances. I did not know it was balanced by giving another child to a woman counting stairs in Transtiberim. If I had known—” Again he catches the conditional doing its elegant smuggling. “I do not know what I would have done. That is part of the apology, though it is not an attractive part. I would like to tell you I would have burned the house down. The boy who was at that muster liked being a Cornelius more than he liked being accurate.”

“I know,” Antonia says. “I saw the boy.”

It is not absolution. It is evidence, and he takes it as a man takes a bitter olive from his own tree—without pretending to be nourished by theatre.

“I have paid what can be paid in money because money was the language in which I could not pretend to be misunderstood. The clause—” He almost smiles, stops, resumes with a citizen’s care. “The clause was written because I had seen a room of women who could not make refusal legible and, for one dawn, I recognised the plumbing of my own life in it. Other people have made it real. Prima—” The name remains difficult, not as a scandal but as a weight he has no licence to lift. “Prima owes me nothing. Not a letter. Not a dynasty. If she never writes my name, I will take that as a text accurately delivered.”

“You may be cross-examined in another year. Not her betrothal winter, not her wedding season. She decides.”

“I am learning.”

“You are reciting.”

“Is there a difference in our families?”

“One of them signs.”

He stands in the terraces he has inherited and is learning, late, to inspect at ground level. “I am sorry,” he says at last, without committees. “Not as a settlement. As a fact to be entered where you keep such things.”

Antonia hears the sorting of twenty years: stairs counted, money stopped, a girl folding linen with knowledge in her hands. “I keep facts by whether they can be used without spending the person they describe,” she says. “We will see.”

Antonia listens the way she listens to men after the fish course: for the truth under the arrangement. It is there. It has the unsatisfying shape of truth, which does not unlock rooms or unclimb stairs. It merely stops charging interest at the moment of its payment, and not an hour before.

“You were twenty-four,” she says at last. “I have Marcus's measure now; I know what your family's twenty-four is worth in the market. I was seventeen and *unpaid*, which is a different currency, and no apology is legal tender for it anywhere in this empire. So we will not pretend. The money is settled quarterly. The girl is settled better than money. The clause —” she pauses, and finds, to her housekeeping soul's surprise, something approaching warmth available, unbolted, between the quarters “— the clause is walking about Rome in other women's hands, Lentulus, doing what no apology of yours could stock. If a Cornelius must pay his debts, I will let the record show he paid in infrastructure.”

“Aqueducts and adverbs,” he says, with the first unguarded smile she has seen on him since a courtyard she does not visit. “My grandfather is revolving in his mask.”

There is, in the wake of it, an interval in which neither has a professional reason to remain on that terrace. The manager is waiting at a diplomatic distance with a tablet. The Greek is not waiting at all, which means he has anticipated the next three hours and is probably improving them in another language. Diomedes has become absorbed in the number of amphorae, an absorption Antonia recognises as mercy.

“Cornelia writes that her floor complains,” Lentulus says, because ordinary ground has to be tested after confessional weather. “It appears to be an asset. Her husband has had it re-laid in the music room and keeps the complaint under the stairs, as a souvenir.”

“She sings?”

“She directs. Avidius pays the master before he pays certain relatives. The harbour calls it romance; Servilia calls it scheduling.”

“And Marcus?” Antonia asks, and hears how easily the circuit completes itself: young people distributed among adults like weather reports.

“Marcus is learning that a reply is not a campaign. He has taken up aqueduct Greek—*gradient, pressure, leakage*—and applies it to the family at dinner in ways his mother corrects.”

“Prima wrote four lines.”

“So I have been informed at third hand, through plumbing and a doorkeeper’s sense of theatre.” He picks up a fallen olive and puts it down without tasting it this time. Progress can be small and still be entered. “I have not read them.”

“Good.”

“I thought you might prefer direct evidence.”

“I prefer no trespass. It photographs better in the third book.”

The phrase surprises a real smile from him and, more dangerously, something like gratitude. Antonia refuses delivery by turning to practicalities: the press beam, Soter’s drainage crew, the autumn payment schedule, a clause in the estate papers ensuring seasonal workers are not made to lend tools as a feudal courtesy.

“You would incorporate my sentence into an olive estate,” Lentulus says.

“I would incorporate a receipt,” Antonia says. “The sentence has other employment.”

“Refusals, legible, among trees.”

“Do not let it go to the family head. A clause in an orchard is still only as good as the manager’s handwriting.”

He calls the manager over. Terms are amended on the tablet in the open air—not as moral legislation, which would require trumpets and a committee, but as line items. Tool hire paid. Harvest food uncharged. A woman in the press house to be named on the stores tally by her own name, not as her husband’s portable labour. Soter, summoned to witness drainage, hears this and regards the sky as if it were a document he is checking for forgery.

“The seasons will notice us less if we stop charging them rent,” he says.

It is the best political speech of the autumn. Antonia watches Lentulus make no move to own it.

“Your grandfather is filing it. Masks can't move; that's their whole tragedy and your family's business model.” She turns back toward the presses, the agent, the Greek's beautiful silences. “Replace the press, Lentulus. Your olives deserve machinery that isn't a metaphor. So, if we are itemising, do your children. All —” she lets the plural stand on the terrace in the autumn light — at length, in the open, between the press and the sea “— of them.”

That evening, in the villa’s plainest room—which Lentulus now uses because the grand dining room smells of three centuries of applause—they sign the estate amendments. The documents are read aloud by Diomedes because Antonia insists and because the manager’s voice tends to soften difficult numerals. Tool hire. Harvest food. Press replacement to begin when the vintage allows. A reserve for drainage. The press-house tally to carry women’s names where women have done the work, *for payment purposes*, a phrase which in Rome can revolutionise heaven if left in a dull enough hand.

The woman from the press house is present for her line. She signs with a mark and then, surprising everyone except Soter, writes her initials under it in letters learned from a son who had a season with a travelling grammarian. “For payment purposes,” she repeats, and makes it sound like a door.

Lentulus signs. Antonia signs. The Greek witnesses in the signature that has probably survived better governments. There is no applause. In the courtyard someone is washing a cart and singing a song about a mule with political opinions.

When the others have gone, Lentulus remains looking at the duplicate. “My father would have called this sentimentality with accountants.”

“Your father called many invoices character when he wanted them cheaper.”

“He did.” He lays the document flat under his hand, not as a seal but as a table thing. “When Cornelia asks—” He cannot yet put the afternoon visit, the apology or the plural into an order fit for a daughter who has her own music room. “When Servilia tells her, she may come to the Caelian.”

“She may come to a house that serves the public by appointment.” Antonia’s face does not change. “If she asks for me, Lentulus, it will be in her own hand. If she asks for her sister, that is Prima’s house and Prima’s door. I do not open it with your key.”

“No.” The word is immediate. Good. “No. I only wished to say the family will not send her as a message.”

“Your family is becoming positively postal. Letters, replies, refusals entered.”

“It is the new plumbing.”

“Then mind your pressure.” She takes her copy. Diomedes has already made a second. Rome can proceed with civilisation in duplicate.

Before she leaves the estate, the Greek places one more document on the table. It is not an amendment. It is a sealed instruction, to be held in his office against the possibility that Prima herself one day wants evidence of the terms under which the money moved and the olive paper began.

“No names are to be exhibited,” he says. “No use may be made of it without her hand. If she never asks, it becomes ash at the direction of your estate.”

Lentulus looks at the seal. “A cupboard, with discipline.”

“A room,” the Greek says, “of the smaller kind.”

Antonia turns the document once but does not open it. She knows exactly what this restraint costs Lentulus, who has begun the year with a family talent for staging the opening of cupboards and calling the smell history. “Prima has papers of her own,” she says. “Guild, oven, commission accounts. This is only if the day comes when she wants the joinery inspected.”

“Then the joinery waits,” Lentulus says. “Quarterly.”

“Now you are becoming fluent.” She returns the sealed sheet to the Greek. “Enter it where no son with a metaphor can find it by romance.”

“The office has a drawer for poetry-proof items.”

Diomedes, who has been making a final inventory of amphorae count agree with itself after three attempts, looks up. “By the staircase returns?”

“By the documents nobody is to be grateful for,” the Greek says. “A growing department.”

It settles something for Antonia that the apology could not. The future has been left unlocked from Prima’s side. No revelation is waiting with trumpets. Only a banker, a seal and a woman not yet in the room whose permission is, at last, structurally load-bearing.

They walk down between the trees at a proprietor's pace, two Romans in early middle age with a shared minority holding in each other's ruin and a settlement, finally, in the only currency that has ever cleared in this city: work done on the ground, in season, by name.
