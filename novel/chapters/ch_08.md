# Through the Tile Wall

*Warsaw, 26–27 March 1943*

Dogs changed the physics of a building.

You could forge an X-ray plate so neatly that three German military surgeons swore a bullet track in a boy's femur was childhood osteomyelitis; you could paste red *Typhus exanthematicus* warning strips across ward doors and watch SS clerks back hastily down the steps holding cologne-soaked handkerchiefs to their noses. An Alsatian trained at Oranienburg or Trawniki could not read Latin, did not fear lice, and smelled three hundred milliliters of spilled fresh human blood diluted under carbolic soap from forty yards downwind.

In the gypsum cellar beneath Theater Two, Sylwia blew out the shaded bucket-lamp? No—electric bulb, she unscrewed it half a turn in its brass socket with dry fingers until darkness dropped total, smelling hot tungsten cool in dusty air while overhead ten pairs of nailed boots divided left and right across the ground-floor vestibule.

"Upstairs first," Bronka breathed against Sylwia's ear, so close her warm breath smelled of raw citrate blood and roasted barley. "Always top floor down so nobody climbs onto roofs. Ten minutes—maybe twelve before they bring the dogs down the boiler chute."

Sylwia's mind moved through the brick floor-plan of Pavilion Three like steel probes through a familiar joint.

Two boys lying on planks in the dark at her knees. Sowa—twenty-two, conscious, femoral flesh track packed tight with rivanol, zero chest injury—could breathe sulfur dioxide and cresol vapor for half an hour without suffocating if Halina burned two sulfur disinfection candles in the typhus-delousing anteroom thirty feet down the foundation tunnel; burning sulfur seared nasal mucosa in dogs flat blind inside two sniffs so handlers yanked their leashes back rather than ruin expensive tracking noses.

Put eighteen-year-old Leszek—resected ninth rib, fresh diaphragmatic suture, bruised right lower lung lobe draining pink air-bubbles into a pickle-jar water seal—inside a sulfur-filled delousing box for five minutes, and bronchospasm tore his diaphragm stitches open and drowned him in his own blood before midnight.

Where in Pavilion Three couldn't Kroll's dogs poke their muzzles without tearing through Wehrmacht counter-intelligence tape?

Only one corridor on three floors: the six-room ground-floor east annex behind the stenciled *Reserviert — Wehrmacht-Notdienst* swing doors, where two armed military policemen from Saski Square answered to *Abwehrstelle Warschau* rather than Aleja Szucha.

And directly behind the head wall of Room Fourteen—accessible *not* from the guarded front corridor, accessible from the tiny narrow iron spiral dumbwaiter-and-linen stair rising out of the cellar wash-house because in nineteen-oh-two the Russian municipal architect had built shared plumbing stacks between pairs of private rooms—sat the locked, disused hydrotherapy sluice-closet of Room Thirteen, its hall door bolted from *inside* and blocked on the corridor side by stacked spare horsehair mattresses since forty-one!

Between that six-foot tiled closet and Ludwig Schoeler's iron headboard stood four inches of hollow glazed brick and one rusted brass Louvered air-grille six inches square, eight feet above the floor.

If Leszek made one loud groan while awake—Ludwig heard him.

Leave Leszek down here in the gypsum dust—Kroll's dogs tore his throat out in twelve minutes and hung Sylwia, Bronka, Mirecki, and Halina from balcony railings before dawn.

"Take Sowa down the tunnel to Halina right now," Sylwia whispered into the dark, already clamping Leszek's rubber chest-tube double with heavy Pean forceps so air couldn't suck backward into his pleura when she disconnected the pickle-jar seal. "Tell Halina light two sulfur pots in the outer louse-vestibule—stink the whole cellar ramp yellow so no handler brings an Alsatian past the boiler arch! Then meet me at the bottom of the dumbwaiter spiral in ninety seconds."

"Pani doktor—behind Fourteen's wall—"

"Move!"

Ninety seconds later two women—one forty-six and built like a slaughterhouse dray, one thirty-three and fueled by cold terror sharper than amphetamine—carried sixty-two kilos of limp, chloroform-drowsy eighteen-year-old boy vertical up fourteen narrow cast-iron spiral steps inside a pitch-black plumbing shaft no wider than a coffin stood on end.

Halfway up the spiral, through the cast-iron wall plate six inches from her scraped knuckles, Sylwia heard Kroll's polite Dresden voice out in the main ground-floor lobby instructing Docent Mirecki to unlock Operating Theaters One and Two and produce every surgical dressing book since noon! Down on the ground floor already—he had split his squad, half sweeping top wards downward while Kroll himself went straight for the operating theaters and basements!

At the top landing of the spiral shaft, Bronka slipped a greased skeleton key into the rear iron service latch of Room Thirteen's old sluice-closet. Hinges greased last month by Bronka for hiding black-market lard tubs swung inward without a squeak.

Pitch dark inside; smelled of dry drain-traps, old porcelain, and cold mortar. Barely five feet between the cracked sitz-bath tub and the glazed white brick partition wall—through whose upper brass air-grille filtered a faint rectangle of pale yellow electric light and the slow, regular scratch of a steel pen nib writing in the next room!

Ludwig was awake three feet away on the other side of four inches of brick.

Moving in dead silence, boots wrapped in burlap rags left over from lard sacks, Sylwia and Bronka eased young Leszek down onto two folded winter blankets inside the dry porcelain sitz-bath itself, propping his torso at thirty degrees so liver ooze pooled low rather than pressing his sewn diaphragm, and slipped the lower end of his unclamped intercostal rubber tube back under two inches of sterile boric water inside a flat glass urinal bottle wedged between his knees so not a bubble clicked loud.

Sylwia pressed two fingers against the boy's carotid in the pitch dark. Pulse one-twenty-four; shallow chloroform sleep wearing thin already. Dare she give him ten milligrams of morphine to keep him quiet? Give morphine on top of chloroform and half a liter of lost blood with one bruised lung, and respiratory depression killed him silently in the dark; give nothing, and when pleural raw nerve endings woke in ten minutes he coughed!

She drew five milligrams only—half an adult ampoule, judged pure by thumb-notch on the plunger barrel in blackness—slid the needle subcutaneously into his deltoid, laid four folded layers of dry surgical gauze loosely across his mouth and nose to muffle breath-whistle without starving him of air, and backed out down the spiral iron steps two seconds before heavy boots kicked open the cellar fire-door at the far end of the boiler passage.

By the time Sylwia stepped calmly through the ground-floor dispensary swinging doors buttoning a clean white coat over her wool dress, bare-eyed, blinking innocently at the corridor glare with Mirecki's narcotics ledger tucked open under her elbow—the basement below had erupted into furious canine barking cut abruptly short by violent coughing as sulfur dioxide rolled thick and yellow out of Halina's delousing vestibule up the boiler chute!

"Impossible down there without gas masks, *Herr Hauptsturmführer*!" an SD *Oberscharführer* reported up the cellar stairhead, eyes streaming red tears, holding a snarling, sneezing grey Alsatian back on short leather choke-chain. "The typhus woman's fumigating louse-coats with burning sulfur—dog won't put his nose within ten meters of the ramp, and there's nothing in the coal-bins or plaster room except gypsum barrels!"

Wait: *nothing in the plaster room except gypsum barrels*—Bronka had kicked clean gypsum dust across three dark drops of floor blood on her way out with Sowa thirty seconds ahead of the boots!

In the center of the tiled vestibule stood Reinhart Kroll, immaculate greatcoat unbuttoned over grey SS wool, peaked cap still square on sandy hair, white cotton left index finger stroking *scritch-scritch* across his right wrist cuff while Docent Mirecki trembled beside him holding three ward registers.

Kroll's pale, lashless eyes lifted slowly from the weeping dog handler to Sylwia emerging from the dispensary.

"Good evening again, Frau Doktor Kotarska," Kroll said pleasantly. "You work late on Fridays."

"Perforated duodenal ulcer in Theater Two at five-thirty, *Herr Hauptsturmführer*," Sylwia answered evenly, opening the Theater Two register under his nose where the fifty-year-old seamstress's name, address on Wspólna, and excised callous ulcer specimen—still floating dutifully in a formalin jar on the theatre shelf if he cared to smell stomach acid—stood entered in Mirecki's purple pencil. "She is upstairs in Female Post-Operative Bed Four under gastric suction if your surgeon wishes to examine abdominal stitches."

Kroll barely glanced at the purple ink. His nose wrinkled slightly at the acrid tang of sulfur drifting up from the basement stairs; then his gaze turned ninety degrees eastward down the ground-floor hall toward the closed swing-doors stenciled *Reserviert — Wehrmacht-Notdienst*, where the grizzled Pomeranian *Abwehr* military-police sergeant stood planted square across the middle of the passage with his thumb hooked over his MP40 sling.

"Every Polish bed upstairs accounted for against pre-curfew municipal *Kennkarte* stubs," Kroll murmured thoughtfully, as if conversing with himself in a law library. "Cellars full of typhus lice and sulfur smoke. Which leaves six ground-floor rooms behind those doors—five of them nominally unoccupied since ten February."

Mirecki swallowed audibly. "Under exclusive Wehrmacht counter-intelligence seal, *Herr Hauptsturmführer*, as *Hauptmann* von Berg's order on the door frame specifies—"

"Naturally I would not dream of disturbing *Abwehrstelle* papers," Kroll said with silk courtesy, already walking down the hall toward the swing-doors so Sylwia and Mirecki had no choice on earth except to walk behind him while two SD men and one dog handler—leaving the sulfur-blinded Alsatian tied to the vestibule radiator—followed at his heels. "However, four bandits escaped near Bielańska this evening bleeding heavily from machine-pistol fire after murdering German officers. Empty hospital rooms have windows facing Nowogrodzka courtyard, *Herr Feldwebel*, and municipal orderlies hold master keys. Unlock Rooms Ten through Sixteen for visual inspection."

The Pomeranian MP sergeant didn't budge an inch, jaw set like salted beef. "Rooms Ten, Eleven, Twelve, Fifteen, and Sixteen you may view from thresholds under my escort, *Herr Hauptsturmführer*. Room Thirteen is nailed shut for plumbing rot behind stacked mattresses, and Room Fourteen houses *Oberstleutnant* Schoeler under medical orders."

"I know whom Room Fourteen houses," Kroll said softly. "Let us look at the empty ones first."

Ten: bare iron bedframe, rolled mattress, cold radiator, window painted black and latched from inside with unbroken cobwebs in the corner.

Eleven: identical.

Twelve: stacked broken traction splints and six crates of empty mineral-water bottles.

Outside Room Thirteen, four dusty horsehair mattresses leaned stacked ceiling-high against the Bolted pine doorframe. One of Kroll's young SD *Rottenführer* shoved two mattresses aside with his boot and rattled the brass handle—solidly bolted from the *sluice-closet side* or painted shut since forty-one, dust flaking down off the transom in grey snow onto his sleeve.

"Behind Thirteen sits an old bath-closet backing onto Fourteen's plumbing stack," Kroll observed quietly—*he had read the municipal architectural blueprint at City Hall before driving here!* Sylwia's stomach turned cold without showing a ripple in her face as she stood two paces behind his grey shoulder. "Where does that plumbing stack open?"

"Service spiral down to the laundry cellar, locked since the pipes burst in the forty winter," Mirecki lied bravely, voice squeaking only slightly on *forty*, "and vented only into Room Fourteen's upper air shaft."

Kroll turned three paces right and knocked twice—*tap, tap*, white cotton knuckles polite against painted pine—on the door of Room Fourteen before pushing it inward without waiting for Mekelburg's bolt.

Mekelburg hadn't bolted it; the boy stood six feet inside the room with his good right hand hanging dangerously close to his holstered pistol flap until Ludwig, sitting half-propped in his hospital linen shirt with Thucydides lying closed on the blanket, raised two quiet fingers to still him.

"Forgive the late intrusion, *Herr Oberstleutnant*," Kroll said, stepping well inside the room until he stood four paces from the iron bed—and less than *seven feet* from the glazed white partition wall behind Ludwig's headboard, where eight feet up near the ceiling molding the rusted six-inch brass air-grille showed black square holes opening straight down into the pitch-dark porcelain tub where an eighteen-year-old boy with a sewn liver and bruised lung lay breathing through four layers of dry gauze!

Sylwia stepped inside the doorway to Kroll's left, forcing herself to look bored and mildly affronted rather than staring upward at that lethal six-inch square of rusted brass.

In the dead hush as Kroll paused to take in the sparse room—washstand, closed cupboard, chair, narrow bed—a faint, unmistakable sound came out of the upper plumbing wall.

Not loud. Just a wet, bubbling intake—*hrrk*—as five milligrams of light morphine failed to suppress pleural irritation from the intercostal rubber tube rubbing parietal pleura, and behind four inches of hollow brick a half-conscious boy drew breath to cough!

Kroll's sandy head tilted three degrees toward the headboard wall. His left cotton fingertip stopped scratching his wrist.

"Rats in your ventilation shaft, *Herr Oberstleutnant*?" Kroll murmured, taking one slow, deliberate step past the foot rail toward the head of the bed—three yards from the grille, ears cocked sharp as a terrier's in dead quiet—just as a second, deeper, unmistakable human rattle gathered behind the rusted brass louvers!

In the tarnished six-inch shaving mirror still hanging from the window latch across the room, Ludwig's winter-green eyes met Sylwia's for one tenth of a second.

She hadn't moved a lash, hadn't looked up at the grille—yet he had heard that wet pleural *hrrk* two seconds before Kroll walked in (any thoracic surgeon knew human bronchial blood-rattle from rats in half a note!), had heard muffled movement behind his headboard twenty minutes earlier when sulfur smoke hit the basement, and saw in one flash of dark glass across Room Fourteen that if Kroll stepped two paces closer when that boy coughed aloud, Sylwia Kotarska swung from a meat-hook by Sunday noon.

Ludwig didn't argue law with Kroll, and he didn't pretend to cough politely into a handkerchief.

With cold, shocking physical violence that made Sylwia's breath catch for real in her throat, Ludwig braced his bare left elbow hard against the iron vertical bar behind his pillow and *wrenched* his healing left shoulder girdle backward and upward at full leverage—tearing five weeks of fresh, vascularized red granulation tissue and newborn marginal epithelium straight apart across his resected collarbone gap in a single silent rip—while biting down savagely onto the inside of his own left cheek until bright arterial mouth-blood flooded across his tongue!

Simultaneously he doubled forward off the bolsters in a violent, strangled paroxysm of racking coughs loud enough to drown five men choking in a closet—hacking bright red blood mixed with saliva straight across the white hospital sheet and spattering three scarlet drops squarely onto the polished black toe-caps of Reinhart Kroll's jackboots while bright dark-red venous and capillary blood soaked rapidly outward in a spreading crimson palm-print across the left breast of his white linen shirt!

"Mekelburg—basin!" Ludwig choked out raw between genuine pain-spasms—nobody tore five centimeters of living chest granulation apart raw without going chalk-white to the lips and sweating ice inside two seconds!—slamming his blood-flecked right fist onto the mattress edge as Kroll stepped hurriedly *backward* two paces to save his immaculate grey trouser cuffs from infectious hospital blood.

Sylwia moved faster than thought, shoving straight past Kroll's white-gloved elbow to drop both hands onto Ludwig's heaving shoulders while shouting in furious, ringing Berlin German loud enough to cover anything short of cannon-fire behind the tiles:

"Out! Clear the room this second! *Bronka*—sterile tamponade packs and hemostatic clamps on the double! Didn't you read Stabsarzt Vesper's vascular hemorrhage warning on the door, *Herr Hauptsturmführer*?! Ten men tramping dust and sulfur fumes up the stairs set him coughing into a secondary granulation bleed over an open subclavian repair—get your troopers out of my doorway before he aspirates or blows the main trunk!"

Behind the tile grille, whatever cough had broken loose died completely unheard beneath Ludwig's rasping, blood-spitting paroxysm, Mekelburg's overturned wooden chair crashing across pine boards as the boy lunged for the enamel kidney dish, and Docent Mirecki bustling frantically forward at the door yelling in Polish down the hall for ice-bags and coagulen ampoules!

Kroll stood just outside the threshold now, looking down with fastidious Saxon distaste at the three bright red droplets drying dark across his left boot-toe while inside the room Sylwia ripped Ludwig's blood-soaked linen shirt open down the front, exposing five inches of freshly torn, furiously bleeding red chest flesh welling dark scarlet down his ribs onto the rubber sheet.

Nothing fake about *that* blood—real human granulation tissue bled like cut liver when ripped off underlying fascia, soaking two big gauze pads red through Sylwia's bare fingers in ten seconds flat!

"Unfortunate timing," Kroll said mildly from the hall, pulling a clean folded handkerchief from his skirt pocket—never using his white cotton gloves on leather—and stooping neatly to wipe his boot-cap clean before tossing the spotted square into Bronka's corridor slop-pail. "Pray convey my regrets to Stabsarzt Vesper for the disturbance to his patient, Docent Mirecki. Clearly nobody crawled through Room Fourteen's window tonight. Good recovery, *Herr Oberstleutnant*."

Ludwig didn't answer him—head bowed over the enamel basin held by Mekelburg, spitting one last mouthful of pink-streaked spit from his bitten inner cheek into the tin, breathing ragged and genuine through clenched teeth while sweat shone wet down his white temples.

Ninety seconds later, through the frosted courtyard glass, three six-wheeled SD lorries ground into first gear along Lindleya and roared away south toward Koszykowa and Aleja Szucha.

* * *

Not until quarter past one in the morning—after Leszek had been eased back down the dark spiral plumbing shaft into Theater Two's scrubbed recovery cubby under a dead seventy-year-old peasant's pneumonia chart, and Sowa had been driven safely out to Grochów under municipal night-soil barrels—did Sylwia come back through the quiet east-wing doors alone.

Both *Abwehr* MPs sat drowsing outside on their bench; inside Room Fourteen, Mekelburg had washed the blood off the floorboards and been sent next door into unlocked Room Twelve to sleep horizontal on stacked blankets for four hours after Sylwia swore on her mother's grave nobody from Szucha came back twice in the same night.

One shaded sixty-watt bulb burned low beside the washstand.

Ludwig lay back flat on one low bolster, naked to the waist under the grey wool blanket pulled up to his hipbones, eyes closed, right forearm flung across his forehead shielding his face from the bulb glare. Beneath her temporary tight pressure pad strapped at midnight, fresh dark red serum had seeped through one corner onto his skin.

Sylwia locked the hall door softly from inside, washed her hands for three full minutes in warm sublimate until the shaking that had waited patiently behind her knees since eleven-thirty finally consented to stop, and brought the boiled dressing tray to the left rail.

When she peeled the clotted midnight pad gently away with warm saline soak so it wouldn't tear a second time, Ludwig lowered his right arm off his eyes and looked at her.

In the quiet yellow light she saw the full damage he had done himself in half a second: three weeks of slow, painstaking pink epithelial creep round the margins ripped jaggedly back to raw dermis; a deep fissure split straight across the center of the granulating bed down to pectoralis minor fascia—thank heaven two millimeters shallow of her deep arterial bridge, which still beat strong and intact underneath, though another ten pounds of backward wrench might genuinely have avulsed a side-branch and killed him on the mattress. Inside his mouth, when he parted dry lips to draw breath, the left buccal mucosa was swollen dark purple where he had bitten clean into his own cheek artery to get instant bright mouth-blood onto Kroll's boots, because torn chest wounds didn't make you cough red foam on cue!

"Open your mouth," Sylwia said quietly, voice stripped bare of every sharp edge she normally carried into this room.

He opened his jaws half an inch without sarcasm. She shone her tiny pocket diagnostic torch inside: two-centimeter ragged bite laceration opposite the upper first molar, clotted firm now, no stitch needed if he rinsed with salt water.

She clicked the torch off, took warm boric sponges, and began cleaning dried crimson crust off his left ribs, sternum, and throat hollow with slow, feather-light strokes utterly unlike her brisk daytime ward wipe.

Neither of them spoke while she dusted sterile sulfanilamide snow across the torn granulation fissure, laid double Vaseline-tulle so nothing stuck raw, and strapped a fresh thick pad gently in place—adding five days, maybe seven, to his stay under Lindleya's roof with a single wrench of his own arm.

Only when she dipped a fresh linen corner in clean warm water to wipe a dried streak of mouth-blood off the corner of his jaw—her bare knuckles grazing the crisp gold-ash stubble along his chin—did his right hand rise quietly off the blanket and close round her forearm just below the rolled cuff of her white smock.

Warm fingers, dry now, zero fever, holding firm without crushing bone like last month's forty-point-four vise.

"How old is he?" Ludwig asked in a murmur barely louder than the radiator tick.

Sylwia didn't insult him—or cheapen what he had just ripped his own flesh open to cover—with one syllable of pretense.

"Eighteen," she whispered back in German just as low. "Right lower lung graze and torn liver dome. He'll live if pneumonia doesn't take him by Tuesday."

Ludwig's thumb moved once—barely two millimeters—against the bare inner skin of her forearm above her scarred wrist, testing the fast, hard hammer of her radial pulse through thin skin. Up close his grey-green eyes looked exhausted down to bone-ash, dark rings bruised under pale lashes, yet clear as winter stars.

"Move him out of this building before Sunday noon," he murmured. "Kroll wiped my blood off his boot with his bare pocket-handkerchief—not into the stove, into your nurse's corridor bucket, and I watched his rear man pick that handkerchief out of the pail on his way through the swing-doors."

Cold went straight through Sylwia's chest. *Blood-typing or microscopic check!* Mixed buccal squamous cells and saliva in coughed "lung" blood—any decent pathology lab at the State Institute of Hygiene or German Forensics on Oczki Street looking under a hundred-power lens saw flat mouth pavement-epithelium and zero alveolar macrophages inside five minutes, proving an *Abwehr* lieutenant-colonel had bitten his own cheek to clear a Gestapo search out of Room Fourteen!

"Wait—if Kroll puts that handkerchief under a microscope tomorrow—" Sylwia started, dropping three inches lower over the bed.

"Bronka dumped half a pint of lysol and sheep-broth slops into that bucket ten seconds before his man fished it out—I watched her through the glass panel," Ludwig said, the faintest ghost of grim respect touching the bruised corner of his mouth. "Your fat Wola matron has faster eyes than half Canaris's majors. Lysol lyses epithelial cells to mush inside twenty minutes. Kroll gets nothing provable—only certainty in his Saxon belly, which he had already."

For four seconds Sylwia stood motionless beside the bed with his warm fingers wrapped round her bare forearm and the smell of fresh blood, boric acid, and clean male skin filling every breath she drew. All her neat categories from Berlin and Warsaw—enemy, butcher, debt, ledger—lay smashed in pieces on the floor beside Mekelburg's chair. Ten days ago Marek had told her *Kontrwywiad* meant to machine-gun this man's car the morning he left Lindleya; two hours ago this man had torn his own chest raw and spat his own blood across SS leather so an eighteen-year-old boy from *Kedyw* wouldn't strangle on a Gestapo rope and Sylwia Kotarska wouldn't go down to Aleja Szucha's cellar.

"Why did you do that?" she whispered, hating how raw the words scraped out of her throat.

Ludwig looked straight up into her dark eyes—no smile at all now, grave, pitiless, ancient Berlin steel gleaming clear through ten years of ash.

"Don't go soft in the head, *Kotarska*," he said softly. "Nothing in Warsaw is free. You added a week to my granulation tonight; by the middle of April I still walk out of your hospital. And two days before I sign Vesper's discharge book, you are going to carry one small parcel ten blocks across town for me where Kroll's grey Adler cars don't watch Polish women doctors—and then our ledger from Table One and tonight sits square at zero."

Slowly she pulled her forearm backward; this time he let her skin slide cleanly out through his fingers without holding on, though the warm ring where his palm had rested burned up to her elbow all the way down the dark corridor.

At the door she paused with her hand on the light switch, looking back once across shadowed white linen at the lean outline of his unbandaged right shoulder and the pale crescent scar she had stitched in Schöneberg snow thirteen winters ago.

"Good night, Ludwig," she said—the first time she had spoken his given name awake since March nineteen-thirty-three—and clicked the room dark.
