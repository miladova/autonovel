Saturnalia Postponed

Winter lays its flat hand on the city: smoke, short light, the poor burning what the rich have renovated out of. Vibius the fuller declines in the manner of his trade, which is to say by water. The cough brings up the vats' whole history; the guild sends a physician who speaks kindly to Prima and uselessly to the disease; the baker's mother sends soup with inspections in it and finds, to her own surprise, that she is staying to administer both. She is a hard woman, but hardness in Transtiberim is just tenderness that has been charged by the hour for forty years, and Prima, watching her future mother-in-law wipe her father's chin with a cloth she herself has hemmed, revises a ledger she did not know she was keeping.

In Transtiberim the approach of Saturnalia is measured differently from the Palatine, where the festival arrives by invitation. Across the river it arrives by eaves: by the smell of other people's roasting drifting over the vats at the dinner hour, by the price of the little clay figures going up in inverse proportion to their quality, by masters in the fuller’s guild debating, as they debate every year with the enthusiasm of men discovering generosity as a climate, whether the holiday dole shall be in oil, in coin, or in the form of a speech. Prima's household measures it by her father's breathing at the fourth hour, when the yard gets its one bar of sun and the garments go up on the frames like a population being aired after a siege.

The cough has learned the house. That is how Prima thinks of it in the hours when thinking is a luxury after water and honey: it knows the step up to the vat-room that costs him, it knows the advantage of taking him halfway through a sentence so that other people finish it wrong, it knows the nights. His own account is different and delivered, on good mornings, like a trade report: “Chest's full of river this week. Trade will have its customs. We pay on the day.” Then he wants the bench by the frames, where he can supervise. A man who has held a household up for thirty years by being the still point of its washing does not retire to a bed without the household conspiring in his fiction. The guild boys bring him the dry garments to inspect. He inspects them with his thumbs, the customs officer's bequest to the whole bank of the river, and pronounces on rinses at a distance like a rich man on morals.

The guild physician is Nikias' poor relation by profession rather than blood: a younger Greek with a travelling case and a manner he is still learning to spend appropriately in rooms where money is soup-thin. He speaks kindly to Prima because Prima asks the questions in an order that allows him to answer them, and uselessly to the disease because the disease has never in its history attended a consultation. He prescribes the honey, thyme in the steam, rest — that palace word, issued to the poor like a lottery ticket — and, at the door, with the air of a man telling the truth in its overcoat, he says to her, “Keep him warm past the festival, fuller’s daughter. Winter is the tax. Spring sometimes remits.”

She pays him out of the Caelian money for the spring hangings, itemised recently to a fare-thee-well, and he takes it with an embarrassment that tells her the guild has already mentioned his fee in full. She says nothing. In this neighbourhood, where Antonia Cassia's kindness arrives by a plumbing of entitlements and suppliers, saying nothing is also a talent being purchased somewhere upstream. She has begun — third treacherous comparison, unfiled — to recognise good plumbing when it doesn't splash.

Mother Livia's soup comes on Mondays and, increasingly, on other days that have had to be renamed Mondays for the purpose. She brings it herself because (a) Titus at the ovens is careless with liquids and (b), never to be minuted at the guild women’s rota, because she has discovered in Vibius's sickroom a quality her own male household, with its flour and its second columns, does not supply: an audience that cannot get up. She tells him the neighbourhood. She tells it thoroughly, with names, because names in Transtiberim are news and news, dosed correctly, is medicine that doesn't taste of honey. She tells him which of the tanners' widows has taken a young husband and what the guild thinks of the wool trade, and what the priest at the island temple charged a bargee for a vow, and Vibius receives it all with his eyes half shut like a senator at a debate he has already decided, and says, at intervals that prove he has been following the items like an auditor: “That one's lying.” “That one pays.” “That one'll be filed by spring.”

It is doing something to Prima's accounts of the woman, this discovery of her future mother-in-law in a posture the contracts never itemised: on her knees at a sick man's fire, skimming soup with one hand and conducting the street with the other. Between them, over Vibius's bowl, the two women have arrived at the form of government their men call, in cleaner rooms, an understanding. The dowry has not been mentioned in a fortnight. The reading has been promoted, in Mother Livia's reported speech at the bakehouse, from defect to “that business with the second columns; a girl who finds money in columns can find weevils in flour.” Weevils, Prima understands, are the producing class’s poetry.

Saturnalia arrives round them all the same, without asking the house's permission. It inverts the world at the proper addresses: in Transtiberim it mostly clarifies it. Masters serve their slaves a meal and expect, by January, compound gratitude. The guild gives its dole in oil, the speech having been voted, mercifully, into a cask. Titus bakes the festival loaves with his sleeves at the senatorial stripe and brings Prima one shaped, by a baker's grandson learning the trade on her betrothal, like a flower with too many petals, which Vibius inspects with his thumbs and pronounces “a fair attempt at your border, girl, in bread. Let no one register it.” For half an hour the sickroom is a room with laughing in it, and the cough — that tenant who has learned the house — sits politely in the corner all tenants reserve for the family furniture, and Prima keeps the laughter's accounts exactly, the way her mother kept stairs: not because she thinks a count will change the sum, but so the sum will not change her.

Antonia hears of the decline from Eirene, who hears of it from Barbatus, who has started walking Prima to the crossing on his own time and bringing back, with the emptied lantern, the news of the street the way other men bring back winnings. Antonia does the things that do not constitute interference in the filings of her conscience: the guild pension enquiries discover a forgotten entitlement; honey of a quality that needs no discussion arrives from a supplier; the physician's second visit is paid before the first has been billed. She stops there. She has built a life out of knowing where a kindness becomes a claim, and she will not begin her motherhood — the word arrives and is set down carefully, like a full glass carried across her own courtyard — as a purchase.

His reports become a feature of the Caelian mornings, filed between the carter's weather and Diomedes' first book. “Fuller was sitting by the frames.” “Fuller asked after the price of wool, mistress, and called the guild physician a Greek theatre.” “Fuller's not beaten, but the house is heating honey at hours that suggest the night staff ain't sleeping.” Barbatus delivers them standing, out of a habit older than his door, and Antonia receives them standing too, whatever room it is, a discipline her household begins, without being told, to imitate at the edges: Eirene stops arranging; Diomedes stops writing. Eleven years of staff know when information has a face.

The things that do not constitute interference are arranged with a care that would shame a foreign ministry. Diomedes, instructed to proceed as if buying thread, discovers through the guild chest that Vibius's dues purchase a physician's second visit *where decline is chronic and the householder of good standing*, a clause sleeping in the guild's papers since a consulship nobody at the vats can name. It is woken up. It is minuted. No name of the waker appears. Honey arrives at a fuller's house in winter from a supplier whose cousin works Antonia's kitchens; this is Rome; cousins are a currency. A warmer for the sickroom is conveyed by the guild's common stock because the common stock discovers, in the frost, a surplus. Antonia governs the whole campaign the way she governs a banquet for Alexandrians: from the seating. Nothing is placed where its donor would have to be looked at.

“You could simply give them money,” Eirene says, once, trimming the lamps down in the small hours when the house's Saturnalia has done its worst and best. The great room has hosted the festival in its professional form: masters pretending to serve, slaves permitted to be witty within quotations, everybody paying Antonia for an evening of inversion staged so competently that no hierarchy was actually harmed in the performance. A senator's freedman won at dice. A senator lost with the grace of a man whose grandfather is on a mask across the city saying the same. Daphne sang, for the set that is hers now by custom and contract, the slow street song at full speed, as a holiday, and made the room laugh in all the right places like a fortune returning.

“I could give them money. I could walk across the bridge with it in a bag marked *Caelian*. The neighbourhood would have the story by the watch-change: the mistress buys the fuller's daughter for a mushroom. By January it's a scandal. By the hanging's border it's her reputation unhemmed and his pride the poorer. No.” Antonia watches a servant — allowed at Saturnalia to answer back, doing it with the restraint of a man who has read his contract's new clause in his own hand at New Year and is rich now in a way the festival cannot lend him — refuse a guest's third undiluted cup with a bow. “Kindness has to travel like their other news, Eirene: through the guild, through cousins, through the plumbing. If it arrives with my name on it, it arrives as a claim. I know about claims. I have a committee's worth of education in claims, and I paid fees.”

Eirene trims. “And the clause you wrote somewhere yourself — between the quarters — the one about debts in deathwatches?”

“That one is family law. We don't minute it.” But she is looking, as she says it, at the box in Diomedes' office, where, between tax receipts, are now filed: an unsigned note that taught her a phrase; a posted text with a law in it; a December bill marked settled in copy; and nothing at all, in any hand, from across the river, because the fourth set of books is being kept where it has always been kept, and some mornings at thirty-eight, after a Saturnalia at full price, holding it feels like carrying a full glass very slowly across a courtyard she owns, among roses, in the sight of gods whose inspectors make their rounds by appointment. The word arrives. She sets it down. It does not spill. That is both the achievement and its whole cost, and Diomedes, entering with the day-book, tactfully prices something else.

She goes herself, once. Not as the mistress of the Caelian consulting a tradeswoman; veiled, in a cloak Eirene calls her widow-inventing cloak, with Barbatus at a distance that suggests he is escorting someone else entirely. The fuller's house is two rooms and a lean-to over the vats' yard, and it is ordered with the ferocious neatness of people who know disorder is the first luxury they will be blamed for. Vibius is propped against the wall-bed, grey as undyed wool, and he looks at Antonia for a long moment with eyes that the trade has made professionally sceptical about the true colour of anything.

“The thread lady,” he says.

“The house on the Caelian,” Prima says, flustered, “has come about the hangings for spring, Father. I told her you were to rest.”

“I am resting. It's my busy season.” Vibius's breath goes in like something being wrung. “Sit, mistress, if a stool is not beneath you. Prima, put water on. The mistress drinks—” he looks at Antonia again “—watered wine and not much of it. Working women.” A pause, which the cough fills in its own handwriting. “My wife worked in Baiae.”

“Prima told me. Stairs.”

“Ninety. She counted everything so it wouldn't count her.” The old man is holding the edge of his blanket the way men hold a rail in weather. “She kept what she was given to keep, my wife. Garments. Money. People.” His eyes do not leave Antonia's face. “We were decent people in the way that matters, I hope. We continued after the paying stopped being the reason. Twelve years after. A long soak, mistress. Most things come clean if you don't rush it.”

“Ninety was the grand house, on the point,” Vibius says, correcting a legend with a sick man's priorities. “There was a smaller, by the shore road, sixty-four and a turn where the water slopped if you counted wrong. Claudia did the shore house the last winter. She came up with the dealer's party in the spring and I saw her at the island market with a tally in her hand, counting a porter's load for him because he'd got his figures in the second column wrong.” He intends this as a joke for the room, and it becomes, on its way to Prima, something she will keep in her work-box forever: her mother, newly in Rome, doing sums at a market stall; a fuller three weeks freed watching with a plan arriving in him like weather off a river. It is the first courtship scene her parents’ marriage has ever been granted in her hearing, and it is being told to a veiled woman on a stool while the kettle wastes its breath at the hearth.

“I told this house you were to rest,” Prima says, to the water, furiously.

“Rest is being reported. I'm filling in the between-times.” Vibius's eyes go back to the visitor, who is holding herself like a person carrying something full across his room. “She could price a household by its linen, Claudia could. 'You can tell a house by what it sends out dirty,' she'd say. 'And a woman by what she's sent out with.' She had the sayings filed like a guild clerk, mistress. It's where the girl gets them. Not from me. I only kept things clean.”

Antonia hears *It's where the girl gets them* arrive and settle in the second column. There is no expression available to a widow-inventing cloak for the information that the sayings have two mothers too, so she makes none. She sits on the offered stool, in the posture of a woman consulting a tradesman about spring hangings, while Prima performs at the hearth the rites of water already hot, and the room's whole archive — bench, wall-bed, tools on a bench, a hanging folded face inward over a chair, the festival loaf's successor under a cloth, a bowl of honey dressed with thyme like medicine making an effort — conducts its silent examination of her. She has been inspected by aediles, prefects, committees and one publisher of cohorts of younger men. A fuller's two rooms, ferociously neat, are the most thorough tribunal she has faced in Rome. It is also, she understands with a clarity that arrives without roadworks, the first room she has been in for twenty years that her younger self would have known how to work in straight off: heat the water, count the stairs, hold the mirror steady, be furniture until somebody's arithmetic promotes you.

“Your daughter's work is known across the river,” she says, because work is the passport she has brought and it is not false. “A contractor's wife tried to order the same border from a man at the Forum who copies. He brought me his copy to admire. The flowers were all the same.”

Vibius smiles into the blanket's edge he is holding like a rail. “That's how you know it's not hers. Prima's won't march in step if you pay them extra. Her mother taught her that out of the leaves — no, girl, the water's done, cease fire — she taught her that a made thing should keep the maker's weather in it somewhere. For the reading, later.” He coughs then, properly, the whole house rearranging itself around the sound — prima donna of this winter's programme — and Prima is there with the cup and the cloth, and Antonia is on her feet without having given the order, like staff, like (her body says before she can file it) family. She steadies the bowl. That is all. Bowl-steadying is not in any set of books. Her hands have been holding things steady for other people's houses since she was nine years old, and here is her hand, bare of rings for the Basilica and the sickroom alike, doing it in a fuller's lean-to for a man who knows exactly who she is and has decided, at the pace of water, not to file it anywhere the tax office reads.

Prima is at the hearth with her back turned, very busy with water that is already hot. Antonia understands that she is being given, by a dying fuller in a two-room house across the river, the exact respect of not being named. She repays it in kind: “The guild speaks well of this house. The spring hangings will want a firm hand. Your daughter's dowry, I am told, is to include her tools. My house will commission her trousseau work at her own prices. It is how we file respect, across the river. Quarterly.”

Vibius closes his eyes. A smile moves under the greyness like a fish in the Tiber, briefly visible, unquestionably there. “Then we're all filing the same thing, mistress. In different books.”

On the walk back, Barbatus maintains his fiction of escorting nobody until they are at the bridge, where he says, to the river, in the voice men use for the fights they are remembering instead of the ones they won: “In the arena we had a word for a man who holds the rail and jokes. We called him *not beaten yet*. The fuller's not beaten.”

“No,” Antonia says. “He is being succeeded. It is different, and worse, and nobody makes songs about it. Which is why women write the accounts instead.”

The river in winter dusk is doing its old work of carrying the city's lights away in pieces too small to invoice. On the island the temple lamps are being lit for a god who specialises, if Barbatus's theology is reliable, in the illnesses of people who have already been given up by more fashionable addresses. A barge goes down unlit. Ice is thinking about the banks and not committing itself, like a committee. Antonia walks beside the fiction of her escort with her veil still at the widow-inventing angle and thinks about water, because in her life everything she is not ready to think about directly files itself under water first.

She thinks about Baiae, which is to say she does not, professionally, for whole minutes at a time. The sea there has a different manners from a river: it gives things back altered and refuses to discuss the post. Rome's river keeps them. Twenty years ago a steward put a child into the current of other people's decency and charged the house for the mileage, and the current — unlike any Cornelius, unlike a certain dealer, unlike (she will be exact, in the second column, where it matters) herself, standing frozen with a basket on a landing learning how arrangements sound — the current carried the parcel home. Fullers. Dues paid in bad winters first. A guild book that writes *arrivals* where the Senate would write claims. It is not that the city is kind. The city is a market. But markets, at the unglamorous level where the plumbing runs and women file kindnesses as oil and thread, perform a kind of laundering that brings some things out cleaner than their owners intended.

“I could use a word,” Barbatus says to the river, resuming his career as a furniture of speech. “In the old house, in the Subura days of the mistress, there was a laundress told fortunes off wet cloth. She'd lay a sheet out smoking and read the steam. Rubbish, mostly, but she read *people* off their laundry better than any priest off an offal. Said the stains knew before the owners did.”

“And did she read me?”

“She read the house. She said the stairs would outlive it. We've kept the stairs at the Caelian house in excellent repair, mistress, but I take her meaning at this distance to've been methods.” He stops at the bridge's crown, where his escort duties officially evaporate, and looks back at Transtiberim going into its lamplight: vats settling, frames down, a physician's boy trotting with a case, somebody's daughter heating honey in a room two doors and one whole world away. “Fuller'll want his say in the naming of things before—” He evaluates the phrase and leaves it in the river. “Men do. Even them as files everything at the back. Something to look to, mistress, at the guild's usual hours.”

At home that night the Saturnalia debris has been converted, by staff who are no longer required to find it amusing, into the ordinary evening. A guest's luck-piece has been left on a couch, to be entered in the Guests' Property box and claimed at dusk in a panic of discretion. Ammonius is proposing, for the Alexandrians' return, a sauce that will require Antonia's second word again and receiving it with a dignity that suggests he has finally understood that *again* is this house's word for faith. She stands in the office doorway watching Diomedes enter the day in three hands and thinks: I have held a dying man's soup bowl steady with the hand that held the mirror at seventeen, in a house that sold the mirror's holder, and nobody in that room charged anybody for history. It is not forgiveness. She does not trade in forgiveness, whose market her childhood cornered and ruined. It is better and smaller and will keep like money. It is an unpaid thing, done in the right house at last.

She does not know yet in what hour of what night she will sit in a linen room with a daughter arrived and tell it in the order of the evening while a needle makes its small red row between them like a frontier being gardened. She knows the conditions. Conditions are her department: first the dying at the pace of water, then the mourning at its proper tariff, then — not a door held open by strangers, not a father drafting her life into two names, not the baths — a woman she already trusts with a needle, who is herself, which is the terrible and sufficient clause. Late, the family opens things late. She will open this one, unlike a certain will in a sticking cupboard, *on time*: a concept she intends, at thirty-eight, to teach the Cornelii at her usual rates, quarterly, with the thread itemised separately.

The days after develop their own winter offices. Prima comes to the Caelian on the Thursday, because the spring hangings are work and work, in her street, is how families inform the world that nobody has died yet. She brings the finished border for inspection — inspection now being a word with soup in it, per the house's arrangements — and Antonia, who has commissioned cloth from half the looms in Rome, receives it in the small room off the courtyard where the light is, with Eirene in attendance and Diomedes' stylus performing the liturgy of by-the-piece. Prima's face has the winter in it. It also has, at nineteen, the thing her mother bequeathed under *Kept* and her other circumstances bequeathed under nothing at all: the refusal to let a roomful of richer people tell her how to hold her own material.

“The count's off by three flowers at the return,” she says, before anyone can be kind at her. “I did them in again at my own prices. It's entered.”

“Show me the stitch that doesn't remember itself,” Antonia says, and it is out before the drill can stop it: not the phrase she audits other people's work with, but the one from a rainy night in a mending room, with a senator's heir getting rained on in the doorway of his education. Prima looks up. Her chin tucks a fraction. Her mouth is open on no number at all.

“Daphne's strap,” Prima says. “I remember.” She turns the border's underside to the light. “Nothing is ever that mended, mistress. Only the person who did it knows. To everyone else it's just the straight of the thing.” She ties off a thread that does not require tying, a fuller's daughter's certainty: *she will be told in this order*. “Father says to thank the house for the soup inspection system. He says he's being governed by women in two constituencies now and the empire's the better for it.”

“Tell him—” Antonia finds, in the third book's cedar-dark behind her ribs, a dozen sentences, all addressed to the wrong person: apologies, explanations, a report of a death falsely filed, a name. The drill, rehearsed like fire procedures, holds. “Tell him the house is governed the same way and charges more for it.”

She does not touch Prima's work with her mother's hands. She pays for it with a creditor's. The difference, God and the guild help her, is where she currently lives.

Barbatus's lantern does the Thursday dusk as escort furniture. On the bridge Prima says — and this will come back to the Caelian in the emptied lantern, faithfully, in a doorkeeper's Latin — that the house smells of roses even in winter, and that she dreams now of courtyards; of a woman counting coins in the dark, getting it wrong, beginning again. Barbatus reports it to Antonia in the doorway without interpretation, because interpretation is what kills messengers in every other sort of household, and Antonia stands a long moment among the evening's resuming preparations with her hands doing nothing at all for the first recorded time that winter.

“Get it right tomorrow, then,” Eirene says from somewhere near the lamps, in the tone of a woman announcing soup. “Cream before the Alexandrians, love. And your counts generally.”

She tells Prima nothing that day, and nothing the next, because a fuller is dying at the pace of water and a girl is heating honey and counting stairs so they do not count her, and because Antonia Cassia, who has waited nineteen years inside a false report of a death, can wait until a true one has had its mourning. There are debts you do not collect during a deathwatch, whatever the currency. She has written the clause somewhere herself. Between the quarters.
