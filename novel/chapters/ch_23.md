# Corpse Number Seven

*Warsaw, 7–8 September 1943*

At four minutes past ten on Tuesday morning the seventh of September, ninety seconds of ferocious submachine-gun fire tore open the sunny autumn air south down Marszałkowska toward Zbawiciela Square—not two pistol cracks and running boots, sustained Sten and Thompson bursts answered by German MP40s and the flat *whump* of a hand-grenade blowing plate-glass shopwindows across tram-rails at Litewska corner.

By quarter to eleven, when the first shot-up *Schutzpolizei* patrol car screamed past Chałubińskiego trailing blue radiator steam, Bronka came up from the vegetable gate carrying news blacker than April smoke:

Boys with violin-cases and briefcases had ambushed **SS-Oberscharführer Franz Bürkl**—deputy commandant of Pawiak prison, famous across Warsaw for setting his Alsatian dog onto pregnant women's bellies in cellars—walking his dog with wife and child outside Litewska Four, riddling Bürkl and six accompanying SS men dead on the pavement before roaring off down Mokotowska without losing a single car!

Inside Pavilion Three's instrument room Sylwia stood staring down at Stabsarzt Vesper's signed blank green *Sanitätsabteilung* rural-movement voucher lying ready inside her pocketbook—filled out last night for Halina Różańska to board the six-fifteen evening steam train from Dworzec Główny to Kielce five hours from now.

"Every railway gate at Główny, Wschodni, and Zachodni just dropped double SS *Sonderfahndung* chains," Bronka whispered grimly, wiping cabbage-water off her red forearms onto her apron. "Walenty's nephew from the tram depot says nobody under sixty gets onto a platform today without Aleja Szucha clerks telephoning parish registries on every *Kennkarte* number."

Sylwia shut her eyes for two seconds to clear noise out of the equation.

At eight o'clock *tomorrow morning*—Wednesday the eighth of September, thirteen hours from sunset—Reinhart Kroll's *Erkennungsdienst* team arrived at Pavilion Three's basement laboratory specifically to audit the fingerprints and Łowicz parish baptismal extract of auxiliary bacteriologist **"Irena Kwiatkowska."**

Why hadn't Sylwia sent Halina out to Kielce five days ago right after Palais Brühl? Because Dawid Różański down in Konstancin woods had spiked high recurrent relapsing fever on September first requiring Halina's typed cross-matched convalescent serum harvested over four days from guinea-pigs, finished only last evening at nine!

Now look at the trap jaws closing from both ends of the street:
1. Put Halina onto a train tonight with Bürkl's blood still wet on Litewska pavement, and SS platform police catch her before Grochów.
2. Hide Halina alive inside some Warsaw attic tonight so tomorrow morning at eight o'clock "Panna Kwiatkowska" simply *fails to report for work*—and Reinhart Kroll needs zero further proof that somebody tipped Pavilion Three off! By eight-thirty Kroll arrests Acting Chief *Dr. Sylwia Kotarska* and invalid *Docent Tadeusz Mirecki* straight out of his third-floor cardiac bed for harboring a fugitive Jewish bacteriologist, circulates Halina's nineteen-forty-one *Kennkarte* photograph to every *szmalcownik* and police post in the Generalgouvernement, and tears Pavilion Three down brick by brick!

Only one door on earth stopped a police manhunt dead at eight-zero-five tomorrow morning while clearing both hospital and fugitive from every search bulletin in Poland:

When SS clerks walked through Lindleya's gate at eight a.m., **"Irena Kwiatkowska" had to be lying stone-dead inside Pavilion Three's contagious typhus morgue**, killed overnight by the very occupational terror Germans respected above God Himself—fulminant laboratory *Fleckfieber*!

Sylwia walked fast down the vaulted cellar ramp and rapped three-and-one on the iron *Fleckfieber* door.

Halina already had two small cardboard suitcases packed behind her incubator cabinet, wearing her grey moth-eaten cardigan despite mild September air, face calm and pared down to pure dark flint. "Trains are shut after Litewska, aren't they?"

"Tighter than drums," Sylwia said, locking the iron door behind her. "And if you simply vanish off tomorrow morning's roster when Kroll's clerks arrive at eight, Kroll puts your photograph on every street-post from here to Cracow and hangs Mirecki before lunch. You don't vanish tonight, Halina—you die at twenty minutes to ten this evening in Infectious Isolation Cubicle Four."

Halina blinked once below her chamomile-and-peroxide yellow parting. "Die inside what skin? Even dumb SS clerks count bodies in coffins."

"Thirty minutes ago Infectious Ward Six over at Pavilion One sent down three fresh pauper typhus deaths from Wola for tomorrow's municipal lime-lorry," Sylwia said, voice dropping flat and clinical as Philippstraße dissecting-hall slate. "Corpse Number Seven on the stone cooling-slab right now is registered as *Anna Nowak, unmarried seamstress, aged twenty-nine, evicted lodger from Górczewska, zero surviving relatives in Warsaw*. Five feet three inches, forty-nine kilos, dark brown eyes, narrow build, confluent purple day-eleven typhus petechiae from collarbones to ankles."

Halina's sharp scientist's mind leaped ahead down the checklist faster than ordinary mortals breathed, ticking fatal discrepancies off on thin fingers:

"One: my hair's bleached ash-yellow down to half-inch dark roots; hers will be brown or black."

"You have twenty-volume hydrogen peroxide and concentrated ammonium hydroxide sitting on that shelf behind your elbow—dead keratin bleaches twice as fast under heat-lamps because nobody screams when ammonia burns scalp."

"Two: Kroll's *Erkennungsdienst* clerk carries ten-print ink rollers to compare corpses against municipal *Kennkarte* right-index thumbprints filed at City Hall in forty-one!"

Sylwia's eyes glinted cold black beneath the low tin microscope shade. "Only when right-index epidermis still owns papillary ridges. Suppose yesterday afternoon during Litewska's siren panic Panna Kwiatkowska dropped a boiling two-liter Pyrex flask of eighty-percent liquefied crystalline carbolic phenol across her right hand and right cheek while autoclaving virulent *Rickettsia prowazekii* louse-guts—inhaling concentrated rickettsial aerosol into raw pharyngeal mucosa on top of latent prodromal typhus while third-degree phenol eschar cooked all five right-hand finger-pads white and featureless down to corium?"

Halina stared at her for three stunned seconds—then let out a single breathless, incredulous whistle between her teeth. "Fulminant toxic typhus myocarditis rarely kills inside thirty hours from aerosol alone—you'll have to chart five days' concealed ambulatory fever she hid from you out of stubbornness until collapsing after yesterday's phenol spill!"

"Five days' concealed fever is already half-inked in my head—every lab worker hides thirty-nine degrees so Vesper doesn't dock pay," Sylwia said ruthlessly. "What else sits on your forty-one *Kennkarte* physical marks line?"

Halina pulled the grey card out of her skirt pocket and opened flap two: *Besondere Kennzeichen: Narbe am rechten Unterbauch (Blinddarm).* Special marks: appendectomy scar right lower quadrant.

"Does Anna Nowak have a McBurney scar?" Halina asked quietly.

"No. Unscarred belly."

Cut fresh post-mortem skin with a scalpel tonight and any police medical orderly glancing under a shroud saw gaping unhealed white fat instead of a nine-year-old white scar—UNLESS nobody looked at a typhus abdomen when four hundred livid purple Weigl louse-feeder stipples sat crusted on the right thigh *and* a ruptured turpentine fixation-abscess bandage covered McBurney's point, OR better still: how did mortuary embalmers fake old linear scars in criminal Berlin? No—collodion! Pinch a six-centimeter diagonal fold of pale right-iliac skin together with buried subcuticular white linen thread, paint flexible *Collodium elasticum* blended with zinc-oxide white powder smooth across the depressed white crease until under dim morgue lamplight it formed an exact sunken, shiny pearl-white ten-year-old McBurney scar unless someone sliced it open with a histologist's razor—and no German SS clerk alive bent his nose six inches over a stink-bloated, louse-spotted typhus belly in September heat to slice old appendectomy scars!

"Bring your peroxide, ammonia, Weigl box-straps, and last week's worn stockings over to the contagious dead-house at eleven tonight," Sylwia ordered quietly. "Bronka's cousin Jurek drives the municipal disinfection steam-van out through Ochota barrier at five-thirty tomorrow morning under Vesper's signed sanitary pass—taking one 'shaved convalescent typhus scrub-woman' in deloused grey sackcloth down to Grójec orchard kitchens, five kilometers from Dawid's pines."

Halina looked down at her grey *Kennkarte*—*Irena Kwiatkowska*, the paper skin that had kept her breathing twenty-six months while parents, aunts, cousins, and sixteen-year-old Rachela turned to smoke and lime—and nodded once without a tremor. "Eleven o'clock."

* * *

Nothing in Dante's *Inferno* smelled quite like a windowless brick infectious dead-house at midnight in early September when city ice-deliveries failed.

Six unpainted pine coffins lay ranged across wet concrete trestles under two yellow paraffin storm-lanterns hung from iron ceiling hooks; bluebottle flies sluggish with night cool crawled silently along red-painted *FLECKFIEBER* stencils until Bronka shut the outer iron ventilator and lit a shallow pan of crude cresol tar by the threshold—not enough to choke living lungs, enough to make tomorrow morning's visitors hold silk handkerchiefs clamped tight over both nostrils from thirty yards off.

On Zinc Cooling-Slab Two lay twenty-nine-year-old Anna Nowak from Górczewska.

Sylwia stood thirty seconds silent at the head of the slab before anyone touched hair or cloth—looking down at the sunken, peaceful, famine-hollowed face of a Warsaw seamstress whose rough left index finger bore fifty tiny black needle-pricks from sewing German uniform linings at twelve groszy an hour until one infected body-louse ended four winters of cold soup. Nothing Sylwia did to this quiet shell tonight hurt Anna Nowak; under Anna Nowak's own name tomorrow afternoon Bronka had already paid three hundred złoty to the cemetery chaplain at Bródno to read real burial prayers over Rachela's neighboring turf so the seamstress didn't go unprayed-for under borrowed ink.

"Let's work," Sylwia said softly.

Ninety minutes of macabre, diamond-hard precision moved without one wasted syllable among the three women.

First, hair: Halina mixed twenty-volume hydrogen peroxide two-to-one with strong liquid ammonia and a pinch of soap-flakes in a porcelain bowl, brushing the foaming caustic paste down Anna Nowak's dark chestnut hair starting *six millimeters out from the scalp*—preserving exact half-inch dark roots along the center part!—and holding a shielded tin reflector lamp six inches overhead for twenty-five minutes until wet strands lifted to that unmistakable harsh, slightly brassy wartime ash-blond every corridor sentry at Pavilion Three knew by sight, rinsed clean with chamomile water and dried with hot towels tucked round the ears.

Second, thighs and belly: while Bronka dressed the corpse's ankles in Halina's own darned brown woollen stockings, Sylwia ran a fine buried subcuticular white silk gather six centimeters diagonal across McBurney's point in the right iliac fossa, drawing dead dermis inward into a neat puckered trough, dusted trace talc-and-zinc into two layers of drying *Collodium elasticum* brushed feather-thin along the crease—under yellow storm-lantern glare from three feet away, even Bronka squinted twice and swore she was looking at a childhood appendectomy scar! Six inches lower on the anterior right thigh, using a bundle of six fine entomological pins bound round a cork and dipped in dark hemolyzed venous blood, Sylwia stippled two neat rectangular matchbox grids mimicking four weeks of crusted Weigl louse-feeding wheals.

Third—ugliest and indispensable—fingerprints and right cheek:

Sylwia put on thick rubber post-mortem gloves, uncorked eighty-eight-percent liquefied *Acidum carbolicum liquefactum*, and pressed phenol-soaked cotton pads firmly for four minutes across all five distal finger-pulps of the corpse's right hand, plus a splash-track across right palm, wrist, and right malar prominence where a bursting flask would splatter an operator leaning over an autoclave! Dead epidermis coagulated dense, opaque parchment-white and wrinkled featureless inside three minutes flat—destroying every papillary ridge beyond ten-print ink rolling while filling the whole stone room with authentic, overpowering hospital carbolic stench on top of cresol!

Last, Halina unbuttoned her own familiar moth-eaten grey cardigan—the cardigan every orderly, Vesper's clerk, and Kroll's January gate-watchers had seen "Panna Kwiatkowska" wear winter and summer—slipped it gently onto Anna Nowak's stiffened arms over a clean hospital smock, tucked a broken glass Pasteur pipette smear-sheet into the pocket record, and lifted the body with Sylwia and Bronka into Pine Coffin Four.

Then Bronka sat Halina down on an upturned wooden crate beside the zinc sink and took out Walenty's steel barber clippers.

*Zzzzzzt. Zzzzzzt.*

Long locks of brass-yellow-and-black hair tumbled silent onto wet concrete round Halina's shoes until her narrow skull sat cropped quarter-inch short like every female typhus survivor discharged bald from St. Stanislaus ward Six! Rubbing walnut-hull stain diluted in weak tea lightly round Halina's pale eye-sockets and temples to give peasant sallow sun-tan, wrapping a coarse grey unbleached linen kerchief low across her brow and buttoning her into ankle-length homespun country ticking smelling authentically of cabbage and steam-disinfectant, Bronka stepped back holding the lantern high.

Gone was crisp Warsaw bacteriologist Dr. Halina Różańska; gone too was nervous blonde Łowicz pharmacist's daughter Irena Kwiatkowska. Sitting on the crate sat twenty-odd dumb-looking Tekla from Grójec powiat going home half-deaf after spotted fever to peel orchard apples.

At twenty minutes past four, out in the pitch-black coal-alley behind the mortuary before eastern sky showed a crack of grey, Jurek's municipal steam-disinfection van idled muffled behind canvas curtains.

Halina paused one second on the iron rear step before climbing inside among rolled sulfur hoses.

No tears—neither woman owned tears anymore. Halina reached down one thin, cold, short-nailed hand without Weigl boxes strapped to her skin for the first time since forty-one, gripped Sylwia's mercury-cracked right hand hard enough to grind bones together once, and spoke six words barely audible above the engine tappet:

"Don't let your Prussian get you hung, Sylwia."

"Tell Dawid not to read fine print in bad lamplight before Christmas," Sylwia whispered back, slapping the canvas flap shut and bolting the outside iron hasp herself as the van crept dark and unchallenged out Nowogrodzka gate carrying Stabsarzt Vesper's genuine violet eagle pass in Jurek's pocket.

Between four-twenty-five and half-past six lay the longest two hours of Sylwia's autumn.

Instead of climbing upstairs to lie down, she locked herself alone inside the abandoned basement *Fleckfieber* laboratory—putting on thin surgical gloves so zero fresh fingerprint overlaid the wiped slate benches—to manufacture the five preceding days of "Irena Kwiatkowska's" fatal clinical pride before eight o'clock brought Charlottenburg eyes down the ramp.

Using Halina's own stubby chewed-end copying pencil and an old dried drop of typhus-positive guinea-pig ear blood re-wetted in saline on two glass microscope slides, Sylwia back-entered subtle, utterly convincing shaky marginal tallies across Friday, Saturday, and Sunday's louse-colony feeding sheets (*38.4° — Kopfschmerz, Aspirin 1,0*), topped on Monday afternoon at *16:15* by a violently jagged, broken-off ink stroke across Batch Nineteen's autoclave sheet stained brown at one corner with genuine splashed eighty-eight-percent carbolic phenol alongside swept-up shards of a shattered two-liter Pyrex Erlenmeyer flask!

At five-fifty—exact minute Jurek's slow charcoal-gasogene steam van would hit the double post-Bürkl SS *Sonderfahndung* roadblock out at **Okęcie–Grójecka toll-bar**, where dogs and bayonet squads were searching milk-carts down to axle grease!—Sylwia stood motionless beside Theater One's tall second-floor east casement watching September dawn bleed rusty orange above Mokotów's chimneys.

If one alert SS corporal at Grójecka barrier noticed that the bald "convalescent typhus scrub-woman" sitting hunched among sulfur hoses owned smooth microscope-calloused fingertips instead of washboard-cracked knuckles—and telephoned Aleja Szucha at six-fifteen while Anna Nowak lay bleached blonde in Coffin Four back at Lindleya—nobody inside Pavilion Three survived ten o'clock.

Six-fifteen passed. Six-thirty. Quarter to seven.

At six-fifty-two a battered country milk-float rattled routinely into Pavilion One's kitchen yard delivering sour curds from Raszyn—three miles *south* beyond the Okęcie barrier!—and down below the window old Walenty stepped casually out onto the gravel to tip his broom-cap once toward the second-floor surgical glass while whistling two bars of *Szła dzieweczka do laseczka*.

*Cleared Okęcie barrier at five-forty-eight without canvas unbolted; rolling clean past Sękocin woods.*

Sylwia let out one silent, four-second breath against cold window-glass, went downstairs to drink two cups of scalding black chicory with Bronka, and put on fresh starched linen for the Gestapo.

* * *

At eight-twelve sharp on Wednesday morning the eighth of September, a closed grey Opel Olympia sedan bearing Security Police plates pulled briskly through Lindleya's front iron gates and parked square across Pavilion Three's ambulance ramp.

Reinhart Kroll hadn't come himself today—sending juniors to execute clerical arrests showed Saxon tidiness. Out stepped **SS-Untersturmführer Mahler**, twenty-six, pink-cheeked Berlin police-academy graduate with horn-rimmed spectacles, carrying a leather black-ink fingerprinting case in his left hand and a crisp folder containing Łowicz St. Stanislaus Catholic Parish's certified statement that *the real Irena Kwiatkowska died of diphtheria aged twenty-three in June 1939*, flanked by two burly SD *Scharführer* holding steel handcuffs loose inside pocket slits.

Sylwia met them at the top of Pavilion Three's stone steps wearing a spotless starched white surgical coat, gauze typhus-mask dangling ready round her neck, holding a clipboard inked black-bordered across the top margin.

"Dr. Kotarska?" Untersturmführer Mahler snapped heels briskly, already glancing past her hip toward the basement stairs. "Security Police *Abteilung Vier*. Unlock your basement *Fleckfieber* laboratory without telephoning down—you employ an auxiliary bacteriologist entered as *Kwiatkowska, Irena*, *Kennkarte* Nr. W-088412."

"Employed until twenty-one forty hours last night, *Herr Untersturmführer*," Sylwia corrected him in level, weary German, turning half-round to point southwest across the sunny gravel courtyard toward the low red-brick dead-house annex where red *SEUCHENGEFAHR — FLECKFIEBER* boards hung beside open iron doors. "If your office requires Panna Kwiatkowska's *Kennkarte* for municipal cancellation, here it is in her deceased-effects envelope—along with Stabsarzt Vesper's mandatory epidemic death notification filed at seven a.m. today."

Mahler stopped dead on the top step, pink cheeks going blank behind horn-rims. "Deceased?!"

"Fulminant laboratory *Fleckfieber* complicated by massive virulent rickettsial inhalation after an autoclave carbolic-flask explosion Monday afternoon during the Litewska street-alarm," Sylwia recited with bored, unimpeachable clinical precision, handing him the disinfectant-sprayed envelope by two forceps tips as if sparing his clean fingers typhus contamination! "Concealed prodromal fever five days at her bench rather than miss Wola vaccine quotas, aspirated concentrated louse-gut suspension when boiling phenol shattered across her right hand and face Monday at four, died in Toxic Isolation Four last night at nine-forty in irreversible myocarditic shock. Coffin Four awaits ten-o'clock chloride-of-lime sealing in the contagious annex if you wish visual verification before burial."

Mahler stared suspiciously from Sylwia's calm dark eyes down to his Łowicz forgery sheet—every instinct taught at Charlottenburg police school screaming *convenient timing!*

"Open Coffin Four," Mahler ordered curtly, waving both SD sergeants behind her across the courtyard gravel. "Nobody buries an *Abteilung Vier* suspect on Polish paper."

"Tie these carbolized gauze pads over mouth and nose and keep trousers tucked tight inside boot-tops so wandering body-lice off fresh slabs don't climb wool seams," Sylwia advised helpfully at the dead-house threshold—watching both burly *Scharführer* immediately step back two yards onto sunny gravel slapping nervously at their ankles while Mahler alone, sweating fiercely behind horn-rimmed lenses fogged by gauze breath, stepped three paces inside the dark, fly-buzzing, cresol-and-rot-reeking stone vault!

Bronka stood waiting beside Pine Trestle Four holding a heavy iron tire-crowbar. At Sylwia's nod, Bronka pried the unnailed pine lid back two feet and held a yellow storm-lantern directly over the head end.

Out rolled ninety-degree enclosed carbolic, dead typhus sweat, and sour cresol thick enough to make Mahler gag aloud behind white gauze!

Inside the rough pine box lay the unmistakable grey-purple moth-eaten cardigan every Pavilion Three watcher knew; brassy peroxide-yellow hair showing dark roots above a sunken, confluent-spotted face half-eaten across the right cheekbone by white phenol eschar; right hand folded across chest displaying five shriveled, snow-white, completely ridge-obliterated third-degree carbolic-cooked fingertips; and down below the hiked hospital smock hem where Bronka had carelessly left shroud linen turned back for "post-mortem spleen check," two crusted purple Weigl louse-box grids above a shiny pale right-lower-quadrant appendectomy crease!

Mahler held his leather fingerprint kit clutched uselessly against his stomach—one two-second squint at five cooked white phenol finger-pulps told any academy graduate ten-print rollers would peel wet dead slough off onto glass without printing a single whorl, while two fat bluebottle flies buzzing lazily off the spotted collar toward Mahler's spectacles finished whatever courage Berlin had issued him!

"Close it! Nail it down with lime immediately!" Mahler barked muffled behind both gloved hands clapped over his mask, backing fast out into September sunshine and tearing his fogged spectacles off to wipe sweat off white eyebrows. Pulling out his fountain pen on open gravel twenty feet upwind, he scrawled *Leiche besichtigt 8.IX.43, 08:26 — Identität bestätigt, Akte Kwiatkowska wegen Tod geschlossen — Mahler, SS-Ustuf.* straight across Sylwia's epidemic death duplicate, confiscated the cancelled *Irena Kwiatkowska* *Kennkarte* for Kroll's closed-file archive, and had his Opel Olympia reversing out Lindleya's gate inside ninety seconds to go scrub himself in alcohol on Aleja Szucha!

Beside the dead-house door, Bronka lowered the heavy iron crowbar slowly to her clog-tip and spat once into the nettles.

"Rest eternal grant unto her, O Lord," the old Wola butcher's daughter muttered piously, crossing herself over bloodstained apron linen for poor Anna Nowak going to Bródno under two women's sins while down past Grójec apple orchards a bald girl in peasant sackcloth rode free through morning sun toward pine trees.

Sylwia stood watching the grey dust settle behind Lindleya's iron gates—heart slowing steady from one-twenty back to seventy—and realized that with Halina gone safe into the woods, Mirecki flat upstairs, and Kroll's 1933 original ledger sitting frustrated in Szucha's safe, only one locked door remained unopened between herself and Ludwig Schoeler before autumn broke Warsaw's back.
