The Sealed Tablet

He opens it on a night of rain because the rain keeps the clients away and leaves the atrium to the masks, and because a man does everything eventually for a reason he would strike from the record. He tells the household he is reviewing the estate papers. He is, in the largest sense his family permits the word: the estate is the past; the papers are its rent book.

The rain has been deciding since the third hour. It is the Roman winter kind that does not fall so much as take up residence in the air, insinuating itself into wool, into the joints of old houses and older shoulders, into the acoustics of an atrium so that every household sound arrives with a small, wet echo, as if the building were being read back to itself in a poorer copy. Clients have sent boys with apologies shaped like fish. A cousin has sent fish shaped like an apology. Servilia has deployed both where they will do the house credit: the fish to the kitchen, the apologies to the steward's book, in which *called, weather* is a category of loyalty with its own rate of interest.

Lentulus has spent the day, accordingly, in the company of things he can postpone. There is the aqueduct business, early yet, a leaking arcade on the Praeneste road that two municipalities are using to conduct a feud by other means; he has written three letters about water that say, in descending order of candour, repair it, pay for it jointly, and stop writing to me as if gravity were a party. There is the committee draft, general, eventual and dull, as agreed in this house by a power who did not need to be named in the minutes. There is a speech for the harbour dues that his future relations by marriage require and that he keeps revising toward a decency its beneficiaries would find alarmingly principled if they read drafts, which, in the manner of rich men, they will not.

At intervals — the household could set a water-clock by them; Onesimus, forgetting professionally, does — he passes the atrium. The masks are being cleaned today, the year's smoke sponged off by two slaves under Servilia's steward's eye, in a ceremony the house performs the way other families visit tombs: regularly, and with gossip. Grandfather Gnaeus comes down off his cupboard into safer hands than any that ever carried him in life. His wax face, undarkened for an hour by the sponge's diplomacy, looks younger than his son's memory allows, which Lentulus finds an impertinence in a material. Under the mask, the cupboard's interior shows for a moment — scroll-cases, a campaign chest's worth of family paper, the particular case — before the steward, who knows which things in this house are seen by appointment, moves his body a fraction into the light.

“Shall I have the case's leather fed, dominus? The damp—”

“Leave it,” Lentulus says. “Leather in this family is expected to endure climate.”

He has left it, in various formulations, for nine years. A man can leave a thing in a sentence for nine years the way a house can leave a room locked: by building his daily routes around it until the skirting of it feels like the architecture itself. The tablet came to him at his father's will-reading in a winter of committees — older wars: a governorship refused, a consulship (suffect, footnoted) accepted, Marcus loud as a gate at eleven, Cornelia not yet arrived, the nine-day girl newly unspoken-of in the grammar of previous consulships. The notary had read the item without emphasis, *a sealed tablet for my son's eyes*, in the tone men use for the minutes of other people's earthquakes, and Lentulus, even then, filing things between quarters in a system he would later learn a woman across the city had invented independently and better, had put the case under his grandfather and told himself it concerned the estates.

It might have done. His father, on his deathbed, had talked estates for a full day — drainage, a bailiff's peculation handled by Philodorus' successor with Philodorus' own knife, trimming, trimming — and had then, in the small hours, asked for his seal and a fresh tablet with a clarity the household reported, awed, as the old man *sitting up in himself again*. Philodorus was nine months dead by then. The old Gnaeus wrote alone. What a man writes alone, after a day of drainage, with death already in the room gathering the letters at the ends of lines, is unlikely to be drainage. Lentulus has known this the way he knows most structural facts about his family: exactly, at the back, with the accounts.

Marcus comes home at dusk, wet through from the baths that are his postings abroad, and brings the rain indoors on his shoulders and in his news: the inquiry is filed, the prefect's dullness is being quoted as integrity, an Alexandrian in the warm room has a version of last week's death in which the physician arrives by chariot. Cornelia, at the far side of the garden room, is practising at half volume because rain on the roof turns the house into an instrument she is not officially playing. Lentulus watches his children being young in a wet house at the exact ages — twenty, sixteen — at which their parents, in two different cities of the same empire, were being arranged and sold respectively, and the thought does not so much arrive as take the seat it apparently had reserved: *everything in this family requires a death to become sayable. A man has died at her table, laughing at a tax farmer, with a singer holding his shoulders.*

Servilia, from the doorway, sees it arrive. She sees everything arrive; it is her quartermaster's genius and her marriage's weather. What she says is, “The household will eat early. The rain.” What she does, over the next hour, is clear the atrium the way stages are cleared in the theatres when the machine-god is expected: no clients, no lessons, the masks back in their cupboards cleaned and looking younger than their descendants, a lamp ration calculated for a long sitting, and the steward informed, in the sentence Lentulus will later know word for word, that the master is reviewing the estate papers and is not to be improved upon by attendance.

She does not say *open it*. Servilia has never spent a direct order where an arrangement would do the work at half the price and twice the dignity. She arranges. And Lentulus, who has been commanded by emperors in both languages the libraries keep, goes into the atrium at nightfall with the rain sending its small messengers down through the compluvium, in the old rhythm, the one that measured his childhood lessons, and puts his hand, at last, to the cupboard that sticks in winter.

The cupboard under his grandfather's mask sticks, as it always sticks in winter. The tablet is where he left it nine years ago, in the scroll-case whose leather has gone the colour of everything in this house that is not wax or wool. His father's seal: the cornel-wood sprig, the old joke of the Cornelii, a family that turned a tree into three centuries of magistracies. He breaks it with his thumb the way you break the skin on cooling milk, hating the delicacy, wanting the violence minuted somewhere.

His father's hand had been a public instrument even in private: large, exact, unhurried by death, which was already in the room when he wrote this — Lentulus can see it at the ends of lines, where the letters gather themselves like men deciding not to run.

*Gnaeus to Gnaeus. You will open this late. We are a family that opens things late and calls it dignity. Attend.*

*The girl from Baiae did not die there. The child — a daughter — lived. I paid the midwife to say otherwise and the steward to place her where the Cornelii would never have to spell her name. A freed couple across the river, fullers, decent people in the way that matters, which is to say they were paid and then, to their credit, continued after the paying stopped being their reason. I had the payments put through Philodorus so that you could truthfully say you knew nothing. You asked me once if there was a child. I said there was nothing living with a claim on this house. It was the truest sentence available that preserved the house, and I offer you now the observation, too late to be useful to me, that a lifetime of such sentences will preserve a house the way brine preserves meat.*

The rain. The compluvium dripping into its basin in the old rhythm, the one that measured his childhood lessons. He reads on, because stopping has never yet been a policy of his family's that survived contact with a document.

The muster morning comes back the way these things do when a dead man starts initialling paragraphs: not as a scene but as smells in an order. Horse first. Then leather — the campaign chest open at dawn in a house that was putting a son into the army for a season the way it put a name into the Senate for a generation. Then his mother's women somewhere offstage, a corridor of voices in the part of the house he was already too old, at twenty-four, to be found in without an errand. He had an errand. He had, in fact, been manufacturing errands in that corridor for a year, with a rental roll as his passport and a copyist's quick hand for a view, and on that morning the corridor was empty of everything but light behaving as if nothing in Rome had changed addresses in the night.

He remembers asking the question only once and he remembers, with an accuracy his conscience has kept like a Greek keeps a strongbox, the use his father made of the pause before answering. The study smelled of wax. It is the detail that has over the years arranged all the others around itself: hot wax, seal-warm, in a room where land changed families and girls changed owners in the same hand, the hand now in front of him on the tablet, large, exact, gathering at the ends of lines.

“Was there a child?”

He had said it to the side of his father's face, a young man's profile held still by nothing but the house. His father had not looked up from the rolls. A province is being governed somewhere in a man's tone when he does that: the tone says the question has arrived without its paperwork and will be answered as a climate, not a claim. *There is nothing living with a claim on this house.*

Twenty-one years later, in the rain, with the tablet open under a lamp nobody comes to trim, Lentulus conducts the cross-examination he has been supplying internally, at his usual rates, since he was old enough to have rates. *Nothing living.* A phrase with room in it for a cradle across a river and a midwife's improved sum. *With a claim.* A phrase with a steward in it, settling things at a distance and charging the house for the mileage. *On this house.* As if the child might be living, might have a claim, but the house — the house was a fortress that language could be stationed around like a garrison. His father had not lied. Lying is for men who intend to be found out cheaply. His father had done the thing this family does instead, in excellent Latin, at length: he had chosen the currency, and he had made his son rich in it, and the son had spent it for twenty-one years, in committees, in a marriage, in the small hours, at compound interest.

You tell yourself, he thinks — second person, the pronoun men in his family keep for the minutes of the meetings they hold with themselves — you tell yourself you were twenty-four. Marcus is twenty. You have watched Marcus, this winter, be twenty in baths and dining rooms with a singer's glass necklace and a sister's face at a dinner table, and you have seen the exact moment, in a study with a duplicated grandfather, when the boy began paying for his arithmetic at something nearer the full rate. You were given the same moment, by a father with a seal in his hand, and you took the sentence as an answer because the alternative would have cost you your inheritance of yourself. It is very important, in what remains of the night, not to call that anything else. The tablet in front of you has not called it anything else. That is the courtesy the dead can still perform, when they have stopped needing the living to behave: they stop drafting.

*Brine preserves meat.* He reads the sentence again. His father, at the end, had a style his son had never heard at table: a kitchen style, unbuttoned, smelling of stores. Perhaps dying men write like freedwomen keep books — in the hand for facts that must survive fashion. Perhaps old Gnaeus, alone with his seal after a day of drainage, had at last met the one client the atrium had never admitted: his own morning, at twenty-four, on the other side of the desk, asking once. And had answered it, a generation too late, addressed to the wrong person. The tablet says so. The tablet — he has not read the rest; the rain is doing his childhood lessons in the basin, and the letters are gathering themselves like men deciding not to run — the tablet has the decency to know its own address is wrong.

The girl was not asked after. That is the sentence under all the sentences, the one the family did not inscribe. Antonia — the name she credits to herself; his father has that right too, damn the old man, damn his accuracy, arriving now like a bailiff with the right papers — Antonia was sold, was told, stood up, climbed. Lentulus is a man who has governed his opinions in public with distinction for twenty years. He has nothing to set against *climbed* except a career and a committee and an unsigned note about the order of the evening, sent this month by a hand that taught a girl the letter her name begins with. The arithmetic does itself, in stripes, through the shutters of whatever he still calls his conscience: he is not the wronged party in this document. He is its forwarding address.

*I do not write this to be forgiven; your forgiveness would be worth what mine was, a generation too late and addressed to the wrong person. The person wronged is not you. The girl was sold on your muster morning with her increase, as the contract said. I wrote those words too. If you have lived as I surmise you have lived — quietly with it, like a stone in the shoe of a marching man — then you may draw what comfort there is in knowing the girl Antonia lives and prospers in Rome under a name she credits to herself, which is more than I can say for the name I left you. The daughter, if she lives, is called by whatever name decency gave her. I never asked it. There are questions a man of my sort does not ask in case the answer turns out to have a face.*

*Do nothing dramatic. Drama is a tax the rich impose on the poor to pay for their remorse. If the matter ever stands where this family can settle it at a price that is not the girl's life a second time, settle it. A Cornelius pays his debts. We merely prefer, historically, to choose the currency. Choose better.*

Lentulus sits with the tablet open until the lamp needs trimming and no slave comes, because Servilia has instructed the household, with the tactical sense that has kept this marriage upright for nineteen years, that the master reviewing estate papers is not to be improved upon by attendance.

A daughter. Called by whatever name decency gave her. He does the arithmetic that Antonia, across the city, in a house with damp in one wall, has done in exactly the same hour of the same winter: nineteen. A person of nineteen, across the river, in a fuller's household, walking about Rome in a body half-made of the Quirinal and half of a mirror-girl's, unknown to herself as either, while her father's committee inspects her mother's drains and her half-brother drinks Falernian downstairs from the accounts room where her existence has just been itemised as thread.

He gets up with the tablet still open, which in a house of sealed things is a kind of rowdiness, and walks the night rooms the way his wife does her round — no noise until he is in a room, then the room owing him an explanation. The slaves have banked the kitchen fire. The garden door has been witnessed and barred, with a marginal note, delivered to nobody in particular by the door slave as Lentulus passes, that the young master is in and the rain is considering a treaty. Cornelia's door is shut, with Tyche's sandals outside it in the position of a small, loyal frontier. From behind it comes nothing but the sound sleep makes in a house where a girl has been well fed, well taught, and — Servilia's system again, in every wall he passes — well defended, mostly against her own family's conversation. He thinks of a settlement with a heading for musicians. He thinks of ballast, a word a shipping heir used at his table, and of girls as ballast in every house that has ever sent its cargo out under old names: his daughter, dowered and scheduled and singing on ration; another daughter, unregistered in any settlement, arriving in a fuller's guild book in the column for households; his niece — no. Let the arithmetic be clean, for once, in the second column. His daughters. The word does what Avidius' figure did when Servilia read it: it stops pretending to be anything else.

He writes nothing that night. This will surprise everyone who knows him by his paperwork, which is everyone who knows him. There is a draft composed — a man composes; it is his family's substitute for every other form of action — in which money is settled through the Greek (of course through the Greek; the Greek is Rome's confessional, with better acoustics) on a fuller’s household across the river, in the form of a legacy from a Baiae mistress neither alive nor safely dead enough to be invoiced. There is a second draft, worse, in which he goes himself. He can see that second draft as its intended recipients would: a senator in a doorway in Transtiberim with the ruined poetry of apology all over his good cloak, a dying man holding a rail, a girl hearing her life read back to her in somebody else's Latin like a contract she is expected to witness. *Drama is a tax the rich impose on the poor to pay for their remorse.* His father, dead nine years, graded out of a tablet, gets the best line in his son's worst night. It would be funny if the family kept its humour in rooms this small.

There is also — least drafted, most minuted in the body, where the Moesia shoulder files its weather reports — the question of Antonia, which is not, tonight, the question of the girl at all. She knows. He is certain of it with the certainty men of his experience reserve for harbours and wives: she will have had the information before him, cheaper, in the invisible denominations, with the thread itemised separately. She has held his paper at the Greek's for two quarters and held her peace at a higher rate. She returned a dead man's December bill marked settled and sent flowers that cost less, so it should not look like arithmetic. She instructs her people to answer in the order of the evening. Every draft of his that does not go through her first is, he understands with the clarity the masks specialise in after midnight, another sealed tablet: a settlement made about Antonia Cassia, by a Cornelius, in her absence, in a house that has been doing exactly that for three hundred years and calling the house honest.

So the first payment, when he makes it — not tonight; tonight a man is being unsealed at approximately the speed of drainage — will be information, delivered at a banker's back office, in daylight, with chairs. It is the only currency he has never tried with her. At twenty-four he gave her letters, which were luggage. At forty-five his committee gave her an inspection at dawn, which was theft in ministry's clothing. The thing in between, the thing he accepted a statesman's sentence instead of, is apparently the thing that must now be carried across the city in his own hands and set down, without a steward, without mileage. He does not look forward to it with any part of himself that is still his father's son. The rest of him — the part that told the prefect's business to be general, eventual and dull; the part that stood in an atrium at clients' hour seeing his daughter insert an unlicensed turn into music older than the family — that part is quiet in the way of a room that has finished being arranged.

He goes back to the atrium. The lamp is low. The tablet has waited, open, like a client who no longer needs the morning's performance. He reads the end again. *Choose better.* Two words; his father's whole late style, the brine and the kitchen and the unbuttoned truth of it. Under the mask, the sticking cupboard gives the case back to the dark with its usual sound: a small, drowned click, like a count coming right at the second attempt in another house entirely.

He has, until tonight, been able to tell himself that his quarrel with Antonia Cassia is about Rome. It is a story with columns: public morals, young men's debts, Syria. The tablet does not contradict the columns. It simply shows him the ground they stand on, and the ground is a muster morning and a contract phrase and a question asked once by a young man who accepted a statesman's sentence as an answer because the alternative would have cost him his inheritance of himself.

He closes the tablet. He does not reseal it; a Cornelius does not pretend with wax. He puts it back in the scroll-case, under the mask, in the sticking cupboard, and stands a while in the company of the ancestors, who look down with their smoke-dark patience, every one of them a man who chose his currency.

“Choose better,” he says aloud, to no one, and the atrium, being Roman, keeps the remark in the family.

Onesimus is waiting in the corridor with a dry towel over his arm, as if a man reviewing estate papers in a rainy atrium were a form of bathing that concluded with towelling. He has been stationed there by Servilia since the lamp's second hour: not to attend, which has been forbidden in a sentence the whole household can now recite, but to *be in the building's memory of the evening*, so that tomorrow, when the baths and the secretaries begin their work, the house will be able to say truthfully that a slave was present in the corridor and heard nothing, saw a lamp, and can describe the weather. Onesimus forgets professionally. It remains his masterpiece.

“The rain is giving its evidence in the order of the evening, dominus,” he says, and then, hearing himself deliver a phrase he has picked up in somebody else's plumbing, he files it, visibly, where the house keeps its confiscated eloquence. “Shall I say the master will not shave before the clients?”

“Tell Felix the master will shave at the usual hour.” Lentulus takes the towel he does not need and holds it a moment like a client holding a petition he intends to grant. “District business as usual, Onesimus. Aqueducts. A committee, general and dull. A letter to be left unsealed on the mistress's table.” He looks back once at the atrium, where his grandfather has resumed the dark with a cleaned face and a cupboard full of the future. “And if a room at a banker's office can be had tomorrow without the household's livery being read into it — cedar door, deaf slave, the usual quiet Greek — you will arrange it as if it were being arranged for somebody else's conscience.”

Onesimus bows with the towel still doing its emblematic work. In eleven years he has arranged trysts, bribes, a resignation, two reconciliations that the families concerned describe as military victories, and a committee's entire soft landing into a registry. He will arrange this too, forget it in three languages, and be free in another man's will with the sum named, still forgetting. Somewhere in the house a kitchen fire settles in its ash, keeping itself, the way the poor keep everything: alive, against the morning, at a price no ledger has ever acknowledged because it has always, somehow, been paid.

Servilia is awake when he comes to bed. She lies in the posture of sleep that she maintains, like the household order, for the benefit of anyone conducting inspections, and says, without opening her eyes, “The estate papers.”

“Yes.”

“Olives?”

“Not olives.” It is the most intimate sentence they have exchanged in some years. The rain goes on sending its small messengers down through the compluvium of the old house into the basin that has received them for three centuries, and somewhere below, a slave who has not been told to stay away from the whole house, only from one room of it, is damping the kitchen fire for the night, banking it — keeping it, the way the poor keep everything: alive under ash, against the morning, at a price that appears in no one's ledger because it has always been paid.

Servilia says, “Then we will need to be more careful than your father was.”

He looks at her. She has still not opened her eyes. “How long have you—”

“I will tell you the sentence once,” Servilia says, still with her eyes closed, in the voice she uses for reading figures she has already appealed in private. “It was in our first month. Your steward — Philodorus' successor; the knife went with the office — was arranging a dinner to show the new wife the Campanian agents could be received without the knives. Oysters from Baiae were proposed. I said the house in Baiae had been sold, I understood, with the staff dispersed, and would the oysters still be reliable. You went —” She pauses. “There is no gesture. That was the information. You went entirely still, the way Felix says men do under the razor when a household steps on its own hem. My mother, who married me with instructions I have spent nineteen years deciding which of to be disobeyed in, taught me to read a husband's silences the way the house reads a steward's book: in the second column. Yours, over oysters, had a whole province in it.”

Lentulus sits on the edge of the bed in the dark room he has shared, on and off in the manner of great houses, for half his adult life, and looks at the shape of the woman who has been running the war in the adjacent room of every campaign he thought he was conducting alone. “And you said nothing. For nineteen years.”

“I said a great deal. About roofs, and girls' music, and what grain money smells of, and whether a son being twenty is a condition a family can insure. I did not say the word Baiae at that altitude again. A marriage, Lentulus, is a committee that reports when it is told to." A small movement under the covers that might, in a lesser quartermaster, be a shrug. “You had a physician's statement for a conscience and a father issuing your vocabulary. I had a settlement, a mother, and presently two children who lived. One does not table motions in a house whose drainage one is still mapping.”

“I am —” He tries the apology drafts in the dark and finds them all, like the one he abandoned for Transtiberim, addressed to the wrong person in the wrong currency. Servilia, who can hear drafts the way musicians hear tuning, interrupts the attempt at cost to nobody.

“Do not perform it tonight. You have had a death become sayable, a tablet become readable, and a daughter become arithmetic, all before the lamps. The house has a policy about doing two of those at once; we learned it from a woman who holds our olives.” It comes out without acid, an entry read in the upright hand. “What does your father advise, in the document? It was written by a Gnaeus, Lentulus. It will have instructions. They will be excellently drafted and a generation too late in both directions.”

“‘Do nothing dramatic. Settle it. Choose better.’”

She is silent for exactly as long as a clerk verifying standing should be silent, and no longer. “Then for once the estate papers and I are in agreement. Frame the phenomenon.” A breath, in the posture of sleep entirely given up now, though her eyes remain closed as a matter of constitutional form: some things in this marriage are still negotiated without the vulnerability of looking. “The girl across the river — I will call her that tonight; tomorrow we may be issued better words at a higher rate — has a father dying and a betrothal being inspected. She does not have, unless Antonia is losing her grip in her old age, a senator due at her door with his conscience in a carrying chair. Money she can use, in a form with a hem she can show her future mother-in-law. Information she can keep, at an hour somebody she trusts chooses. Those are settlements. The rest is us, imposing a tax and calling it remorse. Your father had a phrase for that too, I collect.”

“He had phrases for most of it. It was his whole technology.”

“Then let the technology rust.” Servilia turns, at last, toward him in the dark. The rain has settled into the house's old rhythm entirely: compluvium, basin, childhood lessons, clients' weather, a fuller's cough in another quarter of the same city, everything arriving in the order of the evening at last. “I married a settlement, Lentulus, and I made it work like rain on a roof — by finding the holes. You will not now drill a new one over your daughter's bed in either house because a dead man has finally filed his paperwork. Are we in agreement, or shall I have the steward minute us?”

“We are in agreement,” he says, and it is, after *not olives*, the most intimate sentence available for cataloguing this year.

“Since the oysters,” she says. “Nineteen years. Sleep, Lentulus. Tomorrow you have a committee. Committees are how this family thinks. God knows it isn't how it acts.”
