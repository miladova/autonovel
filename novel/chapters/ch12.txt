Negotiations

They meet at last because Rome, which has been conducting their correspondence through clerks and committees and unsigned walks across the city, finally requires a room. The room is neutral the way a knife is neutral between hands: the back office of the Greek banker at the Basilica, smelling of cedar and other people's solvency, with two chairs and a brazier and a slave who withdraws after the watered wine with the deafness that is his employer's true product.

Antonia arrives first, because in another's office the first arrival owns the silence. Lentulus arrives with rain still in the weave of his cloak, and stands — she remembers this, her body remembers it before she can file it — taking the room the way he used to take a page of Greek, top to bottom, before allowing himself the beginning.

She has dressed for the Basilica the way her house dresses a room for men who must not be allowed to mistake the occasion: plainly, at full price. No jewellery a clerk could describe. A veil arranged in the style women use for temples and assignations of state. Diomedes walks two paces behind with a document case containing nothing the morning is permitted to require — copies, all; originals live with the Greek, with the notary, and in a third place Diomedes has never been told, a piece of tradecraft Antonia maintains even against loyalty, on the principle Eirene phrases as *nobody should be the only copy of you, love, including us*.

“If he offers terms in writing,” Diomedes says, at the corner where the Basilica's shade begins to govern the temperature of the talk, “madam will not sign in the room.”

“If he offers terms in writing I shall suspect him of being an impostor in a borrowed life. Lentulus inhabits paper the way fish inhabit apologies, Diomedes: reluctantly, and only after it has been presented as water.”

The Greek's outer office is doing its usual imitation of a temple where the god is discretion. A Spanish silver man is *not* negotiating a loan over a sample ingot he keeps putting back into its cloth. A widow of equestrian family, chaperoned by a freedwoman of ferocious respectability — Antonia knows the pair; the widow hears unsuitable music in a certain house on the Caelian and has lately been observed laughing at it — is settling a grandson's expectations in a voice that suggests the grandson is a foreign policy. Their eyes meet Antonia's across the room: client to proprietor, a current of perfect, municipal blankness. Nobody is in this office. That is what the office sells. The Greek, coming himself from the cedar door to perform the morning's pièce — he performs one a day, like a sacrifice, to remind the room who owns the silence — receives the widow's papers, declines the silver man's ingot with a courtesy that will cost the silver man a quarter point by Thursday, and inclines his head to Antonia in the smallest degree a rich man can manage, etcetera. She has seen Avidius do it since, over fish. It is apparently denominational.

“The small room is prepared,” Eutyches says, in Latin with the faintest Aegean weather on it. “The senator's man asked for the small room. I informed him the small room was engaged for a lady's business, which had the advantage of being true in every particular his master will check.”

“You are a theologian, Eutyches.”

“I am a banker, madam. We let the truth work in several rooms at once and charge by the door.”

Across the city, at approximately the same inclination of a rich Greek head, Lentulus is being shaved for a negotiation his household believes to be an aqueduct inspection. Felix works in silence until the razor reaches the scar's neighbourhood, where professional tact requires, as always, the question that is not a question.

“Leave it,” Lentulus says, before it can be asked. “It has kept its appointment for twenty-one years. I am keeping mine at the Basilica.”

“Then today is an anniversary, dominus,” Felix says, and wipes the blade, and says nothing else, being a man who has shaved three consuls and buried two and knows exactly which of the morning's sentences are his to carry out of the room. It comes to Lentulus, watching his own face reassemble in the dented bronze — senator, debtor, forwarding address — that he has been dressed by this house for every consequential entrance of his life: muster, marriage, consulship (suffect, footnoted), his father's will-reading, his daughter's betrothal dinners. The hands change; the liturgy doesn't. Only once was he not dressed by the house for a consequential entrance, and it was not an entrance, it was a corridor, and she was holding a mirror steady, and the whole of the rest is paperwork.

Onesimus has arranged the Basilica as if for somebody else's conscience, as instructed. There is no livery to be read. There is a Greek chair for the standing problem of a senator's dignity, a brazier, watered wine of a neutrality that would shame a treaty, and the certain knowledge, carried in by both parties like weather, that the Greek in the next room holds — in different hands, under different names, in wax that melts quarterly as a matter of policy — the financial ruin of each of them in its most portable form, and will use neither, today, except as furniture.

That is what neutral means, Antonia thinks, taking the silence-owning chair while Diomedes withdraws to be nobody in the outer office. Not empty. *Armed, on both sides, with the doors shut.* She folds her hands in the order she has decided. First the committee. First the paper. First the things that can be entered in books. If there is to be a fourth thing in this room, it will arrive, like everything else she has survived, over the threshold in his mouth or not at all. She has rehearsed the fire drill. She knows which door not to open under smoke. What she has not rehearsed — she logs it now, with Diomedes precision, in the third book, behind the ribs — is what to do if he opens it for her, from his side, quietly, like a man at last asking the price in the right shop.

“You sent for me,” he says.

“The Greek sent for us. He has a talent for rooms.” She does not stand. A woman who owns the silence does not surrender the chair. “Your committee has cost me three bookings and gained me a prefect's compliment on my paperwork. I call that, overall, an expensive compliment. I have come to close the account.”

“I did not arrange the man's death to close anything.”

“I know.” She looks at him. “That is how I know it was his heart. If you had arranged it, Lentulus, it would have worked.”

It is the first joke either of them has made to the other in twenty-one years, and it lands between them like a cup set down too hard on glass. Something in his face — she watches it the way she watches a guest's face at the fish course, for the truth under the arrangement, chooses not to pursue it. Business. They are good at business. It is the language Rome taught them both, in different schools with the same fees.

“The committee reports in ten days,” he says. “It will recommend a general registry and quarterly inspection. General, Antonia. Twenty houses. Yours first on the list only by alphabet, which in Latin is mercifully early for nobody.”

“Acilius has been bathing you.”

“Acilius has been right. It happens, even to consuls.” He sits. The chairs are Greek, too low for the dignity of a senator, and he conquers this by ignoring it, a technique his family has applied to provinces. “In return, I ask the house to be — discreet — about young men of senatorial family until the vintage. My daughter's marriage is in negotiation. You know this. Your singer could probably recite the dowry figures.”

“Daphne sings, Lentulus. It is your son who recites.”

A beat. The brazier ticks. Somewhere beyond the cedar door the Greek's fortune goes on being made out of exactly this: two Romans reaching the part they came for by the road they can both describe as the long way round.

“Define discreet,” Antonia says, because she has never in twenty years let a man of his family issue her an adjective without presenting its papers. “In this city it usually means the women are silent in a more expensive register.”

“It means your door-book and my son's name keep separate quarters until Cornelia is married.” He says it flatly, a man stating a case before he has been briefed and hating the advocate. “It means if Marcus comes, Barbatus finds the house full. It means Daphne—” He stops at the singer's name the way men stop at a threshold in a house they are inspecting at dawn. “It means no second necklace becomes a baths story with my family's name for a clasp.”

“The necklace was glass, Lentulus. Its return was the education. Your son is twenty, briefly truthful, and learning manners at the full rate. If Rome must gossip about him, it will do it without my glass.” She lets the brazier tick once. “Barbatus can find the house full on the evenings your committee sits. He will enjoy it; it gives the door a constitution. Beyond that, I do not police your heir for his father's convenience at a discount. If the Cornelii want Marcus invisible, the Cornelii may purchase his invisibility themselves. I believe Syria is the traditional shop.”

It lands. She watches it land — guest's face at the fish course, the truth under the arrangement: not anger, which would be negotiable, but the small, wry Senate mouth that precedes concessions he will later describe as strategy. “You would have made an advocate.”

“I would have made several advocates unemployed. It is not the same career.” Antonia un-folds one hand and lays it flat on the cedar table between them, palm down, the gesture she uses in her own house to stop a sauce being sent out underdone. “Now the registry. Quarterly, by appointment, at stated hours when women may be dressed for an audience with the state instead of surprised by one. Papers shown to officials who can read them, in rooms that are not bedrooms. Minors and the unfree given the same entry as citizens — a name, a status, a person whose *no*—” She chooses the next words the way she chooses seating, for their geography. “A house to keep a person at all must be able to show her refusal exists somewhere in the paperwork. Put it better in Latin. I am told you draft.”

The sentence goes into him and stays. She can see it doing what sentences do in Lentulus when they are going to be carried for years: it finds the locked room and knocks once, correctly. Outside, the Basilica bargains on. Inside, a senator of ancient lineage looks at a freedwoman's hand flat on his banker's table and hears, as if from a committee he has not yet survived, his own future clause being dictated to him by its intended casualty, in the order of the evening.

“No house to profit—” he begins, half aloud, the drafting habit outrunning the statesmanship.

“Finish it at your leisure. I am not in the room to invoice you for authorship.” She withdraws the hand. “Rome will attribute it to a Cornelius with a conscience, and my women will cite it over their contracts, and both of us will have got our preferred anthems out of one sentence. That, Lentulus, is what a settlement looks like in my trade. Yours takes longer and has flutes.”

He laughs then — not the cup-on-glass laugh of the first joke, something shorter and less managed, a sound she last heard in a house that no longer stands, over scansion, in a corridor. Her body files it before she can. She lets the filing happen and keeps her face on business, because business is the language they are both still fluent in, and because the next item on her private agenda has been sitting in the Greek's strongbox earning like furniture for two quarters and it is time it was introduced to its debtor.

“I have a further matter,” Antonia says, in the voice she uses for menus and mercy, “of paper. The Greek will confirm the position if you require it in his hand rather than mine. Loans against Campanian land, rolled twice, now lodged — having passed through the hands of persons who prefer not to hold a senator's vintage in their own cellar — with an investor who files quarterly.” She watches the understanding arrive. It arrives the way age has arrived at his temples: visibly, if you know the face. “I am not calling it in. I am telling you it exists because a weapon a man does not know about is only an asset, and I did not come here to be merely rich at you.”

“How much of it?”

“Enough.” She folds her hands. “I learned that word from men who lend. I find it improves with use.”

“You want the committee buried.”

“I want what you have already offered because Acilius explained the steam to you. A general registry, paid quarterly, like the tax, like the licence, like everything else in my life that Rome condemns and invoices.” She stands, and the room rearranges itself around the single fact of her standing, as rooms have done for her since Baiae. “I have paid this city for twenty years to be let alone in a house I built out of stairs. I will go on paying. It is my civic religion. But I will not be taxed in shame as well as coin because a Cornelius needs Syria to look like cleanliness. We are finished. Unless—”

“Unless,” he says.

She has rehearsed this too, the way she rehearses fire procedures and banquet seating: knowing the exact door she must not open under smoke. She looks at him — forty-five, grey-templed, shoulder that aches in warm water, hands that once set a stylus behind an ear as if it were a — no: that is the smoke. Follow the drill. “Unless there is any matter between our houses that your committee has not minuted. In which case the Greek has other rooms, and better wine than this, and we are both too old for the back street.”

He is very still. The brazier ticks again, an insect noise, an accounting. “There is a sealed matter,” Lentulus says, “that I have recently unsealed.”

“Then we will need the other room,” Antonia says. And is surprised — she logs it with Diomedes precision, beneath the ribs, where the house's third set of books has always been kept — that her voice does not shake on any figure at all.

There is a kind of negotiation the Basilica performs with the doors open, in which nobody appears to be conceding anything and yet, by the afternoon, sums have moved into better light. The Greek conducts it at the speed of furniture. He allows a man’s first figure to stand in the small room until it has heard itself in the local acoustics. Often the figure revises its own tailoring without anybody laying a hand on it.

Diomedes watches this process with the professional envy of a man who usually has to manufacture silence from noisier material.

“You charge for the interval?” he asks, when a client has left to consult a partner who is probably his own conscience in a different cloak.

“I charge for documents,” the Greek says. “Intervals are included where character requires assembly.”

Antonia has learned from him that a price is not always a number. Sometimes it is the right to appear unbought while selling. Sometimes it is the day of payment. Sometimes—most expensively—it is a form of words that will let a man go back to his house and describe a concession as a tradition.

Today the tradition under design has an olive smell.

“The estate wants time,” the agent says, for the third time in increasingly patrician Greek.

“The estate has had three centuries,” Antonia says in Latin. “It may rent a quarter at the market rate.”

The Greek neither translates nor mitigates. It is one of the silences that make his outer office such a good room in which to find out what one actually meant.

Diomedes produces a copy, then another. Paper begins to acquire company. Hands that were recently performing ancestry are now reaching for seals with the practical hunger of people closing shutters before weather. Nothing has been forgiven. Nothing has been disguised as patronage. The figures sit there, unglamorous as water in a correctly cut channel, and are allowed to find their level because no one in the room is being paid to pretend the level is a family crest.

The Greek's other room has a window, which is how she knows it is for the expensive truths. The Forum below goes about its immortal afternoon: litigants, changers, the column-drums inching on their rollers toward a war already won and not yet tall enough to read. Lentulus stands at the window because the sentence he is carrying has no chair designed for it in any room he owns.

“My father left a tablet,” he says. “I opened it last night. Nine years late. We are a family that opens things late and calls it dignity — those are his words; I am reduced to quoting him, which he would count as the estate settling itself.”

Antonia waits. Waiting is a skill she has, like counting in darkness. She can do it with her whole body arranged to look as if she were doing something else.

“The child lived.”

The room does not change. That is the thing she will remember: the drums on their rollers, the ticking brazier, a money-changer's laugh arriving whole through cedar and glass. The world declining to fall down over the oldest news in it.

“I know,” she says.

She watches it cross him — that she knows, that she arrived at the most expensive truth in Rome ahead of its legal owner, through thread — and what crosses him, under the statesmanship, is a young man of twenty-four being corrected on his scansion by a girl with a stylus behind her ear. *Unpaid*, she had said of the faithful woman at the loom. He had laughed. It had been the laugh that did everything, in the locked room, on the stairs.

“By what route?” he asks at last. “Forgive the Basilica manners. In this building one asks for the route before the substance. It may be a—” His mouth finds a word he does not use. “A family technology.”

“Thread,” Antonia says. “A Greek buying it at the seller's price. A tanner's widow. A guild book with a better column than the Senate's.” She gives him the order of the evening in her own case, since he gave her the phrase, unpaid, and a debt is a debt in this room if anywhere on earth. The vats, Diomedes' patience, the hennaed fact, the money stopping when the men who paid to forget had finished dying. She does not give him Baiae. Baiae is not on this agenda; Baiae has been itemised once today already, by a dead man on wax, and she will not have her stairs performing in a banker's window room as evidence. She gives him the years as figures, because figures, she has it on good authority, are his poetry. “Nineteen. Arrived, as the guild so beautifully files it. Taught to read by a woman who counted stairs so they wouldn't count her. Counting, always, before you ask. It is apparently the family trade, on every bank of the river.”

“Alive and —” Lentulus stops. There are words a man's furniture contains that he is only now dusting. “Flourishing is a banker's word. Let me do better. Is she *unharmed*, Antonia, by the manner of her keeping?”

“She is harmed by everything Rome charges her class for, and by nothing her parents —” Antonia deploys the word the dealer's books never contained, “— in Transtiberim could keep off her with the money stopped. She is betrothed to the bakery. Her future mother-in-law inspects her reading as a risk to the yeast. Her father is dying at the pace water finds things, joking from the rail. She has been told nothing except by omission, which is this city's first language, as you and I have grammars to prove. So: no, she is not *unharmed*, Lentulus. Nobody in this story is, except possibly the crocodile in Serapion's version. She is alive, and pointed, and hers. That is the sum I found it under.”

At the window, not seeing the Forum's immortal afternoon in any sense a foreman would accept, Lentulus says, “I asked my father once.”

“I know the sentence he sold you.” She does not make him produce it. It is the one mercy available in the room at these rates. “Your tablet evidently invoices it better than I can.”

“It says the person wronged is not me.”

“Then for once the estate papers and I are in agreement. Do not make me say so in the Senate.” It comes out drier than she priced it, and he — damn him, damn the grey at his temples and the shoulder that aches in warm water and the boy of twenty-four still standing in the ruin like a column drum left on rollers — he comes within an ace of smiling, and the room does not change, and the drums below go on inching a war toward legibility, and that, she thinks, is how the world actually performs its earthquakes: in a banker's back room, between the courses, with nobody's hands doing anything recordable at all.

“My wife will have known the route too,” he says quietly. “Servilia hears things at the speed other people decide. When I tell her — when I confirm to her, after nineteen years of her knowing at oyster-depth — she will ask what I intend in the exact language of logistics. She made a marriage work like rain on a roof. It is only fair to warn a creditor.”

“Warn her that I count faster.” Antonia lets the lamp into it — the smallest warmth, as before, and then the lamp is trimmed and they are back to business, because business is what will fill a bakehouse share and keep a fuller’s guild book dull, and dull, in both their houses tonight, is a form of love Rome will never carve. “The settlement, Lentulus. Not charity, we are agreed. Not a scene, we are agreed twice, in your father's Latin and mine. Tools, extended. The oven share, through the guild as a Baiae bequest: the mistress her mother served, dying conveniently rich and childless, two quarters ago; Diomedes will draft her posthumously, he has a hand for the dead. A sum for Vibius's pyre that arrives as guild money, because a fuller who held a rail and joked is not to be buried at a senator's discount. Nothing with your name, my name, a date in the old emperor's winter, or a smell of the Palatine. If you cannot settle it without spending her, then, in the phrase your family is buying from me this year at a premium — *you are not to settle it*.”

“I can settle it.” He turns from the window decisively, the statesmanship reassembling over the forwarding address like a toga over a shoulder that aches. “Through Soter, through the guild, through the Greek's other rooms. Papers that point at Campania and die there. Servilia will see the drafts. She has a better eye for a hem—” He catches the metaphor arriving from across the river and lets it stand. “—for respectability, than any clerk alive. On those terms, Antonia.”

“On those terms,” she says, “the house accepts your father's apology, at the rate of interest he so carefully specified: none to himself, all of it forward.”

“Across the river,” he says. “A fuller's household.”

“Vibia Prima. Nineteen. She sews for my house. She is betrothed to a baker and corrects your son's manners. She counts stitches with her mouth open.” Antonia's hands find each other and are correctly folded before she has decided anything. “She does not know. She will be told by me, when I judge it, in the order of the evening — you may invoice me for the phrase; it was good advice — and not as a weapon in a committee war or a dowry negotiation or a governor's application. She is not paper, Lentulus. She is the one thing in Rome I did not build, and I will not have the Cornelii spending her.”

“My father spent her. In advance, at a discount.” He turns from the window. “I am not here to reclaim her, Antonia. A nineteen-year-old with a trade and a betrothal is not property to be refiled because two people have finally audited their twenties. I am here to tell you that I know, a day after knowing, and that my wife—” his mouth makes the small, wry shape that in the Senate precedes a concession “—has known since the oysters, apparently, and we will settle money on the household in a form that cannot be traced to the Palatine. Not charity. A debt. My father was precise about the currency, at the end.”

“The fuller is dying,” Antonia says. “Slowly, of the cough they all get, drinking their trade. Settle it as a widow's portion for a wife already dead and he will refuse it. Settle it as Prima's dowry, enlarged — tools, and a share in the bakehouse, paid through the fullers' guild as a bequest from a Baiae mistress her mother served — and the baker's mother will inspect it like a hem and find it respectable. I have thought about this, Lentulus. I have had, by my count, rather longer with the information than you.”

“By how long?”

“Four days.” She lets the smallest warmth into it, the way a lamp is let into a room. “I count faster. It is my civic religion.”

The Greek comes in when the expensive truths have had their hour, because Eutyches has an ear, through cedar, for the moment a conversation stops needing a room and starts needing a receipt. He carries nothing. In his office, carrying nothing is the highest denomination; clerks exist to have hands. He looks at his two clients — neither of whom, officially, is in his office today, at this hour, in these chairs — with the expression of a man pricing weather over two harbours he insures.

“Madam. Senator. The small room gives satisfaction?”

“The small room is a lesson in architecture, Eutyches,” Antonia says. “My compliments to its deafness.”

“The house is always better for a compliment it cannot record.” He does not sit. Bankers in their own rooms are like priests in temples: sitting is for the laity. “There are matters the senator's man and madam's Diomedes have been *not* drafting in my outer office for some twenty minutes. Drafts of that quality usually require a Greek to become invisible in a third language. Guild money, I apprehend. Campania. Tools. An oven with a bequest for a figurehead.” He spreads empty hands, a liturgy. “I apprehend nothing. Let us proceed as if apprehension were my trade, which in the evenings, madam, as you taught me over a silversmith's plate, it is.”

It takes eleven minutes. Antonia counts them against Diomedes' breathing in the outer office, audible, she would swear in any inquiry, through cedar. Sums are mentioned the way fleets are mentioned at her tables, as climates rather than claims. The Baiae mistress is invented with a sound estate and a death at the right quarter. The fullers' guild becomes, on paper, what it has always been in fact: the place where decency keeps its chest. Soter, produced from the outer office with a colleague's tan still on him, bows to Antonia with an exactness that tells her he knows, and to Lentulus with an exactness that tells her he will forget, in the manner of his profession, which is also Onesimus', which is also, if they are all honest in this building for once, hers.

Nobody signs anything with a name that occurs in any history Rome will write. That is the whole ceremony. It is, Antonia thinks, watching a Cornelius put his seal — not the cornel sprig; a steward's seal, a working seal, chosen for the walk across the city — to a guild bequest drafted by her bookkeeper's second hand, the first entirely honourable document his family has produced in this matter in twenty-one years. The honour consists, precisely, in its being a well-made lie in the service of a kept girl's tools. Rome runs on such documents the way it runs on aqueducts: what is sent downhill arrives; the maps claim it always did; the lettering, where it works, is small and unglamorous.

In the outer office the Spanish silver man is still not negotiating his loan; the equestrian widow's freedwoman chaperone is folding expectations into a document case with a grandmother's severity. Diomedes falls in two paces behind Antonia without a word. Onesimus — for a man arranging somebody else's conscience, he has materialised with a senator's dry cloak over his arm as if the Basilica rained indoors — falls in behind Lentulus at the identical, professional distance. The two retainerships pass each other in the doorway with the merest acknowledgement, fellow-craftsmen at a job of work: *we were never an inquiry.*

At the Basilica steps the afternoon has gone the colour of wet bronze. Lentulus stops — against policy, against logistics, against Servilia's whole science of arrangement, he stops in a public place beside a veiled woman whose house his committee inspected at dawn a quarter ago, in view of litigants, changers, and a man selling little clay columns no taller than a thumb, already spiralled, already triumphant.

“You count faster,” he says. “It is minuted.”

“See that the minutes are general, eventual and dull, Lentulus.” She does not look up from the business of descending steps in a veil at the correct angle for a woman of no family attaining the street without assistance. “And see to the clause. I am told Athens is asking who in Rome can draft. Athletics aside, it would be a waste of a Greek education — yours, your tutors', mine — if the answer were Postumus.”

He descends on his side of the stair, into his people's order. She descends on hers, into Diomedes' two paces and the walk uphill to a house that will want its mistress back by sections: fires first, then women, then money, then truth, in the proportions that keep all four lit. Neither of them looks back. Looking back, on Basilica steps, in sight of the changers, would be a scene, and scenes are taxed to women, and they are both — it has taken them until their forties and a banker's cedar to learn it on the same afternoon — finished with being billed for other people's theatre.

Diomedes does not ask until the Caelian street. Even then he asks in bookkeeper's Greek, two paces back, at the gradient where the muleteers swear and a man's breath is his own again: “The account, madam?”

“Closed in the first book. Open in a fourth.” Antonia passes the fuller’s workshop whose lamps her house keeps filled at a neighbour's rate, and the dealer's daughter who was married out of her dining room waves from a door with a baby already itemised on her hip like good news. “We have acquired, Diomedes, a general registry, a clause in draft, an oven in Transtiberim and an understanding with a dead man's apologies. Enter the first three at their market prices.”

“And the fourth?”

“Leave the column blank.” Barbatus is opening her own door with the weather of him, lamps coming up by sections behind his shoulders, the house performing its evening overture: water, linen, Ammonius disputing saffron with a continent. “A completed book, somebody across the river says, is a dead one. We are in the keeping business, Diomedes. We leave spaces where the count isn't finished.”

Below, the drums have stopped for the day; a foreman's shout, the long creak of history parking itself. The two of them stand in a banker's back room between a dead father's Latin and a living daughter's needle, each in possession of the other's ruin in its most portable form — his olives in her book, his name in her locked room, her past in his cupboard — and what they make there, without touching, without the back street or the brazier or any of the instruments their younger selves mistook for fate, is the first entry in a ledger neither of them will ever show the tax office: an agreement to be, for once, jointly and severally, *careful* at somebody else's expense no longer.
