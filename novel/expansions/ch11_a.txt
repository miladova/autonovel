The rain has been deciding since the third hour. It is the Roman winter kind that does not fall so much as take up residence in the air, insinuating itself into wool, into the joints of old houses and older shoulders, into the acoustics of an atrium so that every household sound arrives with a small, wet echo, as if the building were being read back to itself in a poorer copy. Clients have sent boys with apologies shaped like fish. A cousin has sent fish shaped like an apology. Servilia has deployed both where they will do the house credit: the fish to the kitchen, the apologies to the steward's book, in which *called, weather* is a category of loyalty with its own rate of interest.

Lentulus has spent the day, accordingly, in the company of things he can postpone. There is the aqueduct business, early yet, a leaking arcade on the Praeneste road that two municipalities are using to conduct a feud by other means; he has written three letters about water that say, in descending order of candour, repair it, pay for it jointly, and stop writing to me as if gravity were a party. There is the committee draft, general, eventual and dull, as agreed in this house by a power who did not need to be named in the minutes. There is a speech for the harbour dues that his future relations by marriage require and that he keeps revising toward a decency its beneficiaries would find alarmingly principled if they read drafts, which, in the manner of rich men, they will not.

At intervals — the household could set a water-clock by them; Onesimus, forgetting professionally, does — he passes the atrium. The masks are being cleaned today, the year's smoke sponged off by two slaves under Servilia's steward's eye, in a ceremony the house performs the way other families visit tombs: regularly, and with gossip. Grandfather Gnaeus comes down off his cupboard into safer hands than any that ever carried him in life. His wax face, undarkened for an hour by the sponge's diplomacy, looks younger than his son's memory allows, which Lentulus finds an impertinence in a material. Under the mask, the cupboard's interior shows for a moment — scroll-cases, a campaign chest's worth of family paper, the particular case — before the steward, who knows which things in this house are seen by appointment, moves his body a fraction into the light.

“Shall I have the case's leather fed, dominus? The damp—”

“Leave it,” Lentulus says. “Leather in this family is expected to endure climate.”

He has left it, in various formulations, for nine years. A man can leave a thing in a sentence for nine years the way a house can leave a room locked: by building his daily routes around it until the skirting of it feels like the architecture itself. The tablet came to him at his father's will-reading in a winter of committees — older wars: a governorship refused, a consulship (suffect, footnoted) accepted, Marcus loud as a gate at eleven, Cornelia not yet arrived, the nine-day girl newly unspoken-of in the grammar of previous consulships. The notary had read the item without emphasis, *a sealed tablet for my son's eyes*, in the tone men use for the minutes of other people's earthquakes, and Lentulus, even then, filing things between quarters in a system he would later learn a woman across the city had invented independently and better, had put the case under his grandfather and told himself it concerned the estates.

It might have done. His father, on his deathbed, had talked estates for a full day — drainage, a bailiff's peculation handled by Philodorus' successor with Philodorus' own knife, trimming, trimming — and had then, in the small hours, asked for his seal and a fresh tablet with a clarity the household reported, awed, as the old man *sitting up in himself again*. Philodorus was nine months dead by then. The old Gnaeus wrote alone. What a man writes alone, after a day of drainage, with death already in the room gathering the letters at the ends of lines, is unlikely to be drainage. Lentulus has known this the way he knows most structural facts about his family: exactly, at the back, with the accounts.

Marcus comes home at dusk, wet through from the baths that are his postings abroad, and brings the rain indoors on his shoulders and in his news: the inquiry is filed, the prefect's dullness is being quoted as integrity, an Alexandrian in the warm room has a version of last week's death in which the physician arrives by chariot. Cornelia, at the far side of the garden room, is practising at half volume because rain on the roof turns the house into an instrument she is not officially playing. Lentulus watches his children being young in a wet house at the exact ages — twenty, sixteen — at which their parents, in two different cities of the same empire, were being arranged and sold respectively, and the thought does not so much arrive as take the seat it apparently had reserved: *everything in this family requires a death to become sayable. A man has died at her table, laughing at a tax farmer, with a singer holding his shoulders.*

Servilia, from the doorway, sees it arrive. She sees everything arrive; it is her quartermaster's genius and her marriage's weather. What she says is, “The household will eat early. The rain.” What she does, over the next hour, is clear the atrium the way stages are cleared in the theatres when the machine-god is expected: no clients, no lessons, the masks back in their cupboards cleaned and looking younger than their descendants, a lamp ration calculated for a long sitting, and the steward informed, in the sentence Lentulus will later know word for word, that the master is reviewing the estate papers and is not to be improved upon by attendance.

She does not say *open it*. Servilia has never spent a direct order where an arrangement would do the work at half the price and twice the dignity. She arranges. And Lentulus, who has been commanded by emperors in both languages the libraries keep, goes into the atrium at nightfall with the rain sending its small messengers down through the compluvium, in the old rhythm, the one that measured his childhood lessons, and puts his hand, at last, to the cupboard that sticks in winter.
