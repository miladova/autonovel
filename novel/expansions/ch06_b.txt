“Reading,” Marcus says. “Instead of sleeping.” He has found the one dry cushion in the room by instinct and taken it by right of pedigree, and now he addresses Prima across Daphne's mending with the air of a man discovering a province where his coinage is not yet legal tender. “What does a fuller's daughter read? This is not baths talk. I'm asking.”

“Accounts,” Prima says, “that other people do wrong in the second column. Letters for the women in our street whose men are at Ostia with the fleets — reading them, writing back; that's most of reading, in my street: other people's news, held steady.” The phrase arrives in her mouth with a familiarity she can't invoice; painters of words would call it inheritance. “And a book my mother kept. Not a book. Leaves. Recipes. Counts. Names of women in Baiae with the stairs they worked and what the houses owed them when they died. She called it her *ledger*, though there was no money in it that ever got paid.”

Daphne's needle stops. Daphne, who can hear a false note through a closed door and a true story through Vestals, says, “Your mother kept a ledger of the unpaid.”

“She kept the stairs counted. So they wouldn't count us.” Prima bites a thread. “When she died the guild women wanted to burn it with her — *her things*, they said. My father pottered about with the honey and said no, very quiet, the way he says things that are already in the accounts. It's in my work-box now, under the needles. It weighs nothing. That's how you can tell it's ours.”

Marcus is looking at the work-box with an expression his tutors were paid to correct and didn't, the exact face Diomedes will see in the spring on a banker's clerk being shown a fourth set of books. “My family keeps the opposite kind,” he says. “Everything paid, entered twice, three hundred years of it, and nobody can say where the money *is*. My sister is the money now. We're trading her for a harbour.” He says it to the work-box, having learned in the last four minutes not to say it to the room. “I shouldn't talk about her like that. You told me. I'm — practising on the furniture.”

“Practise on your father,” Prima says, and then, because she is nineteen and has been correcting a senator's heir for an evening in a house her father has forbidden her to eat in, she hears her own audacity arrive like a dropped tray and has to decide, fast, whether to pick it up.

It is Daphne who picks it up, with a singer's timing, threading a needle by a light she seems to be personally on terms with. “Fathers,” she says, “are the advanced classes. Furniture first. Then brothers. There will be examinations.” She holds the strap up: the tear is a rumour now, a place where light changes its mind. “My father sold my contract to a touring company when I was fourteen, against a debt with the word *honour* in it. The first thing Antonia did when this house bought me out was give me my own copy of the accounts, every quarter, to keep. I cried over a balance sheet. Singers are not supposed to. It was that or the fish course, she said; the house has a policy about doing both at once.”

“Everybody in this house has a policy,” Marcus says. “It's like the Senate, with food.”

“It's like the Senate,” Daphne says, “with the women counting.” It gets the room's laugh — Prima's too, unwilling, unfiled — and into it Barbatus puts his head round the door with a dry cloak over his arm and the expression of a man appointed by the weather.

“Young master. Rain's easing. Street's deciding.” He looks at the sewing, the heir, the general constitutional position. “Mistress is doing the late round. She does it on feet like a tax audit. No noise until she's in a room, then the room owes her an explanation.”

“I was just—” Marcus begins, and gets up, and discovers, standing, that he has been in the one dry cushion and Prima's stool has been taking a drip from somewhere in the old ceiling with the patience of a province. He looks at the damp flower-dark stain being made, stitch by stitch, irrelevant by her shoulder. “Your seat is wet. All this time. You might have said.”

“I might have been offered,” Prima says, to her needle, at her usual rates, “the dry one.”

It is that sentence — its cadence, filed flat as a coin on a counter — that Antonia, doing her late round on feet like a tax audit, hears from the corridor with the towels for a different room entirely over one arm.
