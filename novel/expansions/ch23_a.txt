Diomedes has arranged the journey with his usual respect for the fact that bodies age less forgivingly than reputations. They travel in stages, by carriage where the road permits dignity and by mule cart where it permits progress. The driver, who introduces every emperor by the defects of the road built under him, regards Antonia’s tailored cloak with suspicion until she correctly identifies an approaching change in weather by the behaviour of smoke from a hillside kiln. After that he addresses her as an authority on the south and reserves his sedition for national topics.

“Trajan spends like a man putting his name where rain can read it,” he says. “Hadrian will travel like a man checking whether the ink dried.”

“Keep both hands on the reins,” Diomedes says. “Treason is easier to survive at a walk.”

Antonia watches Campania unroll with the business end of her mind doing sentry duty. There are villages white as knucklebones, black soil in patterns older than Rome’s paperwork, vines trained up trees in green marriages of convenience. There are also stairs: town stairs, shrine stairs, a flight beside a cistern down which girls carry amphorae with the economy of long sentencing. At one inn a servant brings a tray of cups up from a cellar and the past, having no manners, puts its foot on every step beside her.

She sleeps badly in a clean room. It offends her efficiency. She has slept soundly through taxation, death threats delivered by committee and a kitchen fire extinguished mostly in Greek. Here, southward weather gets into the shutters. She dreams not of Baiae exactly but of being counted by somebody else: coins, stairs, girls at a muster, all declining to stay in their columns.

Diomedes at breakfast says nothing and pours the watered wine himself. It is one of his liturgies. “The grove is two hours if the driver does not stop to assassinate geography.”

“And Baiae?”

“Visible from the upper terraces, madam. If required.”

“I am here to inspect olives, Diomedes.”

“Yes. And an institution is entitled to a view.”

She has decided en route what she will not do. She will not seek out the dealer’s successor to make an old office perform horror. She will not climb any staircase out of defiance, like a woman in a recitation. She will not, merely because her money now has a southern accent, ask the landscape for a signed confession. Ground is ground. People put the paperwork in.

And yet, at a fountain where they stop for the mules, she sees a girl of perhaps sixteen carrying a polished tray for a mistress in a litter. The girl’s face is shut like a well-kept shop. For one second Antonia’s own reflection crosses the tray’s bright metal, forty years old and on her way to inspect property, superimposed on a younger trade.

She looks away first. The girl does not need an ancestress appearing in the equipment.

“Drive on,” Antonia says.

The driver, who has been telling Diomedes about a cousin unofficially related to a man who once saw the emperor’s horse, climbs up with the air of a philosopher recalled to public life. “The coast makes everybody thoughtful, lady. It’s the salt. Preserves grievances past their season.”

“Then we shall invoice by weight,” Antonia says, and the cart goes on into the autumn light, which has the honesty of a debt finally presented in the correct office.
