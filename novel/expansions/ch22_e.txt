At home the announcement of the Syrian appointment is received with a discipline that would have impressed a legion. Onesimus carries the official summary on a tray, as if bad news also benefits from crockery. Lentulus reads it standing, gives no speech, and asks whether the kitchen knows Marcus will be late. This is so far from the expected senatorial response that the steward reports a *quietness*, a word the household runs on for two days like an emergency ration.

Servilia waits until evening. She finds her husband not among the masks—that would be the old theatre—but in the workroom where aqueduct returns are being copied in several hands, competently, into duplicates meant to survive both fire and vanity.

“Do you want condolence in the northern manner,” she asks, “or shall I have a district flooded so that you can be needed beautifully?”

“Are those the options in our marriage now?”

“They are the ones with paperwork.”

He lays down a schedule. “I wanted it, Servilia. Let that be entered. I wanted the province, the army, the opportunity to be Gnaeus Cornelius Lentulus at a great distance from the evidence. Then the year happened at home. It is difficult to outsource a conscience once it has acquired local staff.”

She looks at the returns: channels, leaks, an arcade whose repair will cause a market gardener to correspond, a fountain pressure table annotated in Lentulus own hand. His writing has become smaller and more legible. She has never mentioned noticing.

“The appointment will give the baths three days,” she says. “Then Hadrian will praise a building and the public interest will put on sandals. What will you do on the fourth day?”

“Inspect a settling tank.”

“Then we are not ruined.”

It is so close to tenderness that both look elsewhere. Servilia picks up one of the copies and points to a figure. “This is wrong.”

“It is an estimate.”

“It is wrong in the optimistic direction, which in a husband used to be called policy.”

“Correct it.”

“I beg your pardon?”

“You have the pen in all but furniture, in this house. Correct it.”

She does. The clerk, who has gone very still, later tells Onesimus that Rome ended quietly in a workroom and nobody bothered to notify the provinces. Onesimus instructs him to forget at the professional standard. The figure remains corrected.
