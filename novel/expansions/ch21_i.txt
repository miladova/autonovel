On the seventh day after the wedding, a parcel arrives at the Caelian from the Avidian house. It is not addressed to Antonia. That would be an incident, and the harbour families pride themselves on cargo that does not leap from the manifest. It is addressed, through the Greek’s outer office, to the tradeswoman who made the summer hangings in which a customer of the Avidii has lately admired the finishing. There is an order for two lengths, measurements to follow, payment by the piece. The hand on the attached note is a Secretary’s.

Diomedes presents it in the linen room with the expression of a man laying a treaty before an allied power.

Prima reads it twice: once for the commission, once for the join. “Whose thought was this?”

“A shipping family has many clerks.”

“Diomedes.”

“The thought has no return address. That is its charm and its limitation.”

Antonia watches her decide. On a different Tuesday, in a worse world, this might be patronage disguised as lace. On this one, the measurements are real, the price is market rate with a tendency, and the customer has seen the work because the work was good enough to be noticed in rooms where women’s labour ordinarily becomes scenery.

“I’ll send an estimate,” Prima says. “Thread extra, because their rooms are larger and it will matter.”

“An exact reply.”

“If it is her idea—” She stops before the family vocabulary rushes in and upholsters the sentence. “If it is anyone’s idea who cannot sign it, the estimate will still be an answer.”

She writes it herself, in her firm new hand: materials, time, conditions for hanging, no work accepted on mourning days still owed to her father. At the bottom, not in the main invoice, she adds: *The red can be reduced for summer if the room takes strong light.*

It is a professional sentence. It is also, across a city of veils and boards, one young woman telling another—hypothetically, deniably, at market rate—that she understands rooms and what glare does to colour.

Antonia does not inform her that Cornelia’s newlywed home faces, according to the schedule Marcus described in an unguarded moment to Daphne, west into the exact weather described. There are messages women are allowed to send without mothers annotating the postage.

“Seal it,” she says.

Prima presses the seal with the side of her thimble, because she has no family signet and does not intend to acquire a personality at a metalworker’s. The mark comes out indistinct and entirely adequate: a circle, a pressure, an answer.
