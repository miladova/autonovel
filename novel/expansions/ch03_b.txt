Consider the fish. Ammonius does, at the length other men give to mistresses. It has come up from Ostia in wet straw on a mule that is now, by house policy, being fed in the yard at a standard the muleteer describes to the kitchen boy as *senatorial*. The grain contractor, whose unannounced triumph the evening exists to celebrate without announcing, is a man from Puteoli with a face like a harbour wall and manners he has had built for him by a succession of wives, each better born than the last. He knows fish the way bankers know weather. He tastes Ammonius' sauce — the sauce has been sent back once tonight already, by Antonia, with a message consisting of the word *again* — and closes his eyes.

“My ships,” he says, “should carry this instead of grain.”

“Your ships,” says his partner, who drinks water after the fish course and is therefore, as Antonia has already filed, negotiating in earnest, “would arrive empty. The crews have votes too.”

It gets the laugh — from the table, from the corner where Philomela is resting between songs with a cup she is not drinking, for the voice — and into the laugh's warmth the partner slides the actual evening: tonnage, winter sailings, a clause about spoilage that has clearly been the subject of a war in somebody's office. Antonia, passing with the unhurried visibility of a hostess who is also the management, hears the clause resolve itself between the fish and the boar, in the presence of witnesses too well fed to be bought cheaply later. This is the house's true menu. Men book her rooms the way they book notaries: so that agreements acquire flavour and cannot afterwards be said to have happened in an atmosphere of duress. She charges for the food. The peace of mind is itemised as music.

The elderly advocate — he is called Mamercus, he is seventy-one, he has outlived two emperors, three wives and the fashion for his best courtroom manner, which was irony before irony was taxed — holds his usual court in the small room, and his court, as usual, is women. Tonight it is Daphne, between songs, and a widow of equestrian family who comes to the house, chaperoned by a freedwoman of ferocious respectability, to hear music her own household calls unsuitable for a mourning that has entered its fourth year and shows signs of being redecorated. Mamercus is explaining, with the patience of a man who has explained it in basilicas for money, why a will made in a fit of gratitude is the most dangerous document in Roman law.

“Gratitude,” he says, “is an intoxicant. Worse. Wine wears off by morning and a man may plead it. Gratitude *improves* overnight, like your mistress's stock, and by the time the witnesses are called he believes he owes his life to a person who has merely returned his seal-ring from a dining-room floor.” He looks sideways at Antonia, who is passing and has never, in eleven years, returned anything without first ensuring its owner fully experiences having lost it. “Present company professionally excepted.”

“You lost a stylus here in November, Mamercus,” Antonia says. “Shall I make a will about it?”

“You returned the stylus with a lecture on the price of ivory. I have left it to my grandson as a moral instrument.”

The widow laughs — startles herself with it, checks the freedwoman, finds no tribunal there — and laughs again, properly. Antonia marks it. There is a tariff of grief in Rome, precisely observed by the women it is levied on and quoted back at them by everyone else, and part of what her house sells, unitemised anywhere, is a room where a respectable widow may discover at seventy — no, at fifty, the widow is fifty and has been filed by her family under furniture — that her laugh still works at its original specification. She will book again. She will bring, in time, a niece. Empires of Antonia's kind are not built on the appetites Rome confesses to in the baths. They are built on the ones it doesn't: to be listened to, to be well fed without owing a marriage for it, to sit in a room where the women are professionally not shocked and the men are contractually not owners, and hear a very old advocate explain inheritance law like a love song.

In the great room the two equestrians have arrived, by way of Ammonius' boar, at the brink of their partnership, and are toasting it in a Falernian whose route to this table — a carter, a lesson, a cellar book — they will never know. One of them waylays Antonia by the serving door with the confidence of a man who believes charm should be volume-discounted.

“We want the big room in April,” he says. “For the signing feast. Forty covers. Can the house do forty?”

“The house has done sixty for men who sign bigger,” Antonia says. “April is three months away. Rome may fall, your partner may sober up, the price of boar—”

“Then it's booked?”

“It is *pencilled*,” she says, “which in this house means Diomedes has it in wax, and wax, young man, has carried the whole of Roman law for a thousand years. Come in February with a deposit and a list of who must not sit beside whom. That list is the feast. The food is what we serve while the list works.”

He laughs, delighted to be handled; men of his age and order pay extra for the sensation, which Antonia's day-book records without comment under *room hire, premium*. She moves on. The musicians change; Daphne rises to sing, and the room does the thing rooms do for her now, settling with a small, collective adjustment of cushions and standards. She sings a Greek air first, for form, and then a Roman street tune she has slowed down until it sounds like a hymn that has seen things, and the grain contractor — harbour wall, three wives, fleet impounded in a sauce — is seen by Philomela, and reported to the kitchen, to wipe his eyes with the back of his hand and immediately appoint the gesture to the pepper.

This is the second hour's approach. Antonia clocks it by the lamps: the boy has trimmed them once, will trim them again before the steward's hour; the second trimming is her signal to check the door-book and the street, because after it the evening belongs to whoever has stayed, and staying late in her house is a category of person she likes to choose personally. She is at the door-book, with Barbatus' thumb blacking the margin where he has checked a seal, when Diomedes comes up the corridor with the evening's messages on a tray he uses for the purpose — a nice distinction, his, between paper and food, which he maintains the whole of Roman administration fails to. Bills, a booking from a Spaniard with silver interests, a note from a lady whose handwriting Diomedes has placed, correctly, before unfolding it. And underneath, where a man who keeps three books in three hands puts the item a day will be audited by: a folded sheet with no seal at all.
