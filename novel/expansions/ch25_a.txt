The birth, when it comes, has the decency to come after the guild feast and before the worst of harvest weather. This is entered by Mother Livia as the child’s first act of household cooperation and therefore as a promising sign. The hanging has been brought across in a cart with Barbatus walking beside it like an honour guard who has seen too much history to be trusted with perspective. For one day the red flowers have framed a bakery door through which half of Transtiberim has passed carrying baskets, babies, toolboxes and opinions. The Caelian house sent no representative more controversial than Barbatus and a quantity of almond cakes listed as samples. The samples were inspected like hems and destroyed by acclamation.

By the time Lucia arrives, her curriculum has already been settled by four generations of women in two postal districts. Letters before thread. It is Prima’s sentence, but Claudia’s handwriting is all over its ancestry and Antonia’s tenderly withheld applause all over her restraint. Titus, faced with a daughter whose thumb rests during sleep in a position suspiciously like someone testing pressure, announces that she will also learn weights. “Letters, thread and whether a man is shorting the flour,” he says. “After that, poetry can queue.”

The name takes longer. Names do, in families that know how much damage a tidy inscription can do. Lucia for the light, say some. Vibia for the house that already knows how to keep her. Nothing from the Palatine, no ancestral stone unloaded onto a person currently engaged chiefly in appetite and volume. Marcus, informed by a channel so indirect it might legally qualify as weather, sends no name. He sends later, from far away, writing tablets of a quality Prima inspects silently and puts away because a gift need not be accepted at its sender’s pace to be retained.

Antonia does not attend in any capacity the neighbours could be asked to describe. She sends what is asked for, which turns out to be linen whose seams Eirene has pronounced too good for any infant likely to respect them, and a small hoard of coin entered through the guild as a customer’s customary present for feast work. Every instinct trained by a life of surviving rooms wants to make the gift larger. She does not. Prima has not asked to be carried. She has asked—by every sentence she has ever put into Antonia’s office—to be counted correctly.

On the third day, a note arrives at the Caelian not through a courier but with the bread order, folded between an invoice and a list of cushions for repair:

*Born Tuesday. Girl. Loud. Titus says defective silence encouraging. Letters before thread agreed across all departments. The child has your way of looking at a room before deciding whether to pay for it.*

There is no signature. The hand is enough.

Diomedes reads it because Antonia gives it to him to read, which is a new category in their working lives: documents surrendered by choice before they can leak. “A favourable report,” he says.

“File it with the hanging loan.”

“The third book suggests a more durable heading.”

“The third book,” Antonia says, “is between quarters.”

“So is the item, madam.”

It goes there. Some histories melt. This one is given a slightly better room.
