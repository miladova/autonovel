“I will tell you the sentence once,” Servilia says, still with her eyes closed, in the voice she uses for reading figures she has already appealed in private. “It was in our first month. Your steward — Philodorus' successor; the knife went with the office — was arranging a dinner to show the new wife the Campanian agents could be received without the knives. Oysters from Baiae were proposed. I said the house in Baiae had been sold, I understood, with the staff dispersed, and would the oysters still be reliable. You went —” She pauses. “There is no gesture. That was the information. You went entirely still, the way Felix says men do under the razor when a household steps on its own hem. My mother, who married me with instructions I have spent nineteen years deciding which of to be disobeyed in, taught me to read a husband's silences the way the house reads a steward's book: in the second column. Yours, over oysters, had a whole province in it.”

Lentulus sits on the edge of the bed in the dark room he has shared, on and off in the manner of great houses, for half his adult life, and looks at the shape of the woman who has been running the war in the adjacent room of every campaign he thought he was conducting alone. “And you said nothing. For nineteen years.”

“I said a great deal. About roofs, and girls' music, and what grain money smells of, and whether a son being twenty is a condition a family can insure. I did not say the word Baiae at that altitude again. A marriage, Lentulus, is a committee that reports when it is told to.” A small movement under the covers that might, in a lesser quartermaster, be a shrug. “You had a physician's statement for a conscience and a father issuing your vocabulary. I had a settlement, a mother, and presently two children who lived. One does not table motions in a house whose drainage one is still mapping.”

“I am —” He tries the apology drafts in the dark and finds them all, like the one he abandoned for Transtiberim, addressed to the wrong person in the wrong currency. Servilia, who can hear drafts the way musicians hear tuning, interrupts the attempt at cost to nobody.

“Do not perform it tonight. You have had a death become sayable, a tablet become readable, and a daughter become arithmetic, all before the lamps. The house has a policy about doing two of those at once; we learned it from a woman who holds our olives.” It comes out without acid, an entry read in the upright hand. “What does your father advise, in the document? It was written by a Gnaeus, Lentulus. It will have instructions. They will be excellently drafted and a generation too late in both directions.”

“‘Do nothing dramatic. Settle it. Choose better.’”

She is silent for exactly as long as a clerk verifying standing should be silent, and no longer. “Then for once the estate papers and I are in agreement. Frame the phenomenon.” A breath, in the posture of sleep entirely given up now, though her eyes remain closed as a matter of constitutional form: some things in this marriage are still negotiated without the vulnerability of looking. “The girl across the river — I will call her that tonight; tomorrow we may be issued better words at a higher rate — has a father dying and a betrothal being inspected. She does not have, unless Antonia is losing her grip in her old age, a senator due at her door with his conscience in a carrying chair. Money she can use, in a form with a hem she can show her future mother-in-law. Information she can keep, at an hour somebody she trusts chooses. Those are settlements. The rest is us, imposing a tax and calling it remorse. Your father had a phrase for that too, I collect.”

“He had phrases for most of it. It was his whole technology.”

“Then let the technology rust.” Servilia turns, at last, toward him in the dark. The rain has settled into the house's old rhythm entirely: compluvium, basin, childhood lessons, clients' weather, a fuller's cough in another quarter of the same city, everything arriving in the order of the evening at last. “I married a settlement, Lentulus, and I made it work like rain on a roof — by finding the holes. You will not now drill a new one over your daughter's bed in either house because a dead man has finally filed his paperwork. Are we in agreement, or shall I have the steward minute us?”

“We are in agreement,” he says, and it is, after *not olives*, the most intimate sentence available for cataloguing this year.
