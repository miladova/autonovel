The change is visible first in the volume of other people’s stationery. Couriers come off the eastern roads with dust in the seams of their cloaks. Contractors who were unbearable in January acquire, by June, the gracious fatigue of men who have already been paid to think ahead. In the stone yards the conversation shifts from what will be dedicated to what must be shipped: timber for bridging, iron, millstones, rope by the mile. The granaries are quieter about it, as granaries are, but quantities have their own eloquence if a man knows how to read a tally without making it sound like treason.

Hadrian’s letters from Athens are copied almost as soon as they are unsealed. They are not the letters of a mere traveller. They are inventories in the grammatical form of friendship: temples measured, notables sounded, grievances elegantly rephrased, offices considered from the far side of the sea as one might consider pieces left on a board in another room. Acilius, whose name appears in them with a warmth that has caused three younger men to revise their seating arrangements, gives Lentulus a summary before the baths have had it. It is an old courtesy. It is also a warning delivered at a temperature neither of them needs to name.

“There is praise,” Acilius says in the changing room, while slaves make a liturgy of towels. “Of your committee’s drafting hand. Not the clause itself. The *hand*.”

“A distinction with a view,” Lentulus says.

“Hadrian likes men who write as if words have weight. He distrusts men whose words acquire crowds.”

“I had a committee. Crowds were an administrative by-product.”

“You had a sentence, Gnaeus. It has begun to travel without your luggage.”

The appointee for Syria is not yet named in the official ink, but a safe name is being repeated with the confidence of weather already felt in the bones. He is competent. He is well connected. His domestic arrangements, so far as Rome can discover, contain no women whose filing systems might become constitutional history overnight. This last point is not made directly; directness is for engineers. It is conveyed by everybody’s sudden refreshment when his virtues are listed.

Lentulus hears the list and finds, to his own surprise, no stab of intimate outrage. There is disappointment, the old public animal lifting its head. There is also an odd sensation as of a door being shut by somebody else on a room he no longer lives in. All winter he imagined Syria as the place where the family account might be rebalanced by distance. Summer has introduced him to a more humiliating and promising theory: that distance is what has been applied to his obligations, at compound rates, for three decades.

A young secretary passes with a message for Acilius. The message is not for Lentulus, and the secretary makes the small, perfect bow men make when a document’s route is itself news. In another year Lentulus would have felt the omission as a wound dressed in protocol. Today he notices the secretary’s sandals are newly soled and that the man is left-handed. The city has become legible at floor level. It is not consolation. It may be tuition.
