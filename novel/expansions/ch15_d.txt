The days after develop their own winter offices. Prima comes to the Caelian on the Thursday, because the spring hangings are work and work, in her street, is how families inform the world that nobody has died yet. She brings the finished border for inspection — inspection now being a word with soup in it, per the house's arrangements — and Antonia, who has commissioned cloth from half the looms in Rome, receives it in the small room off the courtyard where the light is, with Eirene in attendance and Diomedes' stylus performing the liturgy of by-the-piece. Prima's face has the winter in it. It also has, at nineteen, the thing her mother bequeathed under *Kept* and her other circumstances bequeathed under nothing at all: the refusal to let a roomful of richer people tell her how to hold her own material.

“The count's off by three flowers at the return,” she says, before anyone can be kind at her. “I did them in again at my own prices. It's entered.”

“Show me the stitch that doesn't remember itself,” Antonia says, and it is out before the drill can stop it: not the phrase she audits other people's work with, but the one from a rainy night in a mending room, with a senator's heir getting rained on in the doorway of his education. Prima looks up. Her chin tucks a fraction. Her mouth is open on no number at all.

“Daphne's strap,” Prima says. “I remember.” She turns the border's underside to the light. “Nothing is ever that mended, mistress. Only the person who did it knows. To everyone else it's just the straight of the thing.” She ties off a thread that does not require tying, a fuller's daughter's certainty: *she will be told in this order*. “Father says to thank the house for the soup inspection system. He says he's being governed by women in two constituencies now and the empire's the better for it.”

“Tell him—” Antonia finds, in the third book's cedar-dark behind her ribs, a dozen sentences, all addressed to the wrong person: apologies, explanations, a report of a death falsely filed, a name. The drill, rehearsed like fire procedures, holds. “Tell him the house is governed the same way and charges more for it.”

She does not touch Prima's work with her mother's hands. She pays for it with a creditor's. The difference, God and the guild help her, is where she currently lives.

Barbatus's lantern does the Thursday dusk as escort furniture. On the bridge Prima says — and this will come back to the Caelian in the emptied lantern, faithfully, in a doorkeeper's Latin — that the house smells of roses even in winter, and that she dreams now of courtyards; of a woman counting coins in the dark, getting it wrong, beginning again. Barbatus reports it to Antonia in the doorway without interpretation, because interpretation is what kills messengers in every other sort of household, and Antonia stands a long moment among the evening's resuming preparations with her hands doing nothing at all for the first recorded time that winter.

“Get it right tomorrow, then,” Eirene says from somewhere near the lamps, in the tone of a woman announcing soup. “Cream before the Alexandrians, love. And your counts generally.”
