When she has gone, the atrium settles its morning accounts without her: clients to the street, breakfast to the clients who missed it standing, the clerk's book to the steward, the steward to the kitchens with the day's small change of favour itemised into meals. Lentulus dictates letters in his study with the windows open to the winter garden, where nothing is flowering and everything is being cut back by a gardener who conducts pruning as a branch of political theory. To the Campanian agent: patience, arrangements, the vintage invoked like a god with a good memory. To a fellow senator whose vote he needs on the harbour dues and whose son he has just, at ruinous expense of dignity, praised in the Senate toilets — the true committee rooms of Rome, where men stand in a row and tell each other the truth at a fixed distance — a letter of such warmth that his secretary, who is paid to believe dictation, raises an eyebrow he is not paid for.

“Read it back,” Lentulus says.

The secretary reads. The warmth, read back, sounds like upholstery.

“Send it,” Lentulus says. “Upholstery is what coalitions sit on.”

From the far side of the garden comes Cornelia's lyre, and the music master's voice, and the particular silence where a correction by hand would go if this household permitted what half of Rome permits and calls tuition. The master is a Greek of forty with a reputation he keeps bandaged, and Servilia's instruction about the wrist has clearly reorganised his entire method into metaphor: *as if the string were a thread, domina; as if the hand were coming home from a journey*. Cornelia plays. She plays the figure correctly, and then — Lentulus hears it because a father who has sat through committees develops an ear for the inserted clause — she plays it again with something added, a turn of her own, small and unlicensed and unmistakably hers, and the master's voice, after a pause in which a career is weighed against an ear, says, “Again, domina. The second way.”

Lentulus stands at his window with a letter about harbour dues going cold in the secretary's hand and listens to his daughter insert herself, note by note, into music older than the family. He thinks: this is what we are selling to the Avidii, and the word *selling*, which Marcus used at a dinner table and was rebuked for, stands in his own mind unrebuked, in a locked room, at his usual rates. Not the girl — the form insists, and the form is a kind of truth. The name. The thing the name will do for a shipping family's grandchildren in rooms this girl will decorate with a voice her mother issues like a ration: one song, not the Greek. He thinks of the Greek at the Basilica, in whose quiet office this family's name is presently sitting in a file, being patient like a river. He thinks — the garden, the pruning, the note again, the second way — of the other file, in the cupboard under his grandfather's mask, where a different accounting of his name has been sitting for nine years being patient like something worse than a river. Like a debt in a currency bankers fear to touch.

“Dominus?” the secretary says.

“Nothing. A correction.” Lentulus turns back to the letters. “In the letter to the agent. Where I say the family will make a payment signifying intention — strike *signifying intention*. Put: *the family will pay*. If we are to be hauled up our own aqueduct by our creditors, we may as well arrive with clean Latin.”

The secretary, who has written *signifying intention* for this family for eleven years, strikes it with a stylus stroke so deep it goes through the wax to the wood. Outside, the gardener cuts something back to a knuckle and stands looking at it with the satisfaction of a man who believes, professionally, in return growth. The lyre plays the figure a third time. The second way. It is better. Everyone in earshot of the garden, including the truth, knows it is better.
