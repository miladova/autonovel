The night before is measured in honey. Prima heats it at the hours the watch changes because the watch, passing the end of the street with its buckets, is the only clock the house can afford that works outdoors. First watch: he drinks, and jokes, in the whisper the room has agreed to call his voice this week, that the guild physician should be charged by the spoonful since the medicine is mostly her labour with thyme in it. Second watch: he asks for the bench by the frames, is refused by three women in council, and accepts the refusal the way he accepts weather in his trade, as a finding. Third watch: the cough takes up its residence documents and shows them to the room one by one, and Prima is up with the cup and the cloth and the other cloth, counting breaths with her mouth open the way she counts everything she is afraid of finishing.

By the fourth watch the street knows. Streets know these things in Transtiberim the way houses know them on the Palatine: through the plumbing, in its night register. A light in a fuller's window at an hour when fullers economise lamp oil is a sentence anybody passing can decline. The baker's mother arrives without soup, which is how the neighbourhood understands that the situation has passed the soup stage and entered government. She takes the fire from Prima without asking, as generals relieve exhausted subordinates, and sends Titus — flour still in his eyebrows from a night bake undertaken because tomorrow's bread does not stop for anybody's century — to fetch the guild women in an order she has clearly been rehearsing since the Saturnalia.

They come as the plural always comes: with shawls, basins, spare linen, opinions about water temperature, and a silence that is not the opposite of talk but its container. The eldest of them, the vats woman who has lately sold a great deal of thread to a Greek, puts her hand for one moment on Prima's shoulder — where a stripe would be in a family that promotes its daughters by sewing — and says, “Count with us, girl. It's work. Work's the ride.” And Prima, who has counted stitches, stairs by proxy, breaths, second columns and her father's sayings, understands that the washing of a dead fuller by the women of his trade is also a count: so many cloths, so much water no worse used, a body made ready with the competence the world has always demanded of women it declines to register.

Her father, laid out, looks less grey. The trade in him relaxes out of him like dye out of an unrinsed garment, and what is left is the man under the fuller, the one her mother saw at an island market counting a porter's load wrong and deciding a life on the correction. She washes his hands last. They have carried other people's clothing through urine and earth and out the other side clean for forty years, and they lie now with the nails trimmed by his daughter and the thumbs quiet, like tools set down correctly after use. She lays the needle in beside them, out of a bookkeeping no guild keeps: his making, her instrument; her making, his workmanship. The women see it and say nothing. In this neighbourhood a signature in a shroud is nobody's business except the fire's.

Then there is the house to be governed, because death in two rooms is also an administrative event. The guild chest sends its contribution with a clerk whose cough apologises ahead of him up the street. The pyre place outside the walls is spoken for, at the poor men's hour, with wood whose price Mother Livia negotiates down by a method involving the words *the bakery knows your family*. Titus's father, an oven of a man who speaks in loaves, sends bread for the mourners and stays away himself, from a delicacy the street understands: too many father-shaped men in a room spoil the proportions of grief. By the time the winter light gets its one bar into the yard, the household has become what Vibius made it for thirty years it will not have him — ordered, ferociously, like a thing being held steady for a woman who is about to have to walk behind it through the rain holding nobody.
