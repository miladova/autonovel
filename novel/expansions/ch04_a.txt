Lentulus arrives with his own oil flask, his own strigils in a case his grandfather's campaign chest would recognise, and a slave, Onesimus, whose entire public personality is a talent for standing in the one spot in any room where a senator's conversation cannot be overheard by accident. Onesimus has served in this capacity for eleven years. He has heard, in that time, three treasons discussed as hypotheticals, one discussed as a menu, and enough provincial appointments traded over naked men exercising to staff, on paper, a second empire. He forgets professionally. It is understood in the household that when Onesimus is freed — the will is written, the sum named, Servilia has seen to it with her usual prospective kindness — he will forget his own manumission ceremony by the following morning, out of habit.

The exercise yard first: the ball game is at the stage where old friendships are being audited at speed. A former praetor with a knee like a rusty hinge is holding the centre by reputation alone, directing younger men with a general's economy: a chin here, a lifted eyebrow there, the whole art of command reduced to upholstery. Lentulus takes three rounds, no more; more would be a statement about Syria, less would be a statement about his shoulder, and the yard reads statements the way the tax office reads returns, professionally and without charity. He plays well enough to be unremarkable, which in this company is a form of excellence no one will ever carve.

In the anointing room the news is hung up with the clothes. A letter from Athens is doing the rounds in summary — Hadrian has been archon, Hadrian has been initiated, Hadrian has opinions about a temple that has waited six hundred years for a roof and will now, the summary implies, be roofed by personality alone. A young attaché recently returned to Rome is retailing it all with the exhausted air of a man whose luggage contained, besides linen, the future.

“He spoke Greek to the masons,” the attaché says. “Not through a secretary. To them.”

“Barbarous,” says the former praetor, pleasantly, towelling a knee that predates the fashion. “An emperor-in-waiting who can talk to workmen will expect answers. There'll be an end to government.”

Nobody says *emperor-in-waiting* again. The phrase does a small, satisfied circuit of the room anyway, the way the pickpocket did the Forum crowd, feeling for attention to settle in. Onesimus, at the wall, forgets it in three languages. Lentulus, submitting to oil and the scraper, files it where he files all such futures: under *weather, eastern, approaching*. Trajan is not old as emperors go and is fit as a man can be who has spent his life being carried toward wars expressly to walk through them; but the succession in Rome is a bath-house topic the way rain is a farmer's, not because it is imminent but because everything planted waits on it. A Syrian command under Trajan is a career. The same command remembered under his successor is either a recommendation or a headstone, and a man calculating the one, in warm rooms, naked as an egg, does well to calculate the other.

The scraper finds the Moesia shoulder and the old injury sends up its small, familiar dispatch: *present, not urgent, keeping the minutes*. He got it falling from a horse that had been described to him by a quartermaster as steady, a word he no longer allows in documents. History, in other people's telling, has promoted the fall into a skirmish; a nephew once asked about the skirmish at dinner and Lentulus, to Servilia's audible approval down the table, described the horse instead, at length, until the skirmish died of documentation. It remains his favourite of his own speeches. There are so few of them with a horse in that he is willing to sign.

“Your son was here at the sixth hour, dominus,” says the bath attendant, who is called Felix too — half the useful men in Rome are — and who retails the household's movements to the household as a kind of preventative medicine. “With young Avidius. They won at ball. There was a matter of a wager settled amicably, and the young master paid a debt *for* a friend which the friend had intended to settle by talking. The talk, if I may say so, was filed as insufficient.”

“Whose debt?”

“The friend's name was not in my hearing, dominus. The sum was in everyone's.”

Lentulus holds out his arm for the towel with the expression of a man receiving a committee report in a format he prefers to the Senate's. Marcus paying other men's debts: there is a future in that sentence in several denominations, and all of them will arrive at his atrium in the end, because that is what sons and aqueducts have in common in his life at forty-five — what is sent downhill comes home by the plumbing. He says, “If my son's generosity is to be a season, Felix, the house would like the weather early.”

“I shall keep the sky watched, dominus,” says Felix, and they go through into the heat in their order, senator, slave, oil, news, like a small procession at a dedication nobody has inscribed.
