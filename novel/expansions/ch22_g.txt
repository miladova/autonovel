Barbatus delivers the answer at the Palatine’s garden gate, not the atrium door, because a man can spend twenty years in service and still develop new specialisms. Marcus is there at the appointed hour in travelling clothes for a journey of forty paces back into his own house. The folded paper has no seal. It has only his name on it, written in a hand that makes no attempt to enchant him.

He does not open it on the street. He takes it to his room and reads it by the window over the garden, twice. The first time he reads for what is forbidden; the second for what is permitted, which is less and therefore more.

*Nineteen years of doing nothing, noisily.* He hears her say it in the tone of the dining room, when she was only a needlewoman who had done the unforgivable thing of being his family at him in public without papers. It is not condemnation. It is an estimate. Do the work at your own table, where no one can applaud you for it.

He goes down to dinner and behaves with such ordinary moderation that Servilia becomes alert. Lentulus, too, can see an unopened weather system at the table. Neither asks. The discipline is still new enough to be felt, like shoes before they take the shape of governing.

Afterwards Marcus knocks at his father’s study. Knock: another novelty. Lentulus looks up from a report on a fountain that has opinions.

“She wrote back,” Marcus says.

“I see.”

“Four lines. In Latin.” He smiles before he can arrest the development. “No Greek, Father. It is possible I have been overpaying for sincerity by the syllable.”

“A family weakness. We are reducing imports.”

“Am I to answer?”

Lentulus looks towards the desk where a fresh sheet had once covered a draft. “What does the letter say about the future?”

“It leaves the figures for later.”

“Then perhaps the answer is time.”

Marcus has no counter-proposal fit to be minuted. On his way out he pauses in the corridor, where the masks have had another season of being cleaned into surreal attentiveness. He says to the one whose face most resembles his own impatience: “Nothing, then. Noisily.”

Onesimus, passing below with a lamp, hears and forgets correctly. The household decides within hours that the young master has indeed become religious, though the cult appears to involve cups left unfinished for reasons no priest has yet been able to retail.
