Servilia finds him still in the atrium after the lamps have begun their small domestic campaign against the dark. She is in the indoors dress she wears when she is no longer distributing herself among guests, and she carries, incongruously for a woman who has made an art form of not being interrupted, a cup in each hand.

“Onesimus says you are consulting the management,” she says. “I have brought the committee wine.”

“The management is unanimous.”

“It frequently is, after death. It is their least burdensome qualification.” She gives him a cup. Together they look at the masks, which daylight has lately made cleaner and lamplight makes less forgiving. “Your grandfather looks as though the aqueducts have disappointed him.”

“My grandfather believed water reached the house by status.”

“Most men do, until a pipe is specified.”

For a while neither speaks. The house goes on around them: Cornelia’s music stopped in favour of sleep, Marcus somewhere abusing Greek in a private and improving manner, Onesimus converting evening into the shape it will need tomorrow. It is an ordinary peace, and Lentulus is sufficiently altered to notice that ordinary peace is not a substance automatically renewed by ancestry.

“Hadrian asked after me,” he says.

“So Acilius thinks we should be told.”

“It may mean nothing.”

“It may mean a provincial joke with good postal service.” She drinks. “Do you want it to mean more?”

The honest answer is in three columns and only one belongs in the atrium. “I want the work not to have been reduced to a scandal with one quotable sentence.”

“Then do work,” Servilia says. “Scandals dislike maintenance schedules. They lack the stamina.”

“And if that is all?”

She turns then, not towards the masks but toward him, which in their marriage is still less frequent than negotiations through architecture. “Gnaeus. A man may be remembered for a sentence he was forced by dawn, debt and a woman’s filing system to make true. It is not the worst thing written about this family on a wall.”

The use of his first name is so rare in her mouth that for a moment it has no address. Lentulus looks into his cup. “I am not forgiven.”

“No.”

“I do not expect it.”

“Then stop listening for it like weather.” She says it without cruelty. That may be what reaches him. “Accuracy does not applaud.”

He almost smiles. “I wrote that in a notebook. Or something unnecessarily close.”

“Marriage is plagiarism under management.”

They finish the wine in front of three centuries, who have heard worse settlements.
