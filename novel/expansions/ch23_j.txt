Before she leaves the estate, the Greek places one more document on the table. It is not an amendment. It is a sealed instruction, to be held in his office against the possibility that Prima herself one day wants evidence of the terms under which the money moved and the olive paper began.

“No names are to be exhibited,” he says. “No use may be made of it without her hand. If she never asks, it becomes ash at the direction of your estate.”

Lentulus looks at the seal. “A cupboard, with discipline.”

“A room,” the Greek says, “of the smaller kind.”

Antonia turns the document once but does not open it. She knows exactly what this restraint costs Lentulus, who has begun the year with a family talent for staging the opening of cupboards and calling the smell history. “Prima has papers of her own,” she says. “Guild, oven, commission accounts. This is only if the day comes when she wants the joinery inspected.”

“Then the joinery waits,” Lentulus says. “Quarterly.”

“Now you are becoming fluent.” She returns the sealed sheet to the Greek. “Enter it where no son with a metaphor can find it by romance.”

“The office has a drawer for poetry-proof items.”

Diomedes, who has been making a final inventory of amphorae count agree with itself after three attempts, looks up. “By the staircase returns?”

“By the documents nobody is to be grateful for,” the Greek says. “A growing department.”

It settles something for Antonia that the apology could not. The future has been left unlocked from Prima’s side. No revelation is waiting with trumpets. Only a banker, a seal and a woman not yet in the room whose permission is, at last, structurally load-bearing.
