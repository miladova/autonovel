The seating has been worked out by Servilia on a tablet she will deny exists. A dinner of this kind is a map of a country being negotiated: who reclines where, in whose sightline, within reach of which conversation, is the constitution; the food is merely the government's daily business. Avidius the father is placed where the room's light flatters a man of sixty and where the boar, when it comes, will arrive at his left hand like an ally joining a flank. The son is placed low and opposite Cornelia, who is present because daughters being *assessed* must be visible as evidence and invisible as persons, a branch of optics Servilia has mastered on behalf of a girl she is — though the tablet does not say so — also, in her way, protecting: from this couch Cornelia can see every face at the table and no face can study hers without seeming to. It is the best seat in the room. Nobody will ever tell the guests so.

Cornelia knows. Cornelia, who has been dressed into modesty with a neckline, understands her mother's seating the way she understands her music master's metaphors: as instructions encoded for a reader. From the protected couch she conducts her own assessment, in the hours' long, glazed traffic of being looked past, and her findings, had any committee thought to subpoena them, would have shortened several negotiations. Avidius the father eats like a man who has been hungry in fact and not in rhetoric, and respects a table accordingly. The son eats like a man being watched by a father who has been hungry. Her own father eats like a man making small, exact payments on an enormous loan of attention. Marcus eats like a man refuelling an argument. And her mother eats almost nothing, beautifully, the way generals visit frontiers: to be seen to have confidence in the supply lines.

The talk does its early circuits at the safe altitude: the dedication, the column scaffolding — “A war you can climb inside,” says a cousin of the house, one of the Arpinum line decanted into Rome dinners for ballast, “it's an end to reading, I call it. My books don't have stairs.” — the price of Spanish oil, a lawsuit about a warehouse wall at Ostia, which Servilia steers off the table and into the fish course with a sentence about boundaries being the true poetry of nations. Avidius laughs like a harbour — the sound of timber and money getting along — and Lentulus, who has been waiting for the Ostia wall to come up since a morning client gave it to him like a hot coal in a satchel, disposes of it into the same laugh with a proposal about surveyors over dinner rather than advocates over documents. It is his best moment of the evening. Avidius inclines his great head. Two old Romans have just agreed, between courses, that law is what you threaten and dinner is what you do.

Young Avidius, meanwhile, is being discovered by Cornelia to have a quality the betrothal market does not list because it cannot be faked at scale: he listens with his face. When the cousin from Arpinum tells the story about the augur and the chickens — a story this family has been deploying since before Cornelia was born, with marks on it like height lines on a doorpost — the young man laughs in the new places, the ones the story actually has, rather than the traditional ones. He asks her, across the low table, in the supervised gap between a toast and a sauce, whether she sings.

“I am sung about,” Cornelia says, before she can file the sentence under *the Greek one*, “more than I sing. In this house my voice is issued. One song, at approved houses.”

“My father issues my opinions on a similar system,” he says. “One per dinner, not the nautical ones. He says a young man should be ballast.” He considers the boar being carved with the concentration of a man watching a ship unloaded by a crew he respects. “I think ballast gets a poor report in this city. Ballast is why the cargo arrives.”

It is not wit, exactly. Her brother does wit, beautifully, like a man juggling with his grandfather's glass. This is something her education has fewer words for: a young man making himself unremarkable at exactly the rate that lets a room relax around him, the way her mother's seating lets a girl see. Money from grain, her mother says, when the girl reports nothing that evening and is read anyway. A boy who can wince. There have been worse foundations.

The fish course arrives on a platter with its own escort, as fish does in this house, and with it the evening moves, as Servilia's tablet always intended, from reconnaissance to the first real exchange of fire, which in Roman dinners is called compliments. Avidius raises his cup the width of a wall at Ostia toward his host.
