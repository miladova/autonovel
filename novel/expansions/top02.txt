Registration, in Diomedes opinion, is a form of theatre in which the actors are forbidden to know the plot but required to sign for the scenery. He returns from the office with three new interpretations of one old category and a clerk’s opinion that *related by commission* may, in time, acquire the dignity of a provincial cult.

“Can a category become respectable by being boring?” Eirene asks.

“It is the principal route,” Diomedes says. “Ask marriage.”

Antonia is reviewing the house schedule while Prima, in the next room at that stage of acquaintance, counts stitches aloud in a way that makes privacy difficult for arithmetic. The counting crosses the doorway in small exact numbers. Antonia does not listen as a woman listens; she listens as a banker respects noise from a mint.

“This one wants her name written as the guild writes it,” Diomedes says, indicating a line. “The office prefers her father’s formulation.”

“Then the office may make an appointment with her father’s ghost and come back in writing.”

“The system is not designed for tradeswomen with opinions on spelling.”

“The system is not designed for weather either, Diomedes. It survives by getting wet.”

A banker’s runner arrives with a request to see the Greek’s receipt, then thinks better of it under Barbatus gaze and asks for water instead. Eirene gives him water and a small cake—administrative routine in this house for men who might otherwise become information. He leaves saying the establishment is “very filed”, a compliment Diomedes receives like land.

Later, when the day’s papers have been shut away into their hierarchy of rooms, Antonia walks the courtyard and listens to the house settling into its evening ratio: kitchen, music, water, linen. It is a city in one address. Rome outside is engaged in ruling the known world by means of inscriptions and appointments. Rome inside, at its better rates, is engaged in knowing precisely where things are kept.

“Do you think we are safe?” Eirene asks, in the voice she uses for people rather than ledgers.

“We are legible.”

“Love, you say that as if it were better.”

“It is safer than being interesting in other people’s handwriting.”

From the linen room comes the small uninterrupted sound of counting, then a pause, then the number corrected. Antonia’s hand, unbidden, finds a wall and finds it dry enough for a different season of the year.
