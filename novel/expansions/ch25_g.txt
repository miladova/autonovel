There is a will now. There has always been a will—Antonia is not a woman who would leave Rome to compose her posthumous intentions in its own hand—but it has been revised by the Greek in a room so small that death itself would have to sit correctly. Eirene receives money and the right to choose her own address, which she announces will remain wherever the soup is properly seasoned. Diomedes receives an annuity and the care of archive categories no one else can be trusted to pronounce. Barbatus receives enough to retire into a door of his own, an idea he treats as a security risk.

The women’s savings remain unambiguously theirs, with a sentence inserted at Antonia’s insistence after the banker suggested that clarity could sometimes be detrimental to flexibility. “Flexibility,” she said, “is what a hinge does after other people have installed the lock. Write it straight.”

The house itself goes into an arrangement of shares, stewards and conditions too dull for any poet to steal. Daphne, if she wishes, will have an interest in the music side. Philomela’s funeral harmonies are to be encouraged by capital, within rates. The office copies are to survive whichever tax regime thinks itself eternal.

“Is there,” the Greek asks, with his face wiped to its banking level, “a family acknowledgment I should expect in another document?”

“There are acknowledgments in every document she has signed this year,” Antonia says. “None requires your office to hold its breath.”

He accepts this. Prima, by the name that has done the work,has her own papers. If an additional bequest ever travels, it will do so under instructions that survive public explanation. No child of either bank is to be surprised into dynasty by a seal after somebody’s funeral.

“Your late instructions,” the Greek says, “are structurally unromantic.”

“They are load-bearing.”

“A distinction history underrates.”

That night Antonia tells Eirene only the part concerning soup tenure. Eirene, who has known since the Subura when Antonia is consulting death as another difficult guest, says: “Are you ill, love?”

“I am in my forties and own property near drains.”

“So: mortal, with paperwork.”

“At the full rate.”

“Then set another cover in the dining room. I am not having us mourn your legibility before dinner.”
