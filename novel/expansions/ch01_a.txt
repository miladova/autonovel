The house wakes in sections, like a theatre being lit from the back. First the kitchen yard, where the night porter hands his keys to the morning boy with a recital of grievances against the weather, the watch, and a cat that has adopted the woodpile and is raising, by the sound of it, a dynasty. Then the kitchen itself: fires coaxed, yesterday's ash raked out and saved in a bucket for the fuller, because nothing in this house is thrown away until two people have agreed it cannot be sold, fed to something, or used to clean something else. Then water. A house on the Caelian drinks uphill. Every jar in it has come up the street on a human back, and Antonia, who carried water professionally in her youth, in a town built of stairs, has opinions about waste that she expresses without raising her voice and without repetition. The household can recite them. Guests, who are charged for waste in ways they never identify on the bill, are spared the recital.

Barbatus does the doors at first light, not because the street is dangerous at dawn — the street at dawn is full of bakers' boys and old women, Rome's true garrison — but because a door opened carelessly sets the tone of a day. He stands in the entrance in his vast, weathered calm and looks up and down the street the way he once looked across sand: not for enemies, exactly; for intentions. Tradesmen read him correctly and moderate theirs. A fishmonger's boy who arrived in November intending to short-weight the house on mullet now brings the mullet up to the threshold holding the scales like a votive offering. Barbatus has never threatened him. Barbatus, who spent eleven years being professionally threatened for the entertainment of thousands, has retired from the genre.

“The fish is better than the boy,” he reports to the kitchen, which is his way of passing an invoice.

Antonia does her first round while the house is still honest. Later, with the lamps lit and the hangings down and musicians tuning in the small room, the place will put on the face it is paid for; but at this hour it shows her what it is: a machine for converting money into ease, with all the belts and buckets visible. She checks the great dining room where last night's Alexandrians argued about harbour dues until the lamps drowned in their own oil. The floor has been washed; a couch has a wine stain blooming on its cover like a small purple province; somebody — a guest, a servant, history will not be told — has left a ring on the window ledge, gold, with a stone the colour of weak tea. She picks it up and holds it to the light. In her first years in Rome a ring like that, lost in her rooms, would have gone to the Greek by noon and come back as rent. Now she puts it in the box in Diomedes' office labelled, in his upright hand, *Guests' Property, Forgotten*, where it will wait to be claimed by a man who will arrive at dusk in a panic of discretion, having told his wife one story and his steward another. He will overpay the house for its silence in some future transaction, never knowing he has done it, and the ring will have earned more in the box than it ever would at the Greek's. This is what Antonia means when she tells the younger women that honesty is a long position. They think she is joking. Eirene, who has seen the box's contents turn over the years like a small, glittering tide — rings, a senator's seal-stone, once, memorably, a set of false teeth in a cedar case — knows she is not.

In the kitchen the pastry cook is already at war. He is from Alexandria, he is called Ammonius, and he regards Roman cooking the way Roman generals regard other people's countries: as raw material awaiting improvement. This morning's enemy is the price of pine nuts, which has risen, and the quality of the honey, which in his formulation has *fallen morally*. Antonia listens to the whole dispatch while tasting yesterday's stock. It is a ritual. He requires an audience with the power to fund him; she requires him to believe the funding is in doubt, because a cook who feels secure begins to cook for other cooks, and other cooks do not pay her bills.

“You may have the pine nuts,” she says at last, “at the old price, from the man in the Forum Boarium. Tell him the house remembers the short measure at Saturnalia. He will remember being remembered.”

“And the honey?”

“The honey is from our own man outside the Colline Gate. If it has fallen morally, Ammonius, it has done so in your storeroom, among your jars, under your seal.” She rinses the spoon herself — a hairdresser's daughter does not leave spoons; it is the same religion as the hair — and puts it back in its place. “Reform it.”

Ammonius bows with the grandeur of a man accepting a province and turns back to his fires, already composing the menu for a night still twelve hours away, while the kitchen boy — eleven years old, freeborn, apprenticed by a mother who came to Antonia first for a midwife's fee and stayed, over the years, for arithmetic lessons she pretends are recipes — mouths the words *fallen morally* over the onions with the deep satisfaction of a person stocking a phrase for later use. Antonia sees him do it. She lets him keep it. A house survives on what its people steal from it in language.

Upstairs, the women are waking in the order of their engagements, which is to say that Philomela, who sang until the second watch, is not waking at all, and has hung a strip of red wool on her door, the house's flag for *a voice is sleeping; knock and be billed*. Daphne is awake and practising scales at a volume calculated to annoy nobody who matters. There are rules here too, arrived at over eleven years and written nowhere, which is why they work: no woman in this house descends to the dining rooms before she has eaten; no woman entertains a guest who has struck a servant, once, anywhere, on any provocation — Rome calls this an eccentricity and pays a premium for it; no woman's earnings pass through any hand but Diomedes', and his pass through Antonia's every quarter, in her presence, with the tablets open. Twice a year a new arrival, hired from some lodging house by the theatres where the rules were different, tests the ones about money. Twice a year Antonia explains, sitting down, in the small room, that she did not climb out of Baiae on stairs of other women's wages and does not intend to be carried up fresh ones. The explanation takes four minutes. It has not yet needed repeating to the same woman.

She is on the stairs herself when the carter arrives early, contrary to instruction, and with only half the wine. She hears Barbatus admit him to the yard and hears, in the quality of the cart's complaining axle, a story preparing itself: the warehouses, the dedication crowds, a mule with opinions. By the time she reaches the yard the man has got as far as the mule.

“The other half,” Antonia says, “is where?”

“Coming, mistress. On the second cart. By the third hour, the fourth at the outside. The Forum—”

“The Forum was built over twelve years. It did not surprise the warehouses this morning.” She looks at the amphorae he has brought: sealed, stamped, the Falernian she ordered, a cheaper Campanian she did not, riding among them like a poor relation in a good cloak. She rests her hand, quite gently, on the shoulder of one of the impostors. “And these?”

The carter looks at Barbatus. Barbatus looks at the street. It is a conversation men have in this yard about twice a year, usually at the point where someone's arithmetic meets hers.

“A mistake at loading,” the carter says. “It being dark.”

“Then it can be unloaded in the light. Take the Campanian back, bring the rest of the Falernian by the third hour, and I will pay the full contract, including today's cartage, as agreed. Bring it by the fourth, and I will pay for the wine and not the cartage, because the afternoon of an imperial dedication is not when I conduct my cellar down a public street. Bring me another mistake in the dark at any hour you choose—” she takes her hand off the amphora “—and you may explain the darkness to the Greek at the Basilica, who holds your note for the mule. He holds most people's notes. It is why his office is so quiet.”

The carter begins unloading with the energy of a man who has just remembered an appointment with honesty. Diomedes, arriving with a stylus, records the amended delivery without expression. He has a category for this in the day-book. Every house in Rome has, whether it writes it down or not: *attempted, at dawn, in the dark*. Antonia watches a moment longer, to give the lesson its full weight of witness, and it is while she is standing in the yard among the amphorae, with the January light coming up over the roofs and the whole city beginning to make its dedication-day noise below the hill, that she hears the courtyard door, and Eirene's step on the stones.
