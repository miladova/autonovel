His mother comes in without knocking because in this house Servilia *is* the knock: rooms arrange themselves at her approach the way clients arrange their faces, and by the time she is inside the door, time has been made for her. She looks at her husband; at her son, in the junior chair for once, earned by earthquake; and at the scroll-case Lentulus has taken, somewhere in his son's geometry of standing up and sitting down, from the cupboard under his grandfather's cleaned face and set, unopened for the second time in a Lentulus's life, on the side table.

“He has it from the baths,” Lentulus says. “In the plumbing's version. Baiae money.”

“Then the baths are ahead of my schedule by a day,” Servilia says, “and behind my information by nineteen years. Marcus: your face is performing. Either arrange it or spend it. This is a difficult room to be young in; I apologise for the furniture.” She does not sit. She has never needed the room's permission for altitude. “Let us be clear in the first hand, since the second is where this family keeps its truths and the third melts quarterly. I knew your father had not come to our marriage as a blank tablet. I did not know a child lived across the river because your grandfather — whose wax is grinning in the corridor as if it had been cleaned for a festival — paid for my ignorance as he paid for almost everything: at a distance, through stewards, in a currency of other people's decades. Do you follow me? I am not the injured discovery in this study, Marcus. I am its management committee.”

Marcus looks at her with a young man's first, unscheduled respect, the kind no tutor can issue. “It doesn't reach you,” he quotes, because quoting is what is left when a family runs out of original sentences, and because his father said it nine minutes ago and Marcus is trying it for size in the room where it belongs. “The queue for blame.”

“It reaches me everywhere,” Servilia says calmly, correcting the sentence as a general corrects a map. “Blame is not the department. The department is consequences. Your sister, Marcus.” She says the word the way it is currently spent in this house, without additions, as though the singular were still legal tender; the room, taking it, knows exactly how much plural has just been managed. “Cornelia is to sign papers. Among the papers there will be settlements pointing in every decent direction: money to this house, a name to theirs, a roof re-roped, a father standing like a man who has given a daughter and not sold a storm. The Avidii have already surcharged this family for embarrassability — I believe the boy's father used the phrase *in figures*, which are his poetry — and I will not present them, on the doorstep of the registry office, with a stepsister in a fuller's guild for their cousins to rhyme with.”

“Then Cornelia is to be — not told,” Marcus says. He hears, even as he produces it, the family sentence: a young woman not told, by men who love her in the second column and file her in the first. “Father said: when the telling is for her benefit.”

“Your father,” Servilia says, “is learning a new ledger at an encouraging speed. Cornelia will be told by me, when she has a house of her own with a music room, a husband who pays her master properly, and a copy of every paper with her name in it in a box she can reach. That is not concealment, Marcus. That is timing purchased on behalf of a third party's safety, which is a thing this family is historically illiterate in and is, this season, taking tuition.” She looks at the scroll-case with the expression of a woman pricing furniture at an estate sale. “Will you show him the document?”

Lentulus opens it — not to read, because the tablet has already had that of him in a rainy atrium with nobody improving upon it — and holds it once in the light so that his son can see a dead man's large exact hand and the sentence at the end, which Marcus reads upside down, standing very still, like somebody else's ancestor in a dealer's office. *Choose better.*

“He had a style at the end,” Marcus says, finding, to his own horror, a laugh that is not a laugh at the end of it. “In a crisis, the Cornelii produce *style*.”

“Hold your temper with his ghost for the porticoes,” his mother says. “Here he is only the late management. We have the running of the place.”
