The scaffolding has been coming down for days in a reverse siege, plank by plank, until the city has become used to fragments: a bridge here, a deified shoulder there, a horse’s cannon bone doing more for morale in the market than several speeches. On the dedication morning the last screens come away under the supervision of men whose names will not be carved anywhere, and the crowd makes the sound crowds make when a veil and a building are removed in the same gesture.

It is, everyone agrees before they have seen half of it, magnificent. It is also impossible. Human necks were not designed by Apollodorus.

Vendors have positioned themselves with the strategic genius of barbarian kings. Water is being sold at column prices. Little clay spirals are available for children, inaccurate in every detail except cost. A guide with one eye and absolute pitch in misinformation is explaining to a party from Narbonensis that the emperor appears fifty-eight times, or fifty-nine if you count the sacrifice twice, a theological nuance he is charging extra to settle.

“Does he?” someone behind Antonia asks.

“Count,” says his wife, with the air of a woman who has been asking him to do so in other departments for years.

The priests do what priests do with imperial stone: make it less lonely at the top. Offerings smoke in the officially fragranced direction. A flute player in new sandals watches the conductor as if music were a series of doors opening at public expense. Somewhere on the opposite side, a soldier who served in one of the armies being commemorated is describing a river crossing to his grandson and pointing confidently at the wrong bridge. Neither child nor history appears damaged.

Antonia’s margin is arranged by the purple-dealer with the authority of a man who has kept a viewing privilege alive through four changes of fashion. He has brought a stool for a wife who declines it, cushions nobody admits to needing and shade at a price he waves away because debts, between their houses, have learned to arrive in more useful denominations.

“You chose the morning light,” Antonia says.

“I chose where rich men become silhouettes and ordinary people can see their shoes.” He considers the column with a tradesman’s eye for repetition. “Nice work at the bottom. After the tenth turn it becomes a rumour with masonry.”

“Rome is committed to optimism at altitude.”

“Rome is committed to selling hats. Half this crowd will leave with a neck like a question.”

Eirene, who has brought provisions against the possibility that empire may run long, unwraps bread with the secrecy of a minor cult. “If a god desired us to stand through dedications unfed,” she says, “he would have put kitchens in the Forum and charged admission.”
