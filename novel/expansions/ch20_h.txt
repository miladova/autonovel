Prima hears the story in its upgraded version at the fullers’ guild, where Saufia has become by Friday a woman carrying fire through a locked gate with soldiers at the margins. By the following Tuesday, on the Caelian, Daphne gives her the bathhouse edition with orchestration. Prima listens while mending the hem of a spring hanging that has met a chair leg and lost.

“Did she really hold it like a torch?” she asks.

“No,” Daphne says, with the melancholy of an artist corrected by reality. “It had rained. She held it so the ink wouldn’t spoil. It may be the most serious sentence said in Rome this year. If I sing it that way, nobody will pay.”

“Try a lower line.”

“Oh, the needlewoman has opinions on tessitura.”

“I have opinions on people telling a woman’s departure as if she needed to be on fire to be believed.”

The needle goes in and comes out. Antonia, who is examining cushion braid with more attention than braid can reasonably reward, says nothing. She is still learning when maternal pride—if that is the legal description of the upheaval—can be allowed into a room without changing the price of the work.

Prima has brought her own copy of the clause, purchased from the island-bridge scribe before the price rose. It lives now in her work-box under the needles, not with her mother’s leaves. A difference in filing that Antonia notices and does not annotate aloud.

“Will it apply to guilds?” Prima asks.

“Begin by assuming it applies wherever a man says it doesn’t,” Eirene says from the door, where she has arrived with water and an instinct for questions that can use a coarse first draft. “Then find your Greek.”

“I have a clerk.”

“Then you are civilised. Water?”

Later, when the room suggests refreshment at several speeds, Prima shows Antonia the copy—not for approval, but to ask where the difficult phrase sits if a woman’s refusal is not of work itself but of being held answerable for other people’s tools, debts, breakages. Diomedes, summoned by the ancient authority of a document unrolling in his vicinity, explains what can be explained without turning the explanation into a moat.

“Law is a border,” he says. “It tells where the design repeats and where somebody has put one flower wrong on purpose. Within it, the cloth still has to be kept.”

Prima looks at her border. “Then I want copies for the women who sign the government cloth. At my expense, half rate if the scribe remembers me.”

“Half rate if you remind him of his sister.”

“I do not know his sister.”

“Neither does he, half the year. Rome is run on contextual memory.”

Antonia hears in the joke the sound of machinery changing hands. The clause has travelled from a senator’s shaken conscience to a copyist’s extract to a fullers’ work-box, acquiring in transit the only authority that lasts in the city: use by women who can count.

On her way home Prima stops, not at the bridge this time, but at the scribe’s stall. The price has indeed risen. She buys three copies, negotiates the fourth as thread taken in part exchange, and has them folded small enough to survive other women’s aprons. A revolution may arrive by discount. Distribution is organisation, and she has been raised by people who understood both.
