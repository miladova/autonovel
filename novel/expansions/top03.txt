Morning is when the house performs its most delicate sorting. Night has its reputation, but morning has the information. Diomedes lays the day’s requests out in order of solvency, discretion and the probability that a man’s idea of secrecy involves arriving with three friends and a recognisable cloak. Eirene reads over his shoulder while eating bread, a combination that has prevented more disasters than the city watch.

“This one asks for Daphne exclusively,” she says. “Exclusively. As if she were a hunting dog with ambitions.”

“He means music at his sister’s dinner,” Diomedes says. “The wording is unfortunate.”

“Wording is the whole confession in this town. Send Philomela as well and charge him for harmony. Men behave better when outnumbered by lyrics.”

Antonia, at the other side of the table, is going through a list of clients whose credit requires translation. A grain factor can be trusted to pay after the fleet. A senator’s nephew can be trusted to intend to pay, which is not a currency she accepts. A widow from the Aventine wants a room for six women, music, no men except the one paid to play flute and leave before dessert. Diomedes has marked this request with a star.

“Is she respectable?” Eirene asks.

“She is punctual,” Antonia says. “It is the form respectability should take before lunch.”

“She will want names hypothetical.”

“Then we will give her first names and good water. Rome can survive a number of administrations under those conditions.”

A client arrives before the doors are properly awake. He is not drunk—morning clients have the dignity of business about even their vices—and asks to speak to the mistress about a debt from a deceased friend. It transpires that he wants the debt to die with the friend and the evening’s entertainment to be resurrected at his own table. Antonia hears him out with the expression she reserves for tax receipts and unsatisfactory weather.

“Your friend is dead,” she says. “His account is not. It has relatives.”

“I thought, madam, in the spirit of—”

“My house does not provide spirits.”

Barbatus, in the doorway, becomes the size of a conclusion. The client pays half now and arranges the rest before the next moon, which Eirene calls “a funeral with instalments”. Daphne, passing to rehearsal with her hair still negotiating its terms, hums a phrase so pure it makes the morning office briefly feel underdressed.

“You see?” Antonia tells Diomedes. “Exclusive.”

“Madam, I shall phrase the reply with diplomacy.”

“Phrase it with fees. Diplomacy can harmonise.”
