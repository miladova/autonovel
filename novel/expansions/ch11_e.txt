Onesimus is waiting in the corridor with a dry towel over his arm, as if a man reviewing estate papers in a rainy atrium were a form of bathing that concluded with towelling. He has been stationed there by Servilia since the lamp's second hour: not to attend, which has been forbidden in a sentence the whole household can now recite, but to *be in the building's memory of the evening*, so that tomorrow, when the baths and the secretaries begin their work, the house will be able to say truthfully that a slave was present in the corridor and heard nothing, saw a lamp, and can describe the weather. Onesimus forgets professionally. It remains his masterpiece.

“The rain is giving its evidence in the order of the evening, dominus,” he says, and then, hearing himself deliver a phrase he has picked up in somebody else's plumbing, he files it, visibly, where the house keeps its confiscated eloquence. “Shall I say the master will not shave before the clients?”

“Tell Felix the master will shave at the usual hour.” Lentulus takes the towel he does not need and holds it a moment like a client holding a petition he intends to grant. “District business as usual, Onesimus. Aqueducts. A committee, general and dull. A letter to be left unsealed on the mistress's table.” He looks back once at the atrium, where his grandfather has resumed the dark with a cleaned face and a cupboard full of the future. “And if a room at a banker's office can be had tomorrow without the household's livery being read into it — cedar door, deaf slave, the usual quiet Greek — you will arrange it as if it were being arranged for somebody else's conscience.”

Onesimus bows with the towel still doing its emblematic work. In eleven years he has arranged trysts, bribes, a resignation, two reconciliations that the families concerned describe as military victories, and a committee's entire soft landing into a registry. He will arrange this too, forget it in three languages, and be free in another man's will with the sum named, still forgetting. Somewhere in the house a kitchen fire settles in its ash, keeping itself, the way the poor keep everything: alive, against the morning, at a price no ledger has ever acknowledged because it has always, somehow, been paid.
