She has dressed for the Basilica the way her house dresses a room for men who must not be allowed to mistake the occasion: plainly, at full price. No jewellery a clerk could describe. A veil arranged in the style women use for temples and assignations of state. Diomedes walks two paces behind with a document case containing nothing the morning is permitted to require — copies, all; originals live with the Greek, with the notary, and in a third place Diomedes has never been told, a piece of tradecraft Antonia maintains even against loyalty, on the principle Eirene phrases as *nobody should be the only copy of you, love, including us*.

“If he offers terms in writing,” Diomedes says, at the corner where the Basilica's shade begins to govern the temperature of the talk, “madam will not sign in the room.”

“If he offers terms in writing I shall suspect him of being an impostor in a borrowed life. Lentulus inhabits paper the way fish inhabit apologies, Diomedes: reluctantly, and only after it has been presented as water.”

The Greek's outer office is doing its usual imitation of a temple where the god is discretion. A Spanish silver man is *not* negotiating a loan over a sample ingot he keeps putting back into its cloth. A widow of equestrian family, chaperoned by a freedwoman of ferocious respectability — Antonia knows the pair; the widow hears unsuitable music in a certain house on the Caelian and has lately been observed laughing at it — is settling a grandson's expectations in a voice that suggests the grandson is a foreign policy. Their eyes meet Antonia's across the room: client to proprietor, a current of perfect, municipal blankness. Nobody is in this office. That is what the office sells. The Greek, coming himself from the cedar door to perform the morning's pièce — he performs one a day, like a sacrifice, to remind the room who owns the silence — receives the widow's papers, declines the silver man's ingot with a courtesy that will cost the silver man a quarter point by Thursday, and inclines his head to Antonia in the smallest degree a rich man can manage, etcetera. She has seen Avidius do it since, over fish. It is apparently denominational.

“The small room is prepared,” Eutyches says, in Latin with the faintest Aegean weather on it. “The senator's man asked for the small room. I informed him the small room was engaged for a lady's business, which had the advantage of being true in every particular his master will check.”

“You are a theologian, Eutyches.”

“I am a banker, madam. We let the truth work in several rooms at once and charge by the door.”

Across the city, at approximately the same inclination of a rich Greek head, Lentulus is being shaved for a negotiation his household believes to be an aqueduct inspection. Felix works in silence until the razor reaches the scar's neighbourhood, where professional tact requires, as always, the question that is not a question.

“Leave it,” Lentulus says, before it can be asked. “It has kept its appointment for twenty-one years. I am keeping mine at the Basilica.”

“Then today is an anniversary, dominus,” Felix says, and wipes the blade, and says nothing else, being a man who has shaved three consuls and buried two and knows exactly which of the morning's sentences are his to carry out of the room. It comes to Lentulus, watching his own face reassemble in the dented bronze — senator, debtor, forwarding address — that he has been dressed by this house for every consequential entrance of his life: muster, marriage, consulship (suffect, footnoted), his father's will-reading, his daughter's betrothal dinners. The hands change; the liturgy doesn't. Only once was he not dressed by the house for a consequential entrance, and it was not an entrance, it was a corridor, and she was holding a mirror steady, and the whole of the rest is paperwork.

Onesimus has arranged the Basilica as if for somebody else's conscience, as instructed. There is no livery to be read. There is a Greek chair for the standing problem of a senator's dignity, a brazier, watered wine of a neutrality that would shame a treaty, and the certain knowledge, carried in by both parties like weather, that the Greek in the next room holds — in different hands, under different names, in wax that melts quarterly as a matter of policy — the financial ruin of each of them in its most portable form, and will use neither, today, except as furniture.

That is what neutral means, Antonia thinks, taking the silence-owning chair while Diomedes withdraws to be nobody in the outer office. Not empty. *Armed, on both sides, with the doors shut.* She folds her hands in the order she has decided. First the committee. First the paper. First the things that can be entered in books. If there is to be a fourth thing in this room, it will arrive, like everything else she has survived, over the threshold in his mouth or not at all. She has rehearsed the fire drill. She knows which door not to open under smoke. What she has not rehearsed — she logs it now, with Diomedes precision, in the third book, behind the ribs — is what to do if he opens it for her, from his side, quietly, like a man at last asking the price in the right shop.
