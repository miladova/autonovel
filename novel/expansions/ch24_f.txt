The message has come across the river in the late afternoon by a bakery boy who was paid in hot rolls at both ends and regards himself, not unreasonably, as an imperial courier in flour. Barbatus has kept it until the house reached the point in the evening when a mistress could receive family news without guests mistaking it for wine.

Antonia reads it the first time for logistics. Hanging. Bakery door. Guild feast. She is already instructing Diomedes silently about a cart when the sentence about the child crosses the room without asking admission.

She reads it the second time more slowly. *If it’s a girl she will be taught letters before thread. If it’s a boy, thread first, then letters, so he knows what the letters cost.* There is an entire education policy in it, also a joke at every man who ever held a mirror or a stylus over a woman’s life and called the accompanying invoice nature.

Eirene goes by with lamps and says nothing. Then, at the far end of the corridor, comes back without the lamps and says, “Well?”

“She is going to have a child by harvest.”

“Then harvest,” Eirene says, “will have to watch its manners.” She takes the folded paper from Antonia’s hand only to give it back in a better fold, a form of midwifery in any office. “Do not send money in a panic. Send the hanging. She asked for the hanging.”

“I know.”

“You have your knowing face. It worries the younger staff.”

Antonia looks into the great dining room where the red border is holding an evening of other people’s dynasties in a frame. The hanging has been worked by a woman who counted with her mouth open and stopped a room’s history in a doorway. It has belonged to a house that sells arrival as an experience. For one feast day it will go across a river and hang in a bakery door, where flour dust and guild weather will perform their own criticism.

“She wants it lent.”

“Then lend it like you mean it.” Eirene begins mentally marshalling carts. “Ribbons might frighten the flour, love.”

“The pastry cook’s nephew can be trusted with colours up to municipal level.”

“Can Barbatus?”

“He can be trusted to describe it later as a naval engagement.”

The answer Antonia will send is still forming. She does not write *grandmother*, a word whose paperwork Prima never promised to publish. She does not write *daughter*. The family has learned, painfully, to let nouns arrive at their own rates. What she writes that night, after the guests, in her own hand and with Diomedes refusing on principle to improve it, is an acceptance of the loan, terms for anchoring the hanging against bakery steam, and one line that costs her an administrative hour to keep this short: *Tell the child, either model, the house keeps copies.*

Diomedes reads it and makes no correction. “Entered under correspondence?”

“Under family,” Antonia says. “Between the quarters.”

“That heading is becoming untidy.”

“It is becoming a room.”
