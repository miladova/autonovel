The music master has been renegotiated. Nobody calls it that, because in Servilia’s household renegotiation is what happens to peace treaties and laundry arrangements, not to a man with a tuning fork and a professional tenderness about wrists. He is paid quarterly, in advance, in a schedule bearing more initials than some provincial decrees. The effect on his teaching is miraculous. He discovers that breath can be corrected by words. He discovers that Cornelia’s questions cannot be paid off by calling them temperament.

“Again,” he says, “but with less harbour.”

“The harbour is the audience,” Cornelia says.

“The harbour is an influence. The audience is your mother.”

From her chair Servilia does not look up from accounts she has already memorised. “I am not an audience. I am weather.”

The Greek song proceeds. In the middle verse, where the woman keeps the house while men are elsewhere becoming verses, Cornelia slows the line by a breath. The master raises a finger, considers the schedule, and lowers it into a note on phrasing.

“It is not incorrect,” he concedes.

“You may carve that on my education.”

“It would not survive weather.”

Servilia turns a page. She has begun to understand, with an irritation not wholly separable from pride, that her daughter’s voice is not a box in which the family stores accomplishments. It is a room with its own acoustics. Sound goes where the architecture is honest.

After the lesson Cornelia asks for a copy of the music payments “for my box”.

The master looks to Servilia as if a new tax had been proposed in song.

“Secretary will supply it,” Servilia says. “Put it at the front.”

“At the front of everything this year,” Cornelia says. “It is becoming the family shelf.”

“It is becoming a generation. Do not let it go to your head before the wedding guests arrive.”

Cornelia sings one phrase again while putting away the music, carelessly, as if nobody were officially listening. Without the lesson around it, the unlicensed turn sounds less like defiance and more like a person taking the shortest honest route through a line. It reaches the corridor where Lentulus is passing with committee papers. He stops—not enough to be entered, enough for Onesimus, two paces behind, to catalogue the pause among the house’s new weather.
