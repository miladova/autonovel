The baths know a man’s circumstances before his banker does, because the banker requires figures and the baths merely watch who arrives with whom when the steam makes hierarchy hard to wear. Lentulus has been coming here since he was young enough to believe that being naked among equals constituted a republic. Age has added a shoulder wound, a wife who understands seating as strategy and an awareness that attendants see more constitutions altered in a week than the Senate floor.

Acilius has taken the bench with the best acoustics: close enough to hear whether the province-talk has changed temperature, far enough away not to be quoted as the source of the weather. “You are being described now as flexible,” he says.

“That sounds like an insult in a dancer.”

“In a Cornelius it is practically legislation.”

Two benches over a contractor is explaining the eastern appointment to a man who has come only to have his back scraped. The explanation includes the emperor, grain and the dangers of committees whose members take notes. Lentulus’s name passes through it like an item being handed along a dinner table by people who do not intend to eat from it.

Acilius watches him not react. “There was a time you would have had him corrected by a client.”

“There was a time I had clients for that demographic. We are learning where the water goes.”

“You sound almost content.”

“Do not tell the baths.”

The plunge pool is crowded with youth performing invulnerability. Marcus is not among them today, which itself becomes a rumour by the second room. Lentulus finds he approves of the new scarcity. A son who is no longer available in every wet room of the city cannot be priced as easily. He can be imagined, of course—Rome can imagine anything it is not fed—but imagination lacks the authority of a fresh sighting.

Near the oil stand a minor official is asking whether the registry will make women like the Caelian house safer or only better reported. The question is delivered loud enough to be an invitation. Lentulus says nothing. Acilius says, when they are beyond him, “You let it pass.”

“It was not a question. It was a lease application for an opinion.”

The steam takes them, equal and not equal, senators among contractors, grandfathers among thieves of towels. Lentulus thinks how many facts in his life have reached him in rooms where other people were washing. His father used the baths for deciding marriages. He has used them for receiving the news that history had decided him. It may be the family’s true Senate house: hot, echoing and full of people pretending the water is what makes them equal.
