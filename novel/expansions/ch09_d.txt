The verdict, when it comes three days later, comes in the form of things not happening. No second visit. No seal on the dining room. A line in the prefect's morning list, read out among market frauds and a collapsed stair in the Subura: *death by misadventure, the heart; no further interest*. Diomedes enters it in the first book under *officials, concluding*, and the entry is so dull that Antonia, reading it over his shoulder, experiences the professional pleasure she takes in good drainage: the dangerous thing has been conducted underground, by paperwork, to a place where it can rot usefully as precedent.

By noon the baths have amended the record. The poison dies of a heart complaint; the tax farmer is unmarried from the singer; the carrying-out singing survives, as it will survive for thirty years, because it is the only version with music in it. Philomela hears it from a client that same evening — for the house reopens on the fourth day, *in the order of the evening*, with the hanging up and the little salted fish unannounced and Gaius Calpurnius' couch given, by Antonia's instruction, to the elderly advocate, a man whose heart has been reported to *his* wife, by the same Nikias, as *administratively immortal*.

“I am told I carried him out singing,” Philomela says.

“You held his shoulders. The city has scored it for full chorus.” Antonia hands her, across Diomedes' counter, the box with Serapion's ring in it. “The inquiry has concluded. It is now merely a present from a man who watched you work. File it with the Greek.”

Philomela looks at the ring in its bed of forgotten things — beside it, somebody's stylus has been claimed and gone, leaving a stylus-shaped improvement in the cedar dust — and does not put it on. “In Corinth,” she says, “my mother would have called this a down payment on being believed.”

“In Rome it is a receipt for it. The direction of travel matters.” Antonia closes the box on the rest of the city's lowered guard. “Sing the fast version tonight, for the first set. The room will want its characters back.”

The widow comes on the fifth day, in the dead middle of the afternoon, veiled in the grey that costs more than colour, with the sister who started lists. Eirene meets them — Barbatus, who announced them by no name at all, having learned his trade in this house's school of weather — and brings them not to the great room but to the small dining room, sized to nobody's self-regard, where Antonia keeps the April-weather conversations. There is water. There are, inevitably, the almond cakes.

“I wanted to see the room,” the widow says, “and then I got to the street and I didn't.” She is younger than Antonia expected from Barbatus' report — thirty, perhaps; Roma has filed her under mourning so completely that the youth comes as a kind of clerical error. “My sister says a visit is an admission.”

“Of appetite,” Antonia says. “Take a cake. Women's visits are admitted here for any reason they choose to file.”

The sister, at the widow's shoulder, performs the family office of looking for traps in the pastry. The widow takes a cake and eats it — actually eats it, grief being a country where the customs forms do not always arrive — and says, “He wrote elegiacs. Did he tell you? It seems important that the room know. The versions—” Her mouth does the thing Antonia has watched all week in mirrors and at tables: it composes itself at cost. “The versions have him cuckolded and poisoned and carried out singing. None of them have him breathable.”

“Philomela has his measure,” Antonia says. “And the house has his evening in the order it happened, sealed, with two physicians on it like a treaty. Neither will be published. That is a form of mourning Rome is competent at: the unfiled kind.” She does not offer to show the account. Showing accounts is for tax offices and bankers; what she offers, by Diomedes' prior placement on the low table between them, is the December bill marked *settled*, and the flowers Eirene already sent, now three days past flowers and into evidence of having been meant.

The widow looks at the bill a long time. “He owed for a racehorse too,” she says at last, with a sound her sister will later report, inaccurately, as composure giving way. “The horse is still alive. It owes *us*, on form. I have kept the horse, madam. I have given away his library to the uncle, his seal to the household gods, and his elegiacs —” the mouth again, the cost again “— I have kept those too. Somebody in this city is going to have to be the archive of the true ones. The versions.”

“Then keep them where the light is,” Antonia says, “and let the baths keep theirs. Baths evaporate.” It is more advice than she gives widows as a class; but it has been a week of unsigned advice arriving by hand and proving good, and a house, like a singer, develops in the direction of its exercises. The widow rises; the sister's list closes over the visit like water over a stone; at the door the widow turns, in her costly grey, with the December bill folded into her sleeve like a letter.

“He liked your fish,” she says. “He came home in December and said the fish had stopped being ambition. I have thought about that all week, whether it is a fit thing to put on a tomb.”

“Put *he died laughing*,” Antonia says. “Diomedes already has. In the second book, in the hand for facts that must survive fashion. The first book is the one the committee reads.”

When they have gone, Eirene clears the cakes in silence, and the house does a thing it has not done in four days: it simply becomes a house again, being prepared for an evening, with nobody rehearsing fire procedures against the prefect, the committee, or the sound of a laugh changing key. Ammonius is rebuilding a sauce. Daphne is practising scales at a volume calculated to annoy nobody who matters. In the courtyard the garden roses, overwatered as nations, open their January account with the sun. The inquiry is over. What it has cost, and whom, is itemised nowhere the first book keeps.
