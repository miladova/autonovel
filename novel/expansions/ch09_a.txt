The uncle's letter is the first to move because grief, in senatorial families, keeps office hours. He writes to the prefect before the body is cold in its own house — the body having been conveyed home at dawn under Barbatus' direction, in a procession so discreet it was indistinguishable, from any window that mattered, from a delivery of wine. The letter does not accuse. Accusation is for men who can afford to be wrong in public. The letter *deplores, *in the washed vocabulary, requests, in view of certain committees currently sitting, that the prefect satisfy himself, and invites the city, by implication read in every atrium by the second hour, to watch the prefect satisfying himself at a particular address on the Caelian.

The prefect, who is a man of the new school — which in AD 112 means a man who has realised that Trajan's Rome runs on paperwork the way the old Rome ran on omens, and with the same priesthood — reads the letter twice and sends, not men, but a note, to three addresses. To the aediles: has the house a history? To the tax office: is the house in order? To his own senior clerk, a freedman with a memory like Diomedes' third book: who ate there? The answers arrive before the uncle's second letter, which is how a prefect demonstrates that he is not influenced by committees. He is influenced by filing. It is a more durable vice.

Meanwhile, on the Caelian, Antonia is conducting the morning after a death in the house the way she conducts everything: by sections, lit from the back. The great dining room is cleaned by her own instruction *twice*, once for decency and once for the record, so that any man inspecting it shall find, boringly, a room. The couches are re-covered. The hanging — Prima's border marching over last night's geography — she orders left exactly as it was.

“Take it down,” Eirene says, “and it looks like evidence. Leave it up—”

“Leave it up and it looks like Tuesday. A house that redecorates over a death has written its own evidence. We are a dining house, Eirene. Dining houses are allowed Tuesdays.”

The staff are briefed in the kitchen, standing, with Ammonius' fires lit behind them and the boy who cried in the night given, by Antonia personally, a task involving water and distance from the great room. She tells them the order of the evening — she used the phrase before she received it; the house will never know which of them coined it, and the not knowing is the closest the phrase will ever come to a wedding present — and she tells them the rule beneath it: answer what is asked, add nothing that gleams, and if a question arrives wearing tomorrow's clothes, walk it back to the fish. Barbatus, who has given statements to arena officials in two reigns, nods like a man recognising a formation.

“Fish first,” he says to the boy, kindly, as to a recruit. “Then the song. Then the story. Then the laugh. Anything asks you different, it's not a question, it's a door. Don't go through doors other people hold open, in inquiries or in life.”

Philomela is the one Antonia keeps back. The singer has been awake since the fifth hour with her hair still dressed for last night, a state in which, Diomedes will record, she resembles a temple after a festival: garlanded, exhausted and smelling of oil. She has Nikias' Greek copy of the night's statement in her lap and is reading it with a singer's lips, learning it the way she learns a programme.

“Don't learn it,” Antonia says.

“I was at the couch. I had his shoulders.” Philomela looks up. The famous self-possession is on her face like makeup applied by another hand. “If I don't learn it, I'll *sing* it, Antonia. It'll come out arranged. Everything comes out of me arranged. It is the whole disease.”

“Then arrange it in the order it happened. That is all learning is.” Antonia takes the copy, not unkindly, and puts it under a bowl, where paper in this house goes to become less important. “You sang. He spoke about metre. Serapion told a story with a crocodile for a witness. The man laughed. You held him up. Nikias came at a run and was a physician for four minutes against a heart that had filed its resignation last year. That is the whole of your evidence, Philomela. It has the great merit of being true at the speed it occurred.”

“And if they ask whether he was — in love with me, let's say it in their Latin — or I with his uncle's money, or whether the song was the Greek one, for people who know?”

“Then the question has arrived from the wrong end, and you walk it back to the fish.” Antonia meets her eyes, freedwoman to freeborn Corinthian, across every column Rome keeps. “You are registered, your papers are boring, your money is with a Greek who would adopt a tax return before he'd open your strongbox to a committee. You are the cleanest witness in Rome today, Philomela. Behave like a tax receipt and no one can make a song of you without showing their own handwriting.”

The singer laughs — once, unwillingly, and the laugh does what laughs do in this house's mornings, which is recruit the kitchen to normality. Below the talk, the day assembles: Diomedes' tablets in three hands, Nikias' sealed account gone at dawn to a notary who has been being paid for years against precisely this light, Ammonius baking, out of defiance and almonds, at a rate the morning cannot justify. By the third hour the baths have their three deaths, all false, all immortal. The house has one, true, and written down while it was still warm. Antonia, who knows Rome, settles herself to watch which of the four arrives at the prefect's office with a seal on it.
