The city, for its part, spends the following week trying to invoice him for the information. Hostilius tries again at the races, on a bench where the wind takes men's papers and gives them to gods with a taste for other people's losses; he has refined the story in the interval the way baths water refines a knife. Now it has a young laundress in it who is actually a vestal's niece, and a chair-committee, and an emperor laughing under a marble key.

“You see how it improves with handling,” Hostilius says, delighted. “Like your wine. Four hands and it's a classic.”

“It improves away from the truth,” Marcus says. The horses are running. Down on the sand the colours are doing what fortunes do in every generation: answering to their names in public while being owned elsewhere. “In my father's house we say: the second hand is where the giving happens. Yours is mostly taking.”

Hostilius, who is not a fool — foolishness is an expensive hobby and his is dice — looks at the heir of the Cornelii sideways over the rim of an afternoon and does the arithmetic of careers: a young man who has stopped retailing his household at other people's prices is either ruined or has become, in the third column, somebody. “You sound like a caulker,” he says, and young Avidius, on Marcus's other side, occupied with being ballast in a new installation, remarks to the weather: “It's a rule with menaces. They should arrive at your own prices or be filed as weather.”

Marcus pays Hostilius the dice debt that evening in full, in a garden room, with Onesimus in attendance and a receipt in silver-instead — the first time in their acquaintance that a payment between them has not been partly denominated in secrets. Hostilius counts it with the air of a banker invited, embarrassingly, to a wedding. “To what do I owe the full settlement?”

“To a family system,” Marcus says. “It has spread across the river. Money stops being a messenger and starts being money. Don't let it disorient the benches.”

Onesimus forgets the exchange at the full professional standard the instant it becomes necessary, and on the walk home — the slave two paces behind, occupying the exact back-stage geography he has occupied since Marcus's grandfather was the future — says, out of the plumbing of a house that is re-plumbing itself nightly: “In the old master's time, master Marcus, information in this household was freighted like grain. A lot of it spoiled in transit and nobody dared open the sacks. The young mistress” — he means Cornelia; the corridors have not yet been re-indexed, and Marcus feels the miss of the word in his sternum like a stair trodden wrong — “the young mistress is to have papers in a box. The master has a Greek doing sums at the street door. And now the heir pays a creditor in *coin*. One hardly knows the address.”

“I am learning a word, Onesimus. It has overheads.”

“Most of the useful ones do.” The old man falls back into his wake with his face doing, at veteran rates, very little indeed. “At Baiae the master once threw a sandal at a steward. I mention it because it cost him the set. He paid, in the end, for a new pair for the household, in a town where they could not be linked to the incident. That was the old management's idea of privacy, sir. New footwear, far away, unwitnessed.”

There is no information in it and Marcus knows, with the part of him Onesimus has been quietly educating from two paces behind since he was twelve, that it is a dowry of a kind: an old servant handing the third generation a story with no knives in it, chosen out of a cellar of sharper vintages. He takes it home and puts it, as one does with the household's assays, in the drawer over the unfinished third letter, where it sits for a week of dinners being ballast, of baths being managed, of a committee dissolving in the background of Rome like a cake in rain, until — on an evening when the house is between musical instalments and the spring has finally taken the city the way dye takes linen — he finds himself counting his way after dinner toward a street of olives and sofas, where a doorkeeper's shadow makes a small, governed area in the lamps in which a young man may stand choosing not to come in, at his own rate, with the thread entered separately.
