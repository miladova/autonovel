“Ninety was the grand house, on the point,” Vibius says, correcting a legend with a sick man's priorities. “There was a smaller, by the shore road, sixty-four and a turn where the water slopped if you counted wrong. Claudia did the shore house the last winter. She came up with the dealer's party in the spring and I saw her at the island market with a tally in her hand, counting a porter's load for him because he'd got his figures in the second column wrong.” He intends this as a joke for the room, and it becomes, on its way to Prima, something she will keep in her work-box forever: her mother, newly in Rome, doing sums at a market stall; a fuller three weeks freed watching with a plan arriving in him like weather off a river. It is the first courtship scene her parents’ marriage has ever been granted in her hearing, and it is being told to a veiled woman on a stool while the kettle wastes its breath at the hearth.

“I told this house you were to rest,” Prima says, to the water, furiously.

“Rest is being reported. I'm filling in the between-times.” Vibius's eyes go back to the visitor, who is holding herself like a person carrying something full across his room. “She could price a household by its linen, Claudia could. 'You can tell a house by what it sends out dirty,' she'd say. 'And a woman by what she's sent out with.' She had the sayings filed like a guild clerk, mistress. It's where the girl gets them. Not from me. I only kept things clean.”

Antonia hears *It's where the girl gets them* arrive and settle in the second column. There is no expression available to a widow-inventing cloak for the information that the sayings have two mothers too, so she makes none. She sits on the offered stool, in the posture of a woman consulting a tradesman about spring hangings, while Prima performs at the hearth the rites of water already hot, and the room's whole archive — bench, wall-bed, tools on a bench, a hanging folded face inward over a chair, the festival loaf's successor under a cloth, a bowl of honey dressed with thyme like medicine making an effort — conducts its silent examination of her. She has been inspected by aediles, prefects, committees and one publisher of cohorts of younger men. A fuller's two rooms, ferociously neat, are the most thorough tribunal she has faced in Rome. It is also, she understands with a clarity that arrives without roadworks, the first room she has been in for twenty years that her younger self would have known how to work in straight off: heat the water, count the stairs, hold the mirror steady, be furniture until somebody's arithmetic promotes you.

“Your daughter's work is known across the river,” she says, because work is the passport she has brought and it is not false. “A contractor's wife tried to order the same border from a man at the Forum who copies. He brought me his copy to admire. The flowers were all the same.”

Vibius smiles into the blanket's edge he is holding like a rail. “That's how you know it's not hers. Prima's won't march in step if you pay them extra. Her mother taught her that out of the leaves — no, girl, the water's done, cease fire — she taught her that a made thing should keep the maker's weather in it somewhere. For the reading, later.” He coughs then, properly, the whole house rearranging itself around the sound — prima donna of this winter's programme — and Prima is there with the cup and the cloth, and Antonia is on her feet without having given the order, like staff, like (her body says before she can file it) family. She steadies the bowl. That is all. Bowl-steadying is not in any set of books. Her hands have been holding things steady for other people's houses since she was nine years old, and here is her hand, bare of rings for the Basilica and the sickroom alike, doing it in a fuller's lean-to for a man who knows exactly who she is and has decided, at the pace of water, not to file it anywhere the tax office reads.
