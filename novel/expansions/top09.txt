Before any formal question can be answered, the documents are made to answer one another. Diomedes lays out the household copies in the office: receipts, fees, contracts, the Greek’s extracts in their careful boredom, a list of musicians paid on dates that can stand up in court without blushing. Philomela, whose curiosity has never learned to sit outside with the carriages, reads the first copy over his shoulder and says, “We look like a very well-run rumour.”

“That,” Diomedes says, “is the object of the exercise.”

Antonia has barred Eirene from giving evidence in metaphors and Daphne from giving it in song. Barbatus is permitted to be present because his silence, properly aimed, has the force of a locked gate. The inquiry wants names. Diomedes gives it categories, dates and initials in precisely the quantities the forms have room for. It is a lesson in how a narrow column can defend a house better than a broad speech.

When the clerk asks whether women are detained against departure by debts, Philomela—who has been asked merely to identify a payment—looks up from the schedule with an expression no harmony can soften.

“Debts are entered,” she says. “So are doors.”

The clerk, who is young and maintains a brave little beard on behalf of jurisprudence, writes this down and then crosses it out as unhelpful. Antonia makes no sign. The crossing-out pleases her more than the sentence would have in evidence; it proves the phrase has travelled into an office and made a man use a second thought with his own hand.

At the interval, in a corridor that smells of cedar and other people’s wet cloaks, Diomedes gives the women water and the house’s instructions in their second form: say only what your copy can survive. “If asked about happiness,” he says, “request a definition.”

“What if asked about madam?” says Philomela.

“Ask whether they have brought enough paper.”

Even Barbatus smiles. It is one of the reasons inquiries dislike working through lunch. Office light has a way of making institutions smaller and people legible. Antonia stands at the end of the corridor with her hands folded and feels, not triumph, but the beginnings of a different weather: her house no longer needs to be interesting to be defensible. It needs to be readable on her own terms, one exact page at a time.
