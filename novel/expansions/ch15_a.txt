In Transtiberim the approach of Saturnalia is measured differently from the Palatine, where the festival arrives by invitation. Across the river it arrives by eaves: by the smell of other people's roasting drifting over the vats at the dinner hour, by the price of the little clay figures going up in inverse proportion to their quality, by masters in the fuller’s guild debating, as they debate every year with the enthusiasm of men discovering generosity as a climate, whether the holiday dole shall be in oil, in coin, or in the form of a speech. Prima's household measures it by her father's breathing at the fourth hour, when the yard gets its one bar of sun and the garments go up on the frames like a population being aired after a siege.

The cough has learned the house. That is how Prima thinks of it in the hours when thinking is a luxury after water and honey: it knows the step up to the vat-room that costs him, it knows the advantage of taking him halfway through a sentence so that other people finish it wrong, it knows the nights. His own account is different and delivered, on good mornings, like a trade report: “Chest's full of river this week. Trade will have its customs. We pay on the day.” Then he wants the bench by the frames, where he can supervise. A man who has held a household up for thirty years by being the still point of its washing does not retire to a bed without the household conspiring in his fiction. The guild boys bring him the dry garments to inspect. He inspects them with his thumbs, the customs officer's bequest to the whole bank of the river, and pronounces on rinses at a distance like a rich man on morals.

The guild physician is Nikias' poor relation by profession rather than blood: a younger Greek with a travelling case and a manner he is still learning to spend appropriately in rooms where money is soup-thin. He speaks kindly to Prima because Prima asks the questions in an order that allows him to answer them, and uselessly to the disease because the disease has never in its history attended a consultation. He prescribes the honey, thyme in the steam, rest — that palace word, issued to the poor like a lottery ticket — and, at the door, with the air of a man telling the truth in its overcoat, he says to her, “Keep him warm past the festival, fuller’s daughter. Winter is the tax. Spring sometimes remits.”

She pays him out of the Caelian money for the spring hangings, itemised recently to a fare-thee-well, and he takes it with an embarrassment that tells her the guild has already mentioned his fee in full. She says nothing. In this neighbourhood, where Antonia Cassia's kindness arrives by a plumbing of entitlements and suppliers, saying nothing is also a talent being purchased somewhere upstream. She has begun — third treacherous comparison, unfiled — to recognise good plumbing when it doesn't splash.

Mother Livia's soup comes on Mondays and, increasingly, on other days that have had to be renamed Mondays for the purpose. She brings it herself because (a) Titus at the ovens is careless with liquids and (b), never to be minuted at the guild women’s rota, because she has discovered in Vibius's sickroom a quality her own male household, with its flour and its second columns, does not supply: an audience that cannot get up. She tells him the neighbourhood. She tells it thoroughly, with names, because names in Transtiberim are news and news, dosed correctly, is medicine that doesn't taste of honey. She tells him which of the tanners' widows has taken a young husband and what the guild thinks of the wool trade, and what the priest at the island temple charged a bargee for a vow, and Vibius receives it all with his eyes half shut like a senator at a debate he has already decided, and says, at intervals that prove he has been following the items like an auditor: “That one's lying.” “That one pays.” “That one'll be filed by spring.”

It is doing something to Prima's accounts of the woman, this discovery of her future mother-in-law in a posture the contracts never itemised: on her knees at a sick man's fire, skimming soup with one hand and conducting the street with the other. Between them, over Vibius's bowl, the two women have arrived at the form of government their men call, in cleaner rooms, an understanding. The dowry has not been mentioned in a fortnight. The reading has been promoted, in Mother Livia's reported speech at the bakehouse, from defect to “that business with the second columns; a girl who finds money in columns can find weevils in flour.” Weevils, Prima understands, are the producing class’s poetry.

Saturnalia arrives round them all the same, without asking the house's permission. It inverts the world at the proper addresses: in Transtiberim it mostly clarifies it. Masters serve their slaves a meal and expect, by January, compound gratitude. The guild gives its dole in oil, the speech having been voted, mercifully, into a cask. Titus bakes the festival loaves with his sleeves at the senatorial stripe and brings Prima one shaped, by a baker's grandson learning the trade on her betrothal, like a flower with too many petals, which Vibius inspects with his thumbs and pronounces “a fair attempt at your border, girl, in bread. Let no one register it.” For half an hour the sickroom is a room with laughing in it, and the cough — that tenant who has learned the house — sits politely in the corner all tenants reserve for the family furniture, and Prima keeps the laughter's accounts exactly, the way her mother kept stairs: not because she thinks a count will change the sum, but so the sum will not change her.
