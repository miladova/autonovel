By the second spring the clause has acquired what all successful Roman innovations acquire: a nickname, three wrong attributions and a stationer’s edition with commentary nobody admits to needing. Saufia comes to the Caelian at the trades entrance with her cousin’s daughter and a bundle of papers tied this time in red thread. The cousin is taking work in a house whose proprietor speaks of savings as if he invented the weather around them.

“I am not asking for charity,” Saufia says, before soup can be deployed in her direction. “I am asking for Diomedes at reading speed.”

Diomedes reads. The girl—twenty, sharp about cloth measurements and raw about documents—stops him at *retention against contingencies*. “Meaning he keeps it if anything happens?”

“Meaning anything he can name afterwards,” Saufia says. “We have had that rain.”

Antonia, present because a proprietor’s presence at this stage in her life can shorten some negotiations and lengthen the right ones, says nothing until the girl asks whether a copy can be left with someone outside the house. “It can,” she says. “It should. Bring back the version with the wording changed.”

The girl returns three days later with *retention* amended, quantities named, and a rhythm about her that Eirene classifies as a family trait of the recently documented. She does not take the work in the end. A fuller’s guild place opens, less glamorous, better lit. Saufia tells the story in advance of other versions: no torch, just weatherproof paper and a woman who had been told where to get it read.

That is the clause as history. Not a column. A route women tell one another between market stalls, an address in a song’s second hearing, a sentence forwarded at half price and eventually, in the great Roman manner, copied without anyone needing to remember which Cornelius was once afraid of it.

In Antonia books it is entered under Educational one last time. Diomedes considers the heading. “The category has outgrown its room.”

“Then let it be infrastructure,” she says. “Rome will read it better in small letters.”
