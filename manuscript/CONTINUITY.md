# The Water Between — continuity sheet

Working notes only. Not part of the manuscript.

## People

**Arwen Meredith (née Prys)** — b. 3 March 1882, Barmouth, Merioneth. Father Rhys Prys, chandler and part-owner of a coaster; mother Ellen, taught music and died 1909. Married 1903 to Iestyn Meredith, Liverpool shipping broker (Meredith & Vaughan), 14 years her senior. One stillbirth, 1906; no living children. Travelling First Class, cabin B-59? (use C-? — keep vague: "B deck, starboard"). Maid: Bessie Corrigan, 24, Liverpool, berthed second class. Going to New York to join Iestyn, who has taken charge of a New York branch. Keeps her mother's tunes in a hand-sewn book. Carries a drilled half-sovereign on a ribbon — Harold's keepsake, 1897.

**Harold Godfrey Lowe** — b. 21 Nov 1882, Llanrhos; raised Pennrallt, Barmouth. Fifth Officer, R.M.S. Titanic. Father George (chandler), mother Harriet; sister Ada; elder brother died. Ran away to sea at 14, 1897. Master's certificate c. 1908. Served Belgic, Tropic. First transatlantic crossing. Blunt, quick-tempered, exact. Watch 6–8.

**Iestyn Meredith** — 48. Careful, provisionous, not cruel. Believes he is doing good.

**Louisa Penhallow** — Boston widow, 60s, first class, says true things.

**Basil Thorne** — London picture dealer, first class, hearty, forgettable.

**Bessie Corrigan** — Arwen's maid, second class, survives (boat 13?).

**Ada Lowe** — Harold's sister, Barmouth.

## The past (1896–97)

Summer 1896–autumn 1897, Barmouth. Arwen and Harold, both fourteen. Her father turns Harold off the doorstep, Sept 1897; Harold leaves for Liverpool within the week. He leaves a letter with Ada; Ada never delivers it (Rhys Prys frightened her). Arwen believes he simply went.

## Ship timeline

- Wed 10 Apr: Southampton; Cherbourg; Queenstown.
- Fri 12 Apr: at sea. Ch1 (Arwen), Ch2 (Harold).
- Sat 13 Apr: Ch3 (Arwen), Ch4 begins (Harold 6–8 watch, ice chit).
- Sun 14 Apr: Ch5 (Arwen, day + 11.40 p.m.), Ch6 (Harold, 11.40 p.m.–1.30 a.m.).
- Mon 15 Apr: collision 11.40 p.m. Sun; boats down from 12.25 a.m.; boat 14 lowered c. 1.30 a.m.; foundering 2.20 a.m.; Carpathia on scene c. 4 a.m.; boats picked up to 8.30 a.m. Ch7 (Arwen, in boat 12), Ch8 (Harold, the return, collapsible A, dawn).
- Mon 15–Thu 18 Apr: Carpathia; arrives New York 18 Apr, evening, Cunard pier 54. Ch9.
- Fri 19 Apr: American inquiry opens at the Waldorf.
- May: Harold returns on Adriatic (2 May), British inquiry (testifies 22–23 May).
- June 1912: Barmouth. Reception at the Picture Pavilion, 1,300 people, gold watch. Ch10.

## Facts to honour

- Lowe fired three warning shots alongside boat 14 as it was lowered.
- He gathered five boats (10, 12, 14, 4, D), took his own passengers into other boats, kept a volunteer crew, waited for the cries to thin, then returned and pulled four alive from the water; at least twelve more from swamped collapsible A; made sail (only boat to do so); took D in tow.
- British inquiry: "It would have been suicide to go back there until the people had thinned out."
- The ice chit he saw and worked out mentally; concluded the ship would not be in the ice during his 6–8 watch.
- Barmouth: Dinas Oleu (National Trust's first land, 1895), the Mawddach estuary, the bridge, Aberamffra, the harbour.
- Hymn: "Calon Lân" (1890s).

## Motifs

The drilled half-sovereign; sound heard through steel; the tide over the Barmouth bar; ledgers and dead reckoning; gloves; the cold; "cariad" used once, at the end.
