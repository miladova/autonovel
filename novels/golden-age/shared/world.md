# World Bible — Antonine Rome (156–171 CE)

## 1. The Historical Moment (October 156 – March 161 CE)
Rome in the nineteenth year of **Titus Aelius Hadrianus Antoninus Augustus Pius** is a million-strong metropolis convinced its noon will never end. Seventy-year-old Antoninus has never left Italy since taking the purple; wars happen at the edges of parchment maps—along the Antonine Wall in Britannia, in Mauretania, on the Dacian marches—while inside the Aurelian-predecessor customs perimeter (the Pomerium and the *continentia aedificia*), marble veneers gleam over brick-faced concrete.

Beneath the quiet surface, a colossal succession and property realignment is silently tightening:
- **Domitia Lucilla Minor**, mother of heir-apparent **Marcus Aurelius Caesar** and owner of the greatest clay-and-brick monopoly along the Tiber valley (*Figlinae Domitianae*, *Caninianae*, *Terentianae*), has died shortly before the novel opens (c. 155–156 CE). Her vast estates are passing into trusteeship for Marcus's young children and triggering vicious quiet scrambles among equestrian *conductores* (lease-contractors), surveyors, and senatorial neighbors.
- **Two Heirs Under One Roof:** Hadrian forced Antoninus in 138 CE to adopt two boys: serious, thirty-five-year-old **Marcus Aurelius Caesar** (married to Antoninus's daughter **Faustina Minor**, living cleanly, reading Epictetus with **Quintus Junius Rusticus**, suffering chest pains and stomach ulcers) and twenty-five-year-old **Lucius Ceionius Commodus** (son of Hadrian's first beloved heir Aelius Caesar—handsome, golden-bearded, passionate for Greens chariot-teams, Syrian dancers, hunting dogs, and lavish credit, fiercely championed by his older sister **Ceionia Fabia**, whom Marcus once jilted at Antoninus's wish). Officially Antoninus treats Marcus as sole working colleague (*tribunicia potestas*) and keeps Lucius unpromoted as a private senator-questor; privately every financier, grain-broker, and Caelian matron is placing bets on whether Lucius will be discarded or elevated the night the old Emperor dies.
- **Praetorian & Urban Power:** Twenty-year Praetorian Prefect **Marcus Gavius Maximus**—feared, ice-blooded equestrian disciplinarian—is dying in 156–157 CE, succeeded by **Gaius Tattius Maximus** (who himself dies in 160 CE, replaced by dual prefects **Furius Victorinus** and **Cornelius Repentinus**, the latter whispered to owe his rise to Antoninus's concubine **Galeria Lysistrate**!). Meanwhile **Quintus Lollius Urbicus**, builder of the Antonine Wall, presides as **Prefect of the City (*Praefectus Urbi*)** over criminal and commercial order from the Basilica Julia and the Prefecture near the Tellus temple, until his death c. 160 CE.

## 2. Geography, Districts & Sensory Signatures (Orientation + Texture)

1. **Figlinae Subortanae (Tiber Clay-Pits North of the Milvian Bridge)**
   - *What it is:* Wet blue-grey alluvial clay trenches, smoking turf-and-brushwood updraft kilns, drying sheds roofed in cracked pantiles, reed huts along towpaths where ox-teams haul brick-barges downstream to Rome.
   - *Sensory signature:* Sour wood-ash on wet skin, river-fog tasting of sulfur from upstream fuller-drains, the metallic clack of iron-rimmed bronze *signacula* stamping wet clay discs.

2. **The Subura & the Firewall (*Clivus Suburanus*, Region IV)**
   - *What it is:* The roaring valley trough between the Viminal, Esquiline, and Quirinal hills, packed six and seven storeys high with timber-framed *insulae* leaning so close across alleys that neighbors pass chamber-pots window to window. At its western foot stands the **Firewall of the Forum of Augustus**: a hundred-foot cliff of fireproof grey *lapis Gabinus* tuff, pierced by three narrow arches (*Arcus Pantani*), built solely so the Senate and Mars Ultor need neither see the slums nor burn when they catch alight.
   - *Sensory signature:* Rancid olive-dregs (*amurca*) burning in clay lamps, boiled sheep's head and fermented *garum* from street *popinae*, urine fermenting in sunken amphora-urinals outside fulleries (*fullonicae*), and the ceaseless night-thunder of heavy iron-tired supply wagons (*plaustra*) banned from Rome's streets between sunrise and the tenth hour by the *Lex Iulia Municipalis*.

3. **The Argiletum & Forum Transitorium**
   - *What it is:* The narrow, elbow-choked artery connecting the Subura straight into the Forum Romanum—lined at its lower end by booksellers pasting title-strips (*tituli*) on doorposts, cobblers hammering hobnails (*clavi caligarii*), money-changers (*nummularii*) testing denarii with their teeth, and catamites and street-walkers beckoning from archways beneath Minerva's frieze.
   - *Sensory signature:* Pumice dust from smoothed papyrus rolls, tanned goat-hide, hot beeswax from *tabulae*, and the cold draught off Cloaca Maxima's stone vault underfoot.

4. **Basilica Julia & the Centumviral Courts (Forum Romanum, Region VIII)**
   - *What it is:* A cavernous double-aisled marble hall divided by canvas curtains (*vela*) into four simultaneous inheritance and property courts (*hastae*, marked by a ceremonial spear stuck in the floor), while dice-players scratch game-boards directly into the outer marble steps and hired *laudiceni* ("supper-praisers") take three denarii apiece to roar applause at their patron's metaphors.
   - *Sensory signature:* Sweaty wool *togae* whitened with chalk (*creta*) that puffs when advocates slap their thighs, throat-lozenges of tragacanth gum and honey, echoing marble acoustics where four cases shout over one another.

5. **The Emporium, Porticus Aemilia & Monte Testaccio (Region XIII, Aventine Foot)**
   - *What it is:* Half a mile of travertine river-wharves where flat-bottomed *codicariae* barges towed by oxen and river-men (*helciarii*) up from Ostia unload Baetican olive oil, African grain, Numidian marble blocks, and Syrian alum. Behind the warehouses rises **Mons Testaceus**, an artificial mountain of fifty million smashed Spanish oil-amphorae reeking faintly of rancid oil in summer heat, salted with quicklime to kill the stench.
   - *Sensory signature:* Crunch of terracotta shards under sole, tarred hemp, stinging quicklime dust, mule-dung, and Greek/Punic/Syrian tally-calls.

6. **Trans-Tiberim (Region XIV, Across the Tiber)**
   - *What it is:* The immigrant, industrial right bank below the Janiculum Hill, where the Aqua Traiana drives roaring tiers of state and private water-mills (*molinae*), tanners soak hides in dog-dung and alum downwind of the palaces, Jewish and Syrian prayer-houses sit beside brick-kilns, and pleasure-gardens border corpse-burners' yards.
   - *Sensory signature:* Deafening wooden gear-rumble and cold spray from the Aqua Traiana mill-leats, rotten-egg tannery liquor, sesame bread blistered in brick ovens, Syrian spikenard cut with cheap fenugreek.

7. **The Caelian Hill (*Mons Caelius*, Region II)**
   - *What it is:* Shaded, walled senatorial *domus* estates perched above the Colosseum valley and the Temple of Deified Claudius—including the ancestral gardens of the Annii Veri (where Marcus Aurelius was born) and the sprawling townhouses of the Ceionii and Servilii. Cool peristyles fed by private lead *fistulae* water-pipes (many illegally tapped at wider bronze *calices* than licensed!), mosaic floors cooled with snow hauled in straw-pits from the Alban Hills.
   - *Sensory signature:* Boxwood clipped in the sun, wet marble, myrrh and saffron braziers masking chamber-slaves' sweat, the distant animal roar drifting up from the *Ludus Magnus* gladiator barracks in the valley below.

8. **The Esquiline Beyond the Agger (*Campus Esquilinus*) & Lorium**
   - *Esquiline:* Where ancient execution grounds meet Maecenas's manicured gardens; outside the gates, *carnifices* nail condemned slaves and *humiliores* to crossbeams, crows work the pits, and night-witches hunt iron nails and teeth.
   - *Lorium:* Twelve miles west along the paved Via Aurelia—Antoninus Pius's beloved, unpretentious country palace where he grew up, presses his own wine, fishes with a rod, and receives ambassadors without Praetorian pomp.

## 3. Money, Weights, Measures & Everyday Prices (156–161 CE)
- **Coinage Ladder:**
  - `1 aureus` (gold, ~7.3g) = `25 denarii` = `100 sestertii (HS)`
  - `1 denarius` (silver, ~80% fineness under Antoninus) = `4 sestertii (HS)` = `16 asses`
  - `1 sestertius (HS)` (large golden-brass orichalcum coin) = `4 asses`
  - `1 dupondius` (brass, radiate crown) = `2 asses`
  - `1 as` (reddish copper) = `2 semisses` = `4 quadrantes`
- **Census Thresholds:**
  - Municipal decurion: `100,000 HS`
  - **Equestrian order (*ordo equester*, gold ring & narrow purple stripe *angusticlavius*):** `400,000 HS` + free birth (*ingenuitas*) for two generations (or quiet imperial fiction *natalium restitutio*).
  - **Senatorial order (*laticlavius*, broad purple stripe):** `1,000,000 HS` + imperial grant.
- **Concrete Street Prices:**
  - Public bath entry (*balneum*): `1 quadrans` (men), `2 quadrantes` or `1 as` (women).
  - Two-pound coarse wheat loaf (*panis plebeius*): `2 asses`.
  - Cup of sour Sabine wine + bowl of chickpeas at a Subura *popina*: `1–2 asses`.
  - Cheap street trick under an archway (*prostibula*): `2–8 asses`; skilled courtesan/flute-girl for an evening: `5–25 denarii` (`20–100 HS`).
  - Unskilled day-laborer / brick-hauler wage: `3–4 sestertii` (`12–16 asses`) per day.
  - Garret room (*cenaculum*) under the tiles of a six-storey Subura *insula*: `200–500 HS` per year (sublet weekly by ruthless *insularii* rent-collectors who pull down the ladder and throw tenants' rags into the mud on the Kalends of July!).
  - Working slave (unskilled): `1,200–2,000 HS`; literate Greek secretary or trained baker: `4,000–8,000 HS`; prized imperial eunuch or gourmet cook: `50,000+ HS`.
  - Hired applause claque (*laudiceni*) for one court morning in Basilica Julia: `12 HS` (`3 denarii`) per shouter.

## 4. Time, Calendar & Light
- **No minutes, no seconds:** The Roman day between sunrise and sunset is divided into **twelve seasonal *horae*** (`prima hora` to `duodecima hora`). In December, a Roman daytime hour lasts ~45 modern minutes; in June, ~75 modern minutes! Night is divided into **four military watches (*vigiliae*)** sounded by the *bucina* horn of the *Cohortes Vigilum* (night fire-and-police watchmen patrolling with vinegar-soaked Blankets, bronze siphon-pumps, and iron hooks *unci* for tearing down burning tenements before flames jump the street).
- **Monthly Anchors:** **Kalends** (1st — debt interest due! Hence *tristes Kalendae*, "the grim Kalends"), **Nones** (5th, or 7th in March/May/July/October), **Ides** (13th, or 15th in March/May/July/October). Dates count backward inclusively (*ante diem III Nonas Martias*).

## 5. The Legal Engines Driving the Plot (Exact 2nd-Century Jurisprudence)
1. **Freeborn (*Ingenuus*) vs. Freed (*Libertinus*) vs. Junian Latin (*Latinus Iunianus*):**
   - Under the *Lex Iunia Norbana* and *Lex Aelia Sentia*, a slave freed informally (between friends *inter amicos*, by letter, or under age thirty without council *consilium* before the Urban Praetor) becomes a **Junian Latin**: free while breathing, yet barred from making a Roman will or taking direct Roman inheritances; at death, their entire estate reverts to their patron (*velut iure peculii*, "as though slave pocket-money").
   - **Statutory Escapes into Full Roman Citizenship (*Ius Quiritium*, Gaius *Inst.* 1.28–34):**
     - **The Mill (*Pistrinum*):** Under Trajan's edict, a Junian Latin who operates a commercial mill-bakery in Rome grinding **at least 100 *modii* of grain per day for three full years** earns full Roman citizenship and freedom from the slave-death reversion!
     - **The Tenement (*Aedificatio*):** Spending at least half a patrimony of `200,000+ HS` building or rebuilding a Roman house/*insula* after fire.
     - **The Ship (*Navis*):** Building a sea-going vessel of `10,000 modii` carrying grain to Rome for six years.
     - **Iteratio / Imperial Rescript:** Formal re-manumission before the magistrate by the legal patron (*dominus*), OR a direct rescript (*beneficium principis*) signed by Augustus!
2. ***Mancipatio per aes et libram* ("By Bronze and Balance"):**
   - The solemn archaic Roman conveyance requiring **five adult Roman citizen witnesses**, a **scale-holder (*libripens*)** holding bronze balance-scales, and the buyer striking the scales with a piece of bronze (*raudusculum*) while speaking the exact formula claiming ownership—used to convey Italic land, slaves, easements, emancipations, and *testamenta per aes et libram* (Roman wills!). One defective witness (e.g., an unrecognized Junian Latin or *infamis* gladiator/pimp!) invalidates a million-sesterce will!
3. ***Humiliores* vs. *Honestiores*:**
   - By Antoninus Pius's reign, criminal law formally splits mankind in two: senators, equestrians, and decurions (*honestiores*) cannot be flogged, tortured, crucified, or sent to the mines—their worst non-treason penalty is pleasant island exile (*relegatio* / *deportatio*); everyone below (*humiliores*), even freeborn poor citizens like fifteen-year-old Corvus, can be beaten with rods by the Urban Prefect's lictors, thrown to the beasts *ad bestias*, or condemned to sulfur and lead mines (*in metallum*) if they lack the `400,000 HS` equestrian ring! Climbing is not vanity—it is literally keeping the iron hook and the whip off your spine.
