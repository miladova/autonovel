# Book One Seed — By Bronze and Balance (156–161 CE)

## 1. The Pitch
On the Ides of October, 156 CE, at the zenith of the Antonine peace, two fifteen-year-old childhood companions—Lucius Cassius Corvus, the penniless freeborn son of a dead Sabine land-surveyor, and Lucida, the razor-tongued daughter of a Tyrian freedwoman dyer trapped in the legal purgatory of Junian Latin status—walk through the Flaminian Gate into Rome with stolen brick-tallies, blood on their hems, and three silver denarii between them. Over five ruthless years leading to the death of Emperor Antoninus Pius in March 161 CE, Rome's twin ladders of law and shadow-commerce pull them upward out of the Subura's filth and steadily turn the one person each loves most into the one rival neither can afford to leave standing.

## 2. Viewpoint Architecture & Register
Dual close third-person past tense in the lineage of Hilary Mantel: strictly anchored behind the eyes and ribs of either Corvus or Lucida (fifteen chapters each, alternating with structural variation where pressure demands). Zero omniscient overview, zero modern moralizing, zero euphemism. Unfiltered adult historical realism (18+): Rome rendered in lime-dust, fuller's urine, blood, court Latin, commercial arithmetic, bathhouse steam, tenement terror, and raw bodily appetite.

## 3. Scale & Chapter Contract
- 30 chapters across Five Parts (156–161 CE, ages 15 to 20).
- Every chapter ~6,000 words, constructed of 2–3 distinct, fully dramatized scenes (max 2 scene breaks per chapter).
- Every single scene occupies a distinct Roman space, dramatic engine, and power configuration—no repeated room shapes or redundant beats.

## 4. Trilogy Horizon (15 Years: 156–171 CE)
- **Book I — By Bronze and Balance (156–161 CE, ages 15–20):** Childhood indivisibility in the mud of the Tiber brickyards and the Subura → asymmetric ascent through Basilica Julia courts and Caelian/Subura bakeries, tenements, and cosmetic-dye syndicates → agonizing unspoken physical pull → double betrayal on the night Antoninus Pius dies at Lorium, hardening them into sworn mortal enemies on opposite sides of the new dual-emperor court (Marcus Aurelius vs. Lucius Verus).
- **Book II — Two Suns in the West (161–166 CE, ages 20–25):** Open commercial, legal, and political warfare between equestrian advocate-administrator Corvus and newly enfranchised shipping-and-grain magnate Lucida through the Great Tiber Flood, Ostian grain crises, and Eastern war contracts; obsessive hatred laced with destructive carnal collisions.
- **Book III — The Auction of Trajan (166–171 CE, ages 25–30):** The returning legions bring the Antonine Plague; palaces empty, fortunes burn, Lucius Verus dies on the road from Altinum, and Marcus Aurelius auctions the imperial purple and pearls in the Forum of Trajan. Having failed to kill what binds them, the two thirty-year-old survivors tear down the last remnants of their old masters together—enemies broken open into equals and lovers atop the remade city.
