# Chapter XXI — Red Wind in August

*Nineteenth Day Before the Kalends of September, AD 160 · Corvus*

Forty-one days without rain had baked the clay roof-tiles of Rome hot enough by noon to blister a pigeon's claws, and at the third watch of an August night when the hot southern *Auster* blew dry dust all the way from Libya, the brass fire-bells along the Carinae began clanging all at once.

Corvus did not wake to the bells; he was already sitting shirtless at his open third-floor Carinae window in the stifling dark, squinting by a single tallow dip over municipal archive sheets while warm wind rattled dry papyrus corners like locust wings.

First came a low, greasy orange smear blooming behind the black pantile ridge of the lower **Clivus Suburanus** three hundred yards north.

Forty breaths later—too fast, ten times too fast for an accidental overturned kitchen-brazier or cobbler's lamp!, a solid **seventy-foot column of roaring white-and-scarlet oil-flame** tore clean through the seventh-floor roof-shingles of the condemned timber fuller's *insula* directly behind **Marcus Ulpius Felix**'s—behind **Lucida**'s, new four-storey brick grain-vaults!

Superheated Spanish lamp-oil drums bursting high under dry pine rafters sent blazing golden gouts arching thirty feet sideways across the narrow drip-alley on the back of the howling southern wind; instantly the terrifying nocturnal chorus of a Subura conflagration split the dark from hill to hill: brass *vigiles* bugles (*buccinae*) blaring alarm from the Viminal barracks, dogs howling frantic in locked yards, mothers screaming children's names down pitch-black wooden staircases already filling with suffocating pine-smoke, and the sharp *crack-crack-BOOM* of sun-dried terracotta roof-pantiles exploding off burning oak purlins like legionary sling-stones!

Corvus dragged on rough grey woollen tunic and thick hobnailed boots in ten heartbeats, snatched two items off his oak table—his stamped bronze **Urban Prefecture Building-Commission tessera** (still valid under the temporary seal of the urban *vicarius* three weeks after old Lollius Urbicus died of dropsy in July!) and a three-foot solid Noric iron pry-bar, soaked his coarse winter woollen cloak dripping wet inside his room's clay water-amphora, tied a wet linen rag tight across his nose and scarred mouth, and bolted down the dark Carinae steps four treads at a leap.

Halfway down the stepped cut into the Subura canyon, the street ceased to be a road and became a panicking cattle-chute.

Hundreds of half-naked shoemakers, weavers, prostitutes, and smoky-faced children poured uphill screaming against Corvus's chest, dragging straw mattresses, birdcages, brass kettles, and crippled grandparents out of the roaring orange furnace below while sparks big as winter chestnuts whirled overhead on the hot Libyan wind, setting canvas shop-awnings ablaze sixty yards downwind! Runaway butcher's mules with singed manes bolted blind over dropped bundles; Corvus flattened his wet-cloaked shoulder against stone shop-piers, hooking bruised children up onto high baker's counters out of the trampling hooves as he fought his way yard by yard upstream into heat fierce enough to crisp his eyelashes.

Fifty paces below the Sandaliarius corner, the seven-storey timber fuller's block behind **Lot Fourteen** was already a single skeletal cage of white-hot vertical pine beams collapsing inward floor by roaring floor!

Up on one unburned fifth-floor balcony overhang—cut off from the blazing central pine stair, a fuller's journeyman tossed two bundled feather-mattresses down onto the basalt cobbles, dropped his screaming six-year-old boy into twenty outstretched neighbors' arms, and jumped blind through orange smoke himself, breaking his collarbone with an audible crack ten feet from Corvus's boots.

And directly across the narrow five-foot rear drip-alley stood **Lot Fourteen**—Lucida's fifty-eight-foot fireproof red brick *insula*!

Her twenty-four-inch *opus testaceum* brick walls and iron-shuttered rear vents were **holding clean against the blast**, not one inner timber joist had caught yet!—except out in the front smoke of the Clivus Suburanus, blocking the street with forty freedmen troopers swinging twenty-foot iron-shod demolition hooks (*falces*) and a heavy wheeled battering-beam, stood **Optio Gaius Nymphidius** of the Fourth Cohort of *Vigiles*!

"Hook the third-storey brick window-piers on Felix's block, heave together on the horn!" Nymphidius was bellowing at the top of his greased lungs, pointing his vine-switch *away* from the collapsed arson-pile straight at **Lucida's intact red brick facade**! "Pull that whole brick front flat across the street for a firebreak before flames jump the coppersmith's roof!"

"Centurion Aper's pump-wagons aren't even on the street yet!" an honest grey-haired *siphonarius* protested, squinting up through blowing sparks at Lot Fourteen's untouched tile cornice. "Optio, those twenty-four-inch brick piers are blocking the east draft—pull Felix's wall down and the wind blows straight into the coppersmith's charcoal-sheds!"

"Shut your teeth and set four iron claws into that middle brick arch, soldier, before I put you on bread-and-water!" Nymphidius roared, doing **Hermippus**'s exact paid butcher-work in broad torchlight: pulling Lucida's twelve-thousand-sesterce brick building down into rubble under untouchable emergency fire-law while burying her underground wheat-vaults beneath collapsed masonry!

Corvus burst through the cordon of spear-butts, tore the wet rag down off his chin, and thrust his stamped bronze Prefecture *tessera* two inches in front of Nymphidius's smoke-streaked nose while jamming his three-foot iron pry-bar horizontally beneath the nearest hook-pole!

"Drop that iron hook off Sound Masonry, *Optio*, by order of the Urban Prefecture Building Commission!" Corvus shouted over the roar of collapsing pine rafters fifty feet behind the block. "Twenty-four-inch *opus testaceum* standing *upwind* of the coppersmith's court *is* your firebreak—pull that pier down and I charge you personally before Tribune Sempronius at dawn with corrupt destruction under the *Lex Iulia de Vi*! Look under the ground-arch, there are living folk inside those vaults!"

Nymphidius recoiled half a pace at seeing a six-foot Roman citizen commissioner brandishing the dead Prefect's bronze seal—yet already six iron claws had bitten deep into the unhardened autumn mortar of Lot Fourteen's upper fourth-storey brick parapet while twelve troopers down the smoky line heaved blind on hemp drag-ropes without hearing Nymphidius's whistle!

High overhead, mortar groaned like splitting ice; red *bipedales* bricks began peeling outward sixty feet above the street!

And down inside the open vaulted ground-arch of Lot Fourteen, lit bright crimson through billowing black smoke—nineteen-year-old **Lucida**, sixteen-year-old **Sura**, and two soot-blackened Marsian kneaders soaked head-to-foot in dripping brown vinegar were hauling heavy sixty-pound sacks of Sicilian reserve wheat frantically up the narrow stone cellar steps before buckling hot iron shutters trapped them below!

Corvus didn't waste another breath on Nymphidius; vaulting the slackened drag-rope, he plunged straight under the trembling ground-floor brick arch into blinding grey lime-dust!

"Out of the arch, Nymphidius's hooks already cracked the fourth-storey parapet!" Corvus roared down the smoky tufa stair-mouth as two loose bricks crashed splintering onto the basalt flags three feet behind his heels.

Up out of the smoking cellar stair stumbled Lucida—hair plastered wet with sour brown vinegar to her soot-streaked cheeks, her linen tunic torn open at one singed shoulder, carrying one end of a heavy wooden grain-stretcher while burly Marsian **Marcus** carried the front! Right behind them scrambled Sura, dragging eighteen-year-old junior baker's boy **Titus**, who had gone back down for the bronze tally-chest and twisted his ankle on the bottom step!

Overhead a sickening, thunderous *CRACK* split the air from wall to wall: twelve *vigiles* troopers hauling blindly down in the smoke had just torn the entire unfinished eastern fourth-storey brick gable loose off its timber centering, dropping **ten cartloads of solid brickwork and flaming pine scaffold-planks** straight down through the third- and second-floor wells toward the ground-floor vault-arch!

"Back, under the Republican peperino pier!" Corvus yelled, dropping his wet wool cloak and driving his three-foot Noric iron bar angled like a siege-prop under the buckling oak lintel-beam of the cellar-head while grabbing Lucida's vinegar-soaked belt with his left fist to haul both her and Sura flat beneath the indestructible three-foot-thick Republican stone arch-pier!

Down came the sky.

Deafening white lime-dust, shattered red bricks, and blazing pine beams hammered the courtyard floor two feet in front of their faces in a five-heartbeat avalanche that knocked every breath out of their lungs and turned midnight pitch-black; a massive twelve-inch fir beam bounced end-over-end off the fallen masonry pile and **slammed sideways across young Marsian Titus's outstretched left shin** a fraction of a heartbeat before Marcus could yank the boy clear!

Titus's shriek cut thin and terrible through the choking brick-dust.

For three heartbeats chunks of broken tile pattered down onto Corvus's braced back—he had thrown his own body curved like a masonry arch straight over crouched Lucida and Sura against the peperino pier, taking a glancing fist-sized lump of hard mortar square across his left shoulder-blade that numbed his arm to the elbow!

In the pitch-black lime-cloud underneath his ribs, Lucida coughed sour vinegar-air once against his throat, hands flying wildly up his chest until her wet fingers found his face whole and uncrushed.

"Corvus, your back!" she gasped raw into his ear, before Titus screamed again in the rubble six feet away and both of them scrambled out simultaneously into the clearing red glare.

Centurion **Gaius Arrius Aper** had just arrived at a dead run from the Viminal with his second detachment and two bronze water-*siphones*, knocking Nymphidius's hook-gang violently off the drag-ropes with the flat of his vine-staff and turning twin hissing white jets of water straight onto the collapsed fuller's embers!

On the rubble-choked threshold, Corvus heaved the fallen twelve-inch fir beam off young Titus's leg with bleeding, blistered hands while Lucida dropped to both knees beside the boy, slitting his blood-soaked trouser-linen away with her boot-knife: both tibia and fibula jutted white-splintered through torn skin four inches above the ankle—a compound crush-fracture that meant either immediate Pergamene bone-setting or gangrene within four days, and meant Titus would never stand twelve hours at a kneading-trough again!

Lucida whipped off her own vinegar-wet leather belt, twisted a tight tourniquet four inches above the boy's knee with an oak stick to check the pumping dark blood, and barked at tall Marsian Marcus:

"Lift him steady on that shutter-board, straight down the back steps to **Asclepiades of Pergamum** behind Peace's Temple, tell him I pay fifty silver denarii down if he saves the foot! Sura, run to the Fish-Market right now—tell Barba to allow no panic and keep all four mills turning **Day Eight Hundred Ninety-Seven** past a hundred and ten *modii* even if half the Subura burns to cinders!"

She started up off her bruised knees to follow the stretcher, and froze as four armored soldiers of the **Urban Cohorts** marched through the hissing steam behind tall, hawk-nosed **Tribune Lucius Sempronius** of the *Vigiles*, while crooked *Optio* **Nymphidius** stepped eagerly forward holding up **two half-burned, oil-soaked pine torch-staves** pulled out of the rear drip-alley rubble!

Burned deep into the uncharred wooden butt of both pine staves in two-inch iron branding-letters—visible ten feet off by lantern-light, shone:

*PIST · PISC · BARBAE*

*Fish-Market Bakery of Barba.*

Beside the coppersmith's wall thirty paces off through thinning smoke, Corvus glimpsed **one-eared Gaius Ummidius Scaeva** standing calmly among gawking neighbors with his arms folded across his brown cloak, watching Tribune Sempronius's face—and watching **Lucida**!

Cold horror turned Corvus's stomach to stone.

Scaeva hadn't merely burned down Vedius's condemned seven-storey timber shell to clear forty unprofitable tenants and smash Lot Fourteen's roof; **Scaeva had planted oil-torches branded with Barba's stolen bread-crate iron** in the rear alley so Tribune Sempronius would impound *both* Lot Fourteen AND the Fish-Market *pistrinum* before sunrise under a capital *incendium* investigation!

Shut Barba's Fish-Market ovens for **one single twenty-four-hour inquest** on Day Eight Hundred Ninety-Seven, and **nearly two and a half years of Lucida's three-year citizenship road died before noon**, and worse, a Junian Latin suspected of urban *incendium* went straight into chains at the Castra Urbana!

"Go with Titus's stretcher to Asclepiades right now before the boy bleeds out on the cobbles!" Corvus hissed urgent and low against Lucida's soot-blackened ear before Tribune Sempronius spotted her torn linen shift behind the water-pump. "Don't let Sempronius take your name in this alley while Nymphidius holds those planted brands—go through the coppersmith's yard, let *me* break Nymphidius's inquest on the stones!"

"Corvus, don't touch Felix's, " Lucida began fiercely, catching at his torn sleeve as Titus groaned weakly on the shutter-board behind her and blood seeped past the tourniquet stick—leaving her three heartbeats to choose between a boy bleeding to death in front of her knees and a magistrate thirty feet away!

"Save the boy's leg, I won't let them touch the Fish-Market ovens, I swear by my father's bones!" Corvus said, shoving her gently behind the steaming bronze *sipho* wagon so she vanished after Titus's stretcher down the side-cut two heartbeats before Tribune Sempronius strode into the arch!

* * *

By first grey smear of August dawn, seven burned-out Subura shop-fronts smoked dull white under hissing vinegar-blankets, six charred human corpses lay laid out under coarse matting by the fountain-curb, and **Tribune Lucius Sempronius**—furious, sleepless, facing Palatine wrath over losing half a block three hundred yards from Augustus's Forum, sat straddling a commandeered butcher's stool in the middle of ankle-deep wet ash with his clerks writing summary arrest warrants on his knee.

"Two resined pine fire-brands stamped *PIST · PISC · BARBAE* pulled straight out of the ignition-alley between Lot Fourteen and the burned seven-storey block!" Nymphidius recited virtuously, standing at stiff military attention beside the soot-stained brands. "Old baker Marcus Ulpius Felix registered Lot Fourteen to dry baking-wood and roast grain; clearly his careless night-boys from Barba's Fish-Market ovens stored hot charcoal or oil-brands in the passage, touched off the fuller's lath, and burned sixty citizens out of bed! Give me ten cohort soldiers, Tribune, and I'll padlock Barba's Fish-Market mills and haul Barba and Felix in irons before the first hour!"

Padlock Barba's Fish-Market mills before the first hour—with only thirty *modii* left to finish Day Eight Hundred Ninety-Seven!

Smoke-blackened from hair to boots, left shoulder throbbing purple where the fallen mortar lump had struck him, dried Sidonian glass scars pale across his blistered right palm, nineteen-year-old **Corvus** stepped straight up to Tribune Sempronius's stool beside **Centurion Arrius Aper**.

"Sign a padlock order on the Fish-Market *pistrinum* off those two clean pine butts, Tribune," Corvus said, his voice cold, flat, carrying twenty paces through the dripping quiet so every watching shopkeeper and Centurion Aper heard every syllable, "and by the third hour Senator Quintus Servilius Priscus lays sworn evidence before Marcus Caesar showing your *Optio* Nymphidius just tried to cover **seventh-floor commercial arson** with planted crate-wood!"

Sempronius's hawk-head snapped up. "Who are you—Urban Sub-Commissioner Cassius? Explain yourself in sixty breaths before I clear this street!"

Corvus picked up one of Nymphidius's two stamped pine brands by its unburned square handle and held it next to a fallen, half-calcined red brick from Lot Fourteen's rear wall.

"Three facts of stone and wind, Tribune, ask your own veteran Centurion Aper if one inch lies!" Corvus snapped cleanly. "First: tonight's *Auster* wind blew hard **from south-east to north-west** all night long. Look at the soot-shadow baked onto Lot Fourteen's surviving twenty-four-inch brick wall: clean red clay below twenty feet, blistered white oil-glaze **forty-five feet up at fifth- and sixth-storey level**, proving intense downward-and-westward flame blew *out of the upper fuller's garrets onto Felix's roof*, never up from ground-level! Second: smell the char on Nymphidius's pine handle—soaked in cheap rancid Spanish lamp-dregs, yet no ash baked into the branded letters because whoever tossed it into wet alley rubble *after* Aper's water-pumps cooled the stones forgot five hundred degrees of falling pine rafters would have burned a thin crate-stick to grey powder in forty breaths! Third, and decisive against Nymphidius's lie that Lot Fourteen held baking-furnaces or roasting-flues—**here is the certified Aediles' architectural building-file for Lot Fourteen**!"

Corvus reached into his scorched leather satchel, where he carried duplicate Region IV building-sheets—and slapped the official stamped plan across Sempronius's knees.

"No ovens, no flues, no fire-pits permitted or built inside Lot Fourteen, solely cold subterranean tufa grain-storage vaults!" Corvus drove home triumphantly.

And right there, in the grey light of dawn, **Tribune Sempronius's sharp equestrian finger slid two lines further down the Aediles' attached tax-and-surety schedule**—the fine-print financial rider Phileros's clerk had stapled to the back of Region IV's suspect-building dossier last month!

Sempronius frowned, squinting hard at the bottom wax seal.

"Wait a moment, Commissioner Cassius," Sempronius said slowly, hawk-eyes narrowing. "If cold grain-vaults exonerate the Fish-Market ovens from fire-negligence, and they do, Nymphidius, stand back under arrest in quarters until Centurion Aper inspects that seventh-floor oil-sump!—what is **seventy-year-old pauper Marcus Ulpius Felix**, assessed at four thousand sesterces total property in the Appian census, doing filing a twelve-thousand-sesterce cash purchase and an eighteen-thousand-sesterce masonry contract backed by commercial receipts from **Lucida, Junian Latin co-exercitor of the Fish-Market bakery**?"

Ice shot straight through Corvus's veins.

*No—Phileros's clerk had cross-indexed Felix's Appian pauper census sheet right onto the back of the building-plan!*

"Under Trajan's municipal rebuilding rescript and the *Lex Iunia* restrictions on disguised *fiducia* trusts," Tribune Sempronius said crisply to his clerk, delighted as any bureaucrat at finding a clean fiscal forfeiture to show the Palatine so his morning report didn't come back empty-handed!—"a pauper citizen holding urban land-title purely as unrecorded *interposita persona* to evade municipal surety deposits forfeits the rebuilding ground-lease to summary public re-auction within seventy-two hours! Enter **Fish-Market *Pistrinum*: cleared of all *incendium* suspicion, mills to remain open uninterrupted**. Enter **Clivus Suburanus Lot Fourteen: ground-surface rebuilding lease sequestered for municipal re-auction Thursday at the sixth hour**!"

Corvus opened his mouth to block the sequestration on equitable *impensae utiles* grounds, and stopped as silence parted the smoke-blackened crowd at the foot of the street.

Walking slowly up the ash-choked cobbles from the direction of Peace's surgery came nineteen-year-old **Lucida**.

Young Titus's blood had dried dark rust across both her bare forearms and torn linen skirt; her dark braids hung half-unpinned and grey with cold fuller's ash; behind her walked mute **Sura**, eyes huge and hollow after checking that Barba's four Fish-Market mills were still turning safe at **MODII CXII** on **Day Eight Hundred Ninety-Seven**.

Lucida stopped ten paces away beside a municipal clerk hammering the painted wooden **LOCATIO PVBLICA — EX AUCTORITATE PRAEFECTVRAE** sequestration placard straight into the scorched oak hoarding of **her twelve-thousand-sesterce brick building**, reading in three silent heartbeats both halves of what had just happened:

Her Fish-Market *pistrinum* and her precious citizenship count stood **untouched and safe**... because nineteen-year-old **Lucius Cassius Corvus**, standing five yards away beside the Vigiles Tribune with *her* Aediles' building-file open on the magistrate's knee, had just handed over the exact paper that stripped old Ulpius Felix's straw-mask off in public, **throwing thirty thousand sesterces of her blood, brick, and buried tufa vaults onto Thursday's rubble-auction block—where every vulture in Rome, starting with Publius Vedius Pollio and Senator Priscus's own land-buyers, would smell bare meat**!

Corvus took two steps through the wet grey ash toward her, blistered hands open at his sides.

"Vespera, Nymphidius had planted Barba's branded pine torches in the alley to padlock your Fish-Market mills before the first hour," Corvus said low, urgent, begging her with his eyes to hear the whole equation before judgment. "Showing Sempronius the cold-vault plan was the only way on earth to stop ten soldiers shutting your stones down on Day Eight Hundred Ninety-Seven! By Thursday noon I can put an equestrian bidder into that auction to buy your lease back—"

*"Put one of your equestrian bidders into my auction?"*

Lucida spoke barely above a whisper, her voice raw and scraped thin with pitch-smoke, blood dried brown from young Titus's crushed leg flaking off her knuckles as she looked from the Prefecture placard nailed into her bricks straight into Corvus's soot-streaked face.

"Titus lost three inches of shin-bone on Asclepiades's table twenty breaths ago while you were handing my straw-man's census sheet to a *vigiles* tribune without asking me," Lucida said, every word dropping dead and flat into the wet August ash between their boots. "And now you offer to buy *my* walls back inside *your* equestrian friend's pocket so I rent my own stones off your leash?"

"Lucida, without that plan they were marching to padlock Barba's ovens!"

"I had Centurion Aper on the street and four witnesses from the coppersmith's forge who watched Scaeva's Cappadocians carry oil-drums upstairs in December!" she answered, dark amber eyes dry, unblinking, colder than winter river-ice inside ninety-degree smoke. "Don't bring an equestrian bidder near my bricks on Thursday, Lucius Cassius. If your Caelian friends bid one brass *as* on Lot Fourteen, you'll learn how steep Subura rubble costs."

She turned away without wiping the ash off her cheek, put one blood-stained arm round trembling Sura's shoulders, and walked straight down into the rising sun Smoke—leaving Corvus standing ankle-deep in black vinegar-slush beside the saved vault-arch while municipal hammers nailed his rescue across her door.
