# Chapter XII — Saffron and Sweat

*Ides of October, AD 158 · Lucida*

Under the sixty-foot concrete vaults of the **Baths of Trajan**, three hundred naked women sounded louder than four legions crossing a bridge.

Up through pierced bronze floor-grates billowed thick white steam off subterranean wood-furnaces, mixing with the sharp, expensive reek of Cilician saffron oil, crushed myrtle, wet human hair, and sour wine-sweat scraped off three hundred pairs of thighs by curved bronze *strigiles* and flicked *splat* into the marble perimeter gutters. Every sound multiplied tenfold off the green Karystian marble dadoes and dripping glass mosaic domes overhead: shrill shrieks from the armpit-pluckers' benches where Syrian girls snapped bronze tweezers (*volsellae*) mercilessly along oiled skin; the rhythmic *slap-slap-slap* of flat greased palms kneading fat senators' wives on heated marble slabs; the splash of bodies rolling out of the scalding semicircular *alveus* tub; and the singsong cries of pastry-girls wading bare-legged through the mist selling honeyed sausages and snow-chilled *mulsum* off tin trays.

Eighteen-year-old Lucida—she had passed her eighteenth birthday seventeen days earlier without cake or holiday, marking it only by watching Sura chalk **DIES CCXIII** (Day 213 of 1,095) above Oven One at dawn, knelt in a damp, clinging knee-length linen *subligaculum* inside **Ceionia Fabia**'s curtained corner apse, warming a rock-crystal flask of first-grade Corycian saffron unguent between her palms.

Across the mosaic floor, stretched naked on a slab of veined Phrygian *pavonazzetto* marble with wet red-gold hair coiled high on her crown by two bone pins, twenty-nine-year-old Ceionia Fabia closed her eyes as Dorcas scraped lathering fuller's-earth soda (*nitrum*) off her long, freckled back.

Even stripped bare in pitiless October afternoon glare streaming through forty-foot south-facing glazed panes, an Emperor's adopted daughter carried power in the set of her hip-bones. Three equestrian matrons from the Carinae hovered outside her half-drawn linen curtain pretending to admire the mosaic Nereids while straining both ears for one scrap of Palatine gossip: whether pregnant **Faustina** was carrying another boy at Lorium, whether old **Antoninus Pius**'s winter cough had returned early, whether handsome **Lucius Commodus** would really marry a provincial cousin or stay free for bigger alliances.

"Not so hard over the shoulder-blade, Dorcas; you aren't curry-combing *Volucer*," Fabia murmured lazily, holding out one wet white arm without opening her eyes so Lucida could work warm golden saffron oil—worth forty sesterces the ounce, compounded by Lucida herself from pure Cilician stigmas steeped in sweet Hispanian virgin pressing, down from wrist to collarbone. "Tell me who just waded into the middle basin making noises like a hippopotamus in reeds."

Lucida glanced sideways past the curtain-hem without breaking the long, firm upward stroke of her oiled thumbs along Fabia's forearm.

"Domitia Paulina's cousin **Vibia**, Domina," Lucida murmured smoothly, "accompanied by Senator Aufidius's young wife—wearing three pounds of emerald pearls into steam hot enough to rot silk thread, so everyone sees Aufidius didn't lose his shirt in June's Sandaliarius boundary suit."

Fabia gave a wicked little purr deep in her throat. "Aufidius couldn't win a boundary suit against a dead badger; everyone on the Caelian knows old **Quintus Servilius Priscus** used some terrifying sixteen-year-old Sabine wolf-cub out of Fronto's kennel to tear Vedius Pollio's straw-man apart in six breaths! My brother Lucius heard Fronto bragging about the boy's Cato vowels all last Thursday."

Lucida's oiled thumbs did not falter by the breadth of a hair across Fabia's wet shoulder, though hearing Corvus called *old Priscus's Sabine wolf-cub* inside Ceionia Fabia's curtain sent a tiny, sharp prickle straight down her spine.

Then Lucida's amber eyes—trained by two years in Rome never to watch the front stage when hands moved behind the curtain, caught a flicker of dark brown wool reflected in the wet polished marble pilaster behind Dorcas's back.

Next door in the warm changing-hall (*apodyterium*), where wooden clothes-cubbies rose four tiers high guarded by salaried bath-attendants (*capsariae*) paid to keep thieves away from senators' wives' gold brooches and silk *stolae*, Fabia's hired Cappadocian locker-woman **Phryne** was not sitting on her guard-stool.

Instead Phryne had slipped one skinny arm elbow-deep inside Cubby Four—**Ceionia Fabia's private cedar cubby**, fished a tiny, thumb-long folded ivory *pugillares* love-note out of the inner girdle-pocket of Fabia's purple-bordered *stola*, tucked it inside her own dirty armpit-rag, and ducked swiftly through the narrow brick service-door leading behind the roaring bronze *testudo* water-boilers!

If Phryne had stolen Fabia's emerald bracelet, she would have bolted toward the street steps. Nobody stole a two-inch folded ivory note without touching twenty gold *aurei* of jewelry right beside it unless **somebody outside was paying silver for Ceionia Fabia's handwriting**!

And Lucida knew whose handwriting sat on that tiny ivory diptych, because Dorcas had grumbled about it in the litter coming up the Oppian Hill: **Gaius Petronius Severus**, handsome twenty-four-year-old military tribune of the Praetorian Guard—a dangerous, indiscreet correspondent for a woman married to Plautius Quintillus while Palatine moralists watched every whisper round Lucius Commodus's sister!

"Wait, the saffron crystal needs thirty breaths deeper in the hot wall-niche to thin the myrrh, Domina," Lucida lied smoothly, wiping excess oil off her palms onto a linen towel without startling either Fabia or rheumatism-stiff Dorcas. "Don't let the cold draught touch your back while I warm it."

She slipped barefoot behind the marble partition—not toward the crowded main *apodyterium*, straight through the low iron-sheeted stokers' hatch into the suffocating brick maintenance passage curving behind the bronze bath-cauldrons!

Back here the glamorous marble *Thermae* vanished instantly into soot, dripping lime, and roaring hundred-and-thirty-degree furnace-draft. Naked slave stokers black with char-ash shoveled split oak into sunken fire-mouths below; directly ahead, thirty paces down the narrow vaulted corridor where delivery-boys brought bath-wood in from the Viminal steps, stood a barred iron postern, and through those iron bars **Phryne** was already thrusting Fabia's tiny ivory diptych toward a pale, clean-shaved Greek clerk waiting in the alley shadow with four gold *aurei* gleaming in his palm!

Lucida knew that clean-shaved chin and the **three bone wax-scrapers swinging on a brass ring** at his hip before she got within twenty feet of the bars.

**Phileros.** Chief *librarius* and archive-master to **Senator Quintus Servilius Priscus**—the man who paid Corvus forty-eight sesterces every week!

Thank every god on Olympus and Carmel, smoke from Number Three furnace was blowing thick grey across the iron grating so Phileros stood squinting sideways into the wind and hadn't yet closed his fingers over the ivory!

Shout, and Phileros saw Lucida's face clearly—connecting Priscus's Caelian house to Barba's Fish-Market loft before sunset! Let Phryne pass the note through the bars, and Priscus's Stoic faction locked Ceionia Fabia's indiscretion with a Praetorian tribune right beside Vedius's red water-flask inside Priscus's cedar blackmail chest, destroying Lucida's Caelian patroness and her three-hundred-sesterce monthly Green-stable contract at one blow!

Lucida snatched a wooden bucket of cold furnace-quench water standing beside Boiler Two, kept her head bent low behind her streaming loose damp hair so only a faceless bath-servant's wet shoulder showed through the smoke, lunged the last twelve barefoot strides silently over slick soot, and **slammed her hip into Phryne's ribs** hard enough to knock the Cappadocian locker-thief flat against the brick wall—just as Lucida kicked the iron smoke-damper lever loose with her bare heel!

With a deafening iron *CLANG*, three hundred cubic feet of choking white wood-smoke and live steam belched straight out through the iron grating into the alley, sending Phileros leaping six paces backward coughing into his cloak without ever glimpsing who bumped the lever!

Inside the blinding white steam-cloud on the wet bricks, Phryne opened her mouth to screech; Lucida clamped one oil-slick hand hard over the locker-woman's teeth, pinned Phryne's throat down with one bare knee, ripped the tiny two-inch ivory diptych out of Phryne's fist, and pressed both open black-waxed inner leaves **flat against the scalding bare copper steam-pipe** feeding the *caldarium* tub two inches above their heads!

*Hsssss.*

Within four heartbeats two hundred degrees of Trajanic boiler-copper melted Petronius Severus's compromising cursive script and broken Praetorian signet into two mirror-smooth, completely blank pools of warm black beeswax!

Outside in the windy lane footsteps retreated rapidly down the cobbles—Phileros refusing to linger near a bathhouse boiler-accident where *vigiles* ran up within moments.

Lucida lifted her hand half an inch off Phryne's mouth, keeping her forearm wedged across the terrified slave-woman's windpipe in the clearing steam.

"Open your teeth above a whisper," Lucida breathed in Subura Greek right against Phryne's ear, "and Lady Ceionia Fabia tells the bath-procurator you stole her emerald pin from Cubby Four, which puts iron branding-letters across your forehead before moonrise. Walk out the wood-gate right now without your shoes, keep running till you hit Puteoli, and tell nobody whether steam or ghosts took your four gold pieces. Nod once if you want both eyes."

Phryne nodded frantically, scrambled up on hands and knees when Lucida stepped back, and bolted barefoot down the stokers' ash-ramp without looking behind her.

Lucida shut the iron smoke-damper, rinsed soot off her hands and knee in the quench-bucket, tucked the blank, warm ivory diptych safely inside her damp linen waist-band, and walked calmly back through the marble arch into Fabia's curtained apse carrying the warmed rock-crystal flask of saffron oil—less than ninety breaths after she had stepped out.

Fabia was just sitting up wrapped in soft white Milesian toweling while Dorcas slipped crimson kidskin bath-sandals onto her feet.

Lucida waited until Dorcas knelt to tie the left ankle-strap, leaned down as if anointing Fabia's collarbones with warm saffron, and slid the tiny ivory diptych silently onto Fabia's lap beneath the towel-fold, open half an inch so Fabia saw the freshly melted, completely blank wax leaves.

Fabia's green-brown eyes snapped down to the ivory, widened once as she recognized her missing Praetorian note, and shot straight up into eighteen-year-old Lucida's calm amber gaze.

"Locker-woman Phryne tried passing it through the boiler-grate to a shaved Greek carrying three bone wax-scrapers on a brass ring, Domina," Lucida murmured so low not even Dorcas at Fabia's ankles heard. "The boiler-pipe melted the wax before his fingers touched it, and Phryne just left Rome barefoot."

*Three bone wax-scrapers on a brass ring.* Every political house on the Caelian knew **Phileros**'s trademark belt-ring.

White fury turned Fabia's freckled skin the color of skimmed milk round her nostrils; then, slowly, her fingers closed tight over the blank ivory leaves until her knuckles shone bone-pale, and when she looked back at Lucida, calculation had already replaced shock—she was no longer looking at a convenient eighteen-year-old salve-peddler, rather at an operator worth bringing inside the double doors.

"Quintus Servilius Priscus's Greek bloodhound sniffing inside my bath-cubby," Fabia whispered through motionless lips. She reached into her jewel-casket beside Dorcas's elbow, drew out **five heavy gold *aurei*** (500 HS!), and pressed them straight into Lucida's saffron-oiled palm. "Starting on the Kalends of November, Lucida, you don't wait in the scullery yard or use old Berenice's name at my gate. You bring my cosmetics straight up the private library stair twice a week yourself, and whenever you hear Priscus's clerks whispering down near the Forum Piscarium, you tell me whose name they write."

"You honor my small workshop, Domina," Lucida said smoothly, bowing her damp dark head over the five gold pieces while ice water trickled silently down the inside of her ribs.

* * *

Today was the **Ides of October**—two full solar circuits since two starved, mud-caked fifteen-year-olds had slipped past the Flaminian customs-post behind a flock of bleeding red-ruddled sheep while crowds down on the Campus Martius fought over the bloody tail of the October Horse.

Across the city once again this afternoon the Subura and Sacra Via ward-gangs had brawled in the dust for Mars's horse-head (this autumn the Sacred Way boys had beaten the Subura back past the Regia, leaving half the fuller's apprentices nursing black eyes and split shins at every wine-counter Lucida passed walking down off the Oppian spur).

Back at the Fish-Market *pistrinum*, autumn dusk smelled gloriously of toasted sesame, dry oak embers, and honest prosperity.

Down in the basalt courtyard, swept twice a day with wet pine-boughs now so stale bran never bred beetles—four sleek Reatine hill-mules munched clean bruised barley in whitewashed stalls between shifts; stacked eight feet high under dry tarred sailcloth sat two hundred *modii* of prime amber Sicilian and Sardinian grain paid cash on the nail; and on the black slate beside Oven One fourteen-year-old **Sura** had just chalked in proud, square white letters:

**ANNVS I · DIES CCXXVIII — MODII CXIV**

*Two hundred twenty-eight unbroken days* at better than a hundred *modii*—already past a fifth of Trajan's three-year road, with two thousand one hundred sesterces of the burial-guild bread-advance worked cleanly off on schedule and **four thousand two hundred sesterces** net profit banked safely in Lucida's locked strong-chest!

Even paying Scaeva's hated Kalends collector his silent eighty sesterces out at the corner fish-stall five days ago had felt less like bleeding to death than tossing a meat-scrap to a yard-dog whose chain she was filing halfway through every dawn.

Old **Berenice** had limped down from the Argiletum at sunset carrying a peck of sweet Sabine chestnuts tied in a red kerchief to roast on Barba's oven-lip, her swollen ankles much easier since Lucida began buying forty percent of her cosmetic roots at steady silver—and sat gossiping in rapid Syrian Aramaic with wheezing Barba over warm spiced wine while Lucida washed bath-saffron and street-dust off her arms at the stone trough.

"Watch your shutters round the Kalends, little hawk," Berenice muttered out of the good side of her mouth as Lucida dried her elbows. "Hermippus raised rents ten percent down our whole Argiletum block Tuesday because some Caelian senator's clerks have been poking sticks into his master's boundary-stones and water-pipes all summer! Myrtale saw that pale Greek **Phileros**, Senator Priscus's ink-rat—walking past the Oppian baths three times this week with your tall Sabine boy carrying wax-bundles two paces behind him!"

Lucida hung the linen towel very neatly on its iron peg without turning round for two breaths.

*With your tall Sabine boy carrying wax-bundles two paces behind him.*

Corvus hadn't been outside the women's bath-postern this afternoon, Thursday afternoon he sat at Fronto's Esquiline declamation bench until dusk—yet **he worked six days a week at the elbow of the exact Greek spy whose fingers Lucida had just scalded off Ceionia Fabia's throat with live boiler-steam**!

When Corvus climbed the outside brick stair at the second watch, carrying a fat earthen jug of stolen ten-year-old Caecuban wine Phileros had gifted him from a grateful Tusculan client's cellar, his white citizen *toga* folded carefully over his arm, dark hair wind-tousled above shoulders that now filled the loft doorframe from jamb to jamb—every shadow of June's cobbler-eviction vanished for three precious hours.

Up in their warm terracotta-floored loft above the sleeping ovens, with Sura curled fast asleep behind the sailcloth partition hugging a stuffed rag-horse Barba had whittled her, they kicked off their boots, sat cross-legged across a low iron brazier-pan roasting split chestnuts on an iron baker's peel, and drank dark, resinous Caecuban wine out of two horn mugs.

"Remember two years ago tonight?" Corvus asked softly, peeling char-skin off a steaming chestnut with ink-stained thumbs and dropping the sweet yellow kernel straight into Lucida's open palm before peeling one for himself. Wine and hearth-glow had warmed his grey-green eyes dark as olive-leaves; next month he turned eighteen, the milestone where Priscus promised to advance him from junior factual *cognitor* to lead complex civil suits in his own voice under the Praetor's Edict, with **nine thousand four hundred sesterces** already saved in his Caelian strongbox toward the distant four-hundred-thousand mountain. "Eleven copper asses between us, rain coming through Berenice's tiles, and Sticho's seven-day piss-vats waiting at dawn."

"And you split your lip on Volusenus's stylus four weeks later because you couldn't keep your Sabine mouth shut," Lucida teased low, touching one chestnut-warm fingertip briefly—before she could stop herself, to the tiny white crescent scar at the left corner of his mouth.

Corvus didn't jerk back as he had up by the Janiculum water-wheels last summer. Instead his hand came up halfway, fingers brushing the warm curve of her wrist, eyes holding hers across two feet of red coal-light with an intensity that turned the air inside the low brick loft thick and breathless as unrisen dough.

"Wait till Day One Thousand Ninety-Six, Vespera," Corvus said huskily—still never letting on that he knew about her eighty-sesterce monthly chain to Vedius, believing he was guarding her pride while counting every day until he had the silver and legal teeth to smash Vedius whole. "Two years and four months left on those stones downstairs. The morning the *Annona* prefect stamps your citizen *ius Quiritium* tablet, we buy forty *iugera* of dry Sabine hill-pasture above Crustumerium just so nobody ever threatens us with ground-rent again, and build a stone house where every pipe and brick-stamp carries our own two names."

"Sabine hill-pasture bores me to death after three days; I want two Ostian grain-warehouses and a four-mule litter with green curtains so Ceionia Fabia's cousins have to step out of my mud," Lucida said lightly, clinking her horn mug against his to break the dangerous pull tightening between their mouths before eighteen-year-old blood swept two years of careful boundaries over the cliff.

By the third watch the heavy Caecuban wine and fourteen hours of standing in the Basilica Julia dragged Corvus's eyelids down despite himself. Stretched sideways on his folded grey wool cloak across the warm floor-tiles beside the brazier while listening to Lucida recount how old Barba chased a thieving gull out of the flour-bin with a broom, Corvus answered slower and slower until his deep breathing lengthened into deep, unguarded boy's sleep—his dark temple resting two inches from Lucida's bare left ankle, his scarred right hand lying relaxed palm-up across the warm terracotta between them.

Wide awake in the red twilight rising through the oven-vent, eighteen-year-old Lucida did not move her ankle away.

For a long time she sat completely still listening to the faint nighttime sigh of shifting embers down in Barba's brick domes and the steady rise and fall of Corvus's chest.

Inside her belt-pouch lay **five gold *aurei*** paid three hours ago by **Ceionia Fabia**, sister to **Lucius Ceionius Commodus**, ally to Praetorian tribunes and green chariot-factions—for scalding **Senator Quintus Servilius Priscus**'s chief spy off her private life.

And two inches from Lucida's bare foot slept **Quintus Servilius Priscus's sharpest young blade**, dreaming of Sabine hill-pasture and Cato's clean ledger while serving the exact austere Stoic faction around **Marcus Aurelius Caesar** and **Junius Rusticus** that meant to break Fabia's brother across their knee the day old Emperor Antoninus stopped breathing at Lorium.

Very gently, careful not to wake him, Lucida reached down in the red dark and laid her warm thumb-pad flat against the raised white crescent brick-stamp scar across Corvus's sleeping right knuckle, matching skin to old Subortanae clay.

*Hold on for eight hundred and sixty-seven more mornings,* she begged the silent brick rafters overhead, feeling a sudden cold premonition tighten round her heart despite the ninety-degree warmth rising through the floor. *Let me finish my three years on the stones before the old Emperor dies and their two houses tear Rome in half—or neither of us will recognize the hand holding the knife.*
