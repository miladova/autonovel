# Chapter XV — Lex Aquilia

*Seventeenth Day Before the Kalends of August, AD 159 · Corvus*

Under the blazing July sun outside Vespasian's Temple of Peace, seventy-year-old **Quintus Lollius Urbicus**, Prefect of the City, sat bolt upright in his curule chair wearing heavy winter-weight wool without once wiping the sweat trickling out of his iron-grey Numidian beard onto his purple border.

Men who had marched behind Urbicus twenty winters ago beyond Hadrian's Wall into the black peat-bogs of Caledonia swore the old general had left all human feeling froze solid somewhere north of the Clyde and brought back only a measuring-rod and an executioner's axe. Three cases ahead of Corvus on the morning docket, Urbicus had dispatched a crooked cattle-drover to the Sardinian mines in forty breaths, dismissed an equestrian's boundary-whine in twenty, and ordered forty lashes on the spot for a night-watchman who fell asleep over a charcoal-brazier.

Now two sweaty Syrian porters set down a woven rope-stretcher directly on the scorching travertine pavement in front of the Prefect's stone dais.

Lying flat on a folded horse-blanket across the ropes was **Thais**, a twenty-four-year-old skilled silk-and-linen damask weaver from Antioch—bought out of savings two years ago and married *contubernio* (though not yet formally manumitted before the Praetor because her husband lacked the five-percent *vicesima libertatis* tax!) by **Marcus Licinius Syriacus**, a weeping, round-shouldered freedman cap-maker kneeling beside her stretcher clutching Corvus's toga-hem.

Eleven nights ago, up on the fifth floor of a rotten timber-framed Subura *insula* owned through two straw-leases by **Gaius Munatius's brother-in-law**, worm-eaten fir ceiling-joists overloaded with wet amphora-clay stored illegally upstairs had snapped at midnight without warning, dropping twelve hundred pounds of rubble straight across Thais's sleeping loom-bench and shattering both sides of her pelvic ring.

Asclepiades of Pergamum had kept her breathing; she would never stand upright, never work a foot-treadle loom, and never carry a living child past the fourth month again.

Across the pavement stood the landlord's sneering defense pleader **Vibius Crispus**, waving a two-hundred-sesterce bag of copper and small silver lazily toward the stretcher.

"My client denies all structural *culpa*, Illustrious Prefect," Vibius drawled, fanning his neck with a folded papyrus leaf. "Old Subura fir dries brittle in July heat; Jupiter sends timber-splits just as he sends thunderbolts! Even if Chapter Three of the *Lex Aquilia* applied, look at the woman—both hands whole, eyesight clear, quite capable of spinning coarse wool with a hand-spindle from a stool! Two hundred sesterces generously covers thirty days' lost spinning while her bruises fade."

Down on the rope-stretcher, Thais bit hard on a knotted strip of rag so she wouldn't cry out before the terrible old Prefect as street-flies buzzed round the blood-spotted bandages strapping her crushed hips; beside her, Syriacus looked up in blind terror at eighteen-year-old Corvus, who had taken the desperate cap-maker's brief for **no silver fee at all**, purely because watching broken Subura folk thrown onto cobbles had eaten at his ribs ever since old cobbler Trebatius wept by the bridge last summer.

Except standing here in ninety-eight-degree glare before **Lollius Urbicus**, Corvus faced the cruellest trap in the entire Roman civil law.

Had Syriacus managed to free his beloved Thais before the ceiling fell, Syriacus would have recovered **almost nothing** today—only Asclepiades's twenty-eight-sesterce doctor's bill and bare lost wages, because as **Gaius** wrote in cold black ink, *cicatricium aut deformitatis nulla fit aestimatio, quia liberum corpus nullam recipit aestimationem*: **no money valuation is ever put upon scars or crippling deformity of a free human body, because a free body has no market price**.

Only because Thais was still technically her husband's **slave chattel** on paper could Corvus win the four thousand sesterces Syriacus needed to keep both of them out of the gutter—and only if Corvus stood six feet above that weeping twenty-four-year-old woman in open public square and priced her broken womb and crushed bones **strictly like a cracked wine-jar or a hamstrung pack-mule**!

Cry pity before Lollius Urbicus, and the old Caledonian iron-post cut your clepsydra off in ten words. Speak butcher's arithmetic, and they ate this winter.

Corvus stepped half a pace ahead of Syriacus, adjusted no fold of his crisp white toga, and looked straight up into Lollius Urbicus's unblinking yellow-grey eyes.

"Counsel offers two hundred sesterces for bruises, Prefect," Corvus said, his deep Fronto-honed voice dry as baked brick, devoid of a single tremor of sentiment. "Chapter Three of the *Lex Aquilia* prices neither bruises nor tears; under Sabinus and Julian it awards the owner the **highest market value the damaged chattel held within the thirty days preceding the wrongful *ruptio***, less residual scrap value on the block today."

Down on the stretcher Thais flinched slightly at hearing herself called *damaged chattel* and *scrap value*, turning her pale, sweating face away toward the rope; Syriacus squeezed his eyes shut. Corvus did not look down at either of them; he kept the iron shutter locked behind his ribs and laid three certified wax tablets one by one along the edge of the Prefect's clerk-table.

"First, on *culpa*: here is the sworn deposition of two fourth-floor tenants who warned the landlord's *insularius* nine days before the collapse that wet clay-tubs upstairs were bowing the fifth-floor fir joists two full inches out of level and dropping worm-frass into their soup. Storing heavy industrial wet clay on worm-eaten residential pine after warning isn't Jupiter's thunderbolt; Julian's *Digest* Book Nine calls overloading rotten beams classic actionable *imperitia et negligentia*."

Urbicus didn't nod, yet his heavy chin stayed still, meaning *Go on to quantum; liability is proved.*

"Second, on market depreciation within thirty days," Corvus continued without drawing breath, holding up the second tablet. "Thirty days ago at the Saepta Julia dealers' pens, healthy Syrian four-heddle damask weavers aged twenty to twenty-five fetched **four thousand eight hundred sesterces** certified average—supported here by Syriacus's own workshop ledger showing Thais wove fourteen yards of patterned Coan-style table-linen a month clearing seventy sesterces net above thread. Today, with both iliac crests fractured inward, verified here under the seal of **Asclepiades of Pergamum**—she cannot work foot-treadles, cannot stand at an upright warp-beam, and carries permanent pelvic narrowing fatal to parturition, destroying all *partus ancillae* breeding value that normally accounts for twenty percent of a twenty-four-year-old female slave's Saepta price."

Vibius Crispus opened his mouth to sneer; Corvus's voice cut flat across his teeth like a mason's trowel:

"A crippled hand-spinner who must be lifted onto a night-stool by a second person fetches **at most four hundred sesterces** at the damaged-goods block behind Castor, less two hundred eighty sesterces already spent on surgical splints and squill-wine to keep the owner's capital asset from dying altogether! Four thousand eight hundred less four hundred, plus two hundred eighty salvage expense: **four thousand six hundred eighty sesterces** pure statutory *damnum iniuria datum*, Illustrious Prefect—and since the defendant denied liability *infitiando* despite prior joist-warnings, the *Lex Aquilia* **doubles the condemnation against a liar to nine thousand three hundred sixty sesterces** unless paid down before sunset!"

Silence dropped like lead over the hot travertine paving-stones. Vibius Crispus's fan stopped moving mid-air as the *duplum* penalty for false denial slammed shut over his client's fingers!

Up on the curule dais, old **Lollius Urbicus** stared down for four slow heartbeats at eighteen-year-old Corvus, measuring the boy's unblinking grey-green eyes, the absence of wasted adjectives, the surgical mastery of *infitiatio* doubling—and lifted one gnarled brown forefinger toward his *assessor*.

"Liability proved on overloaded beams," Urbicus rasped in dry camp Latin. "Enter single *damnum* at **four thousand four hundred sesterces** if paid in silver into the plaintiff's hand before the tenth hour today; **eight thousand eight hundred** *in duplum* with bodily seizure of the landlord's rents tomorrow dawn if unpaid. Next scroll!"

Behind Corvus, freedman Syriacus collapsed onto both knees kissing the dusty hem of Corvus's toga and sobbing thanks in Aramaic, four thousand four hundred sesterces was enough not only to rent a sunny ground-floor garden room where Thais could sit propped on cushions in clean air, enough to pay her five-percent manumission tax so she died a free Roman wife!

Yet as the porters lifted the stretcher carefully off the stones, twenty-four-year-old **Thais** looked up once past her sobbing husband straight into eighteen-year-old Corvus's face.

She bowed her dark head in silent gratitude for their saved lives—and at the exact same time shrank half an inch back into her blanket away from his eyes, the way a lamb shrank from a clean-handed butcher who had weighed its kidneys down to the ounce without blinking once.

Corvus watched her stretcher sway out through the colonnade arch into the glare, refusing Syriacus's tearful offer of twenty denarii out of the judgment, and stood still for ten breaths in the shade of a porphyry column wiping dry dust, there was no sweat—off his scarred right thumb.

*That is how you win inside their marble,* he told himself coldly. *Cry over her broken womb and Vibius walks away paying two hundred coppers; price her womb like cracked Syrian pottery and she sleeps in a garden tonight.*

Still, all the way up the Carinae steps toward his lonely bachelor third-floor study, rented in May two weeks after that freezing dusk encounter in Pompey's Portico, because sleeping two yards from Lucida in Barba's ninety-degree summer loft while Tullia Crispina's green bracelets and Ceionia Fabia's unspoken shadow hung between them had become physical torture neither eighteen-year-old body could endure without catching fire or drawing knives—Corvus couldn't quite wash the sound of his own voice pricing *partus ancillae* out of his ears.

* * *

At the eleventh hour, carrying a woven rush basket of yellow Sabine freestone peaches bought off a Crustumerium countryman's mule at the Colline market, Corvus turned down the noisy, brine-sharp alley behind the **Forum Piscarium**.

He still kept half the loft rent paid three months ahead in Habinnas Barba's ledger, ignoring Lucida's curt insistence in May that she needed none of his silver—and still stopped by the bakehouse courtyard every ten or twelve days before sunset to check Sura's arithmetic slate and make sure Hermippus's thugs weren't sniffing round the walls.

Inside the smoky stone arch, heat from two banked twelve-foot brick domes rolled out to meet him mixed with the rich, nutty fragrance of four hundred fresh loaves cooling on willow racks. Up beside Oven One, fifteen-year-old **Sura**, grown tall and willow-straight now, her scarred left forearm bare in July heat without shame inside her own courtyard—spotted Corvus first, beamed with pure, unforced loyalty that hadn't learned adult politics yet, and tapped her chalk proudly against today's square white numerals:

**ANNVS II · DIES CXXXVIII — MODII CXVI**

*Five hundred and three unbroken days* altogether—nearly halfway across the thousand-and-ninety-five-day canyon!

"Good girl, Sura," Corvus said warmly, tossing the biggest Sabine peach straight into her floury hands before pausing short beside the inner grain-room door.

Out through the brick storeroom doorway stepped two women talking low in rapid Greek: crooked old one-eyed **Berenice** leaning on a cornel-wood stick, and beside her a trim, sour-faced fifty-year-old freedwoman wearing an expensive, unmistakable dark-green Caelian house-stola and carrying an embroidered linen tally-bag stamped on the bronze clasp with **a prancing horse above *DOMVS CEIONIA***!

Corvus knew that mole on the left nostril from Phileros's Caelian household intelligence sheets within half a breath: **Dorcas**, chief confidential tire-woman to **Ceionia Fabia**, sister of Lucius Ceionius Commodus!

Right behind Dorcas in the doorway stood eighteen-year-old **Lucida**.

She wore a sleeveless summer baker's shift of fine unbleached Egyptian linen belted snug round her narrow waist with twisted green cord, dark braids pinned high off a damp, glowing olive neck, bare arms dusted faint gold-white to the elbows from checking flour-bolters. At eighteen she had grown into something that stopped grain-porters mid-stride on the street without her ever lifting an eyelash: full breasts shifting unconstrained save for a light inner linen band as she turned, long thighs outlined clean against thin linen by the red oven-draft behind her, amber eyes sharp and sleepless as a peregrine's.

Seeing Corvus standing six feet away looking straight at **Dorcas's *Domus Ceionia* clasp**, Dorcas snapped her tally-bag shut without a word, pulled her hood forward, and brushed briskly past Corvus out into the Fish-Market crowd with Berenice hobbling behind her.

Alone between the stacked Sicilian wheat-sacks and the silent afternoon lava-mills, Barba and the day-kneaders were napping in the shady stable-arch across the yard—Lucida did not flinch, explain, or step back. She folded both flour-dusted forearms lightly below her breasts and met Corvus's grey-green eyes head-on.

"Did Tullia Crispina run short of Puteoli biscuits, Lucius Cassius, or did you take a wrong turn coming down from the Carinae?" Lucida asked smoothly.

The sting landed clean; Corvus set the basket of Sabine peaches down on the clean oak weighing-table without taking the bait.

"Crustumerium peaches off a farmer's cart from my father's road," Corvus said evenly. "And a warning from Lollius Urbicus's tribunal at noon. Two rotten sixth-floor ceilings fell in the lower Subura last week, I just argued the *Lex Aquilia* damage suit on one of them—and Urbicus's clerks are preparing street-by-street building and *horrea* inspections before the August Dog-Days. Keep your upper grain-loft load spread evenly over the stone piers rather than the middle pine beams, or municipal surveyors will slap a temporary *opus novum* suspension on your courtyard, and one suspended day kills five hundred mornings on that slate."

For two breaths the hard Caelian armor softened half a millimeter around Lucida's mouth; whatever walls had gone up between them since spring, she knew a life-or-death operational warning when he brought one.

"Barba braced the middle pine beams with two nine-inch oak posts in June," she answered more quietly, picking up one fuzzy yellow Sabine peach to smell the sun-warmed skin—remembering stealing green peaches together out of Fidenae orchards at thirteen until their stomachs cramped, before setting it carefully back in the basket. "Why was Senator Priscus's clerk watching Dorcas's clasp as if she carried plague?"

Corvus stepped two paces closer into the warm flour-scented shadow so not even napping Barba across the yard could hear.

"Because Dorcas carries **Ceionia Fabia's** purse, Vespera," Corvus said low, dropping his voice to their childhood name for the first time since April, "and Ceionia Fabia spends twelve hours a day building a Praetorian and chariot-faction party around her brother Lucius while **Publius Vedius Pollio** pours silver into Lucius's stables! Half the lawsuits Priscus puts in my hands this year are aimed straight at tearing up the floorboards under Vedius and Fabia's crowd—if you're taking Caelian silver out of Fabia's bedroom, you're standing right on the fault-line when those two stones grind together!"

Lucida's amber eyes flashed bright molten gold in the gloom.

"Oh, *I'm* standing on a fault-line taking silver from Ceionia Fabia's bedroom?" she whispered fiercely, stepping one barefoot pace *forward* instead of back until barely ten inches of ninety-degree air separated her linen-clad chest from his white wool toga, smelling cleanly of warm Sicilian wheat, sweat, and bitter-almond soap. "When Scaeva walked into this courtyard with two cudgel-men on Day One of my three-year count to unhook my mules while you were asleep upstairs, Lucius Cassius, Senator Priscus's pure Stoic name wasn't here to stop him—**Ceionia Fabia's lead horse-seal stopped him cold on that stone threshold**! You take twenty-five-thousand-sesterce shipping shares and seaside suppers at Laurentum from Priscus's rich equestrian widows to climb toward your gold ring; don't walk into *my* mill in chalked wool and lecture me on whose silver keeps my throat uncut!"

Blood hammered hot into Corvus's temples, part fury, part a sudden physical ache so violent looking down six inches at her flushed throat and the dark olive-pip mark rising and falling just above her damp linen edge that both his fists clenched white inside his toga-folds to keep from catching her waist and crushing her mouth against the wheat-sacks right there.

*Tell her you know about Vedius's eighty sesterces every Kalends,* a voice inside his ribs shouted. *Tell her you've banked fifty-eight thousand sesterces already solely to buy an iron hammer heavy enough to smash her patron!*

And right behind it spoke crisp, poisonous eighteen-year-old pride: *Tell her while she has Fabia's gold in her pocket and just threw Crispina's Laurentum bed in your teeth, and she'll laugh in your face and call you Priscus's errand-boy!*

"Keep your beams braced for August, Lucida," Corvus said, his voice flat and cold again, stepping back out of reach of the heat radiating off her bare arms. "I didn't come to count your Caelian jars."

He turned and walked out through the Fish-Market arch into the red evening sun without looking back—missing how Lucida stood motionless beside the basket of Sabine peaches for twenty breaths after his shadow vanished, pressing two floury knuckles silently against her dry lower lip.

* * *

Four hours after sunset, up inside **Senator Quintus Servilius Priscus**'s shady, peperino-walled Caelian library where smoky wax consular masks stared through bronze lamp-glow, eighteen-year-old Corvus sat across a scarred oak campaign-table from **Priscus**, **Quintus Junius Rusticus**, and **Phileros**.

Both double cedar doors were bolted from the inside; not a single household slave had been left in the adjoining peristyle.

"Old Antoninus stumbled twice getting out of his bath at Lorium four mornings ago," Junius Rusticus said quietly without preamble, ink-stained thumb resting motionless on his knee. "Marcus Caesar had to catch his elbow before the chamberlains saw. Seventy-three in September; his mind is clear as spring water, yet his legs swell below the knee whenever damp wind blows off the Tyrrhenian marshes. Two years left, perhaps eighteen months, if winter treats him kindly."

Corvus sat utterly still, stylus poised over blank wax. Never before tonight had Priscus or Rusticus spoken the unspeakable sentence—**the death of Antoninus Pius**, aloud in front of an eighteen-year-old equestrian candidate.

"When that night comes," Senator Priscus picked up, cracking a dry fennel seed between his back teeth, "every greedy equestrian syndicate, racing-faction boss, and Syrian luxury-contractor who has lent gold to young **Lucius Commodus**—with **Publius Vedius Pollio** foremost among the pack, expects Lucius to demand equal tribunician and proconsular *imperium* alongside Marcus, throwing twenty years of clean Stoic administration open to the wolves! Marcus himself is too generous toward his adoptive brother to strike first; therefore Marcus's friends clean the stables *before* the old Emperor stops breathing."

Priscus pushed two heavy, red-sealed municipal survey-rolls across the oak table until they bumped against Corvus's scarred right thumb.

"Prefect Lollius Urbicus watched you argue that *Lex Aquilia* joist-collapse at noon today, Lucius Cassius," Priscus said, grey eyes glinting cold approval. "Starting on the Kalends of August, Urbicus empowers a special three-man technical sub-commission under my supervision to enforce two sleeping statutes without mercy across Regions IV, VI, and VIII: first, Emperor Trajan's sixty-foot maximum height cap on street-front *insulae*—giving us legal crowbars to condemn and raze the top two timber storeys off twelve overpriced Subura tenements Vedius Pollio milks through straw landlords! Second, an unannounced forensic audit of **every private hundred-modius *pistrinum* registered since last year** suspected of fronting undeclared syndicate capital or drawing *annona* privileges on padded donkey-counts!"

Priscus tapped the lower red seal with one bony finger.

"Do this cleanly through autumn and winter without letting a single target buy warning, boy, clearing sixty thousand more out of Crispina's autumn Alexandria fleet and forty thousand in fiscal forfeitures on Vedius's condemned leases—and by next year at nineteen you will hold half the four-hundred-thousand-sesterce census needed for Marcus Caesar himself to sponsor your equestrian gold ring when his day dawns. Open Region VIII and check the flagged bakehouses."

Inside the bolted library, bronze lamp-flames burned straight and motionless.

Corvus broke the red wax seal on the Region VIII *pistrina* schedule with steady fingers, unrolled the crisp white papyrus across his knees, and ran his grey-green eyes down Column One, Column Two, Column Three, all genuine front-companies controlled by Hermippus and Numerius Vettius—and stopped at Line Twelve of Column Four:

*REGIO VIII · FORUM PISCARIUM · PISTRINVM BARBAE ET LVCIDAE LAT · IVN · — DENUNTIATIO ANONYMA DE PECVNIA EXTERNA ET MODIIS SIMVLATIS.*

*Anonymous denunciation alleging concealed outside syndicate backing and padded daily modius tallies.*

Corvus didn't need two guesses to know which Smyrniot steward had slipped that "anonymous" denunciation into the Urban Prefect's box the week Vedius Pollio spotted Lucida inside the Green stables: **Hermippus** was using Senator Priscus's own anti-corruption broom to sweep Lucida's three-year citizenship count into the gutter at Day Five Hundred!

Across the oak table **Junius Rusticus** was watching Corvus's eyelashes with quiet, terrifying acuity.

"Do you know any of the Region VIII mill-lessees on Column Four, Lucius Cassius?" Rusticus asked softly.

Betray a flicker of recognition right now, and Priscus reassigned Region VIII to another clerk who would march ten *vigiles* into Habinnas Barba's courtyard at midnight on the Kalends of August, halt the four lava mills for three days while auditing her Caelian and burial-guild books, and **destroy Lucida's thousand-and-ninety-five-day citizenship run forever**!

Keep Region VIII inside *his own* hands, and Corvus controlled the inspection calendar, controlled the weighing-drums, and could steer the iron wrecking-hook straight into Vedius's straw-mills next door while shielding Barba's threshold, without stubborn, furious Lucida ever knowing whose hand turned the bolt!

Corvus looked smoothly up from Line Twelve into Junius Rusticus's searching dark eyes, not a pulse-beat showing in his throat.

"Numerius Vettius's three sweepings-mills sit on Column Two, Senator—the same Vettius who borrows from Hermippus," Corvus answered cleanly, rolling the papyrus shut and sliding it into his own leather dispatch-case. "Give me two sworn measurers who don't drink Hermippus's wine, and we'll weigh every suspect hopper in Region VIII before September ends."
