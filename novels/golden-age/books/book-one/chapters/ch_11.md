# Chapter XI — Rehearsal in Chalk

*Eighteenth Day Before the Kalends of July, AD 158 · Corvus*

"Nine Roman inches," Corvus whispered, pressing his folding brass *pes Monetalis* flat against the damp red brick-course at the back of the Vicus Sandaliarius cobbler's court while June dawn turned the narrow strip of sky overhead the color of skimmed milk. "Look at the mortar-joint right above your left ear, Vespera. Below your ear it's grey Hadrianic lime mixed with coarse black river-pozzolana; above your ear it's soft pink crushed-tile dust troweled smooth last winter to hide where Carvilius knocked through the party-wall and stole nine inches by forty feet of my client's oil-cellar!"

Seventeen-year-old Lucida leaned her shoulder lazily against the opposite fuller's lintel, chewing a warm sesame crust from her third-watch baking—Day One Hundred and Six on Sura's wall-slate back at the Fish-Market without a single morning under a hundred and six *modii*, and eyed the whitewashed plaster wall with professional disdain.

"Your client Sextus Pompeius is an idiot if somebody stole forty feet of his cellar and he didn't notice till May," she observed, flicking a sesame seed off her bare brown wrist. "Even a blind mole notices nine inches missing off a wine-vault."

"Pompeius lives in Tibur growing roses and only came to town when his ground-floor cobbler complained damp was rotting shoe-leather through the partition," Corvus said, running his scarred right thumb eagerly along the faint vertical hair-crack where new plaster met eighty-year-old *opus testaceum* brick. Three months ago on the *Liberalia* in March he had laid aside his boy's tunic and put on the white citizen *toga virilis*—stored neatly rolled right now inside an oiled cloth bag on Sura's shoulder so Subura mud wouldn't spot the fuller's chalk before the third hour, and his voice had settled over winter into a clean, resonant baritone that still jumped half a beat faster whenever Lucida worked an alley beside him. "Here's the catch: **Spurius Carvilius**—who fronts three shoemakers' blocks on this lane for Hermippus's syndicate, filed a sworn *stipulatio* yesterday claiming that cellar-strip was conveyed to him three years ago by *mancipatio* from Pompeius's dead uncle, and plastered solid from Carvilius's side for two winters so his bailiffs never even set foot in there! Unless we prove active possession *from Carvilius's courtyard* before the Second *Hasta* sits at the fourth hour, the praetor refuses our summary *interdictum uti possidetis* and locks Pompeius into a three-year title suit!"

Lucida's amber eyes gleamed bright in the grey alley-light. All winter and spring they had moved on parallel tracks—she buried knee-deep in Sicilian wheat, burial-guild bread-tokens, and Caelian salve-pots; he buried in Gaius, Fronto's Thursday declamations, and Priscus's Caelian briefs, each keeping quiet cupboards locked from the other. Out here in wet dawn brick-shadow before Rome woke up, hunting a crooked wall together with thirty breaths to spare, five years of shared ditch-craft clicked back into gear so effortlessly Corvus felt drunk on plain air.

"Wait," Lucida said, stepping two light paces across the cobbles to press her ear—not her eye, her *ear*, a baker's trick for checking hollow brick oven-domes, flat against the "solid" whitewashed plaster five feet left of Corvus's mortar-seam, then rapping once with her brass belt-buckle.

*Thock.* Solid two-foot brick pier.

She slid two paces right, directly underneath an innocent-looking wooden shrine of the *Lares Compitales* hung with dried garlic braids, and rapped again.

*Thunk.* Hollow pine board skimmed over with a quarter-inch coat of wet lime-stucco on split reed lath, cut round the edges with a fine knife and painted over to look like continuous wall!

"Solid brick for two winters, my ass," Lucida murmured delightedly, showing small white teeth in the grin nobody on the Caelian ever saw. "There's a three-foot wooden cellar-door right behind those garlic braids, Sabine—and look down at the basalt flagstone two inches under the sill: dried olive-lees and fresh hobnail scratches curving toward Carvilius's back wine-shop! His bailiff **Geta** rolls untaxed Campanian wine-skins through that 'plastered' cellar every morning before the aedile's market-clerks walk the Sandaliarius!"

Corvus stared at the hairline cut hidden behind the hanging garlic, calculating court procedure at lightning speed. "If I bring two witnesses into Carvilius's private passage to pry that plaster-door open with an iron bar, Sulpicius Rufus screams *vis*, unlawful force breaking a neighbor's shrine—and gets my evidence thrown out! How do we make Geta walk *out* through his own 'nonexistent' wall in front of sworn citizens before the third hour?"

Lucida turned her head and whistled two soft thrush-notes up the alley: *Whit-whit.*

Fourteen-year-old **Sura** trotted silently out from the corner arch carrying Corvus's toga-bag on one arm and two cracked terracotta bail-pots of spoiled crimson **Rubia madder-root dye-sludge**, bought for two copper *asses* off a neighboring wool-dyer's scrap-heap—in the other.

"Take your two citizen witnesses across the street into that pastry-cook's doorway where they have a straight sightline down this passage," Lucida told Corvus briskly, already uncorking both clay pots of thick, blood-crimson madder paste, stuff so mordant with alum that one splash dyed white leather or skin indelible scarlet for three weeks. "Wait till the second-hour mule-carts rattle down from the Argiletum. When Geta hears glass smash and smells smoke outside his untaxed wine-stash, let's see whether he walks round the front street or kicks open his 'two-year solid brick wall' in white shoes!"

Ten breaths after Corvus had stationed two respectable equestrian shoemaker-guild wardens inside the opposite pastry-booth with hot honey-fritters in their hands, Lucida and Sura went to work with shameless, breathtaking precision.

First Lucida poured both gallons of thick crimson alum-madder sludge smoothly across the sunken basalt threshold directly outside the hidden reed-and-plaster door, dusting a thin veil of grey street-ash over the top so in dim alley shadow it looked like ordinary dry cobble-dirt. Next Sura lit a fistful of damp, oily fuller's tow inside a broken roof-tile right below Carvilius's upper storeroom grating so acrid black smoke billowed straight through the cellar air-vent, while Lucida hurled an empty four-as green wine-flagon *crash* against the brick pier and yelled at the top of her lungs in shrill Greek street-panic:

*"Fire in the oil-vault! Fetch the vigiles' hooks before the wine-skins burst!"*

Both girls vanished like smoke round the cobbler's water-butt before the last green glass shard stopped spinning.

Five heartbeats. Eight heartbeats.

From behind the hanging garlic braids of the sacred *Lares* shrine came the sharp *clack* of an interior iron bolt shooting back—and **out burst Carvilius's bailiff Geta** straight through the middle of the "solid two-year masonry wall," swinging a brass water-bucket in both hands and wearing freshly pipe-clayed white leather *campagi* boots!

Both boots landed ankle-deep *splat* in two inches of alum-mordanted crimson madder-paste.

Geta skidded, cursed by Priapus's yard, stared wildly down at his ruined scarlet boots and no fire, only a smoking handful of damp rag on a tile—and looked straight across the twelve-foot lane into the polite morning smiles of two sworn equestrian guild-wardens and seventeen-year-old Lucius Cassius Corvus sketching the open hidden doorway and Geta's bright red footprints onto a three-leaved boxwood *testatio*!

"Good morning, Geta," Corvus called across the cobbles as Geta slammed the plaster-faced door shut in horror, leaving a three-foot rectangular black gap framed in broken stucco and four bright scarlet footprints leading straight inside! "Wear those boots to the Basilica Julia at the fourth hour; the Centumviral judges love bright colors!"

Back round the corner by the fuller's arch, where Lucida leaned doubled over with silent laughter pressing both fists against her ribs while Sura beamed from ear to ear, Corvus caught Lucida by both elbows and lifted her clean off her flour-dusted boots for half a turn before setting her down—and then stopped smiling as he glanced down at the wax copy of Carvilius's *mancipatio* deed tucked in his belt.

"Wait a second," Corvus muttered, cold grey-green focus snapping back behind his eyes as he read the five citizen witnesses listed along the bottom edge of Carvilius's alleged purchase-tablet from three years ago: *M. Lollius, C. Vibius, L. Petronius, **D. Atilius**, Q. Fabius*. "Vespera, doesn't that old drunk actor who sings bawdy Atellan farces in a goat-wig outside the Sandaliarius wine-shop call himself **Decimus Atilius**?"

Lucida wiped tears of mirth off her lashes and squinted at the fourth name on the wax. "Decimus Atilius 'Long-Nose'—used to dance *mimus* naked at the *Floralia* under Hadrian before rheumatism stiffened his hips. Drinks free sour wine at Carvilius's counter every noon for signing his name on shop-leases. Why?"

Corvus stared down at those fourteen letters, *DECIMVS ATILIVS*—feeling a sudden lightning jolt run straight from his scarred right thumb up into the roots of his hair, because he wasn't just looking at Spurius Carvilius's cobbler-cellar anymore; **he was looking at the exact statutory lock-pick that could one day shatter Publius Vedius Pollio's entire six-million-sesterce brick-and-tenement empire if a single witness on Vedius's master-deeds carried the same taint**!

"Because under the Praetor's Edict *De his qui notantur infamia* and the Twelve Tables," Corvus breathed slowly, "any man who has appeared on the public stage for hire, *qui artis ludicrae pronuntiandive causa in scaenam prodierit*—is permanently **infamis and intestabilis**! He can buy bread in the market, certainly, he **cannot stand as one of the five statutory Roman citizen witnesses to a formal conveyance *per aes et libram***! Put four honest citizens and one *infamis* stage-player round the bronze scales, and the entire *mancipatio* is dead wood from the hour the bronze struck the balance!"

* * *

At the fifth hour inside the **Basilica Julia**, heat rolled beneath the marble arcades thick enough to wilt starch out of chalked wool.

For the first morning of his life Corvus sat not behind the third clerks' bench with twenty pounds of wood on his collarbone, on the low pine *subsellium* directly behind **Senator Priscus** and **Aulus Volusenus Niger**, wearing his own snow-white citizen *toga virilis* draped in crisp, severe Republican folds Phileros had pinned at dawn.

Out before the upright iron-shod spear of the Second *Hasta*, twenty-year-old Volusenus was already drowning in four feet of still water.

Priscus had handed his nephew an open-and-shut morning victory—two sworn guild-wardens testifying to Geta's scarlet-booted exit through the false plaster wall, destroying Carvilius's two-year undisturbed possession plea in ten breaths, and all Volusenus had needed to do was sit down and collect the interdict. Instead, greedy to show off Asianic periods before three senatorial cousins watching from the colonnade, Volusenus had launched into a half-clepsydra peroration attacking Carvilius's *mancipatio* title itself—walking headfirst into **Gaius Sulpicius Rufus**'s waiting net.

Sulpicius Rufus didn't even stand up completely; leaning one elegant hip against the defense rail, Vedius's sleek Bithynian advocate interrupted gently:

"Before my eloquent young patrician friend bursts a vein over plaster-dust, worthy judges, perhaps he will answer one elementary question out of Sabinus. Even assuming a bailiff cut a temporary service-hatch last week, my client holds a sealed *mancipatio* conveyance witnessed by five enrolled citizens of the Suburana and Palatina tribes from three harvests back, expressly reserving *servitus oneris ferendi*, the servitude of wall-and-vault support! Does young Aulus Volusenus contend before forty-five Centumviral judges that an urban wine-seller loses Quiritary ownership won *per aes et libram* because his servant hung garlic over a door?"

Up in the gallery twenty hired *laudiceni* tittered on cue.

Volusenus opened his mouth, blinked sweat out of his eyes, and looked frantically down at his prepared rhetoric-notes—where nothing whatever stood written about *servitus oneris ferendi* against prior *mancipatio*, because Volusenus hadn't bothered checking the five witness names Corvus handed Phileros an hour before court!

"The, the servitude falls by *non usus* when maliciously concealed behind stucco, judges," Volusenus blustered, getting the law flat backward—urban praedial servitudes required *usucapio libertatis* across two full years, and Sulpicius Rufus smiled like a cat watching a plump thrush hop between its paws!

"Concealed for a week extinguishes a two-year urban servitude?" Sulpicius murmured to the grinning bench. "Perhaps in nursery rhetoric-schools on the Quirinal, "

On the front bench **Senator Quintus Servilius Priscus** stopped chewing his fennel seed.

He did not rescue his stammering sister's son himself; instead Priscus leaned three inches sideways toward seventeen-year-old Corvus—who had already passed the *toga virilis* threshold into full legal *pubertas*, legally competent to speak on the facts when designated by principal and *patronus*, and spoke eight quiet words out of the side of his iron mouth:

"Stand up and kill the deed, Lucius Cassius."

Volusenus heard; his oiled head jerked round in disbelief as the seventeen-year-old surveyor's orphan rose smoothly off the back pine bench in plain, unperfumed white wool and stepped to the oak *cancelli* rail beside him.

Across forty feet of marble **forty-five Centumviral judges**, three rows of spectators, and sleek **Gaius Sulpicius Rufus** stared at the tall, broad-shouldered boy with the pale stylus-scar at the left corner of his mouth. Up in the gallery Sulpicius's fat Greek claque-leader frowned, unsure whether to signal jeers at the boy's youth.

Inside Corvus's ribs, the cold iron bolt slid home. Not one drop of sweat moistened his palms; he heard Cato's dry chisel-strokes in Fronto's conservatory and saw Gaius Mallius's nailed wrists jerking in Esquiline sleet.

"With the President's leave, on a single point of fact touching the wax tablet Counsel just waved at the spear," Corvus said.

He did not shout, chant, or saw the air with ringed fingers like Volusenus; drilled by Gnipho's freedman and sharpened by Fronto, his deep chest-voice cut clean underneath the drone of the three neighboring courts so every judge on the wooden tiers heard every consonant without straining.

"Let him speak," the presiding *decemvir* nodded, curious after May's Salarian surprise.

Corvus picked up the certified municipal stage-payment extract Phileros had pulled from the *aediles plebis*' games-register an hour ago and held it flat—arm still, scarred right thumb steady on the edge.

"Learned Sulpicius asks whether garlic hung over a hidden door voids a *mancipatio* witnessed by five Roman citizens," Corvus said cleanly. "Nobody claims garlic voids bronze and scales. The Twelve Tables and the Praetor's Perpetual Edict void it. Fourth down the spine of Spurius Carvilius's alleged conveyance stands the seal and signature of **Decimus Atilius, son of Marcus, Suburana tribe**. Here is the certified games-account of the Plebeian Aediles from the third consulship of Hadrian showing four hundred sesterces paid out of public funds to that same **Decimus Atilius, *archimimus***, for dancing the adulterer in *Laureolus* for hire upon the public stage, confirmed half an hour ago by Atilius himself over a jug of wine on the Vicus Sandaliarius, where he offered to dance the hornpipe again for two denarii!"

A sharp ripple—authentic laughter this time, no paid clappers needed, ran straight across all forty-five Centumviral benches!

Corvus did not smile into the laugh; he drove the iron chisel home while the stone cracked.

"Under Title Two of Julian's Edict, any man who treads the public boards for silver bears lifelong *infamia*; under the Law of the Twelve Tables, an *infamis* stage-player is *intestabilis*—incompetent either to summon witnesses or to stand beside the bronze balance himself. Take one rotten timber out of five, judges, and Spurius Carvilius has no *mancipatio*, no *servitus*, and no title save nine stolen inches of wet stucco and two scarlet boots! My client Sextus Pompeius prays immediate restitution of his vault and summary ejection of every unlawful occupant holding under Carvilius's void paper."

Sulpicius Rufus didn't even open his mouth to reply; watching his own instructing clerk go green behind him told the whole court the hit was mortal. Within four hundred breaths thirty-nine *A* tablets rattled out of the urn, granting Pompeius immediate *interdictum* possession, triple damages for cellar-spoliation, and court-viatores marched straight out toward the Vicus Sandaliarius with iron crowbars before sunset!

Outside beneath the blazing colonnade, joyful equestrian **Sextus Pompeius**, having just recovered sixty thousand sesterces of urban frontage plus twelve thousand in damages—pressed a heavy sewn chamois bag straight into Corvus's hands with Senator Priscus's approving nod: **five thousand sesterces** (50 gold aurei!), legal *honorarium* below the ten-thousand-sesterce *Lex Cincia* ceiling, earned in a hundred clean breaths of speech before his eighteenth birthday!

Beside the third marble column **Volusenus Niger** didn't curse Corvus today; pale beneath his tan, ignored completely by Pompeius and his own uncle alike, Volusenus walked down the Basilica steps alone without looking back, which Phileros, tucking away his bone scrapers at Corvus's elbow, watched with narrow eyes.

"A man who barks at you is safe, Sabine," Phileros murmured dryly. "Watch your ribs when a patrician goes quiet."

* * *

At dusk Corvus walked west toward the **Pons Aemilius** feeling ten feet tall inside his crisp white *toga virilis*, fifty gold *aurei* weighing deliciously solid inside his leather belt-wallet—adding to the nineteen hundred sesterces already saved from his Caelian pay to put him past **seven thousand sesterces** personal capital at seventeen!, and carrying a snow-cooled jar of amber Chian wine plus honeyed figs to celebrate with Lucida down by the river breeze.

Halfway down the travertine river-steps beside the bridge-head, where evening market-crowds thinned above the swirling brown Tiber, a knot of poorer neighborhood folk had gathered round a piled heap of pathetic household sticks dumped on the cobbles: a cracked three-legged pine cobbler's bench, a bundle of iron shoe-lasts, a straw mattress dark with cellar-damp, and two clay cooking-pots.

Sitting on the damp mattress weeping silently into her knotted grey hair was a crippled sixty-year-old woman with twisted arthritic knees, while her white-bearded husband—wearing a leather shoemaker's apron and the faded wool cap of a freeborn plebeian citizen, argued brokenly with a bored municipal *viator* holding a sealed court eviction-rod.

Kneeling right down in the bridge-dust beside the old woman—her baker's hair-kerchief still tied over her dark braids, an empty wholesale bread-pannier resting at her hip, was **Lucida**, tearing a clean linen strip off her own apron-hem to bind a deep bleeding scrape on the old woman's elbow while Sura tucked four unsold two-pound Sicilian loaves quietly beside their cooking-pots!

"Vespera?" Corvus called warmly, stepping down the travertine flags carefully so his chalked white *toga* hem wouldn't drag in the mule-dung, holding up the Chian wine-jar with a grin. "Look what Pompeius put in my hand after the Second *Hasta*—fifty gold pieces down! Wait, what happened here?"

The municipal *viator* glanced round, recognized Senator Priscus's young *cognitor* from the Vicus Sandaliarius seizure an hour ago, and saluted cheerfully with his ash rod!

"Cleared all four ground-floor bays of Spurius Carvilius's Sandaliarius block clean onto the street before sunset just as your writ ordered, Master Cassius! Plucked Carvilius's wine-bailiff out by the ears—and pitched these sub-tenants' cobbler-benches out too when they couldn't show Pompeius's seal!"

The white-bearded old cobbler looked up at seventeen-year-old Corvus standing gleaming white above him in fresh fuller's chalk, holding expensive Chian wine.

"I paid Spurius Carvilius **two hundred forty sesterces**, six months' honest shop-rent in advance on the Kalends of May out of thirty years' shoe-mending, young sir!" old **Gaius Trebatius** choked out, holding up a worthless wax receipt stamped with Carvilius's voided seal. "I never touched nobody's cellar wall! My wife can't climb third-floor stairs on those knees—and your bailiffs split her arm throwing our bed-frame over the threshold and told me to go sue Carvilius in the gutter!"

Corvus's grin faded half an inch; still, the clean, incontrovertible geometry of Roman civil law clicked automatically into place inside his head.

"Carvilius held *ab initio* under a void *mancipatio*, citizen," Corvus explained, not unkindly, in the exact measured tone Julian's *Digest* used for third-party sub-lessees. "A man who owns no title cannot grant valid *locatio conductio* against the true Quiritary owner. Bring your wax receipt round to Senator Priscus's back vestibule on Tuesday morning and I'll have a junior clerk draft you a personal *actio conducti* claim against Carvilius gratis."

Old Trebatius stared at him as if Corvus had spoken Persian. "Sue Hermippus's straw-man on Tuesday while my wife sleeps on river-stones tonight with not two asses left for porridge?"

Down in the dust **Lucida** finished knotting the clean linen strip round the old woman's bleeding elbow.

Slowly she stood up, brushing grey bridge-grit off her bare knees, and looked straight at Corvus.

She did not shout; she did not make a scene in front of the court *viator*. Instead her dark amber eyes traveled slowly down from Corvus's flushed, triumphant face across the snowy, unspotted chalked folds of his brand-new adult citizen *toga*, down to the heavy bulge of fifty gold *aurei* at his waist, and back up to his scarred mouth.

When she spoke, she didn't use Greek or their private childhood name; she spoke crisp, flat Subura Latin.

"Reach into that heavy new belt-pouch of yours and give Gaius Trebatius six gold pieces to rent a dry ground-floor arch across the Velabrum tonight, Lucius Cassius," Lucida said quietly. "Before you tell me how brilliantly you spoke."

Corvus stiffened inside his white wool, pride stung in front of the watching court-bailiff even as shame pricked hot behind his ribs. Without a word—once the *viator* turned up the steps, Corvus unfastened his chamois bag, counted **three gold *aurei*** (300 HS—sixty sesterces more than the old cobbler's lost rent!) straight into astonished old Trebatius's leather apron, and turned to walk Lucida and Sura across the bridge in fading orange twilight.

Halfway over the stone arch above the brown water, Corvus held out the snow-cooled jar of Chian wine between them as a peace-offering.

"Three hundred sesterces put Trebatius in a better shop than he lost, Vespera, and we still cleared four thousand seven hundred," Corvus said low, trying to recapture the fierce dawn joy of the Sandaliarius alley twelve hours ago. "Plus I proved in open court that one *infamis* witness kills a *mancipatio* chain stone-dead, the exact blade that's going to cut Vedius Pollio's throat open one day!"

Lucida took a small sip from the Chian jar when he uncorked it, wiped her mouth with the back of her flour-scarred wrist, and handed the wine back to him without smiling.

"How much did the fuller charge to chalk that new toga so white, Corvus?" she asked softly, looking down over the travertine parapet at two boys fishing driftwood out of the eddy below.

"Four sesterces," Corvus answered, puzzled. "Why?"

"Because three years ago when Hermippus's bailiffs threw your father's tools onto the Crustumerium road under a strict legal bond," Lucida said—her voice gentle as twilight water, cutting deeper than an iron chisel, "nobody gave you and me three gold pieces on a bridge, and you swore to me in the rain you were learning their law to break their sticks, not to speak Julian's Latin over an old woman's mattress."

She walked ahead up the Fish-Market steps beside silent Sura to check the evening yeast-sponges, leaving Corvus standing still for ten breaths on the bridge with forty-seven gold *aurei* in his belt, cold Chian wine in his hand, and a tiny, invisible hair-crack running right across the happiest day of his seventeen years.
