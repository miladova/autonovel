# Chapter XXII — Sleeping Almond

*Sixteenth Day Before the Kalends of September, AD 160 · Lucida*

Three days after the fire, wet grey ash still lay crusted two inches thick in the basalt wheel-ruts of the **Clivus Suburanus**, smelling sourly under the blazing sixth-hour sun of boiled Campanian vinegar, charred fuller's wool, and roasted rats.

Nineteen-year-old Lucida stood in the narrow strip of blue shade beneath the coppersmith's stone awning across the street from **Lot Fourteen**, wearing an immaculate mourning-dark brown linen *tunica* without ornament—her singed left eyebrow penciled smooth with burnt antimony, blistered palms salved in beeswax and wrapped in narrow white linen strips, watching the municipal *praeco* plant the iron-shod auction spear upright in a bucket of sand before **her own blackened red brick arches**.

At dawn this morning back behind the Fish-Market, sixteen-year-old **Sura** had chalked **ANNVS III · DIES CLXX** across the smoky slate—**Day Nine Hundred overall**, leaving **one hundred ninety-five mornings** between Lucida's throat and Quiritary Roman citizenship, while over behind Vespasian's Temple of Peace, eighteen-year-old Marsian kneader **Titus** lay groaning through fever inside Asclepiades's surgery with iron splints pinning his shattered shin together, paid fifty silver denarii down out of Lucida's purse plus four sesterces a day life-pension sworn before Barba so the crippled boy never begged bread.

Out round the municipal auctioneer's folding table in the glare gathered the carrion-birds who always smelled fresh urban ash before the bricks grew cold.

Closest to the spear stood **Gaius Varius**, a sleek, perfumed freedman land-jobber who bought burned Subura frontages at three pennies on the silver piece for **Hermippus** and **Publius Vedius Pollio**, fanning flies off his chin with a green ivory tablet.

Six paces left of Varius stood amiable, red-cheeked equestrian **Sextus Pompeius** from Tibur—the exact rose-growing client for whom sixteen-year-old Corvus had won his first five-thousand-sesterce courtroom speech two summers ago! Pompeius kept glancing uneasily thirty yards uphill into the shadowed colonnade of a wine-warehouse, where a tall, dark-haired nineteen-year-old in a white citizen toga stood motionless behind a travertine pillar with his bandaged right hand tucked inside his *sinus* fold.

**Corvus.**

Even after she had told him in the wet ash at sunrise not to bring one equestrian bidder near her bricks, Corvus had drawn thirty-five thousand sesterces out of his own Castor strongbox and sent amiable Sextus Pompeius down into the heat to outbid Hermippus's shark and buy Lot Fourteen's sequestered rebuilding lease back for her, believing, with that maddening, unbreakable Sabine arrogance of his, that rescuing a woman against her orders made the chain sweet!

Let Sextus Pompeius win that spear today with Corvus's silver, and **every single brick above her two-thousand-*modius* grain-vaults stood registered in the name of Senator Priscus's client**—attachable tomorrow noon if Priscus ever turned on Ceionia Fabia's purveyor! Let Gaius Varius win it, and **Vedius Pollio** walled her wheat-bins shut before sunset!

Lucida glanced once sideways down her own shady awning. Standing calmly beside old **President Gaius Salvius** of the burial guild (silver skull-plate glinting beneath his straw sun-hat) waited sour-faced fifty-year-old **Dorcas**, wearing **Ceionia Fabia's** dark-green Caelian stewardess *stola* and carrying a sealed bronze warranty-box at her belt.

Out in the sun the municipal *praeco* wiped sweat off his bald pate and bawled the terms across the noisy street:

"By summary decree of Tribune Lucius Sempronius under the Trajanic Rebuilding Edict! Perpetual municipal surface-and-vault *locatio* of **Lot Fourteen on the Clivus Suburanus**, forty feet street-front by ninety deep, forfeited for unrecorded straw-surety! Two subterranean tufa vaults intact; three lower *opus testaceum* brick storeys standing; eastern fourth-storey gable collapsed by hook-clearance! Upset reserve **six thousand sesterces**, who lifts a finger for good Subura stone?"

"Ten thousand!" Hermippus's front-man **Gaius Varius** called lazily, barely lifting one ringed forefinger off his fan.

"Fourteen thousand!" **Sextus Pompeius** barked at once, glancing uphill toward Corvus's pillar for approval.

"Eighteen thousand," Varius purred without looking round.

"Twenty-four thousand!" Pompeius countered stoutly, ready to climb straight to Corvus's thirty-five-thousand ceiling while half the street craned necks at two rich buyers fighting over half-wrecked brick!

Up behind the warehouse pillar thirty yards away, Corvus stood watching Varius prepare to bid twenty-eight—calculating coldly that Hermippus wouldn't authorize past thirty thousand on a fire-damaged lot once Nymphidius sat under arrest.

Lucida nodded half an inch to grey-haired **President Gaius Salvius**.

Old Salvius stepped heavily out of the coppersmith's shade right up to the auctioneer's clerk-table, planted his scarred legionary armorer's knuckles flat across the municipal scroll, and dropped a **heavy triple-sealed bronze-witnessed *tabula*** *clank* beside the sand-bucket!

"Before these two fine gentlemen bid thirty thousand sesterces on hot air, Master Herald," Salvius boomed in rough camp Latin loud enough to stop passing mule-carts, "read out the statutory **prior *pignus tacitum* builder's and materialman's hypothec** registered at the Urban Praetor's table yesterday noon under Emperor Marcus, under **Divus Hadrianus's and Caesar Antoninus's Senate Decree on Rebuilt Ruins**!"

Both Gaius Varius and Sextus Pompeius froze with fingers half-raised in the glare; thirty yards uphill in the colonnade shadow, **Corvus stepped abruptly six inches clear of his travertine pillar**, grey-green eyes narrowing sharp as hawks' down the street!

The municipal clerk broke the seal on Salvius's tablet, squinted rapidly down the notarized masonry accounts, and whistled under his breath before handing it up to the sweating herald.

"Notice to all bidders!" the herald read aloud, squinting against the white sun. "Duly recorded under the *Senatus Consultum* granting privileged statutory first-lien (*privilegium exigendi* and *tacita hypotheca*) to any guild or lender advancing stone, lime, brick, and masons' wages for the erection or repair of an urban edifice! Sworn unpaid masonry, pozzolana, and timber-shoring balance owed upon Lot Fourteen to the master-masons of the **Collegium Aesculapii et Hygiae**: **twenty-two thousand four hundred sesterces**, bearing statutory twelve-percent reconstruction interest—payable **in hard coined silver across this barrel-head by any winning outside purchaser *before* the Prefecture delivers keys**, over and above the hammer-price!"

Dead silence hit the sunny street, followed by three neighbor coppersmiths grinning into their beards!

Lucida hadn't forged a single copper *as* of false debt: over the past ten months she had deliberately routed all **twenty-two thousand four hundred sesterces** of Lot Fourteen's brick, lime, and mason contracts through a formal interest-bearing construction advance on Salvius's guild books (collateralized privately by her own cash sitting safe in a separate guild chest!) **precisely so that if anyone ever stripped old Ulpius Felix's straw title away, twenty-two thousand four hundred sesterces of ironclad first-priority builder's lien sat bolted straight onto the bricks**!

Pay twenty-four thousand hammer-price *plus* twenty-two thousand four hundred cash lien on top—**forty-six thousand four hundred sesterces** for a fire-cracked Subura lot worth thirty thousand whole!

Hermippus's front-man **Gaius Varius** snapped his ivory fan shut with an ugly curse, glaring straight across the gutter at nineteen-year-old Lucida in the coppersmith's shade as he realized who had salted the well. "I withdraw my eighteen thousand on undisclosed encumbrance!"

Beside the sand-bucket poor **Sextus Pompeius** wiped streaming sweat off his red cheeks, looking helplessly uphill toward Corvus, thirty-five thousand total in his wallet couldn't touch forty-six thousand four hundred!—and shook his head apologetically: "On newly noticed twenty-two-thousand statutory *pignus*, Master Herald, my twenty-four thousand stands withdrawn under *error in substantia*!"

"Both prior bids voided on newly read lien, re-opening Lot Fourteen *cum onere pignoris* (subject to the twenty-two-thousand-four-hundred guild masonry hypothec) at the upset reserve of **six thousand sesterces**!" the herald shouted, lifting his wax-headed hammer in ninety-five-degree heat with noon soup calling his belly. "Unless the lien-creditor waives immediate cash call, any bidder brings twenty-eight thousand four hundred down on the nail!"

Out of the coppersmith's shade stepped **Dorcas** in her crisp dark-green *stola*, holding up a written waiver from Salvius's burial guild **deferring cash call for five years solely in favor of Dorcas, Roman citizen freedwoman of Ceionia Fabia**, while laying **sixty gold *aurei*** (6,000 HS exact upset reserve!) ringing flat onto the pine board!

"Six thousand sesterces in the name of **Claudia Dorcas, citizen**, with the guild's five-year rollover attached," Dorcas announced in clipped Caelian Latin—holding secret simultaneous five-year irrevocable operating sub-lease (*locatio perpetua*) to **Lucida** already signed in her purse!

*Rap!* Down came the herald's hammer on the sand-bucket rim before Gaius Varius could even spit into the ash.

Lot Fourteen, all forty street-front feet, three standing brick storeys, and both bone-dry underground tufa vaults still holding four hundred *modii* of untouched Sicilian wheat!—was **hers again for six thousand sesterces song-money**, bought clean out of municipal forfeiture without one coin of Corvus's thirty-five thousand touching her mortar!

Across thirty yards of shimmering August heat and white ash-dust, nineteen-year-old **Lucida** turned her head and looked straight up the street into **Corvus**'s eyes where he stood alone beside the warehouse pillar.

Behind him confused Sextus Pompeius was already trotting uphill carrying Corvus's unspent thirty-five-thousand-sesterce bag back in both hands. Corvus didn't look at Pompeius; his pale, soot-shadowed face stared straight down the Clivus at **Dorcas's dark-green Caelian *stola*** standing beside Lucida at the auction table, watching Lucida drive herself three inches deeper into **Ceionia Fabia**'s pocket purely rather than take one clean silver denarius from his hand.

Neither of them nodded; neither raised a hand. Lucida turned smoothly into the scorched brick archway of Lot Fourteen beside Salvius's masons to order fresh roof-shoring before the September rains, shutting the street behind her back.

* * *

She did not celebrate that night.

At the first watch an unpleasant, expected consequence of Gaius Varius's humiliation arrived at the Fish-Market bakehouse gate: a perfumed Cappadocian runner carrying **Hermippus**'s lead patronal seal, summoning Junian Latin **Lucida** under quarterly *obsequium* obligation to attend **Publius Vedius Pollio** in person at the fourth hour of the night up at his breezy **Oppian Hill summer gardens**—ostensibly to deliver and apply snow-chilled bitter-almond and white-poppy lotion (*diacodion*) to blisters Vedius had taken sunning himself six hours on **Lucius Ceionius Commodus**'s pleasure-barge off Ostia!

Old **Habinnas Barba**, propped upright on two flour-sacks beside Oven One chewing hyssop-root while his single eye blazed fury over grey sunken cheeks—caught Lucida's cloak with chalky, trembling fingers.

"Don't climb that fat viper's hill past midnight, little Tyrian!" Barba rasped hoarsely. "After Varius ate dirt at noon and Nymphidius went into irons? Send Sura's cousin or pay eighty sesterces fine, he means to lock you inside his cellar!"

"Miss a direct *obsequium* summons without bodily sickness, Barba, and tomorrow morning Hermippus files a summary *accusatio ingrati liberti*—ungrateful freedwoman's daughter, before the Urban *Vicarius*, who isn't stern old Lollius Urbicus anymore; it's **Publius Cornelius**, who eats Vedius's turbot twice a week!" Lucida answered calmly, strapping **no steel blade** inside her boot tonight because Scaeva's gate-porters searched ankles to the skin, instead coiling her thick blue-black braids high on her crown and sliding **one heavy, six-inch solid-gold needle-pointed hairpin**—Fabia's Saturnalia gift, sharp enough to pierce bull-hide, clean through the center knot where every respectable woman wore hair-ornaments untouched by male gate-guards!

"Keep two Marsians sitting awake at the bottom of the Oppian steps with horn lanterns until the fourth watch," Lucida told Barba, pressing his dry hot wrist reassuringly once before pulling up her hood. "Vedius Pollio doesn't murder geese in his own summer bedchamber while Ceionia Fabia expects her hair pinned on Friday; he wants to see my hands shake. Keep the hoppers full on **Day Nine Hundred One**."

Up on the high southern terrace of the **Horti Vedii**, ninety-degree Dog-Star suffocation gave way to soft night breezes whispering through imported Syrian pomegranates and trickling snow-chilled fountains—fed now through legal twenty-digit *calices*, though still lush while Subura families two hundred yards downhill slept six abreast on burned pavement!

At the garden gate **one-eared Gaius Ummidius Scaeva** patted her cloak-hips, wrists, and boot-tops himself with rough, practiced contempt, ignoring the single gold hairpin gleaming openly in her coiled black braids—and jerked his scarred chin toward an open-sided white marble summer sleeping-pavilion (*cubiculum aestivum*) glowing softly behind translucent Egyptian linen mosquito-curtains at the crest of the lawn, while Scaeva himself resumed pacing his twenty-yard sentry beat down along the lower cypress walk among deafening August cicadas.

Inside the linen curtains, four silver lamps burned scented rose-oil low around a wide tortoiseshell couch spread with unwarmed, snow-ironed Pelusian flax sheets.

Stretched flat on his belly across the white linen wearing only a loose purple silk loin-cloth lay fifty-one-year-old **Publius Vedius Pollio**, his shaved ivory skull resting sideways on folded plump forearms, his broad, hairless back and shoulders burned bright lobster-scarlet from Ostian sea-glare. On the low citrus-wood table beside his nose stood a half-empty crystal flagon of heavy thirty-year Setine wine mixed cloudy white with **Theban poppy-head syrup** (*meconium*) prescribed by his physician to dull sun-scald so he could sleep, next to an onyx dish holding his emerald signet ring and **a tiny bronze strongbox tag stamped *AEDE · CASTORIS · ARCA · XLII***!

*Temple of Castor, Vault Forty-Two.*

"Come out of the doorway so the gnats don't follow you under the net, little builder," Vedius murmured without lifting his head, his light flute-player's voice slightly thickened at the edges by Setine wine and poppy-milk, though his dark velvet eyes watching her approach across the marble were wide awake and bright with malice. "Uncork your snow-pail and put plenty of white poppy-milk over the shoulder-blades; Lord Lucius insisted on racing twenty-oared galleys round the lighthouse without awnings till the seventh hour, and skins bred for shade blister cruelly."

Lucida knelt noiselessly on a rush mat beside the low couch, opened her double-walled cork snow-pail, scooped two smooth fingers of ice-cold whipped bitter-almond cream and poppy-mucilage onto her palms, and laid both chill hands lightly across the burning scarlet skin below Vedius's neck.

Vedius gave a long, theatrical shiver of pleasure, *"Ahhhh, snow off Soracte!"*—letting his eyelids droop halfway down as Lucida worked steady, soothing circular strokes across his sun-cooked shoulders without pressing hard enough to break a single blister.

"Gaius Varius came home weeping into his beard at the eighth hour," Vedius remarked conversationally into the folded crook of his own elbow while her cold fingers moved across his shoulder-blades. "Twenty-two thousand four hundred sesterces of burial-guild mason's lien tucked inside Claudia Dorcas's green skirt! Very neat, Lucida, truly admirable Puteoli footwork! Even Senator Priscus's sour young Sabine crow stood up by the wine-pillars looking as though somebody had stolen his turnip."

Lucida kept her fingers moving smooth and rhythmic as oiled clockwork—smooth and rhythmic as *well-greased mill-gears*, saying nothing.

Vedius turned his shaved ivory cheek an inch further on his forearm so one moist brown eye looked straight up into her face from twelve inches away.

"You really believe," Vedius murmured softly—almost tenderly, the exact tone in which he had mourned his twelve-thousand-sesterce green parakeet three springs ago, "that one hundred and ninety-four mornings from tonight you are going to walk into the Prefecture of the *Annona* with flour on your eyelashes, collect Emperor Trajan's citizenship bronze, and keep both Fish-Market mills and Lot Fourteen out of my strongroom?"

"I count only today's flour and today's salve, Dominus," Lucida answered quietly, scooping fresh snow-almond onto his lower ribs.

Vedius chuckled—a soft, sleepy, wet purr deep in his fat throat as the heavy poppy-laced Setine wine began pulling his eyelids lower and lower under the rhythmic chill of her hands.

"Count away, little mouse," Vedius slurred gently. "Your Caelian lady Fabia protects nobody until old Antoninus dies... and the week Antoninus dies, **Cornelius Repentinus** holds ten thousand Praetorian swords for my table while old one-eyed **Berenice** down on the Argiletum answers a sealed capital *delatio* already sitting ready in **Castor Vault Forty-Two**... naming which pretty Syrian girl carried green hellebore-and-savin bottles up the Caelian steps four Saturnalias ago! Under Sulla's poison law... Junian Latins don't collect baker's citizenship... they go underground, and every brick Ulpius Felix or Dorcas holds for you... comes... home..."

His voice trailed off into a thick, bubbling sigh.

Within sixty slow counts of the deafening cicadas pulsing in the dark cypress trees outside, the heavy Theban poppy-draft inside his Setine wine dragged Publius Vedius Pollio three fathoms under: jaw dropping slack against the white Pelusian sheet, plump depilated fingers uncurling limp over the couch-edge, loud, rhythmic, dead-drunk snores rasping in and out of his open throat!

Inside the glowing linen mosquito-net, nineteen-year-old Lucida stopped her almond-slicked fingers dead against the back of his neck.

*A sealed capital delatio already sitting in Castor Vault Forty-Two naming which Syrian girl carried green hellebore-and-savin bottles up the Caelian steps four Saturnalias ago.*

Somehow, through frightened Myrtale years ago, or a bribed scullery-maid—Hermippus had pieced together December 156's green abortive phials, prepared a capital *Lex Cornelia de Sicariis et Veneficis* poison denunciation against old Berenice and Lucida, and locked it inside **Vault Forty-Two at the Temple of Castor** timed to unseal the week Antoninus Pius died!

Outside down on the lower terrace, Scaeva's hobnails crunched gravel twenty-five yards away, four full hundred-breath circuits before he walked back near this lawn, every small indoor sound drowned completely beneath ten thousand August cicadas sawing in the pomegranates.

No slave in the pavilion. No witness under heaven.

Slowly, wiping bitter-almond cream silently off her right thumb and forefinger onto her brown linen knee, nineteen-year-old Lucida reached up with clean dry fingers into the center coil of her dark braids and slid out **six solid inches of razor-pointed Caelian gold**.

She held the needle poised vertical in the warm rose-lamp glow, **one finger-breadth above the soft, pale, hairless hollow at the base of Publius Vedius Pollio's shaved skull**, directly between the first cervical vertebra and the occipital bone where butcher-boys at the Forum Boarium pithed unruly calves with a two-inch awl—instant severance of the breath-cord, not three drops of red blood on the sheet if you wiped the pin-prick with cold alum-paste, indistinguishable at sunrise to any sleepy physician from drunken summer apoplexy after six hours of Ostian sunstroke and heavy poppy-wine!

One downward push of eight stone behind her baker's wrist, and Mousa's river-mud, Sura's sulfur-mill terror, sixty-eight Kalends of eighty-sesterce blood-money, young Titus's shattered shin-bone, and Vault Forty-Two's poison snare vanished together into darkness!

Lucida's amber eyes narrowed to slits; her shoulder locked directly above the needle-butt to drive home—

And **stopped dead half an inch above the sweating ivory skin**.

Not out of pity, she would have pithed Publius Vedius Pollio with calmer bowels than she gutted a Fish-Market eel—and not out of fear of Scaeva on the gravel.

Her hand stopped because **three years of reading Gaius's *Institutes* and watching Corvus map Roman property chains slammed an iron ledger-sheet shut across her brain two heartbeats before steel, before gold—touched flesh**:

*Suppose Publius Vedius Pollio lies dead of "apoplexy" on this sheet at dawn on Day Nine Hundred One—one hundred ninety-four days BEFORE Lucida holds Roman citizenship!*

Does a Junian Latin go free because her patron dies in his sleep?

**Never under Roman law!** Under the *Lex Iunia Norbana*, *ius patronatus* and the reversionary slave-claim over a living Junian Latin passed automatically at the instant of death to the dead patron's **heir or receivership creditor**! Vedius Pollio had neither wife nor child; every banker in the Forum knew his sole intestate agnate heir—his drunken cousin **Marcus Vedius** at Baiae—sat pledged four hundred thousand sesterces deep on defaulted Tusculan mortgages held directly by **Senator Quintus Servilius Priscus**!

Kill Vedius Pollio tonight with one hundred ninety-four days still unground on her Fish-Market stones, and before September's Kalends **Senator Quintus Servilius Priscus foreclosed Marcus Vedius's inheritance, putting Lucida's own Junian Latin leash, her Fish-Market ovens, and Castor Vault Forty-Two straight inside Corvus's ruthless Caelian patron's iron cupboard**, where Priscus wouldn't wait lazily for old Antoninus to die before crushing Ceionia Fabia's Caelian confidante!

Whereas while greedy, lazy **Vedius Pollio** breathed—believing he still had till Antoninus's death to spring Vault Forty-Two, Lucida knew **the exact vault number (ARCA XLII) at the Temple of Castor**, had **one hundred ninety-four mornings** to finish her thousand-and-ninety-five-day statutory shield OR pull Vault Forty-Two's teeth first, and kept her own throat out of Senator Priscus's Stoic jaws!

For three slow snores from the fat pink man beneath her knee, nineteen-year-old Lucida held the needle motionless half an inch above death—proving to her own bones who owned the room, then smoothly lifted the six-inch gold *acus* away and slid it silently back through the center coil of her dark braids.

Before rising off the rush mat, she took one lump of stiff white beeswax out of her cosmetic salve-kit, pressed it hard for two heartbeats over **both sides of the tiny bronze AEDE · CASTORIS · ARCA · XLII strongbox tally-key** and Vedius's emerald scarab ring lying beside his empty poppy-cup—lifting crisp, razor-sharp negative impressions into her salve-tin without moving either piece a tenth of an inch on the onyx dish, pulled the linen sheet neatly up over Vedius's greased shoulders like a dutiful handmaid, and stepped silently out through the mosquito-curtains.

Down by the cypress turn Scaeva glared sideways as she walked down the moonlit path swinging her empty cork snow-pail.

"Asleep already?" Scaeva grunted suspiciously.

"Dead to the world under cold almond and poppy, bailiff," Lucida said softly without breaking stride toward the iron gate. "Don't rattle your hobnails on the marble till noon, or he'll dock your tallow."
