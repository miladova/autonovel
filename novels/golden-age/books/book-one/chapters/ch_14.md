# Chapter XIV — The Green Stables

*Thirteenth Day Before the Kalends of May, Ludi Cereales, AD 159 · Lucida*

Before the sun cleared the Baths of Agrippa on the last morning of the **Games of Ceres**, the brick courtyard of the **Stable of the Greens** (*Trigarium Factio Prasina*) smelled of rubbed garlic, boiled Cyprian laurel-fat, nervous stallion dung, and forty thousand gold pieces riding on sixteen hooves wrapped in woven broom-straw *soleae* for the morning walk down to the sand!

Eighteen-year-old Lucida stood beside her two hired mule-carts just inside the bronze-studded stable arch on the western edge of the Campus Martius, checking lead tally-seals off her wax board while white-tunicked African grooms rolled forty-pound stoneware jars of tendon-embrocation and steaming cedar tubs of bruised barley-and-fenugreek mash down the straw-strewn aisle.

Four hundred fifteen unbroken mornings now sat chalked on Sura's black slate back behind the Fish-Market (**ANNVS II · DIES L**—fifty days deep into her second statutory year, every single dawn above a hundred and ten *modii*!), and the burial-guild bread-advance was nearly two-thirds cleared. Better still, nineteen months of supplying **Ceionia Fabia**'s race-stable chests had put nine thousand sesterces of clean reserve silver into Lucida's private strong-chest hidden beneath Oven Two's dry ash-floor, wearing finest-spun unbleached linen and a neat dark-green woollen *palla* today that marked her as a prosperous house-purveyor rather than a dock-runner.

All down the vaulted hundred-foot stable gallery, preparation for the afternoon's twenty-four chariot heats hummed at fever pitch.

Inside open-fronted box-stalls lined with planed oak so frightened thoroughbreds couldn't splinter their hocks kicking stone, forty prized Spanish, Cappadocian, and North African Cyrenaican stallions tossed braided manes threaded with green silk ribbons and green glass evil-eye beads while veterinary slaves sponged down quivering shin-bones with warm vinegar. Out on the sanded harness-floor four star *agitatores*, scarred, bow-legged men worth more silver than ten senators' villas, led by twenty-two-year-old Lusitanian **Diocles Minor**—stood with both arms raised parallel to the ground while trainers hauled wet rawhide rib-thongs (*fasciae*) round and round their chests from armpit to hip, lacing their torsos into rigid leather barrels so a snapped axle wouldn't stove their ribs in on impact. Into each man's waist-belt went the curved, razor-edged steel hook-knife (*falx*) with which a charioteer who wrapped his driving-reins round his own body, as every Roman driver did to steer by trunk-weight—had three heartbeats to slash himself loose if his team went down before four galloping horses dragged his skull to jelly along the barrier wall.

Down at Stall One, where two Praetorian guardsmen in white parade tunics leaned on unstrung javelins to keep stable-boys back, laughter rang warm and careless over the whickering of horses.

Twenty-eight-year-old **Lucius Ceionius Commodus** stood bare-headed in an emerald-green morning tunic embroidered at the wrists with gold palm-branches, holding flat on his open ringed palm a square of warm Attic honey-and-sesame cake while **Volucer**, the famous seven-year-old bay African *funalis* (left-hand trace-horse, the brains and pivot of the entire four-horse team, who swung tightest round the bronze turning-posts!)—velveted the cake delicately off the imperial prince's fingers without grazing skin.

"Look at the eye on him this morning, Publius!" Lucius exulted, wiping sticky honey and horse-saliva cheerfully onto his own fifty-aureus green tunic while patting the bay stallion's arched, shining crest. "Not a drop of night-sweat on the flank, and his frogs hard as boxwood since my sister's woman switched him off your factor's greasy pitch onto pure Cyprian laurel! How much have the Blues laid against him in the seventh *missus*?"

Standing on the far side of *Volucer*'s oak stall-door, immaculate in white holiday wool, shaved skull gleaming like oiled ivory, plump depilated hands folded placidly over a carved citron-wood betting-tablet—**Publius Vedius Pollio** smiled with effortless, musical charm.

"Two hundred thousand sesterces even money across three Carinae tables, my Lord Lucius, and I covered eighty thousand of it in your name before dawn so the odds wouldn't shorten," Vedius purred, and then, glancing down the sunlit aisle past Lucius's shoulder toward the two mule-carts unloading green-sealed stoneware jars by the gate, **Vedius Pollio's moist brown eyes met eighteen-year-old Lucida's straight on across thirty feet of straw**.

For two heartbeats neither Vedius's smile nor Lucida's hand holding her wax delivery-board moved a fraction of an inch.

Behind Vedius's velvet pupils Lucida watched the entire equation lock together inside that terrible accounting-machine of a skull: *Ceionia Fabia's mysterious stable-purveyor... green-pitched forty-pound stoneware jars... the silent eighty-sesterce Kalends tribute-payer who blocked Scaeva at the Fish-Market bakehouse with Fabia's lead seal!*

And right here, twenty paces from **Lucius Ceionius Commodus** feeding honey-cakes to *Volucer*, Vedius Pollio could not lift a finger, could not shout *"My Junian Latin chattel!"*, could not set Scaeva on her without admitting openly to young Lucius that Vedius had been overcharging the prince's beloved horses for years and picked a fight with Ceionia Fabia's own personal supplier on race morning!

Instead, as Lucius turned to inspect *Volucer*'s braided tail-ribbons with the Lusitanian driver, Vedius strolled six leisurely paces down the aisle to admire a white Cyrenaican trace-colt—bringing himself within four feet of the cedar bran-tubs where Lucida stood signing the head-groom's receipt.

Smell of crushed bitter almonds and snow-water drifted across the horse-straw between them.

"Pure Cyprian laurel-fat and bruised Reatine barley," Vedius murmured softly, pitching his musical voice as though praising the colt's pasterns so the groom three yards away heard nothing strange. "Remarkable how industrious one fifteen-year-old orphan grows by eighteen when Rome feeds her well. Four hundred fifteen mornings on those noisy Fish-Market stones now, isn't it, Lucida? Six hundred eighty mornings still to grind before Trajan's little fairy-tale turns pumpkin-rind into gold."

Lucida dipped the head-groom's reed pen calmly, signed *L · T* beneath the jar count, and looked straight at the smooth white fold beneath Vedius's ear without dropping her chin.

"Volucer's off-fore tendon hasn't swelled a hair since September, Dominus," Lucida replied in velvet Caelian Latin, quiet and clear as glass. "Lord Lucius told Lady Fabia at supper last night that whoever kept his bay sound through April deserved Caesar's own grain-warrant. Shall I tell Lord Lucius you were asking after the recipe?"

A tiny, genuine flash of connoisseur's delight flickered deep inside Vedius's dark eyes—a butcher admiring how cleanly his own calf stepped around the pole-axe.

"Keep your jars clean, little Tyrian," Vedius murmured, turning smoothly back toward Lucius's laugh at Stall One, "and sit high out of the sun at the Circus this afternoon. Crowds on Ceres's day trample careless folk underfoot in a blink."

*Crowds on Ceres's day trample careless folk underfoot in a blink.*

Lucida's fingers tightened slightly around her wax tablet as her empty mule-carts rolled back out into the roaring holiday street. Vedius Pollio never wasted breath on weather forecasts; somewhere inside that giant wooden-and-marble bowl between the Palatine and Aventine this afternoon, a trap already had its iron teeth greased.

* * *

One hundred and fifty thousand citizens wearing compulsory snow-white holiday togas in honor of Mother Ceres turned the three soaring tiers of the **Circus Maximus** into a mile-long canyon of blinding white chalk under an April sky blue as painted Egyptian glass.

Up on the high wooden sun-benches (*maeniana*) above the western curve of the **meta secunda**, where honest guild-masters and prosperous shop-folk sat on rented rush cushions eating salted lupins and fried pork-rinds while passing wine-skins down the row—Lucida sat wedged between wheezing old **Habinnas Barba** (wearing a ridiculous green woollen scarf tied round his bald crown, hoarse already from bellowing *"Prasina! Prasina!"* through four preliminary heats) and fifteen-year-old **Sura**, whose grey eyes darted constantly along the vaulted stair-vomitoria below just as Lucida had quietly warned her before noon.

Two hundred feet straight down across shimmering waves of heat and roasted-nut smoke lay the vast yellow ribbon of arena sand divided lengthwise by the marble barrier (*spina*), where seven colossal gilded wooden eggs and seven spouting bronze dolphins stood poised on high frames to count off the seven killer laps of each *missus*, flanking the towering red granite obelisk Augustus had hauled across the sea from Heliopolis to cast noon's shadow down the center of the world.

Directly opposite across the track, beneath crimson silk sun-awnings below the empty Palatine box, seventy-two-year-old **Emperor Antoninus Pius** had stayed quietly out at Lorium pruning vines rather than endure six hours of roaring dust, leaving his two adopted sons to preside side by side—Lucida could pick out tiny dots of purple and white along the senatorial marble podium: **Lucius Commodus** leaning half over the balustrade waving a green silk napkin with both hands; dark-bearded **Marcus Caesar** sitting dutifully beside him reading state dispatches on his knee right through the preliminary crashes!; and thirty seats left along the white-togated senatorial front row, stern grey-bearded **Senator Priscus**, behind whose bench Corvus had told her yesterday morning he wouldn't even be sitting today, because Corvus was spending Ceres's holiday auditing late Ostian grain-tallies and meeting a witness at the tenth hour!

Wait.

*Meeting an Ostian barge-witness at the tenth hour near the Circus arcades*, Corvus had mentioned offhandedly last night while washing ink off his wrists in Barba's courtyard—because holiday crowds covered a frightened clerk slipping away from Emporium watchers better than an empty street!

Lucida leaned forward over her knees, shading her amber eyes with one stain-faded hand to scan the arched travertine exits along the lower Blue-and-Green betting-galleries directly below the *meta secunda* at **Gate Twelve**.

Down on the yellow sand below, attendants in Ceres's white linen had just finished the ancient rural rite of releasing red hill-foxes across the infield to burn winter mildew out of Italy's standing wheat; four trumpeters stepped to the chalk starting-line (*alba linea*); up in the presiding lodge **Lucius Commodus** stood up radiant in gold-trimmed purple and dropped the weighted white linen **mappa** straight toward the dust!

Twelve iron gate-latches clanged back as one; forty-eight stallions exploded out of the twelve curved starting-stalls (*carceres*) in a rolling yellow hurricane of flying sand, cracking rawhide whips, and screaming axles while **one hundred and fifty thousand human throats** detonated in a single animal roar loud enough to rattle roof-tiles half a mile off on the Aventine!

Down the straight first quarter-mile thundered three teams each of **Greens, Blues, Reds, and Whites**, iron tire-rims rasping sparks hub-to-hub as Lusitanian **Diocles Minor** held **Volucer** tucked cunningly half a length behind the leading Blue *quadriga* to let Blue eat the choking dust-wind through Laps One, Two, Three, and Four. One by one four gilded bronze dolphins tipped nose-down into their marble water-basins along the *spina*.

Up beside Lucida, old Barba was standing on his pine bench pounding both flour-scarred fists on his own chest and screaming Cappadocian war-oaths until his solitary eye bulged purple; Lucida wasn't watching *Volucer* anymore.

Her eyes were locked two hundred feet below on **Vomitorium Twelve**, where a thin, nervous forty-year-old harbor clerk in a brown Ostian *paenula* clutching a sealed leather tally-pouch against his belly had just stepped into the shadowed mouth of the stairwell—looking tensely uphill toward the Forum Boarium for Senator Priscus's runner, while **six burly men in Blue and Green scarves** drifted casually into the exact same stairwell arch from opposite sides, with **one-eared Gaius Ummidius Scaeva** leaning calmly against a pastry-pillar outside the outer arcade watching the bronze dolphins tip!

*Crowds on Ceres's day trample careless folk underfoot in a blink.*

*Oh, gods—that's Priscus's Ostian grain-witness down in Gate Twelve, and Corvus or Phileros is walking straight toward that arch right now!*

"Sura—stay with Barba, don't move off this plank!" Lucida shouted straight into Sura's ear, shoving past twenty cursing spectators along the packed wooden row and plunging down the steep travertine interior steps three at a time while out on the track the fifth bronze dolphin tipped and catastrophe struck the sand!

Rounding the razor-tight three-post turn of the **meta secunda** forty yards off at full forty-mile-an-hour gallop, the leading **Blue** trace-horse slipped half a foot in churned sand; **Diocles Minor** whipped bay **Volucer** instantaneously through a four-foot suicide gap along the inner marble curb while behind them **Red One** slammed square into the slewing Blue axle-hub!

Ashwood chariot-boxes detonated like dry kindling hit by ballista-bolts.

Two stallions somersaulted neck-over-crupper in a spray of crimson foam and snapped bronze; Red's driver failed by half a heartbeat to draw his belt-*falx* before three terrified runaway horses dragged him upside down across fifty yards of hard sand, painting a yard-wide dark smear beside the marble wall while *Volucer* and the Greens tore clear into Lap Six and **fifty thousand Blue and Green bettors along the western tiers surged screaming off their benches toward the lower rails and betting-stairs at once**!

Inside the narrow, vaulted stone funnel of **Vomitorium Twelve**, daylight vanished beneath a stampeding human avalanche of drunken men in white wool roaring *"Foul turn! Blue cut! Pay the Green board!"*

Twenty steps above the bottom landing, pinned tight against the sweating travertine wall by surging shoulders, Lucida saw every merciless beat play out ten yards below her in four heartbeats of dim arch-light:

Two of Scaeva's fake "Blue" partisans flashed hidden iron knuckles, not knives, no blades that an urban cohort surgeon could call murder!—driving one short, crushing blow straight into the solar plexus of the brown-cloaked Ostian clerk while a "Green" partisan hooked the clerk's ankle backwards on the slick steps and yelled *"Blue thief stole my purse!"*

Down went the Ostian witness flat on his spine across the bottom three stone treads, and **four hundred panicking, cheering, twelve-stone race-goers** surged straight down the dark stairwell over his chest, throat, and skull like cattle stampeding through a chute!

Bones cracked dull and wet beneath hobnailed boots; someone snatched the crushed leather tally-pouch clean off the dying man's belt in the press; and by the time Lucida fought her elbow clear to the bottom arch-mouth gasping for air while the seventh-lap trumpet blared **Green victory** across the sky, **Scaeva** and his six scarfed thugs were already strolling calmly away past the cattle-market sausage-stalls without looking back, leaving *vigiles* stretchermen cursing over another "holiday crowd-crush victim" whose ribs had been trodden flat!

And twenty paces across the dusty arcade square—running up breathless from the Velabrum side too late because crowd-barriers had blocked the lower street, came **Phileros**, stopping dead beside a portico column as the *vigiles* pulled a coarse sack over the dead Ostian clerk's mangled face!

Lucida shrank flat behind a garlanded Ceres fruit-booth before Phileros's pale eyes swept the arch. Corvus wasn't with Phileros—thank heaven Corvus hadn't walked into those boots himself!, yet watching Phileros turn chalk-grey and stride rapidly northwest toward the **Campus Martius** told Lucida immediately where Corvus was waiting for the afternoon's word.

* * *

Out beyond the stifling dust and drunken victory-bonfires of the Circus valley, the **Porticus Pompei**—four vast parallel avenues of shady plane-trees, splashing marble fountains, and bronze statues of conquered nations behind Pompey's stone theater, lay breezy and green in slanting golden April twilight.

Lucida walked swiftly down the central gravel walk beneath rustling young plane-leaves, heart still knocking hard against her ribs from the wet crunch of ribs inside Vomitorium Twelve, scanning the marble exedra benches for Corvus's tall dark head so she could warn him privately before tonight that **Scaeva**—not accidental Circus drunks, had trampled Priscus's Ostian witness and stolen the leather tally-pouch.

Near the tall marble statue of **Syria** holding a carved bronze palm-branch beside the middle fountain-basin, two figures came strolling slowly toward her out of the western colonnade shadow, deep in low, intimate conversation.

Eighteen-year-old **Corvus** walked on the outside, six feet tall, wearing not his stout Subura-mended working wool, a supple, exquisitely fulled tunic of creamy Tarentine summer fleece under a light toga whose folds smelled from ten paces off of cedar-wood rather than fuller's sulfur.

And walking directly at his left side—so close her sea-green silk *palla* brushed his hip at every stride, her ringed white fingers resting possessively inside the crook of Corvus's right forearm while six pale green Campanian glass bracelets chimed lightly against his scarred right wrist, walked **Tullia Crispina**.

Lucida stopped dead beside the splashing marble rim of the fountain before her brain even gave her feet the order.

She knew who Tullia Crispina was; every merchant round the Emporium and Porticus Minucia knew the rich thirty-two-year-old Puteoli shipping widow worth nearly two million sesterces who rode behind sleek Apulian mules and broke crooked skippers over her knee.

More than that: eighteen-year-old Lucida had spent three winters compounding perfumes and watching Caelian women walk out of inner bedrooms into mirror-light. One single glance took in the faint, lazy flush along Crispina's bare throat above her sea-green silk, the unmistakable proprietary ease with which Crispina's thumb stroked the inner tendon of Corvus's bare forearm as she laughed up at something he said, and—drifting straight across six feet of April fountain-air, **the crisp scent of Campanian lemon-verbena water**, the exact faint ghost-smell Lucida had noticed twice on Corvus's hair-roots up in Barba's loft over the past five weeks without knowing its name!

Corvus looked up—and saw Lucida standing ten feet in front of them beside the marble statue of Syria.

For one naked fraction of a heartbeat, every drop of color left Corvus's sun-browned Sabine cheeks; his right arm twitched involuntarily inside Crispina's clasp before eighteen months of courtroom iron froze it still so he wouldn't insult a two-million-sesterce client on a public walk.

Crispina felt that tiny muscle-check under her fingers immediately. Her dark painted eyes slid slowly over eighteen-year-old Lucida from braided blue-black hair and dark green wool *palla* down to the faint purple-brown dye-shadow that never completely faded around Lucida's cuticles, summing up *pretty Subura baker-or-perfume girl from the boy's old neighborhood* in half a breath, and keeping all five ringed fingers resting lightly, unmistakably, right on Corvus's skin.

Run away or drop her eyes like a shamed servant, and Lucida would have hated herself until the day they put her ash-urn in Salvius's wall.

Instead Lucida's spine went straight as Ceionia Fabia's six-inch gold hairpin. Every trace of breathless alarm over Vomitorium Twelve vanished behind placid, crystalline Caelian velvet.

"Good evening, Lucius Cassius," Lucida said smoothly in crisp Forum Latin, inclining her braided head the exact quarter-inch an independent *exercitor* of a registered hundred-modius *pistrinum* gave an advocate on a public walk.

"Lucida," Corvus said—his voice half a tone lower and rougher than usual, grey-green eyes searching hers fiercely across the ten feet of raked gravel to read why she was in Campus Martius alone at dusk. "May I present my client **Domina Tullia Crispina**, Lucida, co-master of the Piscarium *pistrinum* and purveyor to the Caelian houses."

"Ah—the hundred-modius Sicilian bread-ovens near the Fish-Market!" Crispina said pleasantly, giving Lucida the warm, deadly-gracious smile equestrian ladies bestowed upon clean tradeswomen whose bills got paid by stewards. "Lucius Cassius was telling me down at **Laurentum** last month how hard independent city bakers fight to find clean hard wheat when Ostian factors skim the tubs. Perhaps once our summer Alexandria charters clear Portus, my steward can spare your ovens two hundred *modii* at fair wharf-rate."

*Down at Laurentum last month.*

Not an afternoon meeting in an Ostian warehouse office, *down at her seaside villa at Laurentum*, dropped casually like a poisoned honey-almond straight onto Lucida's tongue so the younger girl knew precisely whose bed-linen smelled of lemon-verbena!

Inside Lucida's chest something hot and jagged twisted hard enough to taste like copper at the back of her throat—remembering Corvus's scarred thumb trembling wet against her collarbone above the Janiculum water-wheels, remembering his sleeping cheek two inches from her bare ankle by the October chestnut-coals while both of them held back out of terror of ruining everything they had built, and not a flicker of pain reached her mouth.

"Domina Crispina is generous," Lucida replied, smiling straight back into the older woman's painted Campanian eyes with luminous, lethal courtesy, "though my *pistrinum* buys directly off Agrigentine hulls cash down without forty-day bottomry paper; we find clean Sicilian grain keeps much sweeter when too many brokers' hands haven't warmed the sack first."

Crispina's dark eyebrows arched a hair's breadth at the sting hidden inside the silk; Corvus's jaw tightened white along the bone.

"I stopped only in passing from the Circus, Lucius Cassius," Lucida added evenly, looking straight into Corvus's eyes now so he caught the iron warning underneath the polite words, "to mention that the crowd-crush down in **Vomitorium Twelve** after *Volucer*'s win trampled an Ostian harbor-clerk in a brown *paenula* dead on the bottom steps—right beside six noisy partisans whose chief had only one ear. Your colleague Phileros saw the stretchers leave; if you had an evening meeting down near the cattle-market, save your clean Tarentine wool the walk."

Corvus's eyes locked hard onto hers, taking the whole warning in one flash (*Vedius killed Fulvius; don't walk into the trap; and yes, she saw the wool*).

"Thank you," Corvus said quietly. "Will you—will Barba have the courtyard shutter unbarred at the second watch tonight?"

*Are you coming home to the loft tonight, or going back to lemon-verbena?* hung unspoken behind his teeth, and *Are you locking me out?* hung behind hers.

"Barba unbars the courtyard only for the midnight grain-carts after the first watch on feast-nights," Lucida said calmly, turning back down the shady avenue of plane-trees before Crispina could speak again. "Good fortune with your Alexandria hulls, Domina. Good evening, Lucius Cassius."

She walked all the way out through Pompey's marble arch onto the noisy, torch-lit April cobbles without once quickening her pace or looking back, holding her head steady while Green chariot-fans danced drunkenly round bonfires singing *Volucer*'s name, until she turned into a dark, empty fuller's passage behind the Temple of Bellona, pressed both fists flat against rough tufa stone out of every human eye, and breathed through clenched teeth until the shaking in her ribs turned cold and hard as volcanic mill-lava.
