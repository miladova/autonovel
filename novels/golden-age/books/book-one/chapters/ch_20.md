# Chapter XX — The Vinegar-Men

*Thirteenth Day Before the Kalends of July, AD 160 · Lucida*

Every barracks of the **Vigiles** in Rome smelled from fifty paces down the street like a pickled-cabbage cellar that had married an ironworks.

Standing inside the arched travertine courtyard of the **Fourth Cohort's Subura Excubitorium** in blazing mid-June glare, nineteen-year-old Lucida watched twelve bare-chested freedmen fire-troopers (*siphonarii* and *centonarii*) haul thick, coarse patchwork woollen blankets out of stone vats of sour Campanian vinegar (*acetum*) with wooden tongs, hanging them dripping brown across pine racks before tonight's first patrol.

Plain aqueduct water boiled into useless steam when thrown onto burning olive-oil cellars or pitch-tarred Subura roof-shingles; heavy woollen *centones* soaked sodden in sour vinegar smothered flaming grease dead on contact without exploding sparks up the stairwells. Beside the vinegar-vats stood two wheeled bronze **Ctesibian double-piston force-pumps** (*siphones*), their greased leather valves hissing *chuff-chuff* as four pump-men tested the swiveling brass gooseneck nozzle by shooting a solid thumb-thick jet of water thirty feet up onto a brick practice-tower; and racked along the shady east wall like siege-weapons for a barbarian town rested forty **twenty-foot ashwood demolition-poles** shod in terrible curved iron hooks (*hami*) and wall-sickles (*falces*).

Centurion **Gaius Arrius Aper**—forty-six, missing two fingers off his left hand from a fallen burning beam in Hadrian's last year, himself a former Junian Latin who had won his Roman citizenship the hard male way by six years of hauling water-buckets before rising twenty ranks to carry the centurion's vine-rod, counted Lucida's **twenty bright silver denarii** into his bronze helmet-liner with brisk, unsentimental respect.

"Eighty sesterces buys clean axle-grease on Carts Three and Four all through July and August, Mistress Lucida," Arrius Aper grunted, tucking the silver into his belt-purse and walking her three paces into the shade beside the racked iron wall-hooks. "I checked your old baker Ulpius Felix's new four-storey brick warehouse on the Clivus Suburanus myself on Tuesday night. Twenty-four-inch *opus testaceum* piers, vaulted tufa cellars, iron shutters on the rear alley slits: honest masonry. If every greedy landlord in Region IV built like that instead of stacking seven storeys of dry pine matchwood on mud-brick stilts, my boys would sleep six hours a night."

"It isn't my own brick arches catching fire first that keeps me awake, Centurion," Lucida said quietly, touching one finger to the cold, sharpened iron point of a twenty-foot *falx* demolition-hook. "Five feet across our eastern rear drip-alley stands a leaning seven-storey timber fuller's block whose top garrets have four Cappadocian caretakers storing unsealed lamp-oil jars right against rotten lath. Suppose a wind blows west off the Esquiline one dry night in August and that seven-storey pine torch goes up?"

Arrius Aper stopped smiling. He glanced once across the sunny yard to make sure his junior *optio*—a slick Greek-speaking clerk near the water-trough, was out of earshot, then looked squarely back at nineteen-year-old Lucida.

"You grew up thin enough to know how Rome burns, girl, so I won't feed you nursery porridge," the scarred fire-centurion said low. "When a seven-storey timber *insula* catches in August wind, four bronze *siphones* and all the sour vinegar in Campania won't save one stick of it; flames jump ten-foot streets faster than a horse runs. What the Cohort *does* inside three hundred breaths of arriving is **pull down everything standing twenty paces downwind with these iron hooks and ballista-chains** to starve the fire before it eats Augustus's marble!"

Cold certainty slid down Lucida's spine. "And who chooses *which* neighbor's wall gets pulled flat into rubble first when smoke blinds the lane?"

"On my watch, wind and stone decide," Aper answered grimly. "On the third watch of alternate nights when *Optio* **Gaius Nymphidius** commands the forward hook-party—and Nymphidius drinks free Chian wine twice a week with **Hermippus's** rent-collectors down by the Sandaliarius, I've seen hook-crews spend half an hour 'clearing' a stout two-storey brick shop whose owner refused to sell his lease, while letting a crooked syndicate's empty timber shell burn clean to the ground so the landlord clears forty tenants overnight without paying one brass *as* in eviction lawsuits! Keep twenty barrels of sour vinegar and four iron crowbars inside your own Fish-Market and Clivus Suburanus cellars from the Kalends of July onward, girl—and if bells ring after midnight on an odd-numbered watch, send a fast runner straight to *my* quarters behind the Viminal gate before Nymphidius gets hooks into your mortar."

"Send your runner a fat roasted kid every Kalends through October, Centurion, and count another twenty denarii if my brick stands whole at November rains," Lucida said cleanly.

Walking down off the Viminal slope toward the Fish-Market, where Sura's morning slate today read **ANNVS III · DIES CXI** (Day 841 of 1,095, **only two hundred fifty-four mornings left** until full Quiritary Roman citizenship shattered Publius Vedius Pollio's leash forever!)—Lucida turned Centurion Aper's warning over and over in her head.

Scaeva's oil-jars in the condemned seventh-floor garret behind Lot Fourteen; Nymphidius drinking Hermippus's wine; and August's Dog-Star wind six weeks away.

Before dusk, however, a perfumed Quirinal footman in purple-trimmed livery arrived at Berenice's old Argiletum counter bearing a polite wax tablet stamped with a red carnelian four-horse chariot, inviting *"Lucida, purveyor of Syrian and Corycian unguents to the Caelian houses,"* at twelve silver denarii show-fee to bring her finest alabaster sample-casket up to the *Domus Volusenia* on the Quirinal Hill at the second hour of the night, ostensibly to display summer scents before **Aulus Volusenus Niger**'s visiting Campanian aunts!

Lucida held that red carnelian *quadriga* wax impression up to the afternoon light for three slow breaths.

She had never seen **Aulus Volusenus Niger**'s face close up; yet she remembered November three and a half years ago like hot iron—fifteen-year-old Corvus climbing Berenice's dark stair spitting blood through a split left lip after Senator Priscus's arrogant patrician nephew drove an ivory stylus into his mouth at Festus's rhetoric school, and Dorcas mentioning only last month on the Caelian that young Volusenus had lost eighty thousand sesterces on Blue chariots this spring and been seen creeping out of **Hermippus's** private moneylending office at dusk!

Walk past a patrician's sealed twelve-denarius summons without checking what game Hermippus's newest debtor was playing? Never. Only a fool walked blind, and only a coward stayed home; instead Lucida left her rarest vials locked at the bakehouse, packed six legitimate commercial sample-pots inside an iron-cornered cedar casket, dressed not in humble shop-brown, in **rich, unadorned deep-saffron Coan linen beneath an immaculate ivory woollen *palla*** that made her look more like a Syrian tetrarch's daughter than any fuller's girl, and took two six-foot Marsian kneaders armed with legal oak carrying-poles right up to Volusenus's Quirinal torch-gate.

* * *

There was not one Campanian aunt or respectable Roman matron inside the **Domus Volusenia** tonight.

Even before Volusenus's smirk-faced Greek *atriensis* led Lucida down the colonnade (ordering her two burly Marsian porters to wait by the outer vestibule fountain, where Lucida quietly signaled them to keep one heavy boot wedged inside the inner door-bolt!), the roar of drunken male laughter, shrill Phrygian double-flutes, and rattling ivory dice rolling out of the garden *triclinium* told the whole story.

Inside a high, frescoed summer banquet-room lit by forty smoking bronze candelabrum-branches, nine couches arranged in three hollow sides around flooded mosaic fish-patterns held twenty-odd flushed young senatorial sons and equestrian advocates reclining on rose-strewn silk with myrtle wreaths askew over sweating brows. Half-naked Gaditane castanet-girls in transparent yellow gauze sat perched along the lower couch-hems pouring unwatered Setine wine into shallow glass bowls.

Up on the host's *lectus imus* lounged twenty-two-year-old **Aulus Volusenus Niger**, plumper in the jowls than at seventeen, cinnamon beard-oil shining wet under his wreath, twisting his red carnelian chariot-ring incessantly around a puffy finger.

And three couches left along the *lectus medius*, reclining upright on one lean elbow in a crisp white dinner-*synthesis* among three junior Forum pleaders, an untouched cup of watered wine held loosely in his scarred right hand, visibly hating every drunken giggle in the room and present solely because Senator Priscus had ordered him not to snub his nephew's reconciliation supper!—sat nineteen-year-old **Lucius Cassius Corvus**.

Corvus had not known she was coming.

The instant Volusenus's steward pulled back the embroidered curtain and nineteen-year-old **Lucida** stepped onto the threshold in deep saffron linen and ivory wool carrying her cedar sample-casket, **Corvus's entire six-foot body locked rigid as carved basalt** on the middle couch.

Up on the host's cushions Volusenus Niger clapped both ringed hands loud enough to silence the Phrygian flute-player, rising half onto his knees with a wet, triumphant grin aimed straight across the mosaic at Corvus!

"Friends! Fellow ornaments of the Centumviral Spear!" Volusenus bellowed thickly across the room, sweeping his wine-cup toward Lucida in the doorway. "My learned colleague **Lucius Cassius** sits over there drinking plain cistern-water tonight as if Cato died this morning, forgetting where he learned such charming street-Latin! Since I hate seeing a rising man grow homesick for Subura mud, my steward tracked down his very own childhood alley-mate from the fuller's piss-vats, **Lucida of the Fish-Market**, come all the way uphill with twelve denarii worth of grease-pots! Step into the middle, sweetheart, take that heavy wool off your shoulders, and rub some Syrian spikenard onto our ankles while you tell the couches what old ditch-friends charge after midnight!"

Drunken laughter started along two of the upper couches—and died halfway in five throats as four perceptive junior advocates glanced sideways at **Corvus**.

Corvus had not stood up yet, and had not uttered a syllable.

Resting against the white linen table-napkin on his couch-edge, Corvus's scarred right hand had closed slowly, convulsively tighter around the thin blown-glass Sidonian drinking-cup he held until, with a tiny, razor-sharp *crick* audible three couches off—**the delicate glass bowl collapsed inward into twenty jagged splinters inside his bare fist**.

Dark red wine and bright arterial crimson ran together between Corvus's knuckles onto the white napkin; Corvus didn't even blink at his cut palm, already shifting his right heel onto the mosaic floor to rise and tear Aulus Volusenus Niger's windpipe out across his own ancestors' table, which under Roman law meant *vis atrox* against a senator's nephew inside his own *domus*, instant ruin before tomorrow noon!

Lucida beat Corvus by half a breath.

Before Corvus's sandal took weight, nineteen-year-old Lucida stepped calmly forward onto the central mosaic fish-medallion—never touching the clasp of her ivory *palla*, standing tall and utterly unhurried beneath forty bronze flames, and looked straight past Volusenus's flushed face first **for one single fraction of a second into Corvus's blazing grey-green eyes**, giving him the tiniest, fiercest downward flick of two eyelashes:

*Sit still. Watch me gut this pig without staining your toga.*

Corvus caught the signal; with superhuman effort his boot paused flat on the stone, his bleeding right hand wrapping silently inside his white linen table-napkin out of sight while his jaw stayed locked white as bone.

Lucida turned her calm, luminous amber gaze onto **Aulus Volusenus Niger** on the host's couch and smiled—the exact thin-lipped, bone-chilling Caelian smile she had watched **Ceionia Fabia** turn on trembling chamberlains.

"My steepest apologies to your couch, Aulus Volusenus," Lucida said, her clear, unhurried contralto carrying into every corner of the hushed *triclinium* in Palatine Latin cleaner than half the drunken knights in the room spoke. "Your steward's tablet named your worthy Campanian aunts; had he mentioned you needed cheap ankle-grease for Gaditane tavern-dancers, I would have sent old Sticho's fuller-boy instead of bringing **the exact Corycian saffron and white-almond caskets Lady Ceionia Fabia and Praetor Aufidius's wife ordered mixed for next week's Palatine supper**."

Along the upper couch, young **Aufidius Minor**—cousin to Praetor Aufidius, sat straight up out of his slouch, blinking hard at the striking saffron-robed young woman as Ceionia Fabia's name hit the mosaic!

Volusenus's wet grin slipped half an inch sideways. "Ceionia Fabia's back-stairs peddler still kneels when—"

"Oh, I never kneel outside temples, worthy host," Lucida interrupted smoothly, setting her cedar casket down on a marble tripod with a crisp snap and opening the lid just long enough for twenty pairs of rich male eyes to glimpse six rock-crystal flasks stoppered in gold leaf, "especially on floors whose mosaic mortar looks damp with heavy mortgages! Though since you kindly mentioned the Argiletum and Sandaliarius alleys, **Hermippus**, chief steward to Publius Vedius Pollio—asked across our grain-street only yesterday whether any Quirinal gentlemen knew when a certain **eighty-thousand-sesterce Blue chariot note stamped with a red carnelian *quadriga*** would pay its June interest before going up on the Basilica steps! Shall I tell Hermippus's clerk tomorrow morning that your silver went into Setine wine tonight, or would Praetor Aufidius's cousin like to smell real Cilician crocus before I take Lady Fabia's casket home?"

Total, suffocating silence dropped over all nine couches.

Not a single guest in that room had known until five heartbeats ago that Senator Priscus's own patrician heir **Aulus Volusenus Niger** was eighty thousand sesterces deep in secret gambling paper to his uncle's mortal enemy **Vedius Pollio**!

Volusenus went the exact greenish-white color of rancid mutton-tallow beneath his cinnamon beard-oil, his left hand jerking instinctively inside his dinner-robe to hide the red carnelian *quadriga* ring on his finger, confirming every syllable to twenty pairs of razor-sharp Roman gossip-ears at once!

Down on Couch One, delighted **Aufidius Minor** burst into a loud, malicious bark of patrician laughter, tossing four gold *aurei* (400 HS!) ringing onto Lucida's cedar tripod!

"Send two flasks of that Corycian saffron round to my mother's house on the Carinae tomorrow noon, Mistress Lucida—and charge my cousin Aulus twelve denarii extra for teaching his table manners!" Aufidius crowed, while a second young quaestor beside him tossed **two more gold *aurei*** onto the tray for his bride's dressing-table!

Lucida swept all six gold pieces, plus Volusenus's advance twelve silver denarii already in her steward's receipt—smoothly into her belt-purse, closed the cedar casket with two unhurried brass latches, inclined her head graciously to Aufidius and the silent couches without glancing once more at paralyzed, humiliated Volusenus, and walked unhurriedly out through the embroidered curtain.

* * *

Out in the dark, laurel-scented Quirinal peristyle on her way toward the street-vestibule where her two Marsian porters waited with oak poles ready, swift footsteps crossed the travertine flags behind her before she reached the colonnade turn.

"Vespera, wait."

Nineteen-year-old Corvus stepped between two moonlit fluted columns ahead of her, his white dinner-*synthesis* gleaming pale in the June dark, his right hand wrapped tight inside a blood-soaked white table-napkin through which fresh dark crimson was already seeping across his knuckles.

Lucida stopped dead, eyes dropping instantly from his pale, furious face straight down to that blood-soaked linen around his scarred right hand!

Without thinking—all evening sophistication vanishing in one heartbeat of visceral childhood reflex, she set her cedar casket down on the column-stylobate, caught his left wrist to pull his wrapped right hand into the patch of moonlight, and unwrapped the bloody napkin with quick, practiced fingers.

Three sharp quarter-inch cuts crossed the heel of his palm and the meat below his scarred thumb; one tiny triangular sliver of clear Sidonian glass still stood wedged bright in the skin above his lifeline.

"Idiot Sabine," Lucida breathed fiercely under her breath, gripping his thick wrist steady against her hip while pinching the glass sliver cleanly out between two fingernails before pressing a clean folded linen square from her own sample-casket hard over the bleeding cuts and binding it tight round his hand. "Breaking twenty-denarius Sidonian glass in your bare fist because a fat debtor with cinnamon grease in his curls barked my name! Three heartbeats slower and you would have jumped across that fish-mosaic at his throat and handed your own career to Lollius Urbicus's lictors on a dish!"

Corvus didn't flinch once as her thumb pressed hard into the bleeding cuts; staring down at the dark crown of her braided head bent close over his hand in the warm June night-scent of bruised laurel and saffron linen, his left hand came up half an inch toward her bare neck—then dropped back to his side, clenched tight.

"How did you know Volusenus owed Hermippus eighty thousand on a red carnelian note?" Corvus asked very quietly over her bent head.

Lucida finished tucking the linen knot snug under his scarred right thumb and lifted her eyes two inches from his chest straight up into his face.

"The same way you know whose straw-men own the Sandaliarius," she answered evenly. "By counting every coin moving down my street before somebody uses it to burn my roof. Listen to me, Corvus, forget your cousin's bruised vanity for ten breaths and hear something real: this afternoon Centurion Arrius Aper at the Fourth Cohort of *Vigiles* warned me that Hermippus has bribed *Optio* Nymphidius on the odd-night demolition hook-crews, and Scaeva still has four men keeping oil-jars in that condemned seven-storey timber garret directly behind Ulpius Felix's new brick grain-vaults! Why hasn't your building-commission torn down those upper two timber storeys yet?"

Corvus's jaw set hard in the moonlight. "Because two days ago **Lollius Urbicus** collapsed on his tribunal with black dropsy in both lungs—he hasn't signed a single demolition warrant since the Ides, and until Antoninus names an acting Prefect or Urbicus wakes up, every contested sixty-foot raze order on Vedius Pollio's Subura blocks stands frozen in Chancery!"

Both nineteen-year-olds stared at each other in the warm, dry June night air, already eighty degrees at midnight, not a drop of rain since the Kalends of May, forty thousand wagon-loads of dry Subura pine baking brittle overhead while Lollius Urbicus lay dying and Scaeva's oil-jars sat untouched behind her wall!

"Two hundred fifty-four mornings left on my Fish-Market stones," Lucida whispered. "Keep your Carinae shutters barred when Dog-Star wind blows next month, Sabine."

"Move Sura and your night-ledgers out of that Clivus Suburanus vault-house back to solid stone until Urbicus recovers—or let me put four Caelian watchmen in your alley tomorrow," Corvus urged low, catching her elbow with his unbandaged left hand as she picked up her cedar casket.

Lucida looked down at his fingers on her saffron sleeve, then up at the fresh red blood soaking dark through her own white linen bandage round his right palm, proof that even across three couches and two rival worlds he would still shatter glass in his bare flesh at an insult to her name, even while carrying secrets inside his Caelian box that could bury everything she knew.

"My Marsians carry stout oak, Lucius Cassius, and nobody guards my alleys for me," she said softly, slipping her elbow free without anger—almost gently, before stepping out toward the torch-gate. "Put turpentine-salve on that palm before morning so your stylus-hand doesn't stiffen. You're going to need it."
