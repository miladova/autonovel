# Chapter XXVI — Forty Days to April

*Seventh Day Before the Kalends of March, Terminalia, AD 161 · Lucida*

White hoarfrost lay thick as spilled salt along the basalt threshold of the Fish-Market bakehouse on the morning of the **Terminalia**, and inside the roaring stone courtyard sixty-five-year-old **Habinnas Barba** could no longer lift a sixty-pound Sicilian wheat-sack above his hip without bracing one knee against the hopper-post.

Twenty-year-old Lucida stepped silently across the flour-dusted flags in the red fourth-watch cresset glare, hooked her own strong forearms beneath the bottom corners of the sack before Barba's proud old spine could buckle, and swung all sixty pounds smoothly up over the iron lip of Number Three *catillus* as if they had rehearsed lifting together.

"Bag's stitched crooked at the ear," Barba wheezed through his teeth, refusing to look at her hands as he leaned both knotted palms against the warm volcanic bell until the blue-grey pinch around his nostrils eased. "Agrigentine weavers use rotten hemp since autumn. Don't stand behind my elbow, girl; go check Sura's steelyard."

When he coughed into his folded woollen neck-rag three breaths later, the sound down inside his barrel chest was no longer dry dry-pea rattle; it bubbled thick and wet like porridge boiling low in an iron pot, and the rag he shoved hastily inside his sheepskin vest showed bright rusty streaks even in dim tallow-light.

Asclepiades of Pergamum had told Lucida the plain truth behind closed shutters ten days ago after listening with his ear pressed flat against Barba's flour-scarred back: forty-five winters of volcanic stone-grit and fine wheat-paste had turned half the old Cappadocian soldier's lower lungs to hard grey scar-stone; take him out to sunny Tibur hills away from mill-dust tomorrow morning and he might breathe two gentle summers yet—leave him standing every midnight inside flour-fog beside four thunderous lava mills, and March cold could drown him in a night.

And three nights ago when Lucida had ordered a sunny room rented for him above an olive-yard on the Aventine with a gentle Syrian boy to boil his goat's-milk, old Barba had picked up his eight-foot iron ember-peel and threatened to smash his own registration plaque off the *Annona* wall if she tried putting him to pasture **forty-five mornings short of the finish-post**!

Up beside Oven One, seventeen-year-old **Sura**, tall, calm-eyed, managing four Marsian kneaders and twelve dawn guild-runners now solely with three sharp taps of an oak tally-stick and lightning chalk, stepped down off her wooden stool and underlined this morning's square white letters twice:

**ANNVS III · DIES CCCXX — MODII CXVIII**

*One thousand and fifty unbroken days and nights.*

Lucida stood beside the glowing twelve-foot brick dome doing the subtraction she could have recited backward under torture: **forty-five mornings left** between today's *Terminalia* sacrifice to Jupiter of the Boundary-Stones and the tenth morning of April, when **Day One Thousand Ninety-Five** closed on the Urban Prefect's roll and Junian Latin *Lucida* walked up the marble steps of the Porticus Minucia to receive the bronze citizen certificate that smashed **Publius Vedius Pollio**'s *ius patronatus* to powder forever!

"Look at those numbers, little Tyrian," Barba rasped beside her, solitary black eye shining fierce and fever-bright under chalk-white brows as he rapped one scarred knuckle against Sura's *CCCXX*. "Forty-five sunrises! Shorter than a recruits' march from Caesarea to the Euphrates! Let my chest rattle till Ceres's April horns blow; on the morning of the forty-sixth day I drink three cups of unwatered Chian on the Prefect's steps, watch you spit across Hermippus's boots as a full Roman citizen of the Palatina tribe, and sleep till July!"

Lucida reached out—ignoring his grumpy flinch, and tucked the warm woollen scarf tighter across his heaving throat with fingers faintly stained from yesterday's Caelian alkanet batch.

"Drink the hot squill-and-hyssop mug Sura set on the ash-ledge right now, old mule, or you don't get a drop of Chian in April," Lucida told him fiercely, swallowing down the sharp ache in her own throat before turning toward the street-arch as red sunrise touched the Forum gables.

* * *

At the seventh hour, wrapped in a sober charcoal-grey woollen *palla* against the biting February river-wind while street-altars on every corner smoked with sacrificial honey-cakes and garlands hung on ancient neighborhood boundary-posts for the *Terminalia*, twenty-year-old Lucida walked slowly along the south flank of the **Temple of Castor** in the Forum Romanum.

High above her head rose the three soaring white Corinthian marble columns supporting the twins' entablature; directly alongside her hip, built straight into the twenty-foot-high concrete-and-travertine podium beneath the temple floor, stood a row of narrow, iron-grated bronze doors (*loculi aerarii*) guarded day and night by armed public watchmen, the safest private safe-deposit vaults in the Roman Empire, where senators, widows, and syndicates stored gold bullion, wills, and sealed criminal denunciations.

Inside her pocket rode the hardened white beeswax impression she had lifted off sleeping **Publius Vedius Pollio**'s bedside dish six months ago: **AEDE · CASTORIS · ARCA · XLII**.

Even sixty gold *aurei* wouldn't bribe a daytime Castor vault-warden to open Door Forty-Two without both Vedius's emerald scarab AND the registered depositor present in flesh; Lucida was walking the narrow travertine lane between Castor's podium and the round smoke-plumed roof of **Vesta's Temple** simply to see which clerk held the fourth-watch night roster—when out from the shadowed colonnade of the **Lacus Juturnae** spring stepped an old, stoop-shouldered Greek freedman wrapped to his ears in a faded dark cloak, tapping the wet travertine curb cautiously ahead of his sandals with a peeled hazel wand.

Three worn bone wax-scrapers hung silent at his belt-ring, though his left eye stared completely milky-blind into the February mist and his right squinted half-shut behind red rims.

**Phileros.**

Since that violent September cloudburst inside Lot Fourteen's brick vault five months ago, Lucida had neither spoken one word to **Lucius Cassius Corvus** nor crossed within fifty paces of Senator Priscus's Caelian household. Seeing Priscus's thirty-year shadow standing ten feet away beside Juturna's cold marble basin made every nerve along Lucida's wrists go taut at once.

She started to step smoothly past without greeting; Phileros tilted his shaved grey chin two inches toward the faint rustle of her Tarsian woollen hem and spoke low in Syrian-inflected Greek:

"Don't hurry past the water, daughter of Lucida Tyria. A blind clerk sells cleaner merchandise than sighted ones, because he can't identify his buyer's face tomorrow before a praetor."

Lucida stopped three paces from the marble spring-curb, checking both ends of the misty travertine colonnade: only two Vestal temple-sweepers forty yards off and idle pigeons ruffling feathers against frost.

"Senator Priscus's archive-master has the wrong woman if he's peddling boundary-scrolls," Lucida said coldly.

Phileros gave a dry, soundless chuckle that showed pale gums. "Four and a half years ago in November I bought your Sabine boy's first brick-stamp whisper in the Clivus Scauri vestibule for four sesterces a day. Next month, once Quintus Servilius Priscus puts the narrow purple stripe on twenty-year-old **Lucius Cassius Corvus**, Priscus sends half-blind old Phileros out to count olives on a damp Tusculan hillside at twelve sesterces a month until dropsy finishes me. Before I go count olives, I intend to sew **fifty gold *aurei*** inside my saddle-pad, and nobody in Rome today needs fifty gold pieces' worth of Castor air half as badly as a Junian baker-girl with forty-five mornings left on her slate."

Lucida's fingers slid inside her cloak-fold without making a sound. "Fifty gold pieces buys facts with dates and seals attached, Greek—never smoke. Give me one corner free so I know your basket isn't empty."

Phileros turned his milky eye toward the iron-barred bronze doors set into Castor's concrete podium twenty paces west.

"Door Forty-Two," Phileros murmured softly, and Lucida's heart gave one cold, silent thud against her ribs. "Two days ago at dusk, **Hermippus** met **Gaius Vibius**, confidential urban clerk to Praetorian Prefect **Sextus Cornelius Repentinus**, outside Vault Forty-Two to add a fresh co-depositor seal. Inside sits a sworn capital *delatio* under the *Lex Cornelia de Sicariis et Veneficis*, signed by your frightened former garret-girl **Myrtale**, whom Scaeva tracked down in Ostia this winter!—accusing one-eyed **Berenice** of the Argiletum and **Lucida, Junian Latin**, of compounding and delivering lethal abortive hellebore-and-savin poisons to Caelian houses, causing the death of a slave-maid three winters back."

Every breath of February mist felt like crushed glass entering Lucida's lungs, though her voice stayed level as weighed iron: "The slave-maid behind Ceionia Fabia's screen in December didn't die; Dorcas married her off to a country bailiff at Praeneste!"

"Dead or picking turnips at Praeneste matters **six weeks after arrest**, girl, never on arrest night!" Phileros hissed gently. "Repentinus's clerk endorsed that packet for summary interregnum execution under public-safety powers **the hour couriers ride in from Lorium**, and yesterday afternoon old **Emperor Antoninus** walked ten paces outside his Lorium colonnade in cold sea-wind, took a shaking chill in his chest, and went to bed refusing supper! If Antoninus dies next week instead of April, Scaeva and Repentinus's urban *stationarii* kick in Berenice's door and your Fish-Market bakehouse **within three hours of midnight**, lock your four lava mills behind military seals while dragging you to the Prefecture cells, and **kill forty-five remaining mornings of your three-year citizenship run before sunrise**, even if Ceionia Fabia pulls you out of chains three days later!"

Fifty gold *aurei* was five thousand sesterces—nearly a sixth of Lucida's entire liquid reserve after repairing Lot Fourteen, and worth ten times the price for warning alone.

"My runner brings twenty-five gold pieces to the Juturna steps within the hour, and twenty-five more when you name the second half of your basket," Lucida said crisply, reading from the tilt of the old Greek's mouth that he had kept his sharpest hook for dessert. "Speak it now."

Phileros leaned two inches closer on his peeled hazel stick, milky eye staring blind past her shoulder toward the Curia Julia.

"You think your childhood friend **Lucius Cassius Corvus** will save your ovens if Vedius falls first?" Phileros whispered, not maliciously, with the terrible, clinical indifference of a man who had watched Roman contracts eat human hearts for forty winters. "Listen to the exact wording of the emergency fiscal receivership decree I helped Corvus engross last night for the *Praetor Aerarii*! The morning old Antoninus dies—before Repentinus can unseal Vault Forty-Two, Corvus intends to spring his *Figlinae Subortanae* brick-fraud and Caelian water-theft before five citizen witnesses, voiding Gaius Munatius's shield-trust *per aes et libram* and sweeping **every single mill, warehouse, and patronal claim of Publius Vedius Pollio** into summary state-and-receivership sequestration under Corvus's own seal!"

"Corvus screamed in September that putting his own name on that receivership sheet was meant to shield my ovens," Lucida said low, throat dry as chalk.

"Perhaps his twenty-year-old heart even believes so," Phileros replied softly, tapping his hazel stick once on wet travertine, "because even clever twenty-year-olds forget **Paragraph Nine of the Treasury Receivership Instructions** until the clerks arrive with lead wire! Whenever *any* commercial *pistrinum* carrying a disputed patronal reversion or syndicate lien enters *aerarium* receivership, regardless of who sits named as *sequester*—**sworn Treasury *tabularii* padlock the hoppers and suspend commercial grinding for three mandatory market-days** while weighing grain-stock against state claims! Three unground mornings on Day One Thousand Fifty-Five, daughter of Tyria, kills Trajan's unbroken three-year statute **just as stone-dead as Scaeva's iron crowbars**, leaving your citizenship dead on the floor and your Junian reversion sitting legally inside **Lucius Cassius Corvus's** four-hundred-thousand-sesterce equestrian strongbox!"

For five heartbeats the entire Forum Romanum, white marble temples, wheeling winter gulls, shouting lawyers pouring out of the Basilica Julia across the square—went dead silent inside Lucida's ears.

*Three mandatory market-days of Treasury hopper-padlocks the hour Vedius Pollio's estate enters fiscal receivership.*

Whether **Vedius Pollio** struck first with Vault Forty-Two's poison warrant, OR **Corvus** struck first with his grand fiscal *per aes et libram* triumph, **either hammer hitting Rome before mid-April snapped her thousand-and-fifty-day mill-run forty mornings short of citizen liberty**!

And once her statutory mill-road broke, with Vedius ruined and Priscus's faction seizing the courts unless **Lucius Ceionius Commodus** took co-equal purple at the **Castra Praetoria**! only **one person on earth** held power to sign an immediate imperial citizenship rescript (*ius Quiritium* and *restitutio natalium*) over the heads of Treasury clerks and poison-warrants alike:

**Emperor Lucius Verus**—for the price **Ceionia Fabia** had named in the dark inn at Lorium!

Lucida reached inside her belt-wallet right there behind Juturna's marble screen, counted **twenty-five heavy gold *aurei***, she always walked the Forum carrying emergency gold sewn flat along her ribs since August! silently into Phileros's dry, papery palm, and promised the remaining twenty-five at Berenice's herb-counter before dusk.

"Enjoy your Tusculan olives, Phileros," Lucida whispered, and turned north toward the Argiletum stairs at a swift, silent walk.

* * *

Seventy-eight worn pine steps above the noisy shoemakers' stalls of the Argiletum, sixty-four-year-old **Berenice**'s sixth-floor garret smelled identically of dried rue, white lead, fried onions, and rat-droppings as it had on the rainy October dusk when two barefoot fifteen-year-olds climbed out of the Subura mud.

Only Berenice herself had shrunk smaller under her motheaten wolf-pelt shawl, her single good right eye squinting sharp as a needle through tallow-smoke as twenty-year-old Lucida bolted the fir door behind her and laid **ten gold *aurei*** (1,000 HS travel-silver!) plus an Ostian mule-voucher flat on the old Hebrew tombstone grinding-slab.

"Myrtale sang to Hermippus's clerk in Ostia, Beroe," Lucida said without wasting a breath on cushions. "Vault Forty-Two at Castor holds a capital Sullan *venena* warrant naming both of us over December's green bottles, countersigned by Repentinus's Prefecture clerk to execute the night old Antoninus dies at Lorium—and Antoninus went to bed with chest-fever yesterday afternoon. Leave the white-lead pots on the shelf, take my Marsian cart waiting downstairs before the ninth hour, and go straight down to my cousin's dye-yard at Puteoli until I send Sura's thrush-sign."

Old Berenice stared down at the ten gold pieces gleaming on the carved Hebrew letters of the dead child's stone, chewed her toothless lower lip twice, and then, with the infuriating, fatal stubbornness of old women who have survived sixty-four hard winters on one square yard of floorboards, **pushed five of the gold coins back** and hobbled to her top cedar shelf instead!

"Not tonight before the Kalends, little hawk," Berenice rasped, dragging down a heavy leaded iron strong-tin where she kept thirty years of buried Syrian gold earrings and emerald-paste beads hidden behind dried squid-bones. "Gaius the Syrian perfume-factor owes me **sixteen hundred sesterces** payable cash across my counter on the third day of March! Leave Rome tonight, and that oily Sidonian thief swears I died and steals forty winters of my burial-silver! I've got the cobbler's boy down at the street-arch paid two denarii to whistle if soldiers turn off the Forum—give me six days to collect Gaius's silver on the Nones, and my mule trots out the Ostian Gate before moonrise!"

"Beroe, if Lorium sends riders before the Nones, "

"Old emperors with four Greek doctors take ten days dying of winter phlegm; my second husband took five in a cold garret!" Berenice snapped, pressing into Lucida's reluctant palm a **flat, thumb-sized lead horn-box sealed with red pitch**. "Keep your five gold pieces for your Marsians, girl, and carry *this* inside your belt-seam till April: finely ground **unslaked Puteoli quicklime mixed with dried euphorbia-resin dust**. Throw one pinch upward across a hound's or a bailiff's open eyes in a narrow doorway, and water in his eyeballs boils white blind before he draws breath to swing! My mother carried it out of Ascalon when Titus's soldiers marched south."

Lucida closed her fingers tight around the cold lead quicklime-box, looking round the tiny fourteen-by-eleven-foot garret, at the sagging wicker screen behind which fifteen-year-old Corvus had coughed rusty blood while she held his freezing ribs against her bare skin in the dark, swearing neither plank, sickness, nor senator would ever tear them apart.

Through the unglazed dormer crack outside, freezing February twilight was turning the vast sea of Subura pantiles iron-blue toward the Caelian ridge on the south and the towering Castra Praetoria ramparts on the north-east.

Forty-five mornings left on her honest millstones.

And if old Emperor Antoninus Pius failed to hold breath for forty-five mornings—if black riders galloped down the Via Aurelia next week while Vedius's noose and Corvus's Treasury padlocks converged simultaneously on her Fish-Market arch, twenty-year-old Lucida tucked Berenice's blinding quicklime-box deep inside her belt beside her boot-knife, knowing down in the marrow of her bones that whatever burned in Rome that night, **she would not die a slave**.
