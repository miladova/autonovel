# Chapter XIX — Letters to Syria

*Sixteenth Day Before the Kalends of April, Liberalia, AD 160 · Corvus*

Chian mastic wax did not smell like honest Italian beeswax when held near a bronze lamp-flame; it melted pale pistachio-green and gave off a sharp, medicinal breath of crushed terebinth pine that clung to a man's cuticles long after he washed.

Inside **Senator Quintus Servilius Priscus**'s inner Caelian *tablinum* at the second watch of the night, nineteen-year-old Corvus held a pierced brass cipher-plate steady over a sheet of thin Egyptian *Augusta* papyrus while **Phileros** squinted through watering, bloodshot eyes two inches from the bronze lamp-spout, reading out numbered Greek letter-pairs from Priscus's private wax draft.

"Row Four, Column Seven: *omicron, kappa, tau, omega*," Phileros murmured hoarsely, rubbing his inflamed left eyelid with a knuckle. Forty years of shaving midnight tablets had finally begun clouding the Greek archive-master's sight since winter, which was why tonight, for the first time in three and a half years, Senator Priscus had ordered Corvus behind the bolted double cedar doors after every household slave had been locked into the lower ergastulum.

At the head of the scarred oak table sat sixty-three-year-old Priscus himself, wrapped in an unbleached woollen night-mantle against the March damp, chewing dry fennel seeds one after another while three sealed dispatches from Antioch lay open beside his elbow.

Corvus dipped his split reed cleanly into gall-and-lampblack ink and wrote the shifted Greek characters through the thirty-six rectangular slots of the brass grille without asking what the words meant—until his own brain, fluent in every twist of Chancery Greek since seventeen, decoded the sentence underneath his moving nib whether he wished to read it or not:

*To Gaius Terentius Scaurianus, Senior Tribune of the Third Gallica at Raphaneae, and Friends of the Old House beside the Orontes: Greeting. The aged vine at Lorium sheds bark daily and will scarce endure a second frost. On the night when two seals arrive from Rome—one bearing purple for both brothers out of fear of the Camp, one bearing the green terebinth ring—administer the military sacramentum of the three Syrian legions solely in the single name of Marcus Aurelius Antoninus Augustus. Grant neither corn-warrant, legionary eagle, nordonative promise to any messenger arriving in the name of Lucius Ceionius, lest Eastern armies divide what Hadrian left crooked.*

Corvus's reed nib did not pause, blot, or scratch loud across the papyrus fiber; only the muscle along his jaw tightened half a millimeter in the bronze lamp-glow.

Two years ago on the Caelian he had copied boundary-suits; last summer he had torn illegal seventh-floor garrets off Subura tenements; tonight he was writing **high treason**, or supreme statecraft, depending entirely upon which Emperor wore purple forty-eight hours after old Antoninus Pius stopped breathing!

Down on the marble Palatine fifty yards west of the Forum, **Marcus Caesar** himself almost certainly knew nothing of this letter; Marcus was too scrupulous, too genuinely fond of his careless younger brother Lucius to conspire behind closed shutters against him. Instead **Priscus** and **Junius Rusticus**'s hard-eyed senatorial faction—watching **Vologases IV of Parthia** already massing heavy armored cataphracts along the Armenian border while **Cornelius Repentinus** (who owed his Praetorian Prefecture to Antoninus's concubine Lysistrata and drank wine with Lucius's sporting friends!) commanded twelve cohorts of Praetorian swords inside Rome, were building an iron wall across the Eastern legions ahead of time so that even if the Praetorian Guard forced Marcus to share titles at home, **not one single Syrian legion** would obey Lucius Commodus's seal!

"Last line," Phileros whispered, sliding the wax draft an inch closer to the flame. "*Burn the outer cover; keep the inner leaf against comparison of rings. Vale.*"

Corvus lifted the pierced brass grille off the papyrus, blew lightly across the wet Greek letters rather than dusting sand that might smudge the fine strokes, and transcribed a **duplicate check-copy** onto a razor-thin leaf of shaved lime-wood bark (*philyra*)—standard Roman intelligence practice when sending couriers across the sea to Seleucia Pieria, so that when an answer returned six months later, its numeric key could be verified word for word against the sender's retained copy.

Priscus reached out with blue-veined fingers, took the bronze signet carved with a tiny fruiting terebinth tree off a chain beneath his tunic, not his public Servilius family ring, a private factional seal—held a stick of pale green Chian mastic wax into the lamp-flame until sweet pine-resin smoked, and pressed the terebinth deep into the green pool sealing the Antioch papyrus.

Then Priscus did not lock the thin lime-bark duplicate inside his own iron-bound cedar *armarium* behind the consular masks.

Instead he folded the bark leaf inside a flat oilskin wallet sealed at one corner with a second drop of pale green mastic, and pushed it straight across the oak table into Corvus's scarred right hand.

"Lock that inside your private strongbox at the Temple of Castor tomorrow morning alongside your own deposit-tablets, Lucius Cassius," Priscus said quietly.

Corvus looked up from the green-sealed oilskin into his patron's slate-grey eyes. Even Phileros blinked once in the dimness; Phileros's own box had held every secret of the Servilius house for thirty years.

"If Lollius Urbicus dies of this dropsy before midsummer," Priscus explained without emotion, as though discussing storage of winter turnips, "Cornelius Repentinus and **Ceionia Fabia**'s Caelian friends will spend three months hunting excuses to put urban search-warrants through my *tablinum* and Rusticus's Tusculan library while old Antoninus naps at Lorium. Nobody searches the Castor strongbox of a nineteen-year-old equestrian pleader from Sabinum whose name sits on maritime bottomry suits. Keep that bark leaf dry until the night Lorium sends black riders; bring it back to my hand unbroken when Marcus enters the Curia, and I will add **fifty thousand sesterces** out of my Tusculan timber-sales toward your equestrian census the morning you put on the narrow stripe."

Fifty thousand sesterces, on top of the **one hundred sixty-two thousand sesterces** Corvus already held clear at Castor after winter settlements! Two hundred twelve thousand in sight before twenty, past the halfway mark to the immortal four-hundred-thousand-sesterce line where no magistrate's iron spike could ever touch his wrists!

And inside the same oilskin wallet rode enough green-waxed Greek explosive to send Quintus Servilius Priscus—and nineteen-year-old Lucius Cassius Corvus beside him, straight down the Gemonian Steps with iron hooks in their throats if Lucius Ceionius Commodus ever read five words of it.

"It won't see daylight until you ask for it, Senator," Corvus said evenly, sliding the thin oilskin packet inside the secret inner leather lining of his dispatch-satchel.

* * *

From the Caelian ridge Corvus walked north across the dark saddle of the Subura—kept clear of cutthroats for him now by his own height, heavy oak walking-staff, and the fact that half the *vigiles* patrols between the Carinae and Viminal saluted Urban Commissioner Cassius on sight, climbing the steep paved ramp up onto the **Quirinal Hill** where **Tullia Crispina** kept her town *domus* through the spring court-term.

It was past the third watch horn; frost-rimed March stars glittered sharp above the marble roof of Quirinus's temple, and Corvus's mind was still humming cold and tight from the Antioch cipher-sheet riding against his ribs. Crispina's steward had sent a runner at dusk asking him to stop by however late to witness the renewed spring Alexandria bottomry powers before her Puteoli factor rode south at cockcrow.

Inside Crispina's warm, myrrh-scented atrium, the sleepy night-porter knew Corvus so well after twelve months that he merely nodded toward the lighted rear *cenatio* without rising off his stool to announce him.

Walking noiselessly in damp leather soles down the carpeted mosaic corridor toward the half-drawn crimson silk *velum* closing off the small winter dining-room, Corvus heard two lazy male voices laughing over dice-cups inside—Crispina's spendthrift equestrian cousins **Sextus Tullius** and **Numerius Balbus**, up from Baiae for next week's Quinquatria gladiator shows, and halted half a pace outside the curtain fold to wait until servants cleared their cups rather than walking in uninvited with street-mud on his cloak.

"Sixty-four thousand clear profit off three Alexandria hulls since September, Crispina!" Numerius Balbus was crowing thickly over the clink of silver ladles. "Everybody at the Baiae baths swears that fierce young Sabine *cognitor* you dug out of Priscus's kennel has teeth like a moray eel! Tell the truth among cousins now—he's nineteen, broad in the loins as a Samnite murmillo, already past a hundred and fifty thousand sesterces on his own slate, and living in your pocket four nights a month. Don't tell us you mean to top up his four hundred thousand next year and marry a country surveyor's boy!"

Outside in the dark mosaic passage, Corvus stood completely motionless, right hand resting still on the leather strap of his satchel.

Inside the warm crimson curtain **Tullia Crispina** laughed, that low, smoky, musical Campanian laugh Corvus knew against his own throat in the dark at Laurentum—followed by the soft chime of her six green blown-glass bracelets as she set down her wine-cup.

"Marry a Crustumina ditch-surveyor's orphan whose fingernails still smell faintly of Subura brick-lime when it rains?" Crispina purred in amused, affectionate derision, as a woman praised a favorite mastiff to dinner-guests. "Have another cup of Falernian before your wits curdle, Numerius! One doesn't put a marriage-contract around a hungry plebeian hound, marriage gives a man half the keys to the Puteoli warehouses and airs at my father's table! No: you toss a clever nineteen-year-old boy twenty-five-percent bones off the disputed charters so he tears your enemies' factors to bloody ribbons, let him warm the couch when winter wind blows cold off the sea, and persuade him next month to lock his hundred and sixty thousand sesterces inside Senator Priscus's ten-year Tusculan land-syndicate where he can't draw it out without our signatures! That way even when he buys his pretty little gold ring at twenty-two, he stays neatly on the leash carrying *our* scroll-boxes!"

Both Baian cousins roared delighted approval; ivory dice rattled across citrus-wood; chairs scraped back as slaves brought torches to light the two drunken equestrians out through the side garden postern toward their litters.

Corvus did not step back down the hall, did not clench his jaw, and did not let a single breath quicken inside his chest.

Standing in the shadow behind a marble bust of Crispina's Puteoli grandfather while the garden postern clanged shut behind her cousins, nineteen-year-old Corvus felt no heartbreak at all—because heartbreak belonged to boys who had brought their hearts into the room, and Corvus had left his heart down beside a snowy Fish-Market oven eating burned Sabine figs three months ago.

What filled Corvus's veins instead was pure, glacial, razor-edged clarity.

*Lock his hundred and sixty thousand inside Priscus's ten-year land-syndicate so even with a gold ring he stays neatly on the leash.*

All of them, austere Stoic **Quintus Servilius Priscus** handing him treasonous Syrian green-wax letters worth fifty thousand sesterces, sleek Campanian **Tullia Crispina** opening her thighs and counting-house at twenty-five percent, kindly moralizing **Junius Rusticus** looking sorrowfully at his sandals—every single man and woman above the four-hundred-thousand-sesterce wall saw nineteen-year-old **Lucius Cassius Corvus** as a useful iron tool to pry open dirty locks while keeping their own ringed hands clean!

Very well. Tools made of Noric iron had no obligation to warn soft thumbs before cutting backward.

Corvus walked calmly forward, parted the crimson silk *velum* with two steady fingers, and stepped into the warm lamp-lit *cenatio*.

* * *

Tullia Crispina reclined alone on the wide purple-draped dining *lectus* in a loose saffron-yellow evening tunic pinned at one bare white shoulder with cameo gold, picking candied Damascus plums out of a silver shell. Seeing Corvus step out of the passage tall and dry-eyed, she smiled warmly, without the faintest suspicion that he had arrived ninety breaths before her cousins' torches cleared the garden, and patted the cushioned place beside her hip.

"There comes my tireless night-owl!" Crispina murmured, holding out the waxed spring Alexandria power-of-attorney roll from the side-table. "You missed my tedious cousin Numerius by three breaths—Jupiter be praised, two hours of Baian horse-gossip gave me a temple-ache. Sign the four Ostian charter-warrants while the wax is soft, darling, and then come drink half a cup of warm spiced Setine; Senator Priscus's steward sent word today about a marvellous ten-year Tusculan land-trust where you and I can pool two hundred thousand sesterces at eight percent starting in April!"

Corvus walked unhurriedly to the citrus-wood table, set his leather dispatch-satchel, with Priscus's green-sealed Syrian bark-leaf locked safely inside—down beside the bronze lamp-stand, read every single Latin word of the four spring Alexandria maritime warrants twice to make sure no hidden tie-in clauses sat buried in the boilerplate, signed *L · CASSIVS · C · F · CRVST · CORVVS* in crisp black ink, and stamped his bronze crow-and-plumbline ring.

"My hundred and sixty-two thousand stays in coined silver and short thirty-day Ostian grain-bills inside Castor's vault through next spring, Domina," Corvus said pleasantly, wiping his nib clean on a linen rag without looking up. "Ten-year Tusculan dirt moves too slowly when port defaults pay quadruple."

Crispina's dark painted brows drew together half a millimeter, unused to having soft pillow-baits declined quite so briskly—then smoothed out as she slid barefoot off the couch, green glass bracelets chiming along her wrists, and came up behind his shoulder, sliding both lemon-verbena-scented palms flat down the front of his coarse woollen tunic from chest to groin.

"Always counting thirty-day hulls, little crow," she whispered against the side of his neck, kissing the hard tendon below his ear while her fingers unfastened his leather belt-buckle with practiced ease. "Put the pen down before you turn into Phileros at twenty. Leave your cloak, stay till cockcrow."

Corvus turned smoothly inside the circle of her arms and looked down four inches into her dark, self-satisfied Campanian eyes.

Twelve months ago on the stormy Laurentine shore he had entered her couch half-grateful, half-dazzled that an equestrian woman worth two million sesterces wanted his eighteen-year-old country hands on her skin.

Tonight he caught the single cameo gold pin at her right shoulder between his scarred thumb and forefinger, snapped the clasp open so the saffron silk dropped straight to her ankles around her bare feet, and backed her two steps flat against the high cushioned head-rest of the purple dining-couch without kissing her lips once.

Crispina gasped—part surprise at the sudden silent force in his wrists, part sharp dark excitement, as Corvus shoved his own tunic up off his shoulders, kicked the fallen silk clear, and bent her forward over the piled embroidered cushions right beside the silver plum-dishes instead of lying beneath her as she liked to direct!

"Lucius—" she started breathlessly, twisting her dark head halfway back over her bare white shoulder with an uncertain laugh as his wide, calloused left palm planted flat between her shoulder-blades holding her chest down into the purple silk while his right hand parted her oiled thighs from behind, 

And broke off into a sharp, ragged cry into the bolster as he drove straight inside her to the hilt in one hard, unsparing stroke!

No boyish haste tonight, and not one grain of tenderness: nineteen-year-old Corvus held both white curves of her equestrian hips locked inside bricklayer's hands that didn't bruise and couldn't be shaken loose, setting a relentless, deep, measured pace that made the bronze lamps tremble in their chains and the six green glass bracelets slide clashing up and down Crispina's outstretched wrists as she clutched the carved tortoiseshell couch-head! Every patronizing word he had just heard through crimson silk—*subura brick-lime, hungry plebeian hound, scraps under the couch*, burned cold and silent behind his unblinking grey-green eyes without ever once making him lose control or raise his voice; he reached round under her belly with his scarred right thumb, working her slick swollen flesh with surgical, pitiless precision while driving deep from behind until Crispina lost every ounce of smooth Campanian poise—sobbing broken Puteoli Greek filth into the crushed purple cushions, nails tearing threads out of her own expensive cushion as two violent, convulsive shudders wrenched through her thighs before Corvus buried himself one final crushing stroke deep, jaw locked silent without a groan, spending hot inside her while staring straight over her bent bare spine at his own leather satchel by the lamp.

When he stepped back off the couch ten breaths later and pulled his rough grey woollen tunic cleanly back down over his shoulders, Crispina lay sprawled sideways across the rumpled purple silk catching her breath, cheeks flushed dark crimson, heavy hair tangled wild across her damp mouth, looking up at him through half-dazed dark lashes with the first trace of genuine wariness she had ever shown him.

"Where are you going at the fourth watch?" she asked huskily, pushing wet hair off her forehead with a trembling braceleted wrist as Corvus buckled his belt and slung his satchel across his chest. "The rain's starting outside."

Corvus reached into his belt-pouch, drew out the heavy bronze Laconian latch-key to her Quirinal garden postern that she had given him last autumn so he could come and go without waking porters, and set it down with a quiet metallic *chink* squarely in the middle of her silver plum-tray.

"My Ostian factor-clerks open the Porticus Minucia rolls at first dawn, Domina," Corvus said, his voice courteous, calm, and thirty miles away. "Have your steward send April's twenty-five-percent settlement tally round to my Carinae office by notary; my docket is full until summer."

Crispina stared from the bronze garden-key gleaming among the sticky plum-stones up into his flat grey-green eyes—understanding in one silent flash, as a sharp Puteoli merchant's daughter always understood a closed ledger, that whatever nineteen-year-old boy had walked down to Laurentum last spring would never cross her bedroom threshold again.

"Be careful how high you fly on wax wings, Lucius Cassius," Crispina said coldly, pulling a purple coverlet up across her bare breasts. "Rome melts clever boys faster than sea-sun."

"Not wax, Domina," Corvus said from the doorway, bowing two exact inches before letting the crimson curtain fall between them. "Brick."

Out in the freezing fourth-watch drizzle before dawn, Corvus did not turn up toward his Carinae room. He walked all the way down the slick basalt steps of the Quirinal until he stood alone beneath the towering grey *pietra gabina* vault of the **Arch of the Firewall** separating Augustus's gleaming marble Forum from the sleeping black canyon of the Subura, the exact stone arch where he and fifteen-year-old Lucida had ducked behind the severed head of the October Horse three and a half years ago.

Half a mile south through the thin cold rain, white wood-smoke was already rising steady and fragrant from **Habinnas Barba**'s and **Lucida**'s two brick oven-flues—Day Seven Hundred Forty-Seven of her thousand and ninety-five, grinding clean wheat in the dark without asking any senator, widow, or emperor for breath.

Touching the pale green Chian mastic seal hidden inside his satchel with fingers that smelled faintly of terebinth resin and rain, nineteen-year-old Corvus turned away from Crispina's hill toward the Forum vaults, counting the two hundred thirty-eight thousand sesterces still standing between his hands and the day he broke every chain in Rome.
