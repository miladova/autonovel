# Chapter XXVIII — Alpine Cheese

*Night of the Day Before the Nones to the Nones of March, AD 161 · Lucida*

Out at Lorium twelve miles west through freezing March rain, when the tribune of the Praetorian night-watch stepped beside the narrow camp-bed at the second watch to ask the Emperor for the night's password, seventy-four-year-old **Antoninus** opened fever-bright blue eyes one final time, ordered the golden household statuette of Fortune carried across the passage into **Marcus**'s bedroom, spoke a single Latin word to the soldier—***"Aequanimitas"***, and turned his face quietly toward the wall as if going to sleep.

Nobody down behind the **Forum Piscarium** heard that noble Latin noun.

Down in the smoky mud of Rome two hours past midnight, an Emperor's death arrived first as the frantic bare-heeled slap of a twelve-year-old Argiletum cobbler's apprentice skidding through black slush to hammer both bleeding fists against Habinnas Barba's bolted oak postern!

Inside the blazing courtyard, where all four Orvieto lava mills thundered steady through **Day One Thousand Sixty-One** (**thirty-four mornings left** until mid-April's citizen bronze!) and Lucida stood fully dressed in stout leather boots with Priscus's stolen Syrian bark-leaf stitched inside a flat oiled kidskin pouch directly beneath her breast-band—she shot back the bronze spy-slit herself before the boy's third blow fell.

White-faced, drenched to the bone, trembling too violently to speak straight Latin, the cobbler's boy pressed his mouth right against the iron grille:

*"Old Caesar's dead out on the coast road, Lysistrata's riders passed the gate an hour back! **One-Ear** and six men in green cloaks with four urban cohort soldiers just went up **Berenice's** seventy-eight stairs without lanterns, they threw her Hebrew stone through the sixth-floor shutter onto the cobbles, Mistress, and her wolf-pelt came down after it with blood on the fur! They're marching down the Sandaliarius cut toward your arch right now with iron crowbars!"*

For one single frozen breath between two revolutions of Number One lava stone, **sixty-four-year-old Berenice**—chewing roasted pine-nuts with one milky eye aimed at rafters, sliding hot goat's-fat across fifteen-year-old Corvus's torn shin, pressing Ascalon quicklime into Lucida's palm forty-eight hours too proud to leave sixteen hundred sesterces behind, died behind Lucida's unblinking amber eyes without Lucida daring to shed one drop of salt before the axes hit her gate.

She shoved two silver denarii through the spy-slit into the terrified cobbler-boy's fingers, *"Run across the Forum Boarium and hide in your aunt's loft till noon!"*—slammed the bronze plate shut, and whirled into the red cresset-lit courtyard.

"Marsians, unhook the four mules from the sweep-bars and drive them into the back stone stall behind the bar!" Lucida barked, her voice cutting clean as a legionary trumpet over the deep *grrrm* of grinding stone. "Sura, grab the waterproof oilskin ledger-bag, two wool cloaks, and unbolt the loft roof-hatch onto the fuller's pantiles! Barba—up the brick ladder right this heartbeat ahead of me, Scaeva's thirty paces up the lane with Repentinus's warrant and Berenice is dead!"

Four burly Marsian kneaders dropped wooden dough-paddles instantly, snatching oak carrying-poles out of wall-racks as seventeen-year-old **Sura** snatched the oiled ledger-sack off its peg and drew her curved six-inch steel baker's dough-scraper knife from her apron sheath.

Only sixty-five-year-old **Habinnas Barba** did not move one step toward the outside brick ladder.

Standing between the white-hot roaring mouth of Oven One and the narrow six-foot vaulted stone passage connecting the front street-postern to the inner mill-yard, the old one-eyed Cappadocian soldier-baker wiped a thin trickle of rusty pink foam off his grey moustache with the back of his forearm, planted both flour-white boots wide on the basalt flags he had swept for twenty-six winters, and lifted his **eight-foot, twenty-pound solid iron oven-peel** straight out of the glowing red coal-bed!

The broad twelve-inch flat iron blade at the end of the shaft glowed dull cherry-orange along both edges, hissing fiercely as stray drops of courtyard rain hit hot metal.

"Four wet Subura roofs between here and the Carinae steps, little Tyrian," Barba wheezed, and for the first time all winter his solitary black eye shone clear and steady, stripped of pain, back at thirty years old holding a Cappadocian mountain pass under Arrian's eagles. "Put my sixty-five-year-old lead lungs on slippery rain-tiles behind you and Sura, and Scaeva's hounds catch all three of us before the second gutter! That front passage is five feet ten inches wide, one iron shovel holds six bastards off that ladder for two hundred breaths while you clear the fullery ridge!"

"Habinnas, drop the peel and climb—that's an order from your co-*exercitor*!" Lucida shouted fiercely, lunging back across the yard to grab his flour-stiffened sheepskin sleeve, 

*CRASH!*

Outside on the Fish-Market lane, a two-man iron-shod municipal felling-axe slammed square into the upper iron hinge-strap of the oak street-postern, splintering two-inch seasoned timber inward in a shower of white oak shards!

Through the jagged split echoed **Gaius Ummidius Scaeva's** harsh camp roar above rain and clanking military hobnails:

*"Open in the name of Prefect Repentinus on capital Sullan *veneficium* warrant! Save your breath screaming for Senator Priscus's pet surveyor, Tyrian bitch, **Cassius Corvus already lodged Treasury receivership seals shutting these hoppers down himself at dusk**! Your mill-count's dead meat from both ends of the street—bring her ankles out whole and smash the stones!"*

*Cassius Corvus already lodged Treasury receivership seals shutting these hoppers down himself at dusk!*

Scaeva had heard half a twisted clerk's leak off Quaestor Caecilius's preliminary docket-entry from afternoon, flinging Corvus's name through the splintered oak like a poisoned javelin purely to freeze defenders' hands, and down in the red oven-glare **both Habinnas Barba and Lucida heard every word**!

*CRASH! CRACK!*

The lower iron drop-bar socket sheared clean out of crumbling travertine mortar; the splintered oak gate burst inward off one hinge; and into the narrow five-foot-ten vaulted stone throat poured **four helmeted urban *stationarii* carrying hide shields and ash clubs** right behind **two bull-necked Cappadocian point-men** and **Scaeva's scarred mastiff-handler leading a hundred-pound brindled Molossian war-hound** straining silent and murderous on an iron chain!

Behind them in the rain-blurred torch-mouth towered **one-eared Scaeva** himself, swinging his leaded vine-wood *vitis* cane and pointing straight past Oven One at twenty-year-old Lucida!

"Loose the dog on the dark one—don't kill her till Hermippus gets Dorcas's safe-keys!" Scaeva yelled!

The handler dropped the iron chain-pin; hundred-pound brindled muscle launched in three silent, terrifying bounds down the wet flags straight for Lucida's throat while both Cappadocian point-men charged shoulder-to-shoulder behind it!

Lucida didn't step back; thumb already snapping the red pitch seal off **old Berenice's flat lead Ascalon horn-box**, she waited until the leaping Molossian mastiff and both charging Cappadocians were six feet out of the passage wind, dropped onto one knee beneath the dog's jaws, and **flung two full ounces of dry, unslaked Puteoli quicklime and caustic euphorbia-resin upward in a tight white fan straight across all three pairs of open eyes**!

Wet eyeballs and throat-mucus turned instant boiling white lye on contact!

The hundred-pound Molossian crashed sideways mid-leap snapping blind at empty air as foaming white lime burned its corneas shut; both Cappadocian point-men dropped their iron crowbars, clawing wildly at blistering, steaming eye-sockets with shrieks high enough to curdle blood—blocking the narrow passage mouth with writhing bodies!

Furious mastiff-handler **Bato** vaulted over his blinded dog swinging a short iron-studded billhook straight down at Lucida's unguarded neck while she knelt, 

And out from beneath the shadow of Number One volcanic lava mill darted **seventeen-year-old mute Sura**, silent as river-mist!

Driving her razor-honed six-inch curved steel dough-scraper horizontally with both scarred hands straight across the taut hamstring tendons behind Bato's bare right knee, Sura **severed tendon to the bone** without making a sound! Bato pitched screaming face-first onto the stone kneading-trough, splitting his own forehead open on volcanic basalt while two of Lucida's brave Marsian kneaders swung heavy oak carrying-poles from the side, cracking an urban *stationarius*'s shield-arm before two more armored soldiers and Scaeva himself surged over the blinded men with iron crowbars and short-swords half-drawn!

"UP THE LADDER, GIRL, NOW!" roared sixty-five-year-old **Habinnas Barba**!

Before Lucida could lunge forward with her five-inch boot-knife against four armored men, old Barba caught the back of her woollen belt in one tremendous, flour-crusted left fist, hurled her bodily six feet backward onto the bottom brick steps beside waiting Sura, and stepped squarely into the narrow stone gap between Oven One and Number One lava mill—**swinging twenty pounds of cherry-hot eight-foot iron oven-peel in a whistling horizontal scythe chest-high across the arch**!

*WHANG, HSSSSSS!*

Glowing twelve-inch flat iron caught the lead *stationarius*'s hide shield square, charring leather in an explosion of stinking black smoke and knocking two soldiers flat back over groaning Bato!

"Come eat Cappadocian bread, Smyrniot whoresons!" Barba bellowed through bloody pink froth flying off his white beard, hooking a panful of live white-hot oak coals out of Oven One's mouth on the back-swing and showering three square yards of blazing orange embers straight across Scaeva's shins and cloak! "Climb, Lucida, by your mother's salt, if they catch you on my floor I curse your urn from Hades! CLIMB!"

Two Marsian kneaders grabbed Lucida under both elbows—she fighting for one ragged heartbeat like a madwoman to pull free as four soldiers with heavy iron crowbars rushed Barba from two angles at once while his lungs hitched in a terrible, strangling cough! and hauled her and Sura bodily up the ten outside brick steps through the loft door, slamming and wedging the oak stair-door with two hundred-pound flour-chests from inside while Sura shoved open the narrow wooden **skylight roof-hatch** above the rafters!

Freezing March rain hit Lucida's face as she hauled Sura out onto the sloped, mossy terracotta pantiles forty feet above the black alley, then paused flat on her belly for **three terrible heartbeats** along the wet clay ridge-coping directly above the open central courtyard opening, staring straight down through driving dark rain into the red pit of her life below!

Down on the flour-white basalt flags, sixty-five-year-old **Habinnas Barba** had taken an iron crowbar across his right forearm, dropping the heavy iron peel; even one-handed, he had snatched up a wooden *modius* measure to swing at **Scaeva**—until two soldiers drove iron-shod clubs together straight into the left side of Barba's barrel chest, **crushing old ribs inward with a dull wet thud audible forty feet up through the rain**, slamming his white head hard against the grey Orvieto stone of Number One mill!

While Barba lay gasping bright crimson foam across spilled white Sicilian flour, Scaeva wiped scorched ember-holes off his brown cloak, spat onto the old baker's chest, and barked at his four crowbar-men:

*"Drive iron wedges straight between all four upper *catilli* and crack the lava collars! Tip every rising trough into the sewer-drain and nail Prefect Repentinus's lead seal, plus young Cassius's Treasury tag! across both oven doors! Let's see her bake a hundred *modii* before midnight out of broken gravel!"*

*CLANG. CRACK.*

Six-pound sledges drove cold iron wedges deep into forty-year-old Orvieto volcanic stone, splitting Number One and Number Two hourglass bells clean down their flanks with twin thunderbolt cracks; three hundred pounds of warm, living white Sicilian bread-dough tipped sluggishly out of overturned stone troughs into black rainwater and blood swirling toward the gutter; and above Oven One, an urban soldier swung the butt of his club sideways across **Sura's black wall-slate**, smashing **ANNVS III · DIES CCCXXXI — MODII CXIV** (1,061 unbroken days, thirty-four sunrises from Roman liberty!) into ten wet black fragments in the mud!

"Check the upper loft, they went over the fullery roof!" Scaeva's voice roared below as axes bit into the wedged loft door.

Up on the slick, freezing terracotta pantiles, seventeen-year-old Sura clamped one scarred, trembling hand hard over her own mouth so no sob escaped her throat, tears washing down through rain as she tugged with frantic silent fingers at Lucida's soaked hem.

Twenty-year-old Lucida did not make a sound.

Lying flat on freezing clay forty feet above her shattered stones, blood from scraping wet tile-edges blackening under all ten fingernails just as Mousa's blood had blackened under her fifteen-year-old nails on the afternoon of the October Horse, she watched two soldiers drag unconscious, bleeding **Habinnas Barba** by both heels toward the waiting Prefecture mule-wagon, still breathing shallow pink froth, taken alive as hostage to pull Lucida into the cells, while in her ears echoed Scaeva's shouted poison:

*Cassius Corvus already lodged Treasury receivership seals shutting these hoppers down himself at dusk!*

Three years of waking in pitch dark at the third watch. One thousand and sixty-one mornings without missing a single pound of clean wheat. Old Berenice strangled across a child's Hebrew gravestone; Habinnas Barba's ribs crushed beside his split lava stones; thirty-four remaining mornings smashed to wet black slate in the gutter, while **Lucius Cassius Corvus** put on his gold equestrian ring across the Forum on her patron's bones.

Lucida pressed one cold palm flat against the front of her soaked woollen bodice, feeling crisp, unbroken, dry inside oiled kidskin right over her steady, glacial heart **the thin shaved lime-bark leaf of Senator Quintus Servilius Priscus's Syrian treason, written in Corvus's own neat black hand**.

"Move your feet along the ridge-tiles, little thrush, heels sidewise on the mortar-overlap just like we practiced," Lucida whispered against Sura's wet flax ear, her voice devoid of tears, devoid of hurry, colder than the March rain sweeping out of the dark north-east from the **Castra Praetoria**. "Hold my belt. Before sunrise we buy four legions and an Emperor."

* * *

Across four interconnected tenement roofs above the sleeping Subura they moved like wet river-cats through pitch-black rain: straddling narrow brick party-walls sixty feet above reeking fullers' vats, dropping twelve feet down a wooden laundry-drying scaffold onto the outside stone stair of the **Carinae** retaining wall, and slipping barefoot—Lucida had sliced her heavy baker's boots off on the second mossy gable so leather soles wouldn't skid off glazed clay, straight up the steep, dark laurel-lined lane of the **Clivus Scauri**.

Not once did Lucida glance across the wet black garden wall on her left where **Senator Quintus Servilius Priscus's** portico lanterns already burned pale behind closed shutters, and where twenty-year-old **Corvus** was doubtless arraying his clean white citizen wool for dawn.

Two hundred paces higher up the Caelian crest, four mounted couriers wearing **Praetorian Prefect Repentinus's** dark oilskin camp-cloaks already stood holding bridles in the sleet outside **Ceionia Fabia's** bronze garden postern.

When Lucida struck the bronze lion-head ring three fast times and one slow, the emergency night-knock **Claudia Dorcas** had taught her after Lorium—the spy-plate snapped back at once; grey-haired Dorcas took one look at twenty-year-old Lucida standing barefoot on freezing travertine with blood-streaked palms, soaked wool plastered to her ribs, and shivering mute Sura behind her shoulder, and swung the heavy oak wicket wide without wasting a syllable.

Inside the heated marble peristyle, lamps burned in every niche; thirty-three-year-old **Ceionia Fabia** stood fully dressed in deep mourning violet beside a steaming silver brazier, reading a wet Lorium dispatch-tablet while two Bithynian grooms strapped saddle-bags outside.

"Scaeva struck three hours ahead of his orders," Fabia said sharply, dark imperial eyes sweeping down from Lucida's drenched braids to her bleeding bare soles printing red water across white Luna marble. "Vedius Pollio bribed Repentinus's junior night-clerk before my brother Lucius even left Lorium's gate. Did Scaeva take your ledgers?"

"Scaeva took sixty-five-year-old Habinnas Barba with crushed ribs into the Lautumiae cells after murdering old Berenice on her stairs and splitting all four of my Orvieto stones on Day One Thousand Sixty-One," Lucida said, standing straight as an iron spear in dripping Subura rags on Caesar's sister's marble floor, refusing the goblet of hot spiced wine Dorcas held out until Dorcas wrapped a thick dry wolf-fur cloak around trembling Sura's shoulders first. "Tell Prefect Repentinus's rider right now to order the Lautumiae jailer not to lay one finger on old Habinnas Barba before noon, or what I brought inside my bodice goes straight into that brazier unread."

Fabia's nostrils flared once at hearing conditions spoken under her own roof; then she looked into Lucida's black-ringed, unblinking amber eyes, turned half an inch to Repentinus's waiting *optio* by the door, and snapped: *"Send word to the Lautumiae ward: keep the old Cappadocian baker alive till evening on pain of the vine-rod. Go!"*

Only when the soldier's hobnails clattered out into the rain did twenty-year-old Lucida draw her small boot-knife, slit the wet linen thread inside her soaked bodice, and hold up between two blood-rimmed fingers—without letting Fabia touch it yet! **the tiny oiled kidskin square stamped underneath with pale green Chian mastic**.

"Antoninus is dead," Lucida said quietly. "Tomorrow morning in the Senate your brother **Lucius** either takes equal purple beside Marcus, or Quintus Servilius Priscus locks Antioch's three legions behind Marcus's private ring before Lucius ever smells salt water. Give me clean ink and dry papyrus until noon to write out Priscus's five-place Greek shift, have dry Syrian silk ready by the ninth hour, and take me inside the **Castra Praetoria** behind twelve spears when your brother rides to the Guard. I don't hand this bark to stewards; I lay it in **Augustus's** own palm."

For three silent breaths Hadrian's heir's daughter stared at the barefoot, bleeding twenty-year-old baker standing on her marble, seeing every remnant of Subura mercy burned clean out of the girl's face, leaving something cold, imperial, and terrifyingly familiar behind it.

"Dorcas," Ceionia Fabia said softly, "heat rose-water in the corner bath and open my Coan silk chest. Tonight my brother dines with two Caesars' purple on his shoulders—and you, daughter of Tyria, walk out of that Camp as freeborn as I am."
