# Chapter XIII — The Hexagonal Basin

*Seventh Day Before the Ides of March, AD 159 · Corvus*

Seen from the upper brick gallery of Trajan's grain-storehouse, the sea did not end at the shore; a dead emperor had cut six straight stone sides around two thousand feet of captive green water and locked the Mediterranean inside a ledger.

Eighteen-year-old Corvus braced both forearms on the salt-crusted travertine parapet, dark woollen riding-cloak snapping round his calves in the sharp March sea-wind, counting the hulls moored stern-first along Side Four of the **Portus Traiani**.

Four days ago on the *Navigium Isidis*, priests in shaved skulls and white linen had launched their gilded cedar model barque into the surf outside the Ostian lighthouse to declare winter's closed sea open again. Already forty deep-bellied freighters that had gambled an early February crossing from Alexandria, Leptis Magna, and Lilybaeum crowded the six-sided basin hull to hull, painted wooden eyes staring salt-streaked off curved swan-sterns while two-man human treadwheels creaked inside towering timber dock-cranes, swinging fat net-slings of wheat sacks out of dark hatches onto numbered limestone quays.

Every pier-stone carried its carved Roman numeral; every mooring-ring had its sworn harbor clerk; every gangplank swarmed with half-naked dock-porters (*saccarii*) trotting bent double under hundred-pound Egyptian linen sacks, dropping one brass tally-token into an overseer's wooden box at the foot of the plank before vanishing into shadowed, six-foot-thick brick warehouse vaults that stretched half a mile toward the Tiber canal.

Beside Corvus on the gallery, thirty-two-year-old **Tullia Crispina** pulled her sea-green woollen *palla* tighter under her chin, six blown-glass Campanian bracelets clinking pale green at her narrow wrists like harbor ice.

"My late husband Gnaeus used to stand on this exact stone and weep sentimental Campanian tears over the majesty of Caesar's grain-fleet," Crispina said in her low, smoky Puteoli contralto, dark painted eyes fixed coldly on a beamy three-masted freighter—the *Isis Pharia* out of Alexandria, berthed directly below Pier Fourteen. "Then Gnaeus died of a surfeit of Baian oysters last May leaving me four hundred thousand sesterces out on twelve-percent maritime bottomry notes, and I discovered half these sunburned skippers are worse thieves than Cilician pirates. Look down on the *Pharia*'s foredeck, Lucius Cassius. Why are those four *mensores* standing round the bronze drum instead of pouring?"

Corvus narrowed his grey-green eyes against the bright salt glare bouncing off the water.

Down on the *Isis Pharia*'s broad pine deck, thirty feet below, the ship's Greek master **Sarapion**—whose cargo of forty thousand *modii* of prime Fajyum hard wheat carried a hundred-and-twenty-thousand-sesterce bottomry loan (*pecunia traiecticia*) from Tullia Crispina's strongbox, was arguing heatedly with **Gaius Hostilius**, chief sworn grain-measurer of the Ostian *corpus mensorum*, while four muscular measurer's assistants leaned on their curved iron striking-rods (*rutelli*) chewing roasted chickpeas.

Senator Priscus had sent Corvus down to Portus at dawn because Priscus never dirtied his own senatorial shoes in commercial bottomry disputes—senators were forbidden by the old *Lex Claudia* to own large sea-ships openly, yet Crispina's father had backed Priscus's Hispanian legateship twenty years ago, and eighteen-year-old Corvus had won four straight commercial arbitrations since his birthday in November without dropping a single figure.

"They aren't waiting for porters," Corvus said, watching the precise angle at which Hostilius's assistant held an empty linen sack three feet *above* the iron-bound rim of the certified municipal *modius* tub before letting a test-stream of golden Egyptian wheat cascade down into the cylinder. "Watch the boy's elbows, Domina. If you pour dry wheat gently from rim-level, kernels lie loose with air between them and forty thousand honest *modii* fill forty thousand tubs. Drop wheat fast from chest-height onto a tilted side-board and tap the iron hoop once with your knee before drawing the iron *rutellus* across the top, and the grain packs eight percent denser inside the drum—turning Sarapion's forty thousand *modii* into thirty-six thousand eight hundred on the dock tally!"

Crispina turned her head sharply to look up two inches into Corvus's wind-burned face. Around her eyes tiny fan-lines crinkled through sheer, delighted appreciation; she smelled not of heavy Caelian white-lead or suffocating myrrh, of clean lemon-verbena water, cedar wool, and cold sea-spray.

"Three thousand two hundred *modii* 'shortage' at delivery," Crispina calculated instantly, bracelets chiming as she tapped two ringed fingers against the parapet, "which triggers the default forfeiture clause in Sarapion's charter-party, bankrupts my borrower before he can pay off my twelve-percent sea-note, and lets whoever bribed Gaius Hostilius buy up the distressed cargo at half-price on the Ostian steps tomorrow noon!"

"And look who is standing inside the arched doorway of Vault Nine watching Hostilius's knee tap the drum," Corvus added quietly.

Down in the brick shadow behind the weighing-beam stood a dapper Smyrniot factor wearing a green-bordered business *paenula* and carrying a silver-mounted calamarium at his belt: **Philargyrus**, harbor agent for **Publius Vedius Pollio**.

Nothing in Portus happened twice by accident.

"Stay on the dry stones if you wish, Domina, or walk two paces behind me," Corvus said, handing his heavy travel cloak to Crispina's litter-guard so he stood plain and broad-shouldered in his crisp white citizen *toga* girt up tightly *cinctu Gabino* round his waist to leave both arms free for climbing ship-planks. "And bring your notary with fresh wax."

Down the steep travertine stair Corvus went in long, unhurried five-foot surveyor's strides, Crispina following briskly in cork-soled Puteoli half-boots without needing any slave's elbow, her notary trotting behind.

Out across the springing twenty-foot fir gangplank of the *Isis Pharia* Corvus walked, sea-water slapping hollow green against pitched cypress planking underneath his calfskin soles, stepping straight onto the wheat-strewn pine foredeck where Greek skipper Sarapion was tearing his grey curls in despair before bulky **Gaius Hostilius**.

Hostilius, fifty, bull-bellied, wearing the bronze medallion of the *mensores frumentarii* guild over a grease-shining leather jerkin—scowled round as Corvus's shadow fell squarely across the brass-rimmed wooden *modius* measure.

"Clear the plank, city boy!" Hostilius barked, waving his smooth iron *rutellus* rod like a truncheon. "Official harbor measurement under port rules, no advocates on deck till the tally-board's chalked!"

"Read Paragraph Four of Hadrian's Port Regulation nailed behind your own weigh-house door, Gaius Hostilius," Corvus replied pleasantly, planting both feet wide against the slight harbor swell without giving half an inch. "A maritime bottomry creditor holding registered *hypotheca* over hull and lading inspects every tenth *modius* on deck in person or by *procurator*. Allow me to introduce **Tullia Crispina**, creditor for a hundred and twenty thousand sesterces on this hold—and myself, **Lucius Cassius Corvus**, holding Senator Quintus Servilius Priscus's warrant."

Over in the shadow of Vault Nine on the quay, Vedius's agent Philargyrus straightened abruptly out of his slouch.

Hostilius's bluster dropped half a note at Senator Priscus's name, though brazen dock-habit held his chin up. "Inspect till your eyes bleed, young master! Certified municipal *modius* stamped by the *Praefectus Annonae* in January, thirty-two *sextarii* dead true! Not my fault if this Alexandrian thief loaded wet Fajyum sweepings that shrank four thousand measures on the voyage!"

"Shrank four thousand measures in dry wind without a drop of bilge-rot?" Corvus asked mildly. He squatted on his heels beside the heavy oak-and-bronze *modius* cylinder—ignoring the angry assistant Measurer hovering at his shoulder, and ran his scarred right thumb round the inner bottom chine where wooden staves met the circular oak floor-board.

No false wooden bottom; Hostilius wasn't stupid enough to carry a rigged tub when port centurions checked depths once a week with brass rods.

Except Corvus's thumb didn't just check depth; **his surveyor's nose smelled rancid mutton-fat mixed with fine marble-dust**, and his scarred knuckle felt a smooth, bevelled ring of hardened tallow-and-chalk cement: inside *this* tub the inner chime wasn't greased, **the underside of the iron tripod cross-stay** had a fresh wooden wedge knocked loose half an inch!

Corvus looked higher—at the **heavy curved iron *rutellus* striking-rod** in Hostilius's own fist, and then at the *second* wooden *modius* tub sitting "empty" behind the assistant's calves waiting to swap in rotation!

Without asking leave, Corvus reached past the assistant's shin, tipped the second oak *modius* cylinder sideways onto its bronze hoop in the bright March sun, and rapped the center of its circular oak bottom hard from *underneath* with his brass folding rule.

*Clack.*

Underneath the impact, a thin eighth-inch crust of yellow beeswax dusted over with glued wheat-bran **popped clean off the oak floor-board**, Wait: removing inner wax *increased* volume! Hostilius had **concaved the inner oak floor-board three-eighths of an inch on a lathe** after the *Annona* inspector stamped the outer bronze rim in January, hiding the pale fresh-turned oak fibers under a wash of bran-water so every single "standard" *modius* scooped three extra handfuls of Sarapion's Egyptian wheat above legal measure—stealing eight percent across forty thousand tubs while keeping a clean unshaved spare tub beside his left boot in case a port centurion asked to gauge!

Corvus picked up the curved iron *rutellus* rod off the deck-bench and laid its straight back edge flat across the inner floor of Tub Two. Daylight shone a clear quarter-finger wide beneath the iron straight-edge across the scooped wooden belly!

On the quay crisp footsteps halted; Crispina leaned over the low bulwark rail looking down at the daylight gap under the iron bar, and laughed aloud, a low, musical sound cold enough to freeze harbor mullet.

"Well, Gaius Hostilius," Corvus said, rising smoothly to his full six feet while Sarapion's six Egyptian sailors closed silently round the gangway behind the sweating measurers with belaying-pins in their fists. "Carving out the belly of a stamped public *modius* after official sealing falls under the *senatus consultum* applying the *Lex Cornelia de Falsis* to fraudulent public weights and measures. Deportation and total confiscation if you own four hundred thousand sesterces; the Ostian quarry-chains for ten years if you don't. Would you like me to call the Port Centurion off Pier Twelve to gauge Tub Two right now—or would you rather smash Tub Two over the rail yourself as 'cracked in handling,' re-measure Sarapion's cargo from Sack One using the Port Centurion's own bronze standard drum, and tell my notary which Smyrniot factor in Vault Nine paid you ten gold pieces this morning?"

Sweat ran gray down Hostilius's bull-neck into his leather collar. Glancing wildly toward Vault Nine on the quay, he saw **only an empty brick arch**: Vedius Pollio's agent Philargyrus had already melted quietly back into the warehouse crowd the instant Corvus laid the iron straight-edge across the scooped wood!

Abandoned on the plank with six furious Alexandrian sailors behind him and Senator Priscus's name in front of him, Hostilius folded like wet cardboard.

"Ten *aurei* cash at the bathhouse last night from Philargyrus, Vedius's factor," Hostilius croaked, wiping his mouth on his forearm while his knees went slack. "Swore nobody behind Tullia Crispina's widow-veil knew grain-gauges from hair-combs! Don't call the centurion, young sir, I've got three daughters in Ostia—we'll re-tally from Sack One with the harbor bronze standard before dusk!"

Up on the sunny gangplank Tullia Crispina watched Corvus dictate six crisp lines of sworn *testatio* preserving Philargyrus's name on wax before letting terrified Hostilius smash the shaved tub into kindling. When Corvus stepped back onto the limestone quay beside her, brushing dry Fajyum wheat-chaff off his white *toga*, Crispina did not praise him like a clever schoolboy the way Fronto or Priscus did; she looked straight at his mouth and scarred right thumb with level, adult calculation.

"You just saved my chest forty-two thousand sesterces before lunch, Lucius Cassius," Crispina said quietly. "Come into my counting-room before the tavern flies wake up; I have twelve more charters inside my cedar box that smell of this same Smyrniot rat."

* * *

All afternoon inside her private second-floor brick loggia above the *Horrea*, where green sea-light flickered across black-and-white mosaic dolphins on the floor, eighteen-year-old Corvus and Tullia Crispina tore **Publius Vedius Pollio**'s coastal shipping net apart sheet by sheet over salted red mullet, olives, and dry Surrentine wine.

Unlike Caelian senatorial ladies who left numbers to sticky-fingered Greek stewards, Crispina had grown up on her father's Puteoli timber-wharves; she read compound sea-interest, Rhodian jettison averages, and warehouse lien priorities as fast as Corvus himself.

And by the tenth hour, laying twelve bottomry notes side by side across her inlaid *mensa*, Corvus saw the whole architecture of what Vedius Pollio was building down at the river-mouth:

"He isn't chasing state *annona* African wheat," Corvus said, tracing four Sicilian and Sardinian hulls with the butt of his stylus. "Nobody touches state quota grain without bringing the *Praefectus Annonae*'s soldiers down on his head. Vedius is buying up bottomry paper and bribing measurers exclusively on **medium private coastal hulls carrying free-market Sicilian and Sardinian hard wheat**, the exact independent grain supply medium Subura *pistrina* rely on whenever state allocations run twenty *modii* short of their daily hundred!"

Corvus kept his face calm over the wax, though inside his chest every nerve pulled taut. *Subura pistrina running a hundred modii a day.* Day Three Hundred Seventy-Three on Sura's slate back behind the Fish-Market today—two full years still ahead before Lucida stood safe inside Roman citizenship! Squeeze private Sicilian wheat at Ostia next autumn, and twenty independent freedmen and Junian Latin bakers in Rome would miss a single day's hundred-modius quota, forfeit their statutory runs, and drop straight into Hermippus's foreclosing lap!

"Sharp eyes," Crispina murmured, leaning across the corner of the citrus-wood table so her green glass bracelets clicked lightly against Corvus's ink-stained wrist. "Vedius offered my steward eight hundred thousand sesterces flat last month to buy out my entire maritime loan-book at twenty percent discount, claiming a widow couldn't police twenty skippers from Puteoli to Alexandria. Until the fourth hour this morning, I half intended to take his silver and buy quiet Sabine sheep-walks."

"Don't sell him a single plank," Corvus said immediately, grey-green eyes hard as river-flint. "Enforce every charter at Ostia, Puteoli, and Centumcellae with sworn private *mensores* watching the drums, and your four hundred thousand principal clears ninety thousand net before November—while starving Vedius's warehouse corners."

Crispina propped her chin on one ringed hand, dark painted lashes half-lowered as she studied the lean line of his eighteen-year-old jaw, the broad mason-and-surveyor shoulders under his loosened afternoon tunic, and the white stylus-scar at the corner of his mouth.

"Quintus Servilius Priscus pays you what, eighty sesterces a week now, plus whatever *Lex Cincia* crumbs grateful old knights drop in your lap?" Crispina asked softly.

"One hundred sesterces a week since January, plus pleading gifts," Corvus answered evenly—he had **fourteen thousand two hundred sesterces** stacked inside his Caelian box now, proud silver to a Subura clerk, less than four percent of the four-hundred-thousand-sesterce equestrian mountain he had sworn outside the Esquiline Gate to climb before twenty-one.

"Beggar's porridge for a man who spots a three-eighths-inch lathe-scoop in ten breaths," Crispina said. "My coastal villa at **Laurentum** sits six miles south down the pine-road. Ride down in my carriage before dusk so we can draft preventive *interdicta* and factor-warrants on all eleven remaining hulls tonight before Philargyrus sends fast horses to Puteoli at dawn. Sign on as my retained maritime *cognitor* and audit-partner across the summer sailing season, with Senator Priscus's blessing, naturally, I'll send him two barrels of Baian oysters on Monday—and you take **twenty-five percent of every contested recovery and penalty we collect between March and October**."

Corvus's pulse gave one heavy, slow hammer-stroke against his ribs.

Twenty-five percent on contested penalties across four hundred thousand sesterces of maritime paper, **twenty-five to forty thousand sesterces in a single sailing season**, vaulting him past fifty thousand before his nineteenth birthday while jamming an iron bar straight between Vedius Pollio's spokes at Ostia so clean Sicilian wheat kept flowing up the Tiber to Barba's and Lucida's ovens!

"My horse is saddled downstairs, Domina," Corvus said.

* * *

Six miles south of the Tiber mouth along the paved *Via Severiana* shore-track, where dark umbrella-pines leaned landward away from booming white March surf and rich senators kept winter-spring sea-retreats out of Rome's river-fog, **Tullia Crispina**'s Laurentine villa sat built straight out onto black volcanic rock ledges so high tide threw salt mist right against the warm glazed gallery windows.

Until the fifth hour of the night there was nothing between them save relentless, exhilarating work: three tall bronze oil-lamps burning clear olive oil without tallow-stink, Crispina's silent Greek secretary engrossing duplicate charter-notices in neat Chancery script while Corvus paced five-foot surveyor's lengths across warm hypocaust mosaic dictating ironclad shipping clauses out of Labeo and Julian, pausing only to eat roasted Laurentine wild boar tenderloin and salty Lucrine mussels off silver plates without ever laying down his stylus.

At midnight Crispina dismissed the Greek secretary to bed with a flick of two fingers, bolted the cedar folding-doors against the colonnade draught herself, and poured two goblets of dark, unwatered Falernian *vino vecchio* while Corvus pressed his bronze crow-and-plumbline signet—carved for six denarii last winter, into the hot red wax of their partnership *chirographum*: **twenty-five thousand sesterces minimum guaranteed audit-share** against the summer fleet, lying crisp and sealed beside the middle lamp.

Outside the tall mica panes, Tyrrhenian rollers crashed white and rhythmic against the stone sea-wall; inside, resinous pine-cones popped orange sparks inside the bronze brazier.

Crispina set the brimming silver goblet in Corvus's hand, then did not return to her chair across the citrus-wood table. Slowly she lifted both arms behind her dark coiled hair, pulled out two carved tortoiseshell pins so heavy black Campanian waves tumbled past her bare collarbones smelling of lemon-verbena and warm skin, and slid all six green glass bracelets off her wrists *clink-clink-clink* onto the polished table right beside his twenty-five-thousand-sesterce tablet.

"Eighteen in November, Phileros told my steward," Crispina murmured, standing six inches in front of him in a single unpinned sea-green Coan silk house-tunic thin enough that the red brazier-light behind her traced both dark nipples and the soft shadow between her thighs clean through the weave. "And working sixteen hours a day between dusty basilicas and some Subura baker's garret. Tell me, Lucius Cassius: do Cato's speeches keep you warm at night, or do you ever take off your armor?"

Corvus did not pretend country stupidity, and his hand holding the silver goblet did not shake—though every drop of eighteen-year-old blood in his body went hot and heavy into his groin at once.

For two and a half years inside Rome, while fullers' apprentices and junior clerks spent two copper *asses* every market-day grunting five breaths inside airless Subura *lupanar* cribs smelling of sour cabbage and twenty prior men's sweat—Corvus had touched no woman on earth, partly from iron miserliness over every copper coin, mostly because every time desire woke ragged in the dark above Barba's ovens, the face behind his shut eyelids had dark braids dusted with white *siligo* flour and a tiny brown olive-pip mark beneath her left collarbone, separated from his hand by two yards of warm terracotta tile and three unspoken mountains of fear.

Here stood no miserable two-as crib-slave, and no childhood best friend whom one clumsy touch might shatter forever: here stood a thirty-two-year-old equestrian widow worth nearly two million sesterces, smelling of lemon-leaves and salt spray, looking up at his mouth with frank, amused, utterly unsentimental adult appetite.

"Armor comes off when the door's bolted, Domina," Corvus said, voice rough in his throat, setting the untouched silver goblet down beside her green bracelets.

Crispina smiled, reached up with both smooth, ringless hands to catch the neck-hem of his white woollen tunic, and pulled his mouth down onto hers.

She tasted of dark Falernian wine, salt mussels, and smooth, practiced certainty, parting his lips immediately with her tongue, nipping lightly right over the pale white stylus-scar at the left corner of his mouth to feel whether it was tender, then murmuring *"Take the wool off before you tear my silk, Sabine"* as she stepped backward onto the wide, fleece-draped tortoiseshell reading-couch beside the brazier and drew her sea-green Coan shift straight off over her head in one fluid shimmer.

Corvus dragged his tunic off over his shoulders and dropped it on the mosaic. Thirty months of hauling brick-hods, swimming freezing aqueduct tanks, and walking twelve Roman miles of steep basalt hills every day had carved his six-foot frame lean and flat-muscled from rib-cage to hip, only the faint pale cupping-circles under his left shoulder-blade and the jagged red-turned-white trench scar down his right shin marking where Rome had tried to kill him at fifteen.

Crispina's dark eyes ran straight down his bare chest to his hard, springing cock, her breath catching once with genuine Campanian pleasure as she lay back against stacked crimson cushions and parted sleek, depilated thighs oiled faintly with sweet almond.

"Jupiter be thanked—not one ounce of soft Quirinal pudding on your ribs," she whispered, reaching down to close warm, knowing fingers around the base of his shaft, guiding him between her thighs without coyness or delay. "Come here, little crow, don't rush the harbor-mouth just because the sea's open."

When he pushed inside her slick, gripping heat for the first time in his life, a low raw groan tore involuntarily out of Corvus's chest against her throat; his scarred right hand locked into the thick black hair behind her ear while his left braced hard into the fleece mattress beside her hip. Crispina wrapped both strong, smooth calves high around the small of his back, tilting her pelvis expertly to take his full length deep while raking short manicured nails down his sweat-damp shoulder-blades, whispering filthy, laughing Campanian encouragements in Greek and Latin against his jaw whenever eighteen-year-old urgency drove him too hard and fast, slowing his hips with a bite to his earlobe until both of them were slick with sweat in the red brazier-glare.

Halfway through, she rolled him smoothly onto his back across the wide fleece—straddling his hips upright in the flickering bronze lamp-light, fuller thirty-two-year-old breasts swaying oiled and flushed above him while Corvus gripped both curves of her waist in wide, calloused hands, watching through half-blind heat how she took her own sharp, shuddering climax first with head thrown back and teeth gleaming white in the dark before leaning low over his chest, hair curtaining both their faces, driving down hard and fast onto him until his spine arched clean off the fleece and his seed spent hot and convulsive deep inside her while outside the mica panes a ninth wave boomed white thunder against the rocks.

Long afterward, when the bronze lamps had burned low to blue-gold beads on floating wicks and the brazier coals had sunk under grey ash, Crispina slept soundly turned on her side with one warm thigh flung lazily across Corvus's hip, comfortable, satisfied, owning every stone of her villa, her ships, and her morning.

Wide awake in the salt dark, eighteen-year-old Corvus lay flat on his back listening to the receding surf hiss down black Laurentine gravel.

On the citrus-wood table four feet away gleamed the six green glass bracelets beside the red-waxed contract worth **twenty-five thousand sesterces**—more silver won between dawn and midnight today than his dead father Gaius Cassius Corvus had earned trudging muddy Sabine ditches with a *groma* across forty honest years of life.

Corvus lifted his right hand in the dimness, smelled lemon-verbena and another woman's musk drying on his scarred thumb-knuckle where dry flour and river-water had been, and stared straight up at the coffered cedar ceiling without blinking until pale grey sea-light crept through the shutters, telling himself fiercely that twenty-five thousand sesterces brought the gold ring six months closer, and refusing to ask why his chest felt hollow as a scooped wooden *modius*.
