# Chapter XXX — Two Suns Over Rome

*Dusk of the Nones to Dawn of the Eighth Day Before the Ides of March, AD 161 · Lucida*

Twelve thousand iron-shod Praetorian spear-butts crashing in unison against curved oval shields shook grey rainwater off the red-brick battlements of the **Castra Praetoria** hard enough to ripple wine inside gold cups two hundred yards back inside the headquarters pavilion.

*"IMPERATORES CAESARES — MARCVS ET LVCIVS AVGVSTI — VINCANT! VINCANT!"*

Standing just inside the braided crimson velvet portiere of the *praetorium* council-hall beside **Ceionia Fabia**—washed clean of Subura roof-soot in warm rose-water, dressed in an ankle-length *stola* of unbleached Coan silk bordered with dark Tyrian purple, her blue-black braids pinned high with Fabia's six-inch emerald needle while raw crescent scrapes from last night's pantiles still burned invisible beneath her clean nails, twenty-year-old **Lucida** watched Rome divide in half under torchlight without a drop of blood spilled on marble.

Out on the high stone camp *tribunal* facing twelve massed cohorts in gleaming lorica segmentata, forty-year-old **Emperor Marcus Aurelius** stood silent half a pace back in dark mourning wool, stoop-shouldered, grey with exhaustion, visibly shrinking from the raw mercenary roar of twelve thousand swords—while beside him thirty-year-old **Emperor Lucius Verus** stood bare-headed in the torch-wind like gilded Apollo come down from Delos, flinging both sunburned arms wide as his rich, ringing baritone promised **twenty thousand sesterces, five thousand silver denarii per man!**, to every soldier on the Guard rolls!

Two hundred and forty million sesterces bought twelve thousand throats in ten heartbeats.

When the two new masters of the inhabited earth stepped back inside the heated brick council-chamber out of the wind—flanked by bull-necked **Praetorian Prefect Sextus Cornelius Repentinus** and soft-footed **Furius Victorinus**, Marcus Aurelius paused only long enough to sign the Guard treasury warrant before excusing himself back toward the Palatine to oversee dead Antoninus's embalming rites, leaving **Lucius Verus** behind at the camp map-table with Repentinus and his elder sister **Ceionia Fabia**.

The heavy oak doors swung shut behind four towering German bodyguards.

All lazy tavern warmth dropped off thirty-year-old **Lucius Verus's** handsome face the instant his adoptive brother's sandals receded down the stone corridor. Pouring himself unwatered Falernian from a silver camp pitcher without sitting down, the younger Augustus looked straight past Fabia at twenty-year-old **Lucida**.

"Vedius Pollio went down in flames at Saturn's scales before noon, stripped bare of six million sesterces by Quintus Servilius Priscus's twenty-year-old Sabine hound while I was standing in the Curia," Lucius Verus said sharply, golden curls damp with camp rain above his fresh imperial purple cloak. "Fabia tells me her Fish-Market friend climbed over four wet Subura roofs before dawn carrying something sharper than Vedius's brick-tallies. Put it on the oak, girl, before Repentinus rides to the Palatine."

Lucida stepped forward across the pegged legionary map of Armenia and Syria without lowering her eyes once before Caesar's purple.

From inside her silk belt-fold she drew two objects and laid them side by side beneath the five-nozzled bronze camp candelabrum:

First, **the razor-thin three-by-five-inch shaved lime-bark leaf (*philyra*)** lifted four nights ago out of **Lucius Cassius Corvus's** dispatch-satchel—covered in forty-two tight lines of shifted Greek letters written in Corvus's unmistakable upright secretarial hand and stamped at the bottom margin in pale green Chian mastic with **Senator Quintus Servilius Priscus's** personal terebinth ring!

Second, a clean strip of Egyptian papyrus on which Lucida had written out in clear Greek and Latin during her afternoon hours behind Fabia's bolted Caelian shutters **every word of the five-place Caesar shift**.

Praetorian Prefect **Cornelius Repentinus** bent his thick grey bull's neck over both sheets, moving one blunt scarred forefinger line by line from cipher to plain Greek, and halfway down Paragraph Two a low, ugly whistle escaped his teeth.

"Read it out, Sextus," Emperor Lucius Verus commanded quietly.

Repentinus read aloud in clipped camp Latin:

*"Quintus Servilius Priscus Consularis to Lucius Attidius Cornelianus, Legate Propraetor of Syria at Antioch, health. Should fortune compel Caesar Marcus upon Lorium's close to concede nominal purple or Eastern eagles to L. Ceionius out of domestic peace, let neither VIII Augusta's detachments nor III Gallica nor XVI Flavia Firma stir one standard beyond Zeugma upon any warrant bearing the younger hand alone, nor disburse Antioch's military chest above ordinary camp rations until counter-sealed under the inner Stoic ring known between myself and Rusticus..."*

Silence fell across the brick headquarters hall so absolute the hiss of pine-resin torches sounded loud as surf.

Thirty-year-old **Lucius Verus** stared down at the pale green terebinth impression at the foot of the shaved bark, healthy sunburn draining white around his nostrils until he looked terrifyingly like his cold sister **Fabia** standing beside him.

"Before my father's corpse was cold at Lorium," Emperor Lucius Verus whispered, setting his silver wine-cup down on the oak table hard enough to dent the rim, "Priscus had already wired Antioch's three legions to treat **my proconsular *imperium* as painted theatre-wood** while Vologases masses forty thousand Parthian horse across the Euphrates! And how is Priscus's Brundisium courier traveling tonight, Prefect?"

"Stopped at the sixth milestone on the Via Appia twenty breaths ago by four of my mounted *speculatores* on Mistress Fabia's warning, Caesar," Repentinus answered grimly, teeth showing white through his cropped iron beard. "His green-sealed pouch held **nothing inside save blank linden-splint wood**, proving beyond ten thousand oaths that *this* bark leaf sitting on your table is Priscus's genuine master-sheet, written in his sworn *cognitor* **Cassius Corvus's** hand!"

At Corvus's name, Repentinus glanced flat and murderous across the map-table: "Say one word, Caesar, and my *speculatores* haul young **Cassius Corvus** off Saturn's steps in iron chains tonight right alongside old Priscus—newly enrolled Roman Knight or not!"

Inside twenty-year-old Lucida's ribs her heart gave not a single treacherous skip; she had calculated this exact sentence three hundred breaths ahead before stepping through the Castra gate.

"Arrest twenty-year-old Cassius Corvus tonight, Prefect," Lucida said coolly, speaking straight to **Emperor Lucius Verus** before Fabia could intervene, "and **Emperor Marcus Aurelius**, whose Stoic teacher **Junius Rusticus** stands named in Line Twelve! is forced to defend both Priscus *and* the clerk publicly in the Senate tomorrow morning to shield Rusticus's honor, turning your secret trump into an open civil brawl on Day Two of your reign! Lay this bark leaf privately before your brother Marcus alone at sunrise instead: Marcus will be so ashamed of Priscus's unauthorized Eastern cabal behind his back that **Marcus himself will strip Quintus Servilius Priscus of imperial *amicitia* before noon, banish Priscus silently to his Sabine hills for life, and sign your unrestricted supreme command over every legion east of the Aegean without a single senator daring to murmur**—while leaving young Cassius Corvus alive in Rome stripped of his great patron!"

Ceionia Fabia's dark eyes gleamed with sudden, fierce pride in her pupil; **Emperor Lucius Verus** stared across the candelabrum at the twenty-year-old Subura baker's daughter for five long heartbeats, then threw back his golden head in a short, delighted bark of imperial laughter!

"By Pollux, Fabia, you told me she ground clean flour, you didn't tell me you'd found Ulpian's daughter wearing Syrian silk!" Verus exclaimed, pulling his heavy imperial signet-ring from his finger and gesturing his *ab epistulis* secretary forward with wet purple murex ink and two prepared vellum diplomas! "Priscus goes to count Sabine cabbages before noon tomorrow, and Cornelianus answers to *my* lictors! Now name your three lines, daughter of Tyria—a Caesar who just gave five thousand denarii to every spearman in Rome doesn't short-weight the hand that gave him Syria!"

"Three lines, Caesar Augustus," Lucida said clearly into the torchlight:

"First: **full unconditional Roman citizenship (*civitas Romana optimo iure*) paired with imperial *restitutio natalium***, declaring me freeborn in law as **Lucida Tyria of the Palatina tribe**, extinguishing from root to sky every shadow of Junian Latinity or *ius patronatus* ever claimed by **Publius Vedius Pollio**, his heirs, or his Treasury receiver, together with an identical citizen charter for my mute assistant **Sura**."

Verus dipped the reed pen himself into dark purple murex ink and scrawled ***Imperator Caesar Lucius Aurelius Verus Augustus subscripsi*** across both heavy calfskin charters, stamping warm imperial vermilion wax so hard red tallow squeezed round his carved sapphire ring!

"Done and enrolled before sunrise," Verus said briskly. "Second?"

"Second: burn Prefect Repentinus's copy of **Castor Vault Forty-Two's** false poison-sheet in that brazier right now—and execute your Camp warrant before midnight on **Gaius Ummidius Scaeva**, who murdered an unarmed sixty-four-year-old Argiletum herb-woman and crushed veteran **Habinnas Barba**'s ribs before dawn under forged interregnum color."

Repentinus didn't even blink, pulling Myrtale's countersigned Prefecture warrant-copy straight out of his leather breast-case to drop it curling black and hissing onto the red oak coals, while nodding dryly: "My harbor watch already took **One-Ear Scaeva** in chains two hours ago at the Ostian Gate trying to board a Tunisian grain-ship with six bags of his ruined master Vedius's silver plate. Looting during an imperial interregnum carries summary camp sword outside the Esquiline ditch before midnight; his remaining ear stays on the post."

"And third, Lucida Tyria?" Emperor Lucius Verus asked, leaning both palms on the map of Syria and Mesopotamia with keen, sunlit appetite already turning east toward Antioch, Daphne's laurel groves, and Parthian gold.

"Third, Caesar," twenty-year-old **Citizen Lucida Tyria** answered, resting one calm olive forefinger right beside **Seleucia Pieria and Antioch** on the painted legionary map, "when you sail east to take command of the Parthian war, sixty thousand soldiers and five hundred court officers will need unrotted Sicilian wheat, unwatered Cilician tent-linen, clean purple dye, and a confidential household purveyor who knows every Greek freighter-captain from Ostia to Phoenicia and **cannot be bribed by senatorial contractors**. Issue my warrant as **Purveyor-in-Ordinary to the Household of Lucius Augustus**, backed by a two-hundred-fifty-thousand-sesterce Ostian credit line, and every loaf your vanguard breaks on the Euphrates will weigh full sixteen ounces."

"Make the credit **three hundred thousand**, Repentinus—she'll save me three million on rotten army biscuit before we sight Rhodes!" Verus laughed, sliding both warm, purple-signed calfskin citizenship diplomas across the oak table straight into Lucida's hands!

Touching smooth calfskin and hot imperial vermilion wax—*CIVIS ROMANA · INGENUA RESTITUTA · LUCIDA TYRIA*—not one tear rose into twenty-year-old Lucida's dark amber eyes.

One thousand and sixty-one mornings of honest lava stone had failed thirty-four days short in blood and mud.

One stolen strip of shaved bark had bought sky, purple, and three hundred thousand sesterces in five hundred breaths.

* * *

By midnight the three-day March rain blew cleanly out to sea behind a sharp north wind off Soracte, leaving ten thousand icy winter stars blazing white above the black cypresses outside the **Porta Esquilina**.

Not far from the potter's ditch where slaves and paupers were tumbled nameless into lime, two hundred yards up the clean gravel road where **citizen guilds** burned honored dead on dry seasoned oak, blazed **two tall rectangular pyres of split Sabine holm-oak, sweet laurel, and twenty pounds of costly Syrian cinnamon-bark** paid in gold out of Citizen Lucida Tyria's purse.

Four silent Praetorian *speculatores* in dark cloaks stood guard fifty paces down the road beside her curtained litter; right beside the roaring orange heat stood only twenty-year-old **Lucida** and seventeen-year-old **Citizen Sura**, both bare-headed with black ashes streaked across their brows.

On the left pyre lay sixty-four-year-old **Berenice of Ascalon**, washed clean in warm myrrh-water by Lucida's own hands, dressed in fine Sidonian purple linen with **ten heavy gold *aurei*** tucked securely beneath her folded tongue so neither Charon nor any phantom Syrian perfume-factor could ever cheat her across dark water again.

On the right pyre lay sixty-five-year-old **Habinnas Barba** of Comana, veteran of XV Apollinaris and master baker of Rome—his crushed chest bound straight in spotless white wool, his burned baker's forearms crossed peacefully over a **freshly baked two-pound round crown loaf of purest Sicilian *siligo* wheat** Sura had baked that evening in Salvius's brick guild-oven, and beside his right hand a full pitcher of **unwatered old Chian wine**.

Only one thing when Lucida's men claimed Barba's body out of the Lautumiae vestibule at dusk had made Lucida's fingers stop dead for three silent breaths:

Folded carefully as a pillow beneath dead Habinnas Barba's white head, soaked dark brown across the front folds with coughed lung-blood, had lain **a brand-new, costly Roman citizen's toga of finest Tarentine white wool**, stitched inside the neck-hem with the tiny red laundry-mark of the Carinae fuller three doors from **Lucius Cassius Corvus's** house.

Corvus had gone down into that filthy hole after noon. Corvus had held dying Barba's head against his own spotless triumph-day wool while Barba died cursing him—and left his own cloak behind under the old baker's skull in the dark.

Lucida had lifted Barba's white head gently, slipped Corvus's blood-stiffened toga out from beneath his neck without burning it on Barba's clean oak pyre, and folded the ruined white wool into a square bundle inside her litter floor.

Now, as twin pillars of fragrant cinnamon-and-oak flame roared thirty feet straight up toward the cold stars of March and white bone-ash settled singing into glowing red embers, Lucida poured the final ladle of unwatered dark wine hissing across the coals, spoke three ancient Phoenician words her mother **Lucida Tyria** had whispered over dying lamps in Subura winters, *peace upon salt, peace upon breath, peace upon iron*, and turned back toward the dark walls of Rome as faint grey light began thinning the eastern horizon behind Tibur.

* * *

At sunrise on the eighth day before the Ides of March, two hours before dead Antoninus Pius's funeral bier moved toward the Campus Martius, twenty-year-old **Lucida Tyria** rode down the empty, frost-glinting basalt cobbles from the Viminal toward Lot Fourteen to inspect her rebuilt commercial granaries before riding down to Ostia.

Right where the filthy, noisy flagstones of the **Argiletum and Subura** met the blinding white Carrara marble precinct of the **Forum of Augustus**, Augustus's builders had erected a hundred-foot-high barrier wall of grey volcanic **Gabine peperino stone**—fireproof, windowless, massive as a mountain cliff, pierced only by three tall travertine-framed arches, built expressly to stop Subura tenement fires and Subura filth from ever staining imperial marble.

Just as Lucida's four Bithynian litter-bearers reached the Subura mouth of the central **Firewall arch**, her lead Praetorian outrider reined his bay gelding sharp back on ringing iron shoes.

Walking alone straight through the tall stone arch out of the white marble Forum dawn from the opposite side, flanked ten paces behind by two quiet freedman clerks carrying bound ivory *codicilli*—came twenty-year-old **Lucius Cassius Corvus**.

Cold crimson sunrise slanting across the Quirinal ridge struck him full across the shoulders as he stepped beneath the soot-blackened stone keystone.

Over his lean six-foot frame he wore no dark client's cloak today: across his crisp white woollen tunic ran for the very first time the **two-inch-wide crimson purple *angusticlavius* stripe of a Roman Knight**, and upon the fourth finger of his left hand, twin to the white crescent brick-stamp scar across his right thumb, gleamed the heavy **gold equestrian ring (*anulus aureus*)**.

One hour earlier up on the Palatine, **Emperor Lucius Verus** had laid a shaved strip of lime-bark privately across **Marcus Aurelius's** knee; twenty breaths ago a grim Palatine centurion had delivered Marcus's cold handwritten note banishing **Quintus Servilius Priscus** permanently from court and Curia to his Sabine hills—and **Junius Rusticus**, summoning twenty-year-old Knight Corvus straight out of Priscus's shattered atrium into **Emperor Marcus's own private fiscal secretariat**, had told Corvus in three spare Stoic sentences *whose hand had carried that green-sealed bark leaf into the Castra Praetoria*!

Lucida tapped twice on her cedar litter-frame, parted the dark wool curtains, and stepped out alone in her purple-bordered Coan silk *stola* onto the wet black basalt flags directly beneath the hundred-foot grey **Firewall**.

Behind her lay the smoky pantiles, bakeries, fulleries, and blood-alleys of the **Subura**.

Behind Corvus gleamed the soaring white Corinthian marble columns of **Mars the Avenger** in the **Forum of Augustus**.

Motioning both her Praetorian guards and his two clerks thirty paces back out of earshot with two identical, imperious flicks of twenty-year-old wrists, **Lucida Tyria** and **Lucius Cassius Corvus** stopped six feet apart inside the cold stone shadow of the arch.

Neither had slept in forty hours. Dark violet bruised the fine olive skin beneath Lucida's calm amber eyes; pale stone-grey pinched the lean planes around Corvus's scarred mouth. Four and a half Octobers ago they had run barefoot down an alley four hundred yards east of this exact stone, fifteen years old, soaked in horse-blood, owning between them one bronze hairpin, a dead surveyor's debt, and an oath never to let Rome cut them in two.

Without speaking a word, Lucida reached back into her litter, lifted out the **folded, blood-stiffened square of Carinae white Tarentine wool**, and held it out across the cold space between them.

Corvus took his dead baker-stained toga in one hand without looking down at it, his grey-green eyes locked cold and bottomless onto hers.

"You kissed the scar on my thumb while my back was still warm from your brazier," Corvus said quietly, no shout, no boy's crack in his deep Fronto-honed voice, quieter and deadlier than any blade drawn in Lot Fourteen's cellar. "Twelve heartbeats behind my shoulder while Sura went down for water, and a grave-libation on my hand to pay for old Priscus's neck."

"Old Priscus died in a sunny Sabine villa with forty slaves pouring his wine; **Berenice** died strangled across a child's stone and **Habinnas Barba** drowned on his own ribs while Scaeva split all four of my stones thirty-four sunrises short of April screaming *your* receivership docket through my splintered gate," Lucida answered just as quietly, chin level in the crimson dawn light. "You brought me a ten-day custody leash that erased three years of my blood so you could keep me safe inside your pocket, Lucius Cassius. Nobody puts me inside a pocket again while stars burn."

A muscle feathered once along Corvus's lean jaw, because he held inside his belt-pouch right this instant Praetor Acilius's useless, blood-late release-deed that would have handed her every stone free gratis yesterday noon, **and he refused out of sheer, cauterized twenty-year-old pride to pull it out and beg absolution from the woman who had just gutted his house and handed three Syrian legions to Lucius Verus**!

"So you crowned a golden tavern-dancer over half the Eastern frontier while Vologases sharpens forty thousand lances beyond the river, **Citizen Lucida**," Corvus murmured, letting the new title drop off his tongue like weighed silver onto cold stone. "I heard at the Palatine gate what Verus signed in purple ink last night: *Lucida Tyria, ingenuo iure restituta, Purveyor to the Younger Augustus's Eastern Household*."

"Freeborn from the foundation of the sky by Caesar's rescript, Knight of Crustumina," Lucida said evenly, touching the green emerald head of her gold hairpin once against dark coiled braids. "Neither Publius Vedius Pollio on his rock in Gyaros nor Receiver Cassius Corvus at Saturn's scales holds one copper thread over my breath or my urn."

Corvus stepped one single pace closer beneath the towering grey peperino arch—until only four feet of cold, frost-filmed basalt separated crimson equestrian stripe from purple-bordered silk, close enough that warm mist from both mouths mingled white in the freezing dawn air just as their breaths had mingled in Berenice's freezing garret at fifteen, yet farther apart than Sicily and Syria.

"Enjoy your golden Emperor's banquets while Rome burns incense to old Antoninus, Lucida," twenty-year-old Corvus said softly, his grey-green eyes bright as winter steel above the pale scar her teeth had left on his lower lip. "**Emperor Marcus** and **Junius Rusticus** enrolled me an hour ago as Special Equestrian *Procurator ad Annonam Castrensem* auditing military grain, linen, and transport contracts from Ostia to Brundisium and Antioch. Every ship your Syrian captains load, every sack of wheat you bill to Verus's treasury, passes across **my bronze balance**. Short-weight Rome's legions by half a pound of meal between here and the Euphrates, and by my father's earth, I will strip your three hundred thousand sesterces down to bare Subura cobbles with my own hand."

For one breath, sharp, lightning-cold and breathless down the base of her spine—something terrifyingly indistinguishable from desire flashed black across the bottom of twenty-year-old Lucida's rage as she looked straight up into the lean, merciless face of the only equal she had ever met on earth.

"Bring true weights when you sail to Antioch, Lucius Cassius," Lucida whispered back, dark amber eyes unflinching in the rising red glare. "Every scale between the Tiber and the Euphrates already knows my thumb."

High overhead above the hundred-foot grey stone cliff of the **Subura Firewall**, pale March sun broke clear of purple winter cloud, catching simultaneously the gilded bronze chariot of Mars blazing atop Augustus's marble temple behind Corvus's shoulder and its blinding twin reflection glaring up out of the rainwater pool at Lucida's feet, **two fierce cold suns burning white between them across wet black stone**.

Neither bowed. Neither said farewell.

Lucida stepped smoothly back inside her curtained litter and rapped twice with emerald-and-gold toward the Ostian Gate; **Lucius Cassius Corvus** turned cleanly on iron-heeled equestrian boots and walked alone into marble light toward the Senate House, twenty years old, rich, untouchable, sworn enemies across a dividing world, with ten long years of fire, war, plague, and empire waiting before the dark came down.
