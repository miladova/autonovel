# Chapter XXIII — Under the Vault

*Tenth Day Before the Kalends of October, AD 160 · Corvus*

Forty-eight days of brassy sky broke in three heartbeats over the Viminal crest with a clap of autumn thunder loud enough to crack dry roof-tiles from the Baths of Trajan to the river.

Before street-peddlers on the **Clivus Suburanus** could snatch fried-fish pans inside doorways, hail big as broad-beans hammered white across scorching basalt, turning instantly into a solid grey curtain of warm September rain that swept two months of horse-dung, fuller's chalk, and black August fire-ash roaring ankle-deep—then shin-deep, down the steep mountain funnel of the Subura toward the Forum valley.

Down at the bottom of the Sandaliarius turn, where blackened char-timber from August's collapsed seven-storey tenement had washed sideways across the municipal iron sewer-grate, brown flood-water backed up three feet high within two hundred breaths, swirling dead rats, cabbage-crates, and floating wooden sandals straight back across the sidewalks!

Nineteen-year-old Corvus came striding fast down the flooded wall-curb out of the Argiletum—soaked straight through his dark grey woollen *paenula* and tunic to the skin, black hair plastered flat across his brow, holding his waxed leather dispatch-satchel high under his left armpit to keep Senator Priscus's autumn fiscal sheets dry, when through the blinding silver sheet of rain he saw the open ground-floor archway of **Lot Fourteen**.

New red brick shoring already crowned the repaired third storey above; down at street-level, where brown ash-laden flood-water was surging within two inches of topping the low travertine cellar-sill and pouring straight down into the subterranean tufa grain-vaults below, **nobody stood except nineteen-year-old Lucida**.

Her Marsian porters were nowhere in sight—doubtless dispatched half an hour earlier before the sky burst to clear the lower Fish-Market drains! Standing knee-deep in churning brown rainwater at the top of the steep vaulted cellar stair, soaked dark blue linen clinging tight as paint to every line of her back and thighs, Lucida was straining with both braced shoulders to lift the second of two heavy four-inch-thick tarred oak flood-boards (*tabulae cataractae*) into the vertical stone mortise-grooves cut into either door-jamb, while a three-foot wall of backed-up street-water jammed the right plank sideways an inch above its bottom seat!

Once water topped that lower board and cascaded down the narrow stone steps into two-thousand-*modius* tufa vaults holding **four hundred *modii* of dry winter Sicilian reserve wheat**, four hundred *modii* swelled into sour rotten mash overnight—breaking her winter margin with one hundred fifty-nine mornings left on her count!

Corvus did not call her name, thirty-six days had passed since the August rubble-auction without one word spoken between them—and did not ask whether she wanted his hands on her plank.

He vaulted the floating cabbage-crates into thigh-deep rushing water right beside her hip, wedged the chiselled iron toe of his surveyor's walking-staff beneath the jammed right end of the two-hundred-pound oak flood-board to clear a wedged lump of fire-brick out of the stone groove, and leaned all twelve and a half stone of his soaked frame downward onto the top oak edge beside her bare straining forearms!

Lucida's head whipped round half an inch through streaming black hair as his shoulder met hers in the pouring grey roar; she didn't tell him to let go, she threw her entire hip and back down on the left end in wordless, instantaneous childhood rhythm with his heave on the right:

*"One—two—drop!"*

*THUD.*

Tarred four-inch oak slammed dead flush into the pitched travertine sill-slot, cutting off the foaming brown street-surge two inches below the top rim!

"Upper hatch—the gutter-spout's overflowing off the coppersmith's eaves!" Lucida gasped through stinging rain, ducking backward down the first three dry stone cellar steps as four gallons of roof-cascade burst straight off a broken lead gargoyle overhead toward the open sloped trap-doors!

Corvus stepped down onto the stone stair right behind her heels, caught both heavy iron pull-rings of the twin pitched-pine cellar storm-doors overhead, hauled them slamming shut against the inclined stone curb, plunging both of them out of deafening grey rain-glare into dark, dry subterranean brick-and-tufa silence—and shot the heavy interior iron crossbar *clang* across both hooks!

Above their heads a river of September storm-water roared harmlessly over pitched two-inch pine and tarred oak seams without letting through more than a thin trickle down the outer limestone channel.

Down below their wet boots stretched the vaulted subterranean belly of **Lot Fourteen**: forty feet long by twenty-eight wide, carved out of dry grey Republican tufa rock and vaulted overhead in clean new red *bipedales* brick arches, warm from the earth's steady eighty-degree summer hold, smelling deeply and cleanly of dry golden Sicilian wheat stacked eight sacks high on cedar pallets, fresh lime-mortar, and pitched pine.

Hanging from an iron hook driven into the central brick pier burned **one single terracotta olive-oil lamp**, throwing long amber shadows and flickering gold edges across forty yards of silent stone.

Lucida stood four steps down on the dry tufa floor wringing brown rainwater out of her heavy unpinned braids with both hands, chest rising and falling fast beneath soaked dark-blue linen that clung translucent from collarbone to bruised knee.

Two steps above her by the bolted hatch stood Corvus, water dripping steadily off his jaw, eyelashes, and dark grey wool hem onto the clean dry stone, unbuckling his soaked outer *paenula* and setting his leather dispatch-satchel carefully up onto a dry projecting brick shelf shoulder-high beside the central pier so damp wouldn't soak through the seams.

Now that the flood-board sat tight in its groove and not a handful of wheat had wetted, silence rushed in between them ten times louder than the thunder rumbling muffled through six feet of rock overhead.

Lucida let go of her wet hair, wiped rain off her cheekbones with the back of one bandaged palm—the pale pink burn-scars from August's fire-night still fresh across her knuckles, and looked coldly up at him in the amber lamp-glow.

"The storm-sluice down at the Forum opens in half an hour once the *vigiles* lift the grate," she said flatly, taking a step backward toward her stacked grain-pallets to put twelve feet of dry stone between them. "Wait on the top step till the curb drains if you want, Lucius Cassius, then unbolt the hatch and go back to your Caelian dry clothes. Nobody owes silver for pushing an oak board."

Corvus stood motionless by the central brick pier, water trickling cold down the hollow of his throat inside his clinging tunic, staring across twelve feet of warm golden shadow at her pink fire-scarred knuckles—and at **Claudia Dorcas's green Caelian warehouse-seal** hanging brazenly on bronze wire from the first grain-pallet beside Lucida's hip!

Thirty-six days of watching her walk past him in the Forum as if he were dead stone after he broke Nymphidius's arson frame to save her Fish-Market stones, thirty-six nights of lying wide awake in his Carinae room watching young Marsian Titus hobble on one crutch past Peace's portico while knowing **Ceionia Fabia**'s stewardess held the surface-lease over this very vault!—snapped the iron band round Corvus's ribs clean in two.

"Claudia Dorcas's lead horse-seal hanging right on your winter wheat," Corvus said, his deep voice quiet and shaking slightly at the bottom edge in the low brick vault. "Six thousand sesterces out of **Ceionia Fabia's** purse at the August spear, purely so you could spit in Sextus Pompeius's face rather than let thirty-five thousand clean sesterces out of my own Castor box put that roof back in your hands without strings!"

Lucida laughed once—short, jagged, wholly without mirth in the warm underground air.

"Without strings?" she repeated, taking two barefoot-silent strides forward in wet leather soles until only six feet separated them beside the lamp-lit brick pier. "Thirty-five thousand sesterces from **Quintus Servilius Priscus's** right-hand *cognitor*, the exact man who tossed my dead straw-buyer's pauper census sheet onto Tribune Sempronius's knee at sunrise without asking my leave, cost me six thousand sesterces and half an upper storey, and wanted his own Tibur equestrian holding the key over my head so whenever Priscus whistled against Fabia, you could lock my grain indoors 'for my own safety'!"

"Without that plan on Sempronius's knee at dawn," Corvus roared back—all three years of Fronto's quiet courtroom restraint shattering off the low brick vault so the hanging lamp-flame jumped, "ten soldiers of the Urban Cohorts were marching down the Fish-Market steps at the first hour with iron padlocks to **shut Barba's four mills cold on Day Eight Hundred Ninety-Seven**! One unground morning, Lucida—**eight hundred ninety-six days wiped dead to nothing**, and you dragged in chains to the Castra Urbana on planted arson-wood while you knelt stitching Titus's leg! Would you rather sit in irons tonight owning unsequestered rubble?"

"I would rather burn in my own ovens holding my own iron bar than live one hour inside a cage you built behind my back!" Lucida shouted straight up into his face, stepping across the last four feet until her wet chin tilted two inches below his scarred mouth, dark amber eyes blazing bright gold in the single flame. "Three years you've treated me like a lame sheep you have to herd into your clean Sabine pen while you climb toward your precious gold ring! Hiding six denarii in your waistband at fifteen while you coughed blood; hiding for twelve whole months that you knew **Vedius Pollio** bled eighty sesterces off my wrists every Kalends while you fucked **Tullia Crispina** on silk sheets at Laurentum and left me paying blood-silver out of olive-dregs!"

"You threw my hand off on the scaffold when I offered to pay Vedius out, and walked down to the *Horrea Galbana* at midnight to pay **Decimus Valerius** on cedar planks behind Bin Twenty-Four instead!" Corvus snarled, raw murderous pain ripping clean out of his throat before his brain could stop his tongue.

Lucida didn't flinch, apologize, or drop her eyes by a hair; she shoved both wet, fire-scarred palms **hard straight into the center of Corvus's soaked chest**, driving him half a step back against the central red brick pier!

"At least Decimus Valerius named his filthy price straight to my face across a counting-board instead of bringing me roasted figs in the snow with another woman's lemon-verbena still washed into his hair!" she hissed.

Corvus's hands shot out and locked around both her wet wrists before she could shove a second time, spinning her round in one fierce, unbroken half-turn until **her own soaked back slammed flat against the warm, rough red *bipedales* bricks of the central pier**—trapping her between his six-foot frame and her own masonry just as fifty-six feet of Subura air had pinned them in October, except down here there was neither wind, street, nor audience under earth or sky!

"Try pulling your wrists free and telling me to my face you wanted Valerius's hands on you!" Corvus breathed raggedly against her parted lips, chest heaving hard against her breasts through two layers of drenched, clinging wool and linen, grey-green eyes black-pupiled and blind with four years of starvation.

Lucida didn't pull her wrists down; she arched her neck up off the rough red brick, bared small white teeth, and **bit straight down onto Corvus's scarred lower lip**, hard enough that the old white stylus-scar split warm and salt, bright red blood blooming instantly across both their wet mouths—before opening her lips beneath his with a low, fierce animal sound deep in her throat!

Everything broke at once.

Corvus groaned deep inside his ribs against her bleeding kiss, letting go of one wrist to tangle his fingers rough into her heavy, rain-soaked braids while his scarred right hand dropped straight down her wet ribcage to drag the clinging dark-blue linen hem up over her bare, rain-chilled olive thigh and hip!

Lucida tore at the wet woollen neck-seam of his grey tunic with both hands until strong Caelian stitching ripped three inches down his collarbone, dragging his mouth harder down onto hers, tasting his blood, rainwater, and salt together, tongue meeting tongue in raw, bruising, breathless fury that had five years of river-mud, winter pleurisy, flour-dust, terror, and intolerable loneliness packed behind every gasp!

"Damn your gold ring—damn your Caelian house," she panted torn and ragged against his jaw as Corvus dragged his ripped wet tunic clean off over his head, dropping it onto the dry tufa stone, and buried his bleeding mouth down the side of her wet throat straight onto the tiny dark olive-pip birthmark below her left collarbone, sucking hard enough to leave dark purple-red proof on her skin while both hands slipped under her soaked shift, snapping the thin knotted inner linen *strophium* breast-band clean in two with one jerk of bricklayer's wrists and dragging her wet blue linen straight off over her raised arms!

Nineteen years old, stripped naked to the warm amber lamp-glow from unpinned wet black hair falling past full, rain-pebbled breasts down to narrow waist, dark triangle of curls, and strong walker's thighs braced wide against warm red kiln-brick, Lucida didn't cover an inch of herself—she caught Corvus's bare scarred shoulders in both hands, dragged him skin-to-skin flat against her breasts so both pounding hearts slammed unobstructed together, and reached straight down between their bellies to wrench his wet leather belt-buckle and loin-cloth undone, wrapping warm, stain-faded fingers hard around his rigid, aching length!

Corvus hissed through clenched teeth against her ear, forehead dropping hard against the rough brick beside her temple as her thumb slid slick over the head; then he hooked both forearms beneath her bare knees, lifted her clean off her wet leather boots so her calves locked fierce as iron bands round his bare waist and her naked spine pressed into rough warm pozzolana brick, and dropped his mouth onto her bare right breast, teeth grazing nipple until Lucida's head slammed back against the masonry with a choked, shameless Syrian curse, nails raking four white tracks down his scarred shoulder-blades where fallen August fire-mortar had bruised him purple!

"No widows, no gatekeepers—look at me, Sabine, look right in my eyes when you, " Lucida gasped hoarsely, reaching down with one hand to guide him straight to her slick, burning entrance while throwing her left arm blindly sideways onto the shoulder-high brick ledge beside the pier to brace herself against the first hard upward drive of his hips—

And her fingers slammed square across **Corvus's unbuckled leather dispatch-satchel** on the brick shelf, knocking it sideways off the narrow mortar-lip!

Heavy waxed tablets and folded oilskin hit the dry tufa stone beside Corvus's bare heels with a loud *clatter*, popping the damp leather inner flap wide open two feet from the hanging lamp!

Neither of them would have stopped for ordinary court-scrolls if the whole Senate had fallen off the shelf; except **face-up right in the bright yellow puddle of lamp-light** slid two objects side by side:

First, a folded, red-rubricated Urban Prefecture receivership draft headed in **Corvus's own unmistakable upright Sabine hand**:

*BONA VEDII POLLIONIS — SEQUESTRATIO IN FIDEM L · CASSII CORVI : ITEM IVS PATRONATVS ET REVERSIO PISTRINI PISCARIENSIS (LVCIDAE LAT · IVN ·)...*

(*Sequestration into the receivership custody of Lucius Cassius Corvus: Item, the patronal right and reversion of the Fish-Market Bakery of Lucida, Junian Latin!*)

And resting two inches beside it, escaped from the split inner seam—gleamed **a flat oilskin packet sealed with pale pistachio-green Chian mastic wax stamped with Senator Priscus's secret terebinth tree**!

At the exact same instant, as Corvus glanced instinctively down at the clatter and back up at Lucida's freezing shoulder, his bare forearm brushed her discarded blue tunic hanging off the pallet-hook beside his knee, knocking **Ceionia Fabia's solid-gold Palatine rock-crystal scent-bottle** (engraved round the gold collar *C · FABIA · AELII · CAES · F*) rolling bright and chiming across the stones right against Priscus's green-sealed Syrian packet!

One heartbeat ago Corvus's hips had been braced between her parted thighs, notched hot and slick against the very threshold of entering her, both bodies trembling on the edge of the cliff.

Now **Lucida stared straight down over his bare shoulder at her own name and Fish-Market bakery written in Corvus's handwriting under *SEQUESTRATIO IN FIDEM L · CASSII CORVI***—while **Corvus stared straight down at Ceionia Fabia's royal *AELII CAESARIS FILIA* gold bottle** rolling against his treasonous Syrian dispatch!

Lucida went colder than January river-ice against his chest.

Every drop of blood drained from her face; she unlocked both ankles from around his waist and **shoved with arms, knees, and shoulders all at once** like a wildcat tearing out of a wire snare, dropping bare-footed onto the stone floor and snatching her wet blue linen tunic flat across her breasts and thighs while backing six paces away toward the wheat-sacks!

"Wait, Vespera, read the whole clause, don't read four words upside down!" Corvus rasped, standing naked from the waist up, chest heaving raw in the amber glare, blood from her bite smeared dark crimson across his scarred mouth and chin as he bent fast to sweep the green-sealed Syrian packet back out of sight into his satchel while holding out the Prefecture sheet! "That isn't a seizure *against* you! When I spring the Subortanae brick-fraud and Caelian water-theft to bankrupt Vedius Pollio this winter, **every asset in Vedius's estate—including his patronal reversion over you!, gets frozen by fiscal receivership**! I wrote my own name in as temporary court *sequester* specifically so **neither Priscus nor the State Treasury can touch your ovens while you finish your last hundred and fifty mornings**!"

"So **you** hold my Junian slave-reversion inside **your** receivership box instead of Vedius Pollio!" Lucida whispered—shaking from bare heels to wet braids now, not with cold, with revulsion and terror so absolute her voice sounded like breaking glass in the vault. "First Sextus Pompeius tries to buy my Lot Fourteen ground-lease behind my back; and when that fails, **Lucius Cassius Corvus writes a court decree transferring Publius Vedius Pollio's dead-patron leash over my corpse straight into his own pocket**, just in case I don't behave nicely toward Senator Priscus's faction when old Antoninus dies!"

"I am trying to keep Priscus and Vedius *both* off your throat while **Ceionia Fabia's royal gold** rolls out of your pocket onto my boots!" Corvus shouted back, hauling his torn grey woollen tunic roughly down over his wet shoulders with shaking hands, pointing down at the gold-collared rock-crystal bottle gleaming on the stone. "*Aelii Caesaris Filia!* How much Palatine gold has Lucius Commodus's sister poured into your strongbox since last winter to buy ears inside Priscus's house? Was *that* how you paid twelve thousand cash for this lot, and why you let me climb down here tonight—hoping green wax might fall out of my bag?"

Silence hit the subterranean vault like an axe driven into bone.

Even as the monstrous accusation left Corvus's bleeding mouth, half his mind knew she hadn't knocked his satchel down on purpose, yet four years of poisonous suspicion, wounded pride, and finding Fabia's gold rolling two inches from a cipher that could hang him drove the blade past pulling back!

Lucida stopped trembling altogether.

Slowly she pulled her damp, torn dark-blue tunic down over her head, knotted her belt around her waist without hurrying, bent down to pick up Ceionia Fabia's gold-mounted crystal bottle off the tufa floor, and looked across ten feet of amber shadow straight into Corvus's eyes.

On her bare wet throat just above her torn collar-hem, the dark red bruise his mouth had sucked over her olive-pip birthmark burned vivid against olive skin; on his lower lip, a single bright bead of her bite swelled dark red and dropped onto his grey wool chest.

"Your father Gaius Cassius drank sour Sabine wine until he misread blue clay-marl out of sheer sloppy pride and pulled twenty cartloads of wet mud down onto his own neck, leaving two children starving in the rain," Lucida said—speaking softly, clearly, using the one sacred grave neither of them had ever profaned since they were thirteen years old. "You don't drink wine, Lucius Cassius; you drink Senator Priscus's ink. And when your Caelian pit collapses on you, don't tap twice-and-once on my gate."

Corvus's face went white as cut travertine around his bleeding mouth.

"At least Gaius Cassius died a freeborn Roman citizen under open sky with his own name on his tomb," Corvus answered, voice dead flat, matching grave for grave across ten feet of stone as something four years old died screaming between their ribs, "instead of begging freedom off drunken guests over pickled thrushes at Puteoli and leaving his child kissing patrician hairpins in Caelian bedrooms."

Lucida did not blink, weep, or strike him.

She walked barefoot past his shoulder without grazing a thread of his wool, climbed the six dry stone steps to the cellar-head where outside thunder had rolled away east toward Tibur leaving slow rain dripping off eaves, shot back the interior iron crossbar with one wrench of both scarred hands, threw open the pitched pine storm-hatch into cold grey evening air, and stepped out into the empty street without looking back into the dark.
