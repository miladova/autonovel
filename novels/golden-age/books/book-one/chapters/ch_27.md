# Chapter XXVII — The Cracked Tile

*Fourth Day Before the Nones of March, AD 161 · Corvus*

An Empire that had stood still for twenty-two summers began sliding into the sea over three slices of **Alpine cheese**.

At dusk on the fourth day before the Nones of March, while cold sleet-mixed rain blew sideways off the Tiber across Senator Priscus's Caelian courtyard, a mud-plastered freedman groom from Marcus Caesar's private Lorium household flung himself off a blown bay mule before the postern gate had half-opened, tracking yellow Aurelian road-clay straight across black-and-white geometric mosaic into the bolted *tablinum*.

Twenty-year-old Corvus stood beside the glowing oak brazier warming ink-horns for **Priscus** and **Quintus Junius Rusticus**; both consular senators stayed seated like carved peperino tomb-figures while the panting courier knelt and spoke six low sentences in Greek:

"At supper last evening the Emperor called twice for aged Raetian **Alpine cheese** with honeyed mulsum against the Pergamene physician's prayer. Around the third watch he vomited black sour curds till morning; at sunrise violent fever seized both lungs. By noon today he was talking in delirium to dead kings about tribute-defaults and foreign embassies. Caesar Marcus has not left his bedside since the fourth hour and sends word by his own ring: *two nights at most; keep the Curia lists clean*."

Priscus dismissed the soaking courier to the kitchen hearth with two gold pieces and waited until the heavy cedar door-bolt shot home.

Inside the silent study, twenty-two years of Antonine gold turned abruptly to cold iron.

"Alpine cheese at seventy-four," Priscus murmured dryly, cracking a fennel seed between yellow back molars without a trace of grief. "Even gods die through their teeth. Junius: does **Furius Victorinus** hold the night watch at the Praetorian Camp tomorrow, or **Cornelius Repentinus**?"

"Repentinus takes the Guard watch on the sixth and the Nones," Rusticus answered grimly, ink-stained thumb rubbing the coarse dark fold of his *abolla*. "And Repentinus had four horse-couriers saddled outside **Ceionia Fabia's** Caelian gate an hour before sunset—clearly **Galeria Lysistrata** sent her own bedroom runner ahead of Marcus's man! The hour old Antoninus stops breathing, Fabia and **Vedius Pollio** will push young Lucius straight toward the Praetorian ramparts unless two hammers fall before noon: Marcus holding undivided Eastern military loyalty, and Vedius Pollio stripped bare of coined silver before he can underwrite a single Praetorian donative!"

Priscus turned his sharp slate-grey eyes squarely onto twenty-year-old **Corvus**.

"Before the Castor strongroom grilles shut at the second hour tonight, Lucius Cassius," Priscus ordered quietly, "go fetch **my green-sealed Syrian lime-bark duplicate** out of your private arca and bring it back here before cockcrow so Scaurianus's fast Liburnian galley sails from Brundisium with the matched key the instant Lorium flashes torches. Second: are **Aulus Volusenus** and your other four citizen witnesses ready to stand before Urban Fiscal Praetor **Manius Acilius** at thirty breaths' call?"

"All five sworn and waiting inside the first milestone, Senator," Corvus answered cleanly, and added one detail he had quietly engineered that very afternoon over wine with young **Quaestor Caecilius** at the Treasury: "And Quaestor Caecilius has initialed an administrative rider exempting active *annona* bread-ovens from temporary inventory-padlocks during receivership transfer, provided the operating co-master countersigns my interim custody receipt before the seal-clerks march."

Neither Priscus nor Rusticus cared a straw about minor *annona* bakehouse riders at an hour when thrones shook; Priscus nodded once:

"Good. Bring me the green terebinth packet before the fourth watch, boy—and the afternoon Publius Vedius Pollio's scales tip in the Basilica, **four hundred thirty-two thousand sesterces** fiscal bounty puts the gold ring on your finger before the old Emperor's pyre cools."

* * *

At the second hour of the night Corvus unlocked Box Nineteen inside the concrete podium of **Castor's Temple**, passing thirty paces east of **Vault Forty-Two** without knowing Myrtale's capital poison warrant sat ticking like a coiled adder behind its bronze grille! tucked Priscus's thin oilskin packet sealed in pale green Chian mastic safely inside the inner pocket of his leather dispatch-satchel alongside Quaestor Caecilius's fresh *annona* padlock-waiver, and stepped back out into the cold March rain.

Priscus's Caelian house lay straight south up the Clivus Scauri.

Corvus's boots turned north-west toward the smoky red glow behind the **Forum Piscarium** instead.

Five months and twelve days had passed since **Lucida** walked barefoot out of Lot Fourteen's flooded vault leaving her teeth-blood drying on his mouth and two desecrated graves lying like drawn swords between them. Five months during which twenty-year-old Corvus had buried himself eighteen hours a day inside Treasury parchments, bought Volusenus off a hunting-knife, pushed his liquid Castor chest past a hundred and fifty thousand sesterces even after Volusenus's loan, and crossed the street twice in January fog rather than force his shadow across her path.

Tonight old Antoninus Pius lay dying twelve miles west, **Cornelius Repentinus** commanded twelve cohorts of Praetorian iron on the Nones, **Scaeva's** thugs were prowling every Subura corner, and unless Lucida **countersigned Quaestor Caecilius's interim custody receipt** before Corvus sprang his fiscal receivership over Vedius Pollio's estate, routine Treasury clerks *would* slap three-day inventory padlocks across her hoppers and break her three-year citizenship count **fewer than forty mornings from home**!

Inside the Fish-Market alley, warm nutty steam of baking Sicilian loaves rolled out into the freezing drizzle alongside the deep, rhythmic subterranean *grrrm-grrrm-grrrm* of all four volcanic lava mills turning steady through their **thousand-and-fifty-ninth unbroken night**.

Rather than knocking at the lower courtyard gate where Marsian kneaders worked around sick old Habinnas Barba's brazier—through the crack of the shutter Corvus glimpsed white-haired Barba sitting propped on three grain-sacks wrapped in sheepskins beside Oven One, breathing shallow through parted bluish lips while stubbornly watching the third-watch peel-count! Corvus climbed the familiar outside brick staircase to the upper loft and rapped softly on the shuttered pine door:

*Tap-tap.* Pause. *Tap.*

For five rain-dripping heartbeats nothing moved inside.

Then the interior iron bolt slid slowly back, and seventeen-year-old **Sura** opened the door six inches, lifting a horn oil-lamp high.

Her right hand wasn't holding the lamp; Sura's right palm was wrapped clumsily in a blood-spotted linen rag where a sharp edge of sheet-copper off Number Two hopper-chute had sliced across her skin an hour ago, and three paces behind her beside the glowing charcoal pan stood twenty-year-old **Lucida** warming pine-resin salve over the coals in a small bronze spoon.

Lucida wore a dark woollen working tunic belted snug at her waist, blue-black braids coiled high and fastened through the center knot with **Ceionia Fabia's six-inch green emerald-headed gold pin**. Five hard winter months had honed subtle hollows beneath her high olive cheekbones, making her dark amber eyes look larger, fiercer, and older than twenty in the yellow tallow glow.

Seeing Corvus standing tall in the doorway, rain streaming off his dark grey cloak, the faint pale line of September's tooth-scar still visible across his scarred lower lip—Sura stepped back automatically with wide, anxious grey eyes to let him out of the rain before glancing in mute appeal toward Lucida for pardon.

Lucida did not order him down the stairs; neither did she set down the hot bronze salve-spoon.

"Why are you climbing Fish-Market stairs at midnight in March rain, Lucius Cassius?" she asked quietly.

"Shut the door against the draft first," Corvus said, stepping just inside onto the warm terracotta tiles where he had slept with his cheek two inches from her ankle at seventeen, unbuckling his soaked outer cloak and setting his **leather dispatch-satchel** down beside the wooden flour-chest near the door-jamb so wet leather wouldn't spot Sura's sleeping-mat. "Let me bind Sura's hand straight while I talk; that rag's tied crooked across the thumb-web and it'll split open first sack she lifts."

Before proud Lucida could object, Corvus knelt on one knee beside seventeen-year-old Sura on the rush mat, who trusted Corvus with childlike certainty despite five months of adult war, took the warm pine-resin spoon gently out of Lucida's fingers without brushing her skin, and began cleaning and strapping Sura's three-inch copper cut with swift, practiced country-boy care while speaking low and fast so not a syllable carried down through the floor-joints to the Marsians below.

"Listen to every word without interrupting me, Lucida," Corvus said over Sura's bandaged fingers. "Yesterday evening at Lorium **Emperor Antoninus** ate Raetian Alpine cheese, vomited through the third watch, and lies burning in chest-delirium tonight. Marcus Caesar's own courier rode into Priscus's yard at dusk giving the old man **forty-eight hours at most**."

Behind Corvus's bent dark shoulder, twenty-year-old Lucida stood completely still beside the wooden flour-chest—two feet away from Corvus's unlatched wet leather dispatch-satchel resting on the warm tiles!

*Forty-eight hours at most.* Thirty-seven mornings too early for April tenth; four days too early even for stubborn old Berenice's sixth-of-March departure to Puteoli!

"Why bring Caelian death-watches to a Subura bakehouse?" Lucida asked, keeping her voice level as cold water while her eyes dropped once, silent, razor-swift, to the brass buckle of Corvus's wet satchel beside the chest.

"Because **Cornelius Repentinus** holds the Praetorian watch on the sixth and the Nones, and Scaeva's dogs will own every unguarded alley in Rome during the twelve-hour interregnum before the Senate sits!" Corvus said urgently, knotting Sura's clean linen strip firm around her wrist and turning on one knee to look straight up into Lucida's face, pulling **Quaestor Caecilius's waxed administrative custody-and-waiver sheet** out of his tunic breast! "The morning Lorium sends word, before noon, I go before Fiscal Praetor Acilius with five citizen witnesses and **destroy Publius Vedius Pollio root and branch**—breaking his *Figlinae Subortanae* fraud and voiding Gaius Munatius's trust *per aes et libram* in a single hour! Every lease and patronal claim Vedius holds passes into my receivership custody, and if you **countersign this temporary ten-day administrative custody receipt tonight** placing your Fish-Market operating keys formally under my *sequester* seal through the interregnum, Quaestor Caecilius waives inventory padlocks while you and Sura stay safe behind four armed Caelian watchmen inside my Carinae house until Vedius is in exile!"

Lucida looked down at the waxed official sheet held out in Corvus's scarred right hand, headed **TRADITIO CLAVIVM TEMPORARIA IN MANVM L · CASSII CORVI SEQUESTRIS** (*Temporary Delivery of Keys into the Hand of Receiver Lucius Cassius Corvus*)!

Everything half-blind **Phileros** had sold her for fifty gold pieces behind Juturna's spring eight days ago screamed loud as brass trumpets inside her skull—twisted fatally darker by five months of distrust:

*Sign *traditio clavium* handing legal possession of her bakehouse keys over to Receiver Lucius Cassius Corvus tonight, and the instant Corvus ceases to be mere silent landlord and takes formal possession as **operating sequester**, **Lucida ceases in law to be independent *exercitor* personally running the mill under Trajan's Edict**, killing her forty remaining days on fine-print technicality while locking her inside Senator Priscus's camp right when Repentinus unseals Castor Vault Forty-Two!*

Corvus truly thought, she could see the raw, fierce, desperate sincerity blazing in his grey-green eyes!—that dragging her inside his legal fortress for ten days would save her skin from Scaeva's clubs; **he could not even see that his fortress erased three years of her blood and left her dependent on his citizen mercy for every breath she drew afterward**!

Whereas three inches inside Corvus's damp leather satchel beside the flour-chest, she could smell the unmistakable sharp, medicinal breath of **warmed Chian terebinth mastic** drifting up off the damp leather as heat from Barba's ovens below warmed the floor-tiles! lay **Senator Priscus's Syrian succession letter**, the exact talisman for which **Ceionia Fabia** and soon-to-be co-Emperor **Lucius Verus** would burn Vault Forty-Two unopened and sign **Lucida's unconditional imperial Roman citizenship** in wet purple ink before sunrise!

Destroy Corvus's patron Priscus—knowing Corvus himself would cross **four hundred thirty-two thousand sesterces** into equestrian immunity (honestior, safe from the Esquiline cross!) the moment his Vedius bounty cleared! or watch forty remaining days of her freedom die under twin hammers?

"Sura," Lucida said calmly in Greek without taking her eyes off Corvus, "go down to the cooling-arch with your clean bandage and bring Master Cassius and me two warm mugs of spiced barley-water before he walks back into the sleet, and check that Barba drank his squill-syrup."

Seventeen-year-old Sura nodded obediently, picked up her small shutter-lantern, and slipped quietly out down the outside brick steps, pulling the door-latch to behind her.

"Let me fetch the iron ink-horn and my brass bakery stamp from behind the partition screen so you can show me Caecilius's clause under the lamp, Corvus," Lucida said softly—stepping behind Corvus's back toward the low wooden flour-chest beside the door while indicating the charcoal pan in the front corner. "Hold that wax near the coals; rain's chilled the surface too hard to read."

Unsuspecting, heart lifting with sudden, immense relief at hearing her ask to read the clause instead of shouting him out the door! twenty-year-old Corvus turned toward the glowing charcoal brazier in the front corner, crouching on one knee with his back squarely toward the door-chest to tilt Caecilius's damp wax tablet toward the red coals.

Six feet behind his turned broad shoulders, twenty-year-old Lucida moved without drawing one audible breath, hands swifter and stiller than when she melted Fabia's ivory love-note against Trajan's scalding copper steam-pipe.

One noiseless lift of Corvus's damp satchel flap; fingers dipping straight into the inner leather pocket; drawing out the flat three-by-five-inch oilskin wallet sealed at one corner with **pale pistachio-green Chian mastic stamped with Priscus's terebinth tree**!

Touching the warm tip of her thin bronze salve-spatula—still hot from the brazier coals thirty breaths earlier! for two heartbeats underneath the green mastic edge lifted the resin seal cleanly off the oiled skin without cracking a single branch of the stamped terebinth tree. Out slid **one razor-thin shaved lime-bark leaf (*philyra*) covered in neat shifted Greek columns written in Corvus's own unmistakable upright hand**; into its exact place inside the oilskin went an identical-weight blank shaving of thin linden-bark packing from her Caelian salve-box; one firm thumb-press re-tacked the warm green mastic shut; back into the inner satchel pocket slid the oilskin wallet, flap lowered at the exact angle Corvus had left it, all inside **twelve noiseless heartbeats** while rain drummed steady on the roof-pantiles overhead!

Lucida tucked Priscus's treasonous Syrian bark-leaf flat inside her high-laced bodice directly over her pounding ribs, picked up her cold dry ink-horn from the chest, and walked calmly across the warm tiles to stand beside Corvus at the brazier.

Corvus stood up to his full six feet in the red coal-glow, holding out the waxed custody-receipt and stylus with hope shining naked and unguarded across his lean, rain-wet twenty-year-old face—for ten heartbeats looking wholly like the fifteen-year-old boy who had carried half a roast kid up Vicus Cuprius in his first pair of shoes.

"Sign just below Barba's bakery title on Line Six, Vespera," Corvus said low, "and pack one dry tunic; Sura and the Marsians can sleep in my ground-floor study until, "

"Leave the tablet on my counting-stool till tomorrow's sixth hour so Habinnas Barba, who holds half our registered *societas* seal—puts his thumb beside mine after dawn bake," Lucida interrupted gently, taking the wax tablet out of his hand and laying it unbroken on her stool without signing a stroke tonight. "Sign behind old Barba's back while he's spitting blood downstairs, and he'll pull his name off the *Annona* plaque before noon out of pure Cappadocian pride. Send your Carinae clerk round tomorrow afternoon, if Lorium's torches haven't burned yet."

It sounded completely true to old Barba's fierce character; Corvus hesitated half a breath, looking down into her shadowed amber eyes, then nodded slowly.

"Tomorrow at the sixth hour, not one watch later," Corvus agreed, stooping by the chest to swing his wet cloak and leather satchel—feeling its familiar weight wholly unchanged! back across his broad shoulder before pausing with his hand on the wooden latch.

Outside on the stairs Sura had lingered thoughtfully under the eaves to give them peace.

Looking up six inches at twenty-year-old Corvus in the dim red glow, knowing with terrible, crystal finality that within forty-eight hours his Caelian patron **Quintus Servilius Priscus** would fall ruined before Praetorian swords because of the thin bark leaf burning right now against her own breastbone, while Corvus's own 432,000 HS Vedius bounty would carry Corvus across the equestrian line where no executioner's nail could touch him, **and knowing he would hate the ground under her sandals until the day one of them died once he learned whose hand carried Priscus's letter to the Camp**—Lucida stepped across the two feet of warm terracotta between them.

She did not reach for his scarred mouth; September's blood had sealed their mouths shut.

Instead she took Corvus's scarred, rain-cold right hand between both her warm, alkanet-shadowed palms, lifted his knuckles slowly in the quiet loft, and **pressed her lips once, long, dry, still, solemn as wine poured down a February tomb-pipe for the beloved dead, straight onto the raised white crescent brick-stamp scar across his right thumb**.

Corvus's breath caught deep in his chest; his fingers tightened once round hers, mistaking a funeral rite for truce.

"Vespera—" he whispered rough in the dark.

"Go up your hill out of the rain, Lucius Cassius," Lucida said softly, releasing his hand and stepping back behind the threshold. "And stand clear of crooked walls when the wind shifts."

She closed the heavy pine door between their faces and slid the iron bolt home with both hands, leaning her forehead flat against cold iron in the dark until his footsteps faded down the wet brick stairs into the night.
