# Chapter III — The Dead Man's Wax

*Nones of November, AD 156 · Corvus*

Frost sat white along the travertine flutes of Saturn's eight great columns, though down on the paving stones of the Clivus Capitolinus a thousand warm sandals had already trodden the rime into grey paste.

Corvus stood four steps up beside a broken bronze water-spout, balancing a cracked willow hod of pine-charcoal on his left shoulder so soot darkened his neck and collarbone, eyes moving steadily down the row of whitewashed pine notice-boards (*alba*) propped along the podium wall below the treasury doors. Just as Lucida had foretold three weeks back, nobody looked twice at a dirty fifteen-year-old hod-carrier pausing to blow on cold knuckles. Clerks in clean woollen sleeves pushed past his elbow carrying sealed canvas coin-sacks up toward the iron-barred doors of the *Aerarium Saturni*, where the Roman people's bullion and brass laws slept behind six-inch oak and triple locks; litigious country landlords squinted at auction notices through watering eyes; two Syrian money-changers sat cross-legged under the portico arch below, ringing suspect silver *denarii* against flat touchstones of black Lydian basalt.

Corvus read fast, left to right down the red-and-black painted columns without moving his lips.

His father had taught him block-capitals at six with a pointed stick in wet brick-marl, and cursive merchant-hand at nine off old tax-receipts so somebody sober could check the lime-tallies before the bailiff came round. Halfway down the fourth whitewashed board—headed *DE NOMINIBUS TRANSACTIS ET PROSCRIPTIS*, defaulted personal bonds called in on the Kalends of November—Corvus's gaze hooked on three lines brushed in cheap black lamp-ink:

*C · CASSIVS · C · F · CRVST · CORVVS · AGRIMENSOR · DEFUNCTVS*
*HS · MMM · CC · EX CHIROGRAPHO · CVM VSURIS CENTESIMIS*
*PETIT · HERMIPPVS · P · VEDII · POLLIONIS · DISPENSATOR*

Corvus did not move his shoulder under the charcoal-hod. Only his right thumb inside his torn tunic pocket slid once across the pale half-moon scar on his knuckle.

*Hermippus, steward of Publius Vedius Pollio.*

The very freedman Berenice had named on their first morning as ground-collector for the three Argiletum fulleries under their floorboards. Corvus's dead father had borrowed those three thousand two hundred sesterces at twelve percent a year not from Vedius, from a squat Tiburtine cattle-broker called Numerius who smelled of fried onions and bad teeth; which meant Vedius's counting-house had been sweeping the upper Tiber valley with a fine-toothed iron comb since the clay-shacks burned, buying up every small paper claim connected to Figlinae Subortanae at six sesterces on the ten so that if dead Corvus's son ever poked his head above ground inside a Roman court or tribe-roll, Hermippus could clap a judgment-summons on his collar before he drew breath.

Under the Twelve Tables men used to chop a defaulted debtor into weighed pieces and divide his ribs among creditors; in gentle old Emperor Antoninus's Golden Age they merely hauled a penniless *humilior* heir who had not formally abstained from his father's insolvent estate before the magistrate, stripped his cloak, and set him working off principal and interest in a chained brick-gang until his spine folded. And to abstain formally before the Urban Praetor required appearing in person under one's full citizen name, where Scaeva's runners waited beside the scribe's bench with a rope.

Neat as a survey triangle. Three sides, no way out.

"Boy," an irritable Greek voice snapped behind his left heel. "Are you selling that charcoal to the treasury rats, or waiting until Jupiter melts it with lightning? Move your basket off the third step; you're blocking Senator Priscus's clients."

Corvus stepped half a pace sideways onto the worn plinth, dipping his chin like any dull street-carrier, and let six morning clients in damp chalked *togae* troop past him up the treasury stair behind two men who did not hurry for anyone.

The elder was fifty-five or close to it, tall, stoop-shouldered as an iron crane, iron-grey beard cropped two fingers short of the jawbone in the severe military cut of Trajan's old legates. He wore the broad purple senatorial *laticlavius* stripe down a tunic of unbleached Sabine wool and a *toga* hemmed conspicuously higher above the ankle than fashion liked, advertising to the entire Forum that **Quintus Servilius Priscus** wasted neither Syrian dye nor idle cloth. Between thumb and forefinger he carried three dry fennel seeds from a little horn box, cracking one between yellow back molars against morning bile.

Half a step at his left elbow walked the Greek who had spoken: fortyish, clean-shaven twice a day until his hollow cheeks shone blue-olive in the cold, wearing an ink-spotted dark grey *paenula* cloak open at the throat, where a greased leather cord suspended three flat bone wax-scrapers (*scalpra*) of varying widths that knocked softly together like surgical blades whenever he turned.

They stopped four feet from Corvus's charcoal-hod while the treasury porter unlocked the inner grate for a clerk. Neither looked at Corvus; in Rome a hod-boy was a post with ears that didn't count.

"Hermippus filed counter-tallies yesterday evening with the trustees' arbiter," the Greek—*Phileros*, Priscus called him a breath later—was saying in low, rapid Attic Greek, holding open a four-leaved boxwood tablet. "All stamped *Opus Doliare ex Praediis Domitiae Lucillae*, consular year of Silvanus and Augurinus, certifying that the Subortanae kilns delivered only eleven thousand two-foot bricks between April and the September rains because the clay-vein flooded."

Senator Priscus cracked a second fennel seed between his teeth. His eyes, pale grey and flat as slate roof-tiles, scanned down across the Forum pavement toward the white marble portico of the Basilica Julia where court-curtains were already going up. "Flooded in July, Phileros? The Tiber dropped so low at Prima Porta this August you could wade across dry-ankled carrying a sucking-pig. Where did the missing forty thousand bricks go?"

"Into three new six-storey *insulae* going up behind the Vicus Patricius under Hermippus's building lease, beyond doubt," Phileros replied dryly. "Proving it before Rusticus's trust-auditors is another dish of fish. The bailiff at Subortanae—a former guard-sergeant named Scaeva—swore affidavit before an aedile's notary that a runaway Sabine surveyor's brat burned the tally-shed on the Ides of October and bolted with the workshop master-roll and one bronze consular die. Without an original pre-fire kiln leaf or a witness from the trench, Hermippus's sworn numbers stand."

Priscus spat dry fennel husk onto the travertine step two inches from Corvus's bare big toe.

"Vedius Pollio steals dead Domitia's clay while Marcus Caesar is still wearing black wool for his mother," Priscus muttered in Latin hard enough to drive nails, "and uses the profits to buy Spanish horses for young Lucius Commodus's stable-boys. Find me that runaway Sabine brat or find me a mason who laid those stolen bricks, Phileros, before the winter court-recess closes the audit."

"If Scaeva finds the boy first," Phileros murmured, bone wax-scrapers knocking under his chin as the iron treasury gate swung wide, "you'll need a dredge-hook at Ostia to take his deposition, patron."

They passed inside the bronze doors of Saturn.

Corvus stood motionless beside the broken water-spout for twenty full breaths after their clients vanished into the vault gloom, staring straight ahead at the gilded milestone (*Milliarium Aureum*) across the road where every highway of the Empire began, until his heartbeat slowed from a gallop back to a walk.

*Burned the tally-shed himself,* Scaeva had sworn.

And Senator Quintus Servilius Priscus—a consular patrician whose word could squash Hermippus's debt-collector like a bedbug under a sandal—wanted one pre-fire Subortanae kiln leaf bad enough to set his chief Greek *tabularius* hunting river-mud for it.

Corvus shifted the cracked willow hod onto his other shoulder, stepped carefully over Priscus's spat fennel husk without smudging it, and walked east toward the Forum Transitorium to earn his six copper asses before noon.

* * *

Behind Minerva's narrow colonnade, wedged into an upstairs brick gallery above a noisy wheelwright's yard and an importer of dried Damascus plums, **Sextus Pompeius Festus the Younger**—no relation to the lexicographer except by stolen pretension, a plump Sicilian Greek in a violet-bordered pallium smelled through with quince-pomade—taught sixteen rich boys at two thousand sesterces a head how to wave their right hands like Cicero while accusing imaginary stepmothers of poisoning imaginary generals.

Corvus got six copper *asses* a morning there—half what he earned treading piss at Lucceius's fullery in the late shift, and worth ten times the difference—to carry hot charcoal up to the three iron tripod-braziers, sweep plum-stones off the mosaic floor, and plane the previous day's ruined metaphors smooth on forty boxwood wax tablets with a warmed iron *spathula* while the Sicilian paced between two plaster busts of Demosthenes and Hortensius.

Kneeling in the cold draught beside the back pillar with a stack of scarred tablets across his thighs, Corvus kept his eyes down on the beeswax and drank every syllable in the room like stolen Falernian.

For nineteen mornings he had memorized everything Festus threw at those sixteen bored, perfumed skulls: the five parts of a forensic oration (*exordium, narratio, partitio, confirmatio, peroratio*); Hermagoras's doctrine of *status*—whether the fight turned on *coniectura* (did the hand strike?), *definitio* (what did law call the blow?), or *qualitas* (was the blow lawful?); how to drop the voice a fifth at the end of a *membrum* before driving home the *cretic-trochee* cadence (*esse videatur*) that made grey-bearded Centumviral judges nod in their woollen mufflers without noticing a witness had lied about the date. Half the patrician boys yawned behind oiled knuckles, scratched caricatures of charioteers into their wax margins, or whispered bets on Saturday's gladiators while Corvus, scraping their blunders smooth with a hot iron knife, stored every rule away behind his forehead beside his father's land-tables.

That morning's *controversia* was property law, drawn straight from the Urban Praetor's Edict because Senator Priscus's nineteen-year-old nephew **Aulus Volusenus Niger** was slated to sit second bench next spring in his first Centumviral inheritance cause and needed drilling before he embarrassed his uncle's house.

Volusenus stood on the low cedar dais in the center of the gallery, throwing back the spotless, chalk-whitened fold of his *toga* over his left shoulder so sunlight flashed off the heavy red carnelian *quadriga* signet ring on his fourth finger. He was tall, broad-hipped, handsome in a soft, heavy-lipped patrician way, smelling ten feet off of Cosmus's cinnamon-myrrh hair-oil; two slave *pedisequi* sat on folding stools behind him holding his spare handkerchiefs and a silver flask of warm honey-water for his throat.

"*Iudices!*" Volusenus declaimed, voice booming pleasantly off the brick barrel-vault while the wheelwright's iron tire-hammer clanged underneath. "Look upon my client's ancestral boundary! Before the November floods came down from the Sabine hills, fifty *iugera* of rich willow-meadow lay north of the river bend under Caius's plough, marked by terminal stones consecrated to Jupiter Terminus since King Numa's reign! Overnight the raging Tiber tears away ten *iugera* in a single block—oak trees, boundary-cippus, vine-props standing whole—and welds that soil against Maevius's downstream bank! Maevius claims windfall by *alluvio*! Yet what says natural equity, judges? Does a thief who catches my cloak when the wind blows it off my shoulders become master of the wool?"

Four boys on the front bench clapped politely; Festus the Sicilian pursed plum-stained lips, wagging one ringed finger gently to mark rhythm.

"Good fire in the chest, Aulus Volusenus, crisp metaphor on the wind-blown cloak," Festus purred, "though when you argue before the Fourth Spear next spring, give the jurists one grain of black-letter bone under the sauce! Suppose Maevius's advocate cites Sabinus and Gaius against you, claiming river-bank additions pass *iure gentium* the instant mud touches mud—how do you compute the lost boundary line once the river shifts its bed?"

Volusenus twisted his carnelian *quadriga* ring halfway round his finger, waving an airy white hand that had never held anything heavier than a dice-box. "Simple arithmetic, master! You sight straight south from the old hill-stone to the opposite willow-stump at ninety degrees, halve the chord of the river-bend by length alone, and demand ten *iugera* back by *vindicatio* on the spot, trees and all, from the day the mud touched!"

Down by the back pillar, Corvus's warmed iron *spathula* stopped mid-scrape across a wax leaf.

He told himself to shut his teeth. He had six copper *asses* coming at noon, his shin was almost healed under Lucida's stolen sheep-fat, and nobody paid charcoal-boys to know Gaius from turnip-tops. Yet hearing a man in a thousand-sesterce tunic halve a curved river-arc by a single straight chord—an error Gaius Cassius Corvus would have beaten out of an eleven-year-old boy with a thorn-switch in freezing rain—made Corvus's right thumb rub hard against his scarred knuckle before his brain caught his tongue.

"Then Maevius keeps all ten *iugera* and laughs you out of the Basilica before lunch," Corvus said to the wax tablet on his knee.

He had not meant to pitch it loud; only the wheelwright's hammer downstairs paused between blows at that exact instant, leaving the vaulted gallery dead quiet except for pine-charcoal popping in the brazier.

Sixteen oiled heads turned as one toward the back pillar.

Festus blinked as if a terracotta floor-tile had suddenly quoted Homer. Up on the cedar dais, Volusenus's heavy patrician jaw flushed dull brick-red from collar to ear-tip.

"Did the coal-bucket squeak?" Volusenus asked the front bench, drawing an obliging titter from two equestrian cousins. He stepped to the edge of the dais, squinting down past his own chalked hem at Corvus kneeling among wax-shavings in a patched fuller-stained tunic with soot smeared across one collarbone. "Stand up when you look at your betters, gutter-rat. What did you mumble?"

Corvus set the iron *spathula* neatly across the charcoal rim so it would not scorch the mosaic, wiped both palms down his thighs, and stood up. He was half a head shorter than Volusenus, three stone lighter, barefoot on November stone; he kept his eyes fixed level on the clasp of Volusenus's tunic rather than dropping them to the floor, and his blinking stopped dead.

"Two mistakes, master," Corvus said in clean, unhurried Latin, clipping the broad Sabine *o* flat the way he had practiced listening to Festus's best scrolls for three weeks. "First in law: sudden avulsion—*vis fluminis* tearing away a whole recognizable block with standing oaks—is not gradual *alluvio* at all. Under Gaius and Julian's Edict, Caius still owns the soil *until* those oak roots strike new living fiber into Maevius's subsoil. Claim from the day the mud touched and you concede incorporation, which kills your own *vindicatio*."

Behind the doorway curtain at the head of the outside stair, unnoticed by the sixteen boys staring at Corvus's bare feet, a man in a dark grey *paenula* cloak had paused with a bundle of sealed scroll-cases under his arm: **Phileros**, come up from the Forum Transitorium to collect young Volusenus for his uncle Priscus's midday walk. Three bone wax-scrapers hung motionless at his throat as his sharp Greek eyes locked onto Corvus's profile.

Festus the Sicilian's mouth had fallen slightly open. "And—and the second mistake, boy?"

"Geometry," Corvus said, pointing one soot-darkened finger at the invisible river on the mosaic floor between himself and the dais. "Halving the straight chord of a parabolic river-bend cheats your own client of nearly a seventh of his meadow: the *segmentum* between arc and string. A licensed *agrimensor* never sights from a shifting willow-stump; he backs up onto dry *ager arcifinius* above flood-mark, sets his *groma* cross-staff on two fixed *rigores*, drops perpendicular offsets every twenty *actus* into the old dry channel, and sums the trapezoids. Otherwise your opponent calls one ten-denarius land-surveyor after lunch and proves your claim-formula over-demanded by half a *iugerum*—*plus petitio loco*—which forfeits the entire suit without appeal."

Dead silence hung under the brick vault. Downstairs the wheelwright's hammer started clanging again: *clink, clink, clink*.

One of the equestrian cousins in the second row let out an involuntary snort of laughter behind his hand before choking it off.

Volusenus came off the cedar dais in two long strides, cinnamon hair-oil reeking hot as he reached Corvus's pillar. Corvus saw the blow coming two heartbeats early—Volusenus dropped his right shoulder like an untrained tavern brawler—and every muscle in Corvus's legs coiled to step inside the swing and drive his forehead up under the patrician's soft chin the way he had broken the yellow-ribboned dandy at the Firewall arch.

Halfway through shifting his weight, Corvus stopped himself cold.

Strike a senator's nephew in front of sixteen citizen witnesses and two slave footmen, and the Urban Prefect's lictors peeled the skin off a fifteen-year-old *humilior*'s ribs with elm rods before sunset, Crustumina tribe or no Crustumina tribe.

So Corvus locked his heels flat on the mosaic and took it.

Volusenus did not even use his fist; he slashed sideways with the heavy brass-butt end of his eight-inch ivory writing-stylus, catching Corvus hard across the left corner of his mouth. Teeth punched through inner lip; hot salt blood filled Corvus's tongue instantly. The impact knocked his temple back against the plastered brick pier, sending stars skittering white across the bust of Demosthenes.

"Six asses to scrape wax, dung-beetle, not to bark law at men whose threshold you aren't clean enough to piss against!" Volusenus panted, wiping a tiny fleck of Corvus's spit-blood off his ivory stylus onto Corvus's soot-streaked shoulder before turning on Festus. "Keep kennel-sweepings out of the hall, Greek, or my uncle hears what kind of insolence two thousand sesterces buys here! Get my cloak, Dama!"

Festus fluttered both plump hands in theatrical horror, already reaching for a switch to shoo Corvus toward the back buckets so the class wouldn't walk out. "Out to the ash-yard, boy—go rinse your mouth at the cistern and don't show your nose upstairs until they've gone—"

Corvus spat a dark red mouthful neatly into the cold ash-pan beside the brazier—not onto Festus's mosaic, because someone else would only get beaten cleaning it—picked up his iron *spathula*, laid it straight alongside the scraped boxwood stack, and walked out onto the open brick stair-landing without touching his split lip.

Volusenus and his two footmen pushed past him going down, laughing loudly now about some dancing-girl from Gades to cover the awkwardness.

Phileros lingered two steps behind them on the landing, pretending to tighten the leather strap round his scroll-cylinders until Volusenus's white *toga* cleared the wheelwright's archway below. Then the Greek archive-freedman looked sideways at Corvus standing against the parapet with blood beading dark down his chin.

"Plus petitio loco," Phileros murmured in Greek soft as pumice on papyrus. "Where does a barefoot charcoal-carrier with a Crustumina burr learn Julian's rule on over-claiming by survey-trapezoid, boy?"

Corvus looked straight at the three bone wax-scrapers hanging at Phileros's throat, recognizing the exact curve of the narrowest blade: treasury clerks used that bevel to lift spoiled seals cleanly off pine leaves without scarring the grain underneath.

"My father carried a *groma* before he drank it," Corvus answered in Greek just as quiet, tasting iron on his teeth. "And Festus leaves his *Institutes* unrolled on the lectern while he eats plums."

Phileros's thin eyebrows lifted the thickness of a coin. His gaze traveled slowly down from Corvus's split mouth to the soot on his collarbone—noting instantaneously that the soot sat only on top of old yellow fuller's-ammonia bleaching along the wool hem—and dropped lower still to the pale half-moon scar across Corvus's right thumb knuckle: the exact diameter of a round two-inch clay *signaculum* handle when hot bronze kicked sideways in the press.

Neither of them said *Domitia Lucilla*. Neither of them said *Hermippus*.

Down in the yard Volusenus shouted impatiently for his uncle's clerk.

"There is a fried-fish and wine stall called the Three Cranes in the crooked lane behind the Vicus Cuprius," Phileros said conversationally, tucking his scroll-case back under his elbow. "The Syrian behind the counter waters his Veientine less after the second watch, because the *vigiles* corporal drinks in the back booth. If a surveyor's son happened to want forty sesterces for an old pine shingle before frost cracks it, without any one-eared camp-dogs sniffing the stair, a man might sit there tonight until the oil-lamp burns half down."

Phileros trotted lightly down the brick steps into the noon glare without looking back.

Corvus leaned both forearms on the cold brick parapet, spat once more into the wheelwright's sawdust twenty feet below, and counted the heartbeat thudding in his cut lip.

* * *

He did not tell Lucida where he was going when dusk fell.

That was the part that sat cold as unslaked lime behind his breastbone while he waited for Berenice and Lucida to turn toward the back dormer over their evening mortar of white lead. Telling Lucida would have meant Lucida coming down the Vicus Cuprius fifty paces behind him in the rain with her honed amphora shard tucked in her waist-cord; and if Phileros turned out to have sold the meeting to Hermippus or Scaeva after all—unlikely, since Senator Priscus hated Vedius's guts, yet freedmen took coin with both hands—then Lucida walking into the Three Cranes would put Lucida straight back into Vedius Pollio's property-chest while Corvus got his ribs broken in an alley.

Whereas if Corvus went alone carrying *one* secondary pine leaf—Number Four out of the six stolen tallies, recording only nine hundred *tegulae* roof-tiles carted from Kiln Two in August before the big double-stamping fraud even peaked—and left the bronze *signaculum*, his cedar birth-diptych, and the other five leaves hidden safe under Berenice's hearth-brick with Lucida upstairs, then the worst Scaeva could take off Corvus's carcass that night was one sliver of pine and seventy-six pounds of Sabine bone.

"Lucceius's Greek carder says a bookseller down on the Argiletum wants two boys to shift damp papyrus crates out of a leaking cellar after the first watch," Corvus told Lucida casually as he knotted a strip of rag round his ankles against the November mud. In the dim tallow-light behind Berenice's screen he kept the left side of his face angled away from the wick; when Lucida had glanced at his swollen lip coming up from the fullery an hour back, he had told her a wet timber slipped on Festus's stair. "Pays six sesterces flat if we finish before the third watch."

Lucida paused with the porphyry muller in both wet white-lead-streaked hands, studying his shoulder-blades across the smoky garret. Her dark amber eyes narrowed half a hair—fifteen years together meant she heard the slight over-smoothness in his vowels whenever he rounded off a corner—then dropped to his bruised knuckles.

"Leave your fuller's cloak dry here and take Beroe's oiled goat-hide cape," Lucida said after two breaths, nodding toward the peg by the door. "If you catch chest-pleurisy hauling wet crates in November rain, medicine costs more than six sesterces. Wait—come here."

Before he could duck out the fir door she wiped lead-paste meticulously off two fingers onto a clean rag, stepped close enough that he smelled bitter almond, sharp vinegar, and warm girl-skin rising off her collar, and touched one cold fingertip just below his split lip: not pressing the cut, only tilting his chin half an inch toward the lamp so she saw the clean, straight cut an ivory stylus-butt left, nothing at all like rough pine timber.

She said nothing about timber. Her eyes held his for three beats, giving him room to speak if he chose, and when he kept his jaw shut, she pulled the oiled goat-hide hood up over his wet black hair with a single brisk tug.

"Back before the third watch horn blows," Lucida murmured in low Greek so Berenice wouldn't hear. "Or I come looking with a butcher's hook, Sabine."

"Before the third watch," Corvus promised, and went down the seventy-eight dark steps feeling dirtier than eight hours in Lucceius's piss-vat had ever made him.

Rain fell thin and sleet-sharp across the Subura, turning horse-dung and fuller-sludge on the cobbles into black grease. Heavy freight-carts had the main Clivus choked hub-to-hub, oxen steaming under wet hides while link-boys cursed drowned torches, so Corvus threaded the narrow stepped cut-ways behind the coppersmiths' stalls, counting every turn, checking shadows behind water-spouts, ducking twice into dark doorway niches until he was dead certain nobody followed his heels.

The **Popina of the Three Cranes** sat wedged below street-level at the elbow where a vaulted sewer-spur gurgled down toward the Forum Transitorium: three chipped red cranes painted on wet stucco beside a counter of masonry into which four steaming dolia jars were sunk up to their rims, ladling hot watered wine spiced with cheap wormwood, boiled sheep's trotters, and salted lupin-beans to wet carters and night-watchmen.

In the low back room behind a greasy felt curtain, two clay triple-spouted oil-lamps hung on iron chains above three scarred beech tables. Four off-duty *vigiles* of the Fourth Cohort in brown undress tunics rattled knucklebones (*tali*) in a horn cup by the charcoal pan, helmets stacked under the bench; in the far corner booth, back flat against solid tufa wall where neither street door nor privy-passage could surprise him, sat **Phileros** alone behind a pewter jug of dark red Veientine and two clay cups.

Corvus slipped wet goat-hide off his head, shook rain onto the sawdust, and slid onto the narrow bench opposite without waiting to be asked.

Phileros did not look at Corvus's hands; he looked past Corvus's left ear at the felt curtain for five slow counts to make sure no shadow stopped behind the wool, then pushed the second clay cup across scarred beechwood.

"Drink it while it's hot," Phileros said in low Latin. "The Syrian boils cassia-bark twigs in it to hide the vinegar, yet it keeps the river-damp out of the lungs. Volusenus told his uncle over dinner that a half-wild Sabine fuller's-boy corrected his *alluvio* geometry in front of Festus's whole upper bench, and demanded Festus flog you out of the quarter."

Corvus wrapped cold fingers round the warm clay cup without drinking yet. "And what did Senator Priscus say?"

"Senator Priscus told his nephew that a patrician who gets beaten on Julian's Edict by a charcoal-boy should read more Gaius and wear less cinnamon oil," Phileros replied dryly, allowing one corner of his shaved mouth to twitch. "Then he told me to find out whether you stole your Latin along with your brick-wax, Lucius Cassius Corvus, son of Gaius, of the Crustumina tribe."

Corvus's fingers did not tighten on the clay cup; only the grey-green eyes went flat and still over the steam. So Phileros had gone straight back to the Temple of Saturn after lunch and cross-checked *Crustumina* against Hermippus's November debt-board in half an hour. Good. Better dealing with a sharp knife than a dull hoe.

"Hermippus chalked three thousand two hundred sesterces against my dead father's name on Saturn's fourth board," Corvus said evenly. "Bought off Numerius the cattle-broker since the Ides of October so Vedius Pollio can clap irons on me if I step near the Praetor's tribunal to file abstention—or near Senator Priscus's auditors to testify how many bricks came out of Subortanae."

"Three thousand two hundred principal, plus four hundred eighty accrued *centesimae* interest," Phileros corrected mildly, nibbling a salted lupin-bean and slipping the skin neatly onto the table edge. "Hermippus registered transfer of the *chirographum* eight days ago through Aulus Castricius's bank in the Basilica Argentaria. Thorough fellow, Hermippus. Though careless in small countryside matters, such as letting a drunken tally-clerk named Rufus sleep beside six pre-stamped kiln-leaves on the night of the fourteenth."

Across the low room the four *vigiles* roared as someone threw a *Venus*—all four knucklebones landing different faces up—and copper *asses* clattered across wet wood.

Phileros leaned two inches closer across the table. Up close he smelled of cedar scroll-oil, gall-ink, and bitter wormwood wine. "Put what you brought flat under your palm on the bench, boy. If it carries Rufus's own cursive tally-mark and the pre-dated workshop stamp before the September rains, forty sesterces in clean silver sit in my belt-pouch right now."

Corvus reached inside his tunic—not to his waist, behind his left armpit where he had strapped the single pine leaf flat with a strip of rag—and slid Leaf Four face-down onto the scarred beechwood, keeping four fingers planted firmly over the corner.

"Forty sesterces in silver," Corvus said quietly, "buys you a look, Phileros. Not the wood."

Phileros stared down at Corvus's soot-grained knuckles, then up into Corvus's cut mouth. "Don't play forum-usurer before your beard grows, boy. Scaeva's runners asked for a Sabine fifteen-year-old and a dark Syrian girl at two Trans-Tiberim bathhouses yesterday afternoon. By next *nundinae* they work up the Argiletum stair by stair. Forty sesterces buys you bread to walk to Campania."

"Walk to Campania and Scaeva rides us down on hired mules before Bovillae," Corvus said, thumb rubbing once, slow and deliberate, across his white half-moon knuckle-scar as the cold stress-lock clicked shut behind his eyes, pushing him straight past caution into the bigger bet. "Besides, one August tile-leaf doesn't break Hermippus's affidavit before Rusticus's trust-arbiters. Hermippus simply says Rufus mis-dated one wet rack of roof-tiles and docks Rufus's wages. To prove forty thousand stolen *bipedales* went straight out of dead Domitia Lucilla's clay-pits into Vedius Pollio's private Subura tenements, Senator Priscus needs three things: this leaf to show the double-count started in summer; an eyewitness who stood in Number Three trench and knows which barge-masters hauled the unrecorded night-loads downstream; and somebody small enough to crawl inside those new Vicus Patricius walls before plaster goes on and read the backwards consular stamps baked into the mortar-courses."

Phileros stopped chewing his lupin-bean. For five full breaths neither man blinked in the red lamp-smoke.

"And what," the Greek asked very softly, "does this small, dirty miracle-worker imagine Senator Quintus Servilius Priscus pays for all three?"

"Forty sesterces silver on the table tonight for Leaf Four," Corvus said without drawing breath. "Second: Senator Priscus's outer notary enters a pending *in integrum restitutio* petition on behalf of a minor under twenty-five—Lucius Cassius Corvus, aged fifteen—staying execution on Hermippus's bought *chirographum* for six months while my minority status is examined, filed inside a batch of forty routine estate stays so Hermippus's clerk doesn't spot the name until December. Third: a stool in your outer vestibule four afternoons a week scraping Priscus's spoilt wax and copying court-pleadings, at three sesterces a day, where Scaeva's street-dogs cannot lay a finger on Senator Priscus's household runner without answering to the Urban Prefect."

Phileros gazed at him for so long that grease congealed white round the rim of the sheep's-trotter bowl on the next table. Then the Greek let out a silent breath through his nose, reached into his leather belt-wallet, and laid **ten silver denarii**—forty sesterces, heavy Antonine silver with Peace holding an olive-branch on the reverse—in a neat vertical column beside Corvus's scarred thumb.

"Sixteen sesterces a week: four afternoons at four sesterces, starting the day after tomorrow at the eighth hour, back service-gate on the Clivus Scauri," Phileros said, raising Corvus's wage by one sestertius a day without being asked—not from kindness, because four sesterces bound a boy under formal *locatio operarum* household hire whereas three left him casual street-labor. "You wash your feet at the gate-trough before touching my vestibule floor, you never speak to Senator Priscus unless Senator Priscus speaks to your sandals first, and if you ever quote Gaius aloud in front of young Volusenus inside our walls, I feed your tongue to the kitchen eels myself. Take your hand off the pine."

Corvus swept the ten silver denarii off the beechwood with his left hand while lifting his right off Leaf Four in the same clean motion.

Phileros tilted the thin waxed pine tablet three inches toward the oil-lamp, never wide enough for the dice-playing *vigiles* across the smoke to glimpse, checked Rufus's cramped numeral strokes and the faint red clay thumb-smear at the bottom margin with a connoisseur's squint, and slid it smoothly inside his scroll-case.

"Where are the other five leaves and the bronze die, Sabine?" Phileros asked pleasantly, reaching for his wine-cup.

"In dry earth outside the walls where rivers don't flood," Corvus lied without a flicker. "When the stay on my father's debt-bond bears the Praetor's seal in December, we dig again."

Phileros drank two swallows of spiced Veientine, set the clay cup down, and studied Corvus's cut mouth with something halfway between professional disgust and cold kinship. "How old did you say you were—fifteen? By forty you'll either sit on the Praetor's bench in white wool or hang in iron chains off the Gemonian Stairs, boy, and I wouldn't lay two copper *asses* either way. Drink your vinegar before the watch changes."

Corvus drained the hot bitter wine in three gulps—it burned down his empty gullet into his ribs like liquid charcoal—tucked six of the ten silver denarii tight inside the split hem of his inner waist-band where even Berenice wouldn't feel them, and kept **four silver denarii**—sixteen sesterces, still nearly three times the six sesterces he had promised Lucida—loose in his cheek-pouch and fist as he stepped back out into the freezing November rain.

Halfway up the black canyon of the Clivus Suburanus, hugging the wet travertine base of a baker's archway while three chained timber-wagons groaned downhill showering mud from locked iron brake-shoes, Corvus stopped under a sputtering shrine-lamp to the *Lares Compitales* and stared down at the four wet silver coins resting in his palm.

*Tell her all of it upstairs,* said the voice that had shared every crust and frost-bite with her since they were seven years old hiding stolen figs behind Kiln Three. *Put all ten silver pieces in her lap, tell her Phileros has Leaf Four, tell her Senator Priscus's back gate opens day after tomorrow on the Caelian Hill.*

Then he remembered Lucida's fierce, proud chin lifting in Berenice's garret when she heard Vedius Pollio owned her deathbed; remembered how fast she had stepped onto the fuller's coping between Sticho's rod and Sura's burned arm; knew, as surely as he knew the width of that alley, that if Lucida learned tonight that Senator Priscus was hunting Vedius's Subura tenements and brick-leases, she wouldn't stay crouched behind Berenice's reed screen grinding white lead while Corvus carried briefs. She would walk straight down toward the fire to cut her own piece out of Vedius's hide before anyone could treat her as cargo to be rescued.

*Six months,* Corvus told the rain dripping off the shrine-eaves. *Let me get the Praetor's stay sealed on my father's debt and save two hundred sesterces from Priscus's vestibule first, so when Scaeva comes up the stair we have iron under our feet. What she doesn't know until December cannot get her killed in November.*

Down toward the Forum the bronze *bucina* horn of the *Cohortes Vigilum* sounded the third watch of the night: long, brazen, mournful across a hundred thousand wet tile roofs.

Corvus slipped the six hidden denarii flat against his hipbone, climbed the seventy-eight dark steps two at a stride before Lucida could come down the lane with her butcher's hook, and pushed open Berenice's fir door.

Berenice was already snoring harsh and regular through her broken teeth under the wolf-pelt by the dead brazier. Behind the reed screen a single tiny night-wick floated in olive-dregs; Lucida sat cross-legged under the fuller's cloak, hair down in a thick black river over her shoulders to dry, her honed amphora shard resting bare across her knee while she stitched a torn seam in Corvus's spare wool ankle-wrap with bone needle and waxed hemp.

The instant the oak bar dropped back into its brackets, her shoulders loosened half an inch—she had been counting steps on the ladder since the horn blew—and she looked up at his soaked face.

"Late by twelve steps, Sabine," she whispered, setting the amphora shard silently aside on the straw and holding out a dry coarse towel warmed against her own belly under the cloak. "Bookseller's cellar flood?"

"Worse—two crates of rotten Egyptian papyrus split in the mud, and the old miser made me stack every roll dry by lamp-light before he unlocked his cash-box," Corvus whispered back, dropping to his knees on the straw and opening his right fist so **four bright silver denarii**—sixteen sesterces, more silver than either of them had held in one palm since before her mother died—caught the tiny yellow oil-flame between them. "Paid sixteen sesterces instead of six because the other boy bolted when the rain turned to sleet, and wants me back four afternoons a week sorting Greek scroll-tags."

Lucida's breath caught: tiny, sharp, genuine joy lighting her dark eyes gold in the wick-glow as she touched one silver head of Antoninus Pius with a lead-roughened fingertip. Sixteen sesterces was three weeks of garret straw, charcoal for cold nights, real leather soles under both their feet before December frost split their heels, and five days free of Lucceius's burning piss-vats.

"Sixteen," she breathed, looking straight up into his face with fierce, unguarded pride, reaching up with both warm hands to rub sleet-water out of his black hair while staying careful not to brush his split left lip. "I told Beroe you'd own half the street before spring. Come under the wool—your ears are cold as river-stones."

Corvus stripped off the wet outer tunic, careful to fold his inner waist-band flat underneath where the six unmentioned silver coins and Phileros's Caelian gate-hour sat cold as lead weights, and lay down facing her on the narrow barley-straw. Lucida tucked the coarse cloak tight behind his freezing shoulder-blades, pressed her bare feet between his shins to warm them, and blew out the floating wick.

In the pitch dark above the rumbling stone-wagons, Corvus felt her breathing slow against his collarbone, trusting every syllable he had put into her palm; and for a long time after she slept, he lay with both eyes open toward the invisible rafters, listening to November rain beat on the pantiles and tasting salt blood where Volusenus's ivory stylus had cut his mouth.
