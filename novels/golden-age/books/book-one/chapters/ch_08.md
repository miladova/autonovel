# Chapter VIII — A Hundred Modii

*Fourteenth Day Before the Kalends of September, AD 157 · Lucida*

Every chip of grey volcanic stone flying off **Habinnas Barba**'s iron pick smelled of struck flint and burned hair.

It was the fourth watch of an August night hot enough to melt beeswax on a shelf, and down in the sunken basalt courtyard behind the Fish-Market three of Barba's four hourglass-shaped lava mills stood dismantled on oak timber balks. Naked to the waist, sixty-two years old, his barrel chest matted with coarse white curls clotted grey by forty summers of airborne flour-paste, the old Cappadocian straddled the upturned bell of Number Two *catillus* with a horn oil-lamp gripped between his knees, pecking fresh diagonal furrows across worn grey Orvieto stone with a double-ended iron *marra*.

Every fifth blow stopped him short while his ribs hitched in a long, dry, whistling wheeze—*pistrinum* lung, which killed mill-bakers slower than quicklime killed potters and left them spitting stiff white dough-pellets into rags every morning before dawn.

Lucida sat three paces off on an upturned brine-keg in her oldest sleeveless linen shift, dark braids bound tight inside a twisted yellow head-cloth against mill-dust, Sura asleep curled like a barn-cat on clean empty grain-sacks behind her, adding up Barba's greasy beechwood tallies by the light of a second tallow wick.

"Stop pecking the skirt of that stone so deep, Barba, or you'll shred the bran into powder and turn tomorrow's second-grade loaves brown as horse-turds," Lucida said in plain Subura Greek without looking up from his charcoal columns. "Look at your July brushwood bill instead. Who sold you forty bundles of Sabine Broom-faggots at nine asses the bundle when river-drift alder was landing at the Tiber steps for six?"

Barba lowered his iron pick, spat a grey gobbet onto the dry basalt flags, and glared across the lamp-flame with his one clear black eye, the left having clouded milky white ten winters back when a hot oven-flue blew oak-splinters into his face.

"Alder smokes wet and sours the brick dome, girl," Barba growled, voice scraping like two millstones running empty. "You know olive-dregs and ladies' face-paint; don't teach a man who baked army biscuit for Arrian against the Alans how to heat hearth-tile! Why aren't you upstairs sleeping like honest folk?"

"Because upstairs feels like the inside of a boiled sheep's stomach tonight, and because if somebody doesn't read these tallies before the Kalends of March, neither you nor I will have a roof over this courtyard next spring," Lucida answered calmly, turning the split beechwood board round on her knee so her charcoal-blackened finger pointed straight at the bottom notch-row. "Let me read your own winter death-warrant back to you, old man, and tell me where my arithmetic limps."

Barba's good black eye narrowed under flour-crusted brows; he didn't tell her to go upstairs.

"You hold a five-year ground-and-oven *conductio* lease on this courtyard, four Orvieto donkey-mills, two twelve-foot domed brick ovens, and the stone kneading-troughs from **Gaius Munatius**, who mortgaged the reversion last autumn to **Hermippus**—steward to Publius Vedius Pollio," Lucida said, keeping her voice level over that hated name. "Your lease carries a renewal option on the Kalends of March at four thousand eight hundred sesterces a year *provided* your ovens stay enrolled on the Urban Prefect's active *pistrina* list grinding at least fifty *modii* a day. Drop below fifty *modii* for six consecutive months, or default on two quarters' rent, and Hermippus forecloses the equipment-pledge, four lava mills worth two thousand sesterces apiece, two brick ovens worth six thousand—for a song, chains twelve cheap Gallic debt-slaves to those sweep-bars, and throws you out on the Forum Piscarium cobbles to beg fish-heads."

Barba's scarred thumb rubbed slowly along the iron bevel of his stone-pick. "I buried two mill-asses of glanders in April, girl, and state *annona* clerks won't issue subsidized African wheat below a hundred *modii* daily capacity. Private grain-jobbers at the Porticus Minucia charge four sesterces and a half the *modius* on thirty-day credit, and half the sacks they dump at my gate are three inches deep in black chalk-dust and dead grain-weevils!"

Lucida rose off the brine-keg, walked over to the open sack of private "Campanian" wheat leaning against Trough One, plunged her bare arm straight down to the elbow into the warm grain—never skimming the clean top layer, and brought up a fistful from the bottom seam.

She spread the kernels across the flat oak balk directly under Barba's lamp.

"Four and a half sesterces a *modius* from **Numerius Vettius**, wasn't it?" Lucida asked softly. With one fingernail she separated seven hollow, pin-holed hulls chewed empty by *curculio* beetles, three grey pellets of dried river-clay sized to pass a coarse sieve and add dead weight on the steelyard, and two shriveled black seeds of bearded darnel (*lolium*)—the dizzying weed that made cheap Subura bread-eaters stagger blind with *temulentia* headaches! "Numerius Vettius borrows his warehouse silver at one percent a month from **Hermippus's own freedman**, Barba! Every short-weight sack of weevil-husks Vettius sells you on credit bleeds twelve percent off your flour-yield while choking your sieves, deliberately starving your daily output below fifty *modii* so Hermippus takes your four lava mills free in March!"

For ten slow breaths the only sound in the stifling courtyard was the restless stamp of Barba's surviving one-eared mule inside the dark stable-stall and the distant splash of the Fish-Market fountain outside the gate.

Barba stared down at the grey clay pellets and black darnel seeds on the oak beam, his jaw working inside his flour-stiffened beard until the tendons stood out like harness-ropes along his neck.

"Filthy Smyrniot jackals," the old Cappadocian whispered in Greek, raising the four-pound iron stone-pick three inches as though Vettius's skull lay on the beam, then letting it thud dull against the wood as another dry wheeze rattled his chest. "Knowing who poisons the well doesn't buy clean wheat, Tyrian. To throw Vettius out, buy five sound Reatine mill-mules, hire four free night-kneaders, and pay cash on the Ostian barges for clean Sicilian hard-wheat until our daily grind hits **a hundred *modii***—where the *annona* prefect has to grant us state quota wheat at three sesterces flat, takes **eight thousand sesterces** hard silver! Have you got eight thousand sesterces hidden under your little mute girl's straw?"

"I will have eight thousand sesterces on that kneading-table before the middle of February," Lucida said, meeting his solitary fierce eye across the tallow-smoke without blinking, "on three conditions written into a sworn *societas* contract and registered at the Urban Prefect's mill-office."

Barba snorted chalk out of his moustache. "Listen to fifteen years old talking eight thousand sesterces! Let's hear the three hooks inside the dough."

"Sixteen next month," Lucida corrected crisply, "and every copper of my silver will ring clean on a touchstone. First: we register the *pistrinum* jointly at the Prefecture of the *Annona* with **Habinnas Barba and Lucida, freedwoman's daughter**, named side by side as co-*exercitores* personally superintending a minimum daily grind and bake of **one hundred five *modii***—five *modii* safety cushion above Trajan's hundred-modius citizenship threshold, for **three unbroken years**, one thousand and ninety-five days without a single unbaked dawn, even on holidays or floods."

Understanding lit Barba's good black eye at once; every baker in Rome knew Trajan's Edict, because half the rich freedmen in the *corpus pistorum* had climbed out of Junian Latin twilight across four lava millstones.

"Ah," Barba grunted, scratching his sweaty collarbone with chalky knuckles. "Trajan's three-year shoe! That's why you've been counting my oven-bricks since May instead of peddling perfume in deep shade! And Hook Two?"

"Hook Two: **I control all grain purchasing, fuel contracts, and wholesale bread-tallies**; you control stone-dressing, yeast-leavening, and oven-heat. Not one sack crosses that threshold until my bronze grain-thief probes the bottom seam. Hook Three: out of net monthly silver after rent, fodder, and loan-amortization, we split fifty-fifty—and if you die of that rattle in your chest before my thousand and ninety-fifth day finishes, your half of the lease and equipment passes to me for six hundred sesterces burial-honors in your *collegium* columbarium rather than falling intestate to any outside creditor."

Barba stared at her across the upturned volcanic bell for a long, rasping count of five, then slapped his heavy, scarred palm hard against the grey Orvieto lava so dust puffed white between them.

"Blunt as a butcher's cleaver!" the old soldier-baker barked, almost grinning through broken yellow teeth. "My *collegium* is **Aesculapius and Hygia** out past the Porta Capena—sixty honest craftsmen, decent Falernian at the February death-suppers, and a dry brick niche where rain doesn't rot your urn. Bring me eight thousand clean sesterces before Hermippus's bailiff knocks in February, little Tyrian, and by the Mountain Mother of Comana we'll grind a hundred and ten *modii* every dawn till Caesar signs your citizen tablet himself!"

* * *

Eight thousand sesterces in six months could never come out of cracked Emporium amphorae alone, especially while **eighty sesterces** (20 denarii) had to walk secretly every Kalends into Hermippus's back rent-office off the Subura to keep Publius Vedius Pollio's silver pear-knife folded shut.

By August Lucida had cleared four separate March, April, May, June, July, and August Kalends tributes—four hundred eighty sesterces bled silently off her private side-ledger without Corvus ever suspecting a coin was missing from their shared loft savings-box, which already held **one thousand one hundred sesterces** of his steady Caelian clerk's silver and her declared oil-margins. Yet eleven hundred sesterces left nearly seven thousand still to find before February, which was why, at the blazing sixth hour of that same August day while half Rome snored behind wet shutters, Lucida climbed the laurel-shaded steps of the **Caelian Hill** carrying a double-walled cork snow-pail wrapped in damp felt.

Inside the airy north-facing summer *diaeta* of the *Domus Ceionia*, where marble wall-channels trickled chilled Aqua Claudia water behind green Karystian pillars to beat back the Dog-Star heat, **Ceionia Fabia** was not alone today.

Sprawled sideways across a green silk couch near the open colonnade, naked above a loose linen loin-cloth, twenty-six-year-old **Lucius Ceionius Commodus** was tossing hard green unripe figs into the air for a sleek white Laconian greyhound to snap mid-leap while two bath-slaves scraped glistening olive oil and yellow sand off his broad, muscled calves with curved bronze *strigiles*.

Seen ten paces off in harsh midday light without banquet torches, the younger adopted son of Emperor Antoninus Pius looked like an expensive Greek bronze of Apollo somebody had left overnight in a tavern: thick, sunlit golden-brown curls dusted deliberately with fine gold mica-powder by his barber, lazy heavy-lidded eyes full of easy physical good humor, a wide sensual mouth that smiled even while sweating, and not one cruel bone visible in him—only the terrifying carelessness of a young prince who had never once since his cradle picked up anything he dropped himself.

Seated upright on a plain camp-stool in the shade six feet away, wearing a coarse, ink-spotted white woollen tunic despite the ninety-degree heat, dark curly beard trimmed square, pale sunken hollows under dark, chronically sleepless eyes—thirty-six-year-old **Marcus Aurelius Caesar** was correcting marginal notes on a tax-petition from Asia while sipping tepid plain water out of an earthenware mug.

Lucida knelt silently beside Dorcas behind the pierced marble screen at Fabia's elbow, unpacking the cork snow-pail: crushed Praenestine rose-petals, blanched bitter-almond milk, and four grains of powdered Egyptian alum whipped over packed Alban snow to close summer sweat-pores under white lead without caking.

Through the carved marble vine-leaves of the screen, six feet from her ear, imperial Rome talked with its shoes off.

"Drink half a ladle of snow-mulsum before your liver dries into parchment, Mark," Lucius drawled lazily, bouncing up on one oiled elbow as the greyhound gulped a third fig whole. "Even old Father Pius took off his wool belly-band at Lorium Tuesday! What's that scroll, more Bithynian town-councillors whining they spent their aqueduct money on a marble theater?"

Marcus Caesar didn't look up from his reed pen, though the corner of his bearded mouth twitched faintly—genuine, tired affection for a younger brother ten years lighter in the saddle than himself.

"Smyrna grain-shipping exemptions, Lucius," Marcus said, his voice thin from a chronically weak chest, exquisitely precise in every Latin consonant. "If our father grants fifty rich ship-owners immunity from municipal liturgies without checking tonnage, forty smaller bakers in Smyrna pay double tax before winter. Did you read Julian's opinion on the Ostian *navicularii* I sent round yesterday?"

Lucius groaned melodramatically, flopping flat back onto the green silk cushions with one muscular forearm flung over his eyes while the slave finished scraping his ankle. "I read six columns before **Volucer** threw an off-fore shoe at the Green training-track, and the Syrian vet wanted to poultice the frog with turpentine instead of Attic honey and burned vine-ash! Ask me whether turpentine dries a stallion's hoof-wall brittle in August, Mark—there's useful statecraft! Besides, Quintus Servilius Priscus and your gloomy friend Rusticus read enough Julian for ten emperors; why keep dogs and bark yourself?"

Behind the pierced marble screen **Ceionia Fabia**'s narrow shoulders went hard as green Karystian stone under Lucida's cooling rose-sponge.

Marcus did set his reed pen down at that. He looked quietly across at his sprawling adoptive brother for three breaths.

"Because dogs guard whoever feeds them, Lucius," Marcus said mildly, "not whoever sleeps longest on the couch. Father asked twice on the Kalends why you missed the Latin receptor hearings to sit in the *Trigarium* paddock with Vedius Pollio's horse-leech."

*Vedius Pollio.*

Lucida's sponge did not pause by a fraction of a hair across Fabia's pale freckled collarbone, though her ears drank every syllable straight down into cold storage. **Vedius Pollio** wasn't merely entertaining Lucius Commodus with twelve-thousand-sesterce green parakeets; Vedius was worming straight into the young prince's chariot-stables while Senator Priscus and Junius Rusticus, *Corvus's Caelian masters!*—built walls around austere Marcus!

Lucius laughed easily, rolling off the couch to clap Marcus affectionately on one thin wool-clad shoulder, leaving a shiny smear of olive oil on the heir-apparent's clean tunic which Marcus didn't rebuke—and whistled his greyhound to heel.

"Tell Father I'll sit through three solid hours of Umbrian drainage ditches on Thursday without yawning once, provided you let me bet twenty gold pieces on *Volucer* for the September Circus!" Lucius called back brightly, striding off toward the cold plunge-bath with bath-slaves trotting behind him.

A moment later Marcus gathered his Smyrna scrolls, bowed gravely toward his adoptive cousin-by-marriage Fabia behind her screen, and walked out with the slightly stooped, tense-shouldered gait of a man carrying Rome's entire tile roof on six bruised vertebrae.

Only when both princes' footsteps had faded down opposite colonnades did Ceionia Fabia speak.

"Men think crowns are lost in Senate speeches," Fabia hissed softly to her own bronze mirror while Lucida dabbed snow-chilled alum water beneath her jaw. "Crowns are lost because one brother smells of ink and boiled turnips in August while the other lets usurers like Vedius Pollio sell his head-groom rotten Bruttian pitch at triple price! Look at me, Tyrian."

Lucida lifted her eyes into the polished silvered bronze mirror. Over eight months of fortnightly visits, never chattering, never spilling a syllable of December's green phials, always bringing salves cleaner and fresher than old Berenice's greasy thumbs ever compounded—fifteen-year-old Lucida had become the one outside pair of eyes prideful, sidelined Fabia allowed behind her curtain.

"You buy bulk Spanish oil and Syrian gums down on the river-wharves without middle-brokers, don't you?" Fabia asked sharply.

"Direct off Gaditane and Tyrian hulls before warehouse-factors add forty percent, Domina," Lucida answered at once.

"My brother Lucius spends six thousand sesterces a month out of his private allowance on stable-embrocations, Cyprian laurel-berry oil, white wax, pine-rosin, crushed fenugreek, and Chian turpentine—for the twelve prize four-horse teams he backs at the **Factio Prasina** barracks in Campus Martius," Fabia said, green emerald earrings swinging bright against her pale neck. "Right now Vedius Pollio's freedman supplies those stable-chests and skims half into Lucius's betting-tab so Vedius holds my brother's chits! Can you compound clean, unadulterated Green-stable hoof-and-tendon salves in fifty-pound terracotta jars for **three hundred sesterces a month** cash out of *my* steward's chest, cutting Vedius's horse-broker clean out of the *Trigarium* back gate without putting my name on the wagon?"

Three hundred sesterces a month—clearing easily **one hundred forty sesterces net profit every thirty days** above ingredients, almost double her entire secret eighty-sesterce Kalends blood-tax to Vedius, paid straight out of Vedius's own rival's purse for stealing Vedius's stable-racket!

"Give me the Syrian vet's exact weight-ratios on a blank wax leaf, Domina," Lucida said, pulse singing bright and fierce behind serene amber eyes, "and forty-pound stoneware jars sealed in green pitch will sit inside the *Trigarium* stable-gate on the Nones of September at two hundred eighty sesterces, saving your chest twenty sesterces a month to buy the head-groom's silence."

Fabia's thin unpainted mouth curved into genuine, wolfish satisfaction. "Dorcas: count her **thirty silver denarii** advance for jars and Cyprian laurel-oil right now! And don't let Vedius's cross-eyed bailiffs see whose mule drops those pots at the stables, girl, or both of us lose a horse."

* * *

By the eleventh hour of that burning August afternoon, with **one hundred twenty sesterces** of Fabia's bright advance silver tucked safely inside her stitched bodice-hem and her mind already calculating how Green-stable supply plus a burial-club loan could raise Barba's full eight thousand before winter, Lucida met Corvus on the stone steps of the **Pons Aemilius** and led him across the Tiber up the roaring green flank of the **Janiculum Hill**.

High along the ridge Emperor Trajan had brought an entire cold mountain river—the **Aqua Traiana** from Lake Bracciano thirty miles northwest, bursting down the steep eastern slope through stepped masonry mill-races (*euripi*) that drove twenty thunderous overshot oak water-wheels one below another from crest to river-bank.

Compared with Barba's stifling, airless Subura donkey-courtyard, the Janiculum mill-slope at sunset felt like standing inside a waterfall. White spray leaped ten feet off mossy black oak paddles turning steadily in stone chutes; cold mist smelled of crushed mountain fern, wet pine timber, and sweet fresh-ground wheat; and deafening deep wooden rumble vibrated right up through boot-soles into shin-bones so nobody ten paces off could overhear a word spoken beside the race.

Corvus had walked straight from Antonius Gnipho's freedman's voice-school near the Circus Flaminius, still carrying his brass *pes Monetalis* and wax notebook; for half an hour he crouched absorbed along the slick stone wheel-pit of an unlocked municipal mill, timing the bronze lantern-pinion teeth (*tympanum dentatum*) that geared one slow ten-foot oak water-wheel into a spinning three-hundred-pound upper *catillus* at quadruple a donkey's speed!

"Look at the iron spindle-step (*rynd*), Vespera," Corvus shouted joyfully over the white roar, grey-green eyes bright with spray, pointing with wet ink-stained fingers at the iron cross-bar supporting the spinning upper stone. "Raise that iron wedge half a *digitus* with one tap of a hammer and you get coarse army groats; drop it a sixteenth-inch and one water-wheel grinds **sixty *modii* in twelve hours** without feeding a single mule barley! When we have capital in two years, we don't buy more Subura donkeys—we lease a forty-foot drop on this race!"

"Trajan's citizenship edict requires operating the *pistrinum* inside the City administrative roll, and every water-slot on this hill is locked up twenty years deep by senators' freedmen," Lucida called back, unlacing her dusty calfskin boots on a broad, sunlit slab of warm travertine beside the lower tailrace pool where clear cold overflow swirled knee-deep over smooth pebbles before plunging down toward Trans-Tiberim. "Subura donkeys win my citizen shoe first; Janiculum water-wheels make us rich second! Take off your hot *calcei*, advocate, before you blister your heels."

Corvus laughed, a rare, unguarded sixteen-almost-sixteen boy's laugh that wiped eight months of Caelian watchfulness clean off his scarred mouth—pulled off his calfskin shoes and woollen socks, rolled his grey tunic-hem above scarred brown knees, and dropped down onto the warm travertine ledge beside her, plunging both hot, dusty feet straight into the churning emerald-white foam.

Both of them gasped aloud at the icy Bracciano shock, kicking cold spray across each other's shins like twelve-year-olds in the Tiber shallows before the clay-pit took their parents.

Golden-red August sun slanted low over the umbrella-pines crowning the Janiculum crest behind them; spread out across the wide bend of the Tiber below lay the whole tremendous marble-and-brick beast of Rome, a million souls emerging onto flat roofs and riverside steps to breathe evening air, gilded bronze roof-tiles of Capitoline Jupiter blazing like a shield set on fire, grey smoke rising from ten thousand supper-hearths in the Subura valley where they had walked in barefoot ten months ago with eleven copper coins and a broken clay disc.

Lucida leaned back on one bare brown forearm, letting the cold mountain tailrace tug round her ankles, eyes half-closed against the red glare.

"You've got white *siligo* flour all behind your right ear from Barba's loft," Corvus said beside her.

Before she opened her eyes, he scooped a palmful of cold clear Bracciano water out of the race and reached naturally across—just as he had wiped fuller's lime or river-mud off her face a hundred times since they were seven, touching wet fingertips behind the shell of her right ear to rinse the dried flour-dust out of the damp dark curls along her neck.

Except neither of them was seven anymore, and it wasn't winter fever in a freezing garret where death stood over the straw.

Water trickled cold from Corvus's scarred right thumb down the sun-warmed skin of Lucida's neck, following the curve of her throat straight into the hollow above her left collarbone—over the tiny brown olive-pip mark, and sliding in one bright drop beneath the damp linen edge of her sleeveless summer shift between her breasts.

Corvus's thumb stopped dead against the hot pulse beating in the side of her throat.

Lucida opened her eyes six inches from his face.

Over the past six months on Caelian mutton and steady bread Corvus had grown four inches past her crown; dark razor-stubble shadowed his hard Sabine jaw above his open collar; the pale stylus-scar at the corner of his mouth caught the red sunset; and his grey-green eyes were staring down not at her ear at all—at the damp linen clinging tight to the swell of her breasts as her breath caught, and then straight up into her parted mouth.

Under his wet thumb she felt his hand, steady enough to shave wax under two consular senators' noses or hold a glass flask inside a roaring sewer—**tremble once against her skin**.

Heat that had nothing to do with the August sun went straight through Lucida's belly down to the soles of her wet feet in the icy race, sharp and dizzying enough that her fingers curled involuntarily against the warm travertine stone between their thighs instead of pushing his wrist away. Half an inch closer and his scarred mouth would have been on hers, salt spray and flour-dust and sixteen summers breaking open together over the roar of the wheels.

Instead Corvus jerked his wet hand back off her throat as though her skin had burned his fingers, splashing water across his own knee, his jaw locking tight while dull dark color surged up under his sun-browned cheekbones.

Neither of them spoke for five thunderous revolutions of the giant oak wheel above their heads.

"Sun's touching the Aventine," Corvus said hoarsely at last, staring straight ahead across the river while reaching blindly for his dry calfskin shoes without looking at her shift. "Barba bolts the Fish-Market gate at the first watch if we don't cross before dusk."

"Then lace your boots faster, Sabine," Lucida said, making her voice brisk and dry over the wild, traitorous hammering still kicking underneath her breastbone, bending forward to pull her own leather laces tight so her dark braids fell forward hiding her hot cheeks.

All the way down the steep stepped lane to the bridge they walked six careful inches apart without brushing elbows once, yet every time the evening river-wind blew Corvus's clean grey wool sleeve within a hair's breadth of her bare forearm, Lucida felt the exact shape of his scarred right thumb burning cold and wet against her throat, and wondered with a sudden sharp twist in her chest how two people hiding half their ledgers from each other were going to survive turning seventeen under the same low roof.
