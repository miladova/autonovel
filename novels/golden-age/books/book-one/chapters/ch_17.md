# Chapter XVII — Dry Pipes

*Ninth Day Before the Kalends of November, AD 159 · Corvus*

Three strokes of a six-pound iron sledge drove the tapered lead plug cold into the bronze collar, and half a street in Trans-Tiberim went quiet as a cut throat.

Down inside the brick inspection pit at the foot of the Janiculum steps, two public water-slaves (*aquarii*) wiped grey solder-paste round the crushed pipe-stub while **Gaius Caecilius**—a fat, saffron-perfumed wool-dyer whose front gate bore Mercury's painted purse and whose back ledger belonged ninety percent to **Publius Vedius Pollio**, stood wringing both dyed-yellow hands on the cobbles at Corvus's elbow.

"Twelve thousand sesterces of dipped Canusian fleece sitting in hot alum mordant right this instant, Master Cassius!" Caecilius wailed over the sudden dying gurgle of his brick yard-flume. "Leave those vats without cold rinse-water past the sixth hour and the alum burns every thread brittle as dry hay! Take thirty gold pieces deposit while my advocate petitions the *curator* on Monday—forty!"

Corvus, nineteen next month, standing broad-shouldered in an unspotted dark-grey autumn wool *paenula* over his white citizen toga, his surveyor's brass folding-rule tucked cleanly beside his belt-wallet—did not even look down at Caecilius's trembling yellow fingers.

Over the past seven months since Laurentum, thirty-one contested shipping audits at Portus and fourteen Subura building-and-water seizures had hardened Corvus's courtroom manner into something older advocates watched from across the Basilica with wary respect: never loud, never theatrical, moving through opposing paper like an iron saw through green pine. Inside Senator Priscus's strong-room and Tullia Crispina's Puteoli bank-chest now sat **ninety-two thousand sesterces** in Corvus's own clear name, nearly a quarter of the four-hundred-thousand-sesterce equestrian threshold already conquered before nineteen!

"Your advocate won't find any *curator* on Monday willing to unseal an unlicensed twelve-digit branch soldered straight into a municipal *castellum* apron without imperial grant, Gaius Caecilius," Corvus said evenly, holding a waxed two-leaved deposition flat on his left forearm while dipping an iron stylus—not ink, hard iron into black beeswax, with his scarred right hand. "Under the *Lex Quinctia*, piercing the public main carries a hundred-thousand-sesterce fine and summary seizure of your drying-frames. However, Urban Prefect Lollius Urbicus cares much less about one Trans-Tiberim wool-washer than about the undisclosed syndicate principal who financed your illegal lead-work and holds beneficial mortgage over the three over-height Subura tenements at the bottom of the Vicus Patricius."

Corvus turned the wax board toward Caecilius's sweating nose.

"Sign six lines right here naming **Hermippus, steward to Publius Vedius Pollio**, as lender-in-possession on those three Vicus Patricius blocks and producing his private rent-quittances before noon," Corvus said quietly, "and my water-clerk issues you a temporary seven-day emergency cartage-permit to draw rinse-water in barrels from the public *lacus* basin forty paces uphill so your twelve thousand sesterces of Canusian fleece comes out of the alum whole. Refuse, and the masons rip up forty yards of your courtyard pavement before lunch."

Caecilius stared down into the pit where the plumber's iron crowbar already rested under his second flagstone, swallowed hard, and snatched the stylus out of Corvus's fingers.

Within eighty breaths Corvus held Hermippus's own secret rent-schedule for three prime Vicus Patricius *insulae* locked safe inside his leather satchel—another mortar-brick pried cleanly out of Vedius Pollio's lower foundation wall!

Only as Corvus walked back down the steep, stepped brick alley toward the **Pons Aemilius** behind the clanking municipal tool-cart did he notice three barefoot Syrian washerwomen standing bewildered round a small, sunken neighborhood overflow-trough (*lacus inferior*) tucked behind a potter's arch two hundred paces downhill from Caecilius's compound.

Instead of the brisk two-inch spout of clean overflow water that usually spilled down from the upper *castellum* weir to feed poor lane-trades along the river-flat, the worn stone lion's mouth above the trough dribbled thin brown rust-drops and sucked empty air.

"Stopped dead in the middle of rinsing forty yards of Caelian table-linen!" the oldest Syrian woman cried in rapid Antioch Aramaic to a young mule-boy holding two empty bleaching-tubs. "Run across the Fish-Market bridge right now and tell **Lucida Tyria's daughter** somebody plugged the upper pipe behind the wool-yard, or her flax bleaches yellow before sunset!"

Corvus checked half a stride on the basalt cobbles.

*Tell Lucida Tyria's daughter.*

He turned back two steps toward the dry stone lion-spout, running the subterranean hydraulic map through his head in three heartbeats, and swore silently under his breath.

Caecilius's unlicensed twelve-digit branch hadn't tapped the main primary chamber directly; Caecilius's crooked plumber had tapped the **secondary overflow culvert** feeding this lower poor-folk's trough, and when the *aquarii* hammered their broad lead shut-off plate across the cracked junction collar two hundred breaths ago to seal the leak fast without draining the main tank, **they had choked off the legitimate four-digit neighborhood overflow pipe running underneath it at the same stroke**!

"Master Commissioner?" the municipal foreman asked, halting his mule-cart as Corvus frowned back uphill. "Should we go back and chisel a bypass groove round that lower collar? Takes half a day's fresh *opus signinum* mortar to set dry, and you wanted us up on the Viminal before the sixth hour."

Unplugging that junction collar today before Caecilius's wax deposition was formally registered at the Urban Prefecture risked giving Caecilius's lawyer an excuse to claim the water-slaves botched an authorized neighborhood culvert rather than an illegal private tap! Tomorrow morning, once Urbicus's clerk entered Hermippus's rent-roll irrevocably on record, Corvus could send two plumbers back at his own personal expense to re-bore the poor women's four-digit spout wider and cleaner than ever—costing the Syrian linen-bleachers at most one afternoon's mule-haul down to the Tiber bank.

"Leave the lead plate solid until the Prefecture stamps the seizure-roll at noon," Corvus told the foreman briskly, pulling two silver denarii out of his own pouch and tossing them to the startled Syrian mule-boy by the dry trough without giving his name. "Hire a river-donkey to carry water-jars up from the embankment until tomorrow afternoon, boy; the municipal masons re-open the lower lion-spout tomorrow before dusk."

Clean, practical, fair by any sensible administrator's measure, except as Corvus crossed the sunny bridge toward the Forum, he knew down in his ribs how *two silver denarii tossed by an unnamed commissioner who just dried up her washers' trough* would sound by the time that mule-boy reached the Fish-Market ovens.

* * *

Up in Senator Priscus's inner Caelian *tablinum* at the eighth hour, **Phileros** laid Caecilius's wet-sealed Vicus Patricius confession alongside four earlier Subura tenement schedules and rubbed his dry white hands together with the nearest thing to joy his bloodless Greek face ever displayed.

"Three blocks on the Vicus Patricius, two on the Sandaliarius, four behind the Argiletum," Phileros murmured, ticking squares off a large linen street-plan of Regions IV and VIII pinned across the oak table. "Every single one owned beneficially by Publius Vedius Pollio through front-freedmen, every single one carrying illegal seventh-floor timber garrets or unpermitted workshop furnaces! Once Lollius Urbicus signs the consolidated forfeiture decree—Wait until winter, Senator Priscus says, let Vedius borrow deeper on Ostian winter grain-futures first so the margin call breaks both knees at once!, half Vedius's Subura rent-roll falls onto the auction-spear in a single week."

Corvus leaned over the linen map, checking sightlines and fire-breaks down the familiar tangled alleys with a surveyor's automatic eye—and paused at a fresh red cinnabar square inked neatly on the **Clivus Suburanus** sixty yards below the Sandaliarius corner.

"Who filed the new masonry rebuilding notice on Lot Fourteen there?" Corvus asked, tapping the red square with his scarred right thumb. "That lot burned down to the Republican peperino piers before we came to Rome; it stood empty weeds in July."

Phileros glanced down at the municipal *aedes novae* building-register open beside his elbow.

"Bought cash down on the fourteenth day before the Kalends of October for twelve thousand sesterces, without a single sesterce of mortgage recorded, by one **Marcus Ulpius Felix**, aged seventy, freedman baker of the Palatina tribe," Phileros read out, lifting one pale eyebrow fractionally toward Corvus. "Member of the *Collegium Aesculapii et Hygiae*, the exact burial club that registered a bread-supply lien on Habinnas Barba's Fish-Market *pistrinum* eighteen months ago. And look at the architectural plan filed with the aediles: two deep vaulted tufa wheat-cellars holding two thousand *modii* below street-level, ground-floor commercial brick arches, three fireproof brick residential storeys above, **total cornice height fifty-seven Roman feet nine inches**—two feet three inches clear beneath your sixty-foot wrecking-bar!"

Corvus stood completely still above the map, staring down at that neat red square.

Old deaf Marcus Ulpius Felix didn't own twelve thousand spare sesterces; Felix lived on salted turnips and three hive-bees out past the Appian Gate.

**Lucida** had bought forty feet of prime Clivus Suburanus frontage for twelve thousand cash silver behind a citizen straw-man, designed the cornice two feet under Corvus's own statutory blade so he couldn't touch a single brick of her roof, and excavated two-thousand-*modius* grain-vaults right under **Gaius Ummidius Scaeva**'s nose in the dead center of Vedius Pollio's tenement territory!

Fierce, involuntary admiration hit Corvus first, nobody else on earth at eighteen could have turned Spanish oil-dregs and Caelian horse-liniment into four thundering mills and a forty-foot Subura brick-lot while bleeding eighty sesterces a month to a shark!—and hot on admiration's heels came sharp alarm, plus a darker, rougher question tightening his jaw:

*Twelve thousand sesterces cash in September, while still paying off Salvius's burial guild and feeding four mills?* Even Caelian face-paint and Green-stable hoof-jars didn't coin twelve thousand loose silver pieces overnight—**how deeply had Ceionia Fabia bought into Lucida's pocket since summer?**

And worse: Lot Fourteen on the Clivus Suburanus shared its eastern rear party-alley directly with one of the three rotten seven-storey timber death-traps Caecilius had just confessed belonged to **Vedius Pollio**! If Vedius discovered his own Junian Latin tributary was building fireproof wheat-vaults behind a straw freedman sixty paces from Scaeva's rent-office, or if a dry wind carried one spark off Vedius's worm-eaten pine balconies onto unfinished scaffolding—

At the tenth hour, instead of riding out toward the Esquiline gardens or meeting Tullia Crispina's factor at the Forum baths, Corvus left his white toga folded on his Carinae stool, pulled on stout hobnailed boots under his dark grey working tunic and cloak, and walked fast down into the smoky roar of the **Clivus Suburanus**.

* * *

By the eleventh hour of a gusty late-October afternoon, the bricklayers and mortar-hod boys on Lot Fourteen had knocked off at sunset whistle, leaving behind the clean, sharp smell of wet pozzolana lime, sawn Sabine fir scaffolding-poles, and fresh red *bipedales* bricks rising four unfinished storeys above the noisy Subura cobbles.

Down at street-level a stout timber hoarding barred the front arches behind a padlocked wicket; Corvus didn't bother knocking at the padlock. Having grown up round clay-clamps and scaffolding since he could walk, he stepped into the narrow three-foot drip-alley alongside the coppersmith's wall, caught the projecting heel of the first lashed ashwood builder's ladder two feet above his head, hauled himself effortlessly onto the first plank catwalk, and climbed three successive swaying pine ladders past freshly mortared window-arches up onto the **top fourth-storey working deck, fifty-six vertical feet above the basalt street**.

Up here the smelly, frying-pan clatter of the Subura dropped away beneath his boots into a vast sea of blackened clay pantiles, smoking fuller's vents, and red sunset blazing straight across from the distant Janiculum ridge where Trajan's water-wheels turned.

Corvus crouched swiftly beside the eastern corner pier with his folding brass rule, testing the mortar mix with his thumbnail—clean sharp red pit-pozzolana two-to-one with slaked white lime, honest *opus testaceum* twenty-four inches thick, built to stand three hundred years!, while checking how perilously close **Vedius Pollio**'s leaning, seven-storey timber-and-wattle tenement loomed across the narrow five-foot rear alley: rotten pine balconies sagging greased wool-rags almost within jumping reach!

Below his boots on Ladder Three, dry ashwood rungs creaked fast and light—not a heavy mason's tread, someone climbing barefoot-nimble in soft leather boots without touching the side-ropes for balance.

Up through the square scaffold-well rose a dark braided crown, olive shoulders under a slate-blue woollen *tunica* belted tight for climbing, and blazing dark amber eyes.

**Lucida** swung cleanly onto the three-plank pine catwalk twelve feet away, stopped dead as she recognized Corvus standing tall against the blood-red western sky beside her corner brick pier, and let go of the ladder-pole with both hands.

October gusts whipped loose black tendrils across her high cheekbones; fifty-six feet straight down past the unrailed outer edge of the three twelve-inch pine planks, iron-tired butcher's carts rumbled like toys over lethal basalt stones.

"Did you climb up here with a crowbar in your cloak to measure whether Marcus Ulpius Felix laid one brick past sixty feet, Commissioner," Lucida asked, her voice pitched low and flat across the wind, trembling with three months of banked fire, "or did you come to toss me two silver denarii the way you tossed them at my crippled Syrian linen-bleachers in Trans-Tiberim this noon after you plugged their water?"

Corvus straightened slowly beside the brick pier, folding his brass rule shut with a sharp *snick*.

"Caecilius tapped the municipal culvert illegally for Hermippus; shutting that collar broke Vedius's hold on three tenements down this very street, and municipal plumbers re-bore your bleachers' four-digit lion-spout clean tomorrow morning at my own cost," Corvus answered steadily across the swaying planks. "Which you would know if you had asked me instead of listening to street-gossip—just as I wouldn't have had to read on Phileros's Caelian map at noon that you sank twelve thousand sesterces behind old Ulpius Felix five feet away from Vedius Pollio's rottenest fire-trap in Rome!"

"Why on earth would I ask **Senator Priscus's** building-commissioner permission before laying my own brick?" Lucida shot back, taking two fierce strides along the creaking pine planks toward him, ignoring the fifty-six-foot drop three inches left of her boot-heel as completely as she had ignored Number Three kiln's parapet at fifteen. "So you could run back up the Clivus Scauri, or down to **Laurentum**—and mark my straw-buyer's name in red cinnabar right below my Fish-Market ovens on your secret Region VIII audit-scroll?"

Corvus froze half a breath.

*Right below my Fish-Market ovens on your secret Region VIII audit-scroll.*

Only Priscus, Rusticus, Phileros, Lollius Urbicus's chief clerk, and Corvus knew which bakehouses stood flagged on that confidential July scroll, unless **Ceionia Fabia's** Palatine or Prefecture ears had bought a leak and fed it straight to Lucida to poison her against him!

"You think *I* put your Fish-Market ovens on Urbicus's audit-scroll?" Corvus demanded, his calm courtroom iron cracking open for the first time all year as he took two heavy strides forward along the groaning planks until six feet separated them in the howling red wind. "**Hermippus** slipped an anonymous denunciation into the Prefecture box in July to halt your thousand-and-ninety-five-day count! I lied straight into Junius Rusticus's eyes to keep Region VIII inside my own satchel so **no inspector in Rome has set one boot across Habinnas Barba's threshold for four straight months** while I tore Numerius Vettius's mills apart instead!"

For one stunned beat Lucida's parted lips went motionless—nobody had told her *why* the dreaded August *annona* audit had mysteriously skipped her courtyard while hitting three streets round her!, and instead of gratitude, **hotter, wilder terror and fury** blazed up in her amber eyes because being shielded in secret like a helpless child felt ten times more dangerous to her hard-won autonomy than open war!

"Nobody asked you to lie to Junius Rusticus for me, Lucius Cassius!" she cried back into the wind, both fists clenched tight at her sides. "Keep my name inside your satchel today, and tomorrow when **Senator Priscus** orders you to choose between his Stoic war on **Ceionia Fabia** and a Junian baker-girl down by the Fish-Market, whose wax tablet gets burned? Where do you think I got twelve thousand sesterces to dig my own wheat-vaults here—begging? **Hermippus dumped forty-eight *modii* of live black weevils into my bins at midnight in September** while your fine maritime blockades squeezed Ostia, and I bought my own sixty *modii* out of the *Horrea Galbana* before dawn **without needing your Caelian ring or your widow's ships**!"

*Bought my own sixty modii out of the Horrea Galbana at midnight.*

Corvus knew the *Horrea Galbana* night-wardens, knew **Decimus Valerius** in Courtyard Two, knew what midnight favors took when Hermippus blacklisted silver—and watching the defiant, unflinching lift of eighteen-year-old Lucida's chin in the red sunset glare, Corvus read the exact truth written iron-plain in her eyes before his mind even formed words.

Jealousy, black, murderous, primitive, hotter than pitch-fire, stripping three years of Cato and Julian off his bones in half a heartbeat—roared straight behind Corvus's vision.

"Who held the bin-key in Courtyard Two at midnight, Lucida?" Corvus whispered, stepping right into the last four feet between them so the pine scaffold-plank dipped beneath their combined weight. "Valerius with the silver wheat-ear on his thumb? Did six gold pieces open the lock, or did you, "

"Say one more word about whose hand touched me while **Tullia Crispina's** green glass bracelets rattle against your wrist in Pompey's garden," Lucida hissed back, eyes bright gold with rage and unshed salt, taking half a furious step sideways to brush past him toward the ladder-well—

And her right calfskin heel came down square on a wet, slick gob of unhardened white slaked-lime mortar spilled three inches from the **unrailed outer edge of the pine plank fifty-six feet above the basalt cobbles**!

Her boot shot clean out from under her into empty air; gravel and wet lime rained fifty feet straight down onto a passing mule-awning below as her hip pitched outward over the abyss!

Corvus didn't think; before her cry even hit her teeth he lunged sideways across the groaning pine board, hooked his left elbow iron-tight around the rough upright fir scaffold-post until bark tore skin through his sleeve, and **locked his scarred right hand and forearm straight round Lucida's narrow waist and twisting wrist** over fifty-six feet of open Subura sky, yanking all eight and a half stone of her body crashing backward out of empty air, slamming both of them hard back against the solid, rough red masonry of her newly laid corner brick pier!

For ten wild, ragged heartbeats neither of them could draw speech.

Pinned tight from breastbone to thigh between Corvus's six-foot frame and the fresh-mortared bricks—his heart hammering like a trapped stallion's ribs straight through two thin layers of wool into her crushed breasts, his right hand still locked bruising-hard into the bare flesh of her hip under her hitched cloak while her fingers dug white-knuckled into the back of his neck where she had grabbed to keep from falling, they gasped raw October wind into each other's mouths from two inches away.

Wet white lime-dust off the fresh mortar had smeared pale across Corvus's jaw and across the corner of Lucida's parted lower lip.

Corvus looked down at the white lime on her mouth, smelled cedar-oil, warm baker's skin, and terror mixing between their bodies, felt both her thighs pressed solid and trembling against his own—and dropped his forehead half an inch until the white scar on his lip brushed the lime on hers without quite closing the kiss.

"Vespera," he breathed raggedly against her mouth, voice shaking like a fifteen-year-old boy's in river-fog, all ninety-two thousand sesterces and two rival Caelian palaces forgotten for three agonizing breaths on a windy plank. "Drop Fabia's jars, let me pay off Vedius's eighty sesterces tomorrow morning, *yes, I heard Vedius boast of your twenty denarii outside Fronto's gate a year ago!*—let me buy you clean out before, "

Against his ribs, Lucida went rigid as cold iron from heel to crown.

*He had known about her eighty-sesterce monthly chain for an entire year—watching her bleed twenty silver denarii every single Kalends in silence while piling ninety thousand sesterces inside his own Caelian chest and sleeping at Tullia Crispina's seaside villa!*

Humiliation hotter than scalding bath-steam burned every soft nerve out of her throat. She planted both palms flat against Corvus's chest and shoved back with every ounce of baker's wrist-strength she owned—breaking his hold now that both her boots stood safe on solid pine beside the ladder-well, wiping the white lime-smudge roughly off her own mouth with the back of her forearm until her lip glowed dark red.

"Touch Hermippus with one silver coin behind my back," Lucida said, each word quiet, clear, and final over the moan of wind through the scaffolding—"and Vedius Pollio revokes my commuted *operae* agreement the same hour, calls me in for household service to spite Senator Priscus, and breaks six hundred days of my mill-count before noon! Stay off my walls, Lucius Cassius. Keep your ninety thousand sesterces, keep your widow, and keep your hands on your own side of the street."

She swung onto the top ashwood rung without touching his arm and went down all four ladders into the darkening Subura smoke without once looking up.

Fifty-six feet above the street, nineteen-almost-nineteen Corvus stood alone beside the fresh red brick pier until purple night swallowed the Janiculum hills, staring down at five faint white lime-marks where her fingers had gripped his dark cloak over his heart, tasting bitter white lime dry on his own scarred lip.
