# Chapter XXIX — By Bronze and Balance

*Nones of March, AD 161 · Corvus*

Rome woke on the Nones of March to silent shutters, wet black travertine, and six hundred senators walking fast through thin grey rain toward the bronze doors of the **Curia Julia** without knowing whether one master waited inside or two.

Twenty-year-old Corvus stood bare-headed on the third marble step outside the open Senate vestibule—junior *cognitores* below senatorial rank waited outside the sacred threshold while listening unobstructed through the thirty-foot doorway, holding **Senator Quintus Servilius Priscus's** dry toga-cloak over one arm while five sworn Roman citizen witnesses led by pale, clean-shaved **Aulus Volusenus Niger** waited twenty paces across the Comitium beside the steps of **Saturn's Treasury**.

Nobody in Priscus's household had slept a wink since the fourth-watch beacon flashed off the Janiculum signaling that seventy-four-year-old **Antoninus Pius** had breathed his last at Lorium. Up on the Caelian before dawn, Corvus had handed Priscus the unopened green-mastic oilskin wallet from his satchel, Priscus slipping it directly into his confidential Eastern courier's saddle-pouch with strict orders not to break the outer green terebinth seal until the courier boarded ship at Brundisium, unless events inside the Curia required new instructions!—and Corvus had sent a runner down to the Fish-Market at first light to fetch Lucida's countersigned custody-sheet, assuming when the runner hadn't returned yet through the jammed Forum crowds that nervous holiday wagon-blocks around the cattle-market had slowed the boy down.

Inside the towering marble hall of the **Curia Julia**, tiered benches packed white from floor to gallery fell dead silent as forty-year-old **Marcus Aurelius** walked down the central porphyry pavement wearing an unbleached dark *toga pulla* of filial mourning, accompanied two paces behind, sunburned, golden-curled, sober-faced today in matching dark wool, by thirty-year-old **Lucius Ceionius Commodus**.

Sitting in the front consular row beside **Quintus Junius Rusticus**, old **Senator Priscus** leaned forward with hands braced on bony knees, waiting to rise the instant the senior consul moved the *senatus consultum* conferring sole *imperium proconsulare*, *tribunicia potestas*, and the title *Augustus* upon Marcus alone.

The senior consul read the decree trembling; six hundred senatorial voices roared ritual acclamation—*"Dii te servent, Marce Auguste! Imperator unice!"*, intentionally shouting *sole Imperator* to drown out three hesitant voices near the door!

Marcus Aurelius stood up before the gilded Altar of Victory.

Thin, hollow-eyed from five sleepless nights holding a dying old man's swollen hand at Lorium, his quiet, slightly reedy Stoic voice carried clear as cold spring water out past the open bronze doors to where twenty-year-old Corvus stood in the drizzle:

"Conscript Fathers," Marcus said simply, "my deified father left me neither a private farm nor an undivided burden. Sixty million people from Caledonia to the Euphrates require four eyes, and fidelity begins inside one's own house. I accept neither the name of Augustus nor the command of the armies **save on condition that you vote this same hour every equal title, tribunician power, proconsular *imperium*, and the name *Imperator Caesar Lucius Aurelius Verus Augustus* to my brother Lucius standing beside me**, that Rome may be governed henceforth by two Augusti in one mind."

Out on the wet marble steps Corvus heard three nearby equestrians gasp aloud.

Inside on the front consular bench, **Quintus Servilius Priscus** went the exact grey-white color of old peperino tufa—half-rising three inches off his ivory stool to object before **Junius Rusticus** laid one iron-still ink-stained hand flat across Priscus's forearm to hold him down! Argue openly against Marcus's own filial generosity while **Cornelius Repentinus's** Praetorian cohorts waited under arms half a mile north-east calling for double donatives, and you split the Curia on Day One!

Instead thirty-year-old **Lucius, now Emperor Lucius Verus, invested in sixty heartbeats with half the Roman world, including supreme command of the approaching war in the East!**, stepped smoothly beside Marcus at the altar to speak graceful, resonant Fronto-polished thanks while across the Forum arcades **Publius Vedius Pollio** and **Ceionia Fabia's** partisans broke into open, triumphant embraces!

Ten breaths later Senator Priscus strode out onto the wet marble porch ahead of the crowd—chewing two dry fennel seeds so hard his jaw-muscles corded beneath cropped grey beard, and caught twenty-year-old Corvus by the shoulder inside the shadow of a travertine pillar.

"Marcus handed the boy purple out of sheer sentimental philosophy," Priscus hissed so low rain drowned every consonant beyond two feet. "Thank the immortal gods my Brundisium courier hasn't left the Capena Gate yet, he rides at sunset so **Antioch's three legions still answer solely to Marcus's private seal when Verus sails east**! And *you*, Lucius Cassius: Fiscal Praetor **Manius Acilius** just took his curule chair on the Treasury tribunal before the Senate adjourns to the Camp! Strike **Vedius Pollio** flat across Saturn's scales *right this hour* while Vedius thinks Verus's purple makes him untouchable—strip every silver denarius out of his chests before noon so he cannot put one gold piece behind Verus's Praetorian friends!"

Corvus's pulse beat slow, deep, and cold as Sublaqueum snow-water.

"Acilius's clerk already called our number, Senator," Corvus said. "Watch the scales."

* * *

Across the Forum pavement beneath the towering granite porch of the **Aerarium Saturni**, where public *quaestores* and the **Praetor Fiscalis** sat before open bronze vault-doors flanked by the ancient brass balance (*libra*) hung on an iron standard, **Publius Vedius Pollio** had just strolled out of the **Basilica Julia** surrounded by six fawning equestrian tax-farmers, smiling radiantly in chalk-white wool while **Hermippus** trotted behind him carrying an ivory dispatch-box.

Vedius had every reason under heaven to smile at the fifth hour on the Nones of March: his royal patron **Lucius Verus** had just put on Augustus's purple; his ally **Repentinus** commanded the Praetorian Guard; and six hours ago before dawn Scaeva had smashed that troublesome Fish-Market Junian girl's lava mills to gravel!

Then a court *viator* stepped squarely across Vedius Pollio's path with an ashwood summons-rod and called out in loud brassy Forum cadence:

*"Publius Vedius Pollio, equestrian, and Gaius Munatius, fiduciary holder: stand to the bar of Fiscal Praetor Manius Acilius on summary *vindicatio aerarii* and quadruple *Lex Iulia* forfeiture prosecuted by **Lucius Cassius Corvus, son of Gaius, of the Crustumina tribe**!"*

Thirty spectators round Saturn's steps turned heads at once; inside five hundred breaths two hundred Forum idlers, bankers, and clerks had packed six deep round the stone balustrade smelling giant blood.

Vedius Pollio stopped walking, dark velvet eyes blinking once in genuine amusement as he took in the tall, black-haired twenty-year-old standing calm behind the prosecutor's oak rail flanked by **Aulus Volusenus Niger** and four grey-bearded Crustumina and Palatina citizens.

"Wait, Gaius Cassius's little Crustumerium ditch-orphan?" Vedius purred musically to Hermippus, loud enough to draw a nervous titter from two of his hangers-on. "Bringing country boundary-suits on the morning Rome hails two Emperors? Praetor Acilius, my advocate **Sulpicius Rufus** is dining up on the Palatine; grant thirty days' *dilatio* after the imperial funeral, "

"Under Augustus's and Hadrian's Treasury rules, no *dilatio* runs where an embezzler's sham *fiducia* shield is proved void *in continenti* by public archive rolls and sealed bronze," twenty-year-old **Corvus** cut straight across Vedius's velvet purr—his deep Fronto-chiselled baritone ringing off Saturn's granite columns like an iron maul striking bronze, silencing every titter in the square at one blow!

Praetor **Manius Acilius**, an austere Stoic friend of Rusticus, tapped his ivory wand once on the stone arm-rest: "The Treasury sits uninterrupted for public revenue even on interregnum mornings. Proceed on the archive rolls, Lucius Cassius; thirty-six hundred breaths on the clepsydra."

Corvus didn't need thirty-six hundred breaths; he killed six million sesterces in **nine hundred**.

Walking unhurriedly to the stone evidence-table without raising his voice once, scarred right thumb steady on every seal, Corvus laid down **Hammer One**:

The heavy four-pound circular bronze **signaculum** die of *Figlinae Subortanae* alongside Chest Nineteen's certified Treasury payment-vouchers (720,000 HS drawn out of Caesar's public works chest for 240,000 *bipedales*) and dead Procurator **Rufus's** five original notched pine kiln-tallies proving **only 60,000 bricks ever saw fire**—leaving **five hundred forty thousand sesterces of pure stolen public silver**, corroborated by two retired brick-kiln foremen Phileros had tracked down at Tibur!

Vedius Pollio's smile vanished as if wiped off oiled ivory with a rag; behind him **Hermippus** grabbed wildly for his wax pocket-notes, hissing *"Plead Gaius Munatius's separate *mancipatio* trust, Vedius's brick-company holds no assets!"*

Before Hermippus finished whispering, Corvus dropped **Hammer Two** straight onto Hermippus's fingers:

"Foreseeing this hour two summers back," Corvus said cleanly to the fascinated bench, "Publius Vedius Pollio conveyed his Subura commercial tenements, Ostian warehouse leases, and Puteoli patronal reversions *per aes et libram* into a protective *fiducia* shield-trust held by **Gaius Munatius**. Five Roman citizens signed that bronze-and-scales conveyance. Fifth down the spine stands **Sextus Vibius Balbus of Capua**, condemned four months *prior* in the Capuan public criminal court for malicious *calumnia*, certified here under the Praetorian archive seal! Under the Twelve Tables and the Perpetual Edict, a condemned *calumniator* is **infamis and intestabilis**—incapable of standing witness beside bronze and scales! Munatius's two-million-sesterce shield-trust never existed in law; **every brick, lease, and patronal claim stands naked inside Publius Vedius Pollio's personal chest today**!"

Gasps broke across the packed crowd of bankers round Saturn's steps, half the moneylenders on the Janus Medius held paper cross-collateralized by Munatius's trust!

"Sham beneficial enjoyment requires proof beyond a witness-error!" Hermippus shouted shrilly, losing thirty years of Smyrniot self-control as his own liberty tottered.

Corvus turned half an inch and nodded to **Aulus Volusenus Niger**.

Pale, dignified in spotless wide-striped patrician wool, twenty-three-year-old Volusenus stepped to the bar and laid down **Hermippus's own emerald-stamped collection-endorsement** directing eighty thousand sesterces through Munatius's chest straight into *Publius Vedius Pollio's personal Caelian strongbox*, authenticated on oath by Senator Priscus's own nephew!

And before Vedius could draw breath to appeal to his Caelian friends, Corvus lifted out of a felt-lined basket **Hammer Three**: the dusty, four-year-old **Sidonian glass flask still one-third full of settled crimson Pontic Sinope ruddle-silt**, sealed under two equestrian water-witnesses' rings from January AD 157!

"And lest the accused plead honest accounting mistake to beg mercy on quadruple fiscal penalties," Corvus concluded, setting the red glass bottle gleaming in watery March sun beside the bronze brick-die, "here sits sworn proof under the *Lex Quinctia* and criminal *stellionatus* that this same Publius Vedius Pollio hollowed municipal masonry on the Caelian crest to solder **twin illegal fifty-digit lead *fistulae* behind forged twenty-digit imperial collars**, stealing fourteen *quinariae* of public water day and night! Quadruple restitution on five hundred forty thousand sesterces public brick-fraud: **two million one hundred sixty thousand sesterces**. Statutory *Lex Quinctia* water-forfeiture: **one hundred thousand sesterces**, plus summary criminal notation referring **Publius Vedius Pollio** for deportation *interdictio aquae et ignis*!"

Out in the packed Forum square nobody cheered with hired clappers today; two hundred hardened Roman merchants watched in awed, temple-still silence as fifty-one-year-old **Publius Vedius Pollio** reached blindly behind himself for a stool that wasn't there, plump depilated hands grey and shaking as wet dough while four public *lictores* stepped quietly up beside his elbows so he couldn't slip away toward the Palatine!

Praetor **Manius Acilius** consulted his three *assessores* for less than a hundred breaths, stood up before the open doors of Saturn's Treasury, and spoke judgment into bronze:

"Fraud on the *fiscus* proved; Munatius's *mancipatio* adjudged void *ab initio* for an *intestabilis* witness; immediate *missio in possessionem* and Treasury sequestration ordered upon all lands, tenements, warehouses, strongboxes—**including Castor Vault Forty-Two!**, and patronal reversions of Publius Vedius Pollio! Out of collected bullion and first-rank leases, the Quaestors shall pay over across Saturn's balance the statutory fifth-part prosecutor's *praemium* of **four hundred thirty-two thousand sesterces** to **Lucius Cassius Corvus**, whom I hereby note qualified upon the equestrian census roll! Strike the bronze!"

*CLANG.*

The Treasury *libripens* struck the ancient copper ingot once against the hanging brass balance-pan.

Four and a half years after two starving fifteen-year-olds ran bleeding out of *Figlinae Subortanae* mud owing three thousand two hundred sesterces on a dead surveyor's bond, twenty-year-old **Lucius Cassius Corvus** stood on the marble steps of Saturn worth **over six hundred thousand sesterces**, enrolled among the **Roman Knights**, holding inside his scarred right hand the wet-sealed receivership decree that **canceled his dead father's debt, impounded Castor Vault Forty-Two unopened, AND vested Vedius Pollio's voided patronal claim over Junian Latin *Lucida* straight into Corvus's own hand so he could emancipate and release her free gratis before sunset**!

Then, just as Corvus stepped down off the tribunal surrounded by congratulating advocates to dispatch Quaestor Caecilius's clerk with Lucida's clean release-deed—a breathless, white-faced Marsian baker's kneader (**Marcus**, his left temple split and crusted with dried blood from last night's club-fight!) fought through the Forum crowd and grabbed Corvus's toga-sleeve with trembling flour-stained hands!

"Master Cassius, thank Jupiter I found you!" Marcus choked out in broken Greek. "Before dawn, two hours after midnight—**One-Ear Scaeva** and four urban *stationarii* axed down our Fish-Market gate screaming Repentinus's warrant **and shouting your name on a Treasury padlock**! They smashed all four lava mills with iron wedges, tipped the dough into the sewer, and **crushed old Habinnas Barba's chest** while Mistress Lucida and Sura went over the fullery roof! The soldiers dragged dying Barba into the **Lautumiae** Prefecture cells behind the Carcer!"

Every drop of six-hundred-thousand-sesterce triumph froze solid black inside Corvus's veins.

*Screaming Repentinus's warrant and shouting YOUR name on a Treasury padlock! Smashed all four lava mills on Day One Thousand Sixty-One!*

Leaving astonished Volusenus and four congratulating knights standing open-mouthed in the rain on Saturn's steps, twenty-year-old Corvus sprinted hundred-yard strides straight north across the slippery Comitium flagstones toward the dark peperino arches of the **Urban Prefecture detention vaults** beside the Lautumiae quarry-wall!

* * *

Ten damp travertine steps below street-level inside the vaulted *custodia* holding-pen, stinking of wet rotten straw, iron rust, human urine, and cold winter stone, Corvus flashed Praetor Acilius's wet emergency seal past a sullen cohort jailer carrying Repentinus's keys, throwing two gold *aurei* onto the guard-table to drag the iron-grated cell door open.

Six battered street-prisoners huddled shivering along the far tufa wall; alone in the dark puddle beneath the narrow barred air-slit lay sixty-five-year-old **Habinnas Barba**.

Nobody had bound his broken ribs; nobody had wiped the caked flour and dried dark blood off his crushed white beard. Every shallow breath whistled wet and pink through ruptured lung-wall, his solitary clear black eye staring straight up at the dripping grey vault overhead so stubbornly he seemed to be holding Death off by sheer Cappadocian rage until someone walked down those stairs.

"Barba—it's Corvus! Asclepiades's litter is coming right behind me down the Forum steps with clean blankets!" Corvus rasped, dropping onto both knees in the filthy wet straw without caring a damn for his white equestrian-qualified citizen toga, slipping his left arm gently beneath the old baker's flour-stiffened shoulders while holding Praetor Acilius's wet-sealed emancipation-and-release tablet out where Barba's single eye could see the red wax! "Vedius Pollio is finished, ruined and exiled ten breaths ago at Saturn's scales! Look at the seal, old soldier, Castor's warrant is dead, and your courtyard comes back clean without a copper owed—"

Slowly Habinnas Barba shifted his solitary fever-bright black eye off the ceiling down onto twenty-year-old Corvus's face.

He did not look at the red wax seal. He looked at Corvus's clean white Forum wool, and then down at the Treasury receivership ribbon dangling beside Corvus's scarred right thumb.

With his last ounce of sixty-five-year-old soldier's strength, Barba raised his unbroken left hand an inch off the bloody straw, **spat a thick mouthful of bright crimson flour-blood square across the snowy front of Corvus's toga**, and locked iron-cold dying fingers onto Corvus's wrist.

"Thirty-four... sunrises left on the little mute's slate," Barba whispered in Cappadocian Greek, every word bubbling wet red at the corners of his grey moustache while his black eye bored straight into Corvus's soul. "One thousand... sixty-one mornings... she lifted sixty-pound sacks with burned hands so my bad lungs wouldn't stop the count... and **One-Ear nailed *your* Treasury tag beside his crowbar across my broken stones before dawn, Sabine**..."

"Barba, by my father's grave, I never sent Scaeva or any padlock to your yard!" Corvus cried out hoarsely in the dark cell, gripping the old man's freezing, flour-crusted hand between both of his own. "Scaeva lied using a leaked docket-line to break your men's hearts, I came down on the fourth night begging Lucida to take my shield so your stones wouldn't stop! Tell me where she ran—did she go to Salvius's burial hall?"

A terrible, silent ghost of a grin stretched Barba's blood-stained grey beard.

"Begged her... to step inside *your* cage," Barba rattled softly, chest hitching once, twice, shallower against Corvus's arm as the light behind his solitary eye began drawing far back up the mountain pass toward Comana. "Too late for cages, lawyer... Berenice's dead on her stairs... my four Orvieto girls are split gravel... and **Lucida Tyria's daughter went north through the rain carrying green wax... to buy four legions and an Emperor who doesn't count flour**..."

One final long, wet sigh hissed out between broken yellow teeth; the knotted flour-white fingers slackened open off Corvus's wrist; and sixty-five-year-old Habinnas Barba lay still as dressed mill-lava against Corvus's blood-soaked chest.

For twenty heartbeats nobody in the dark stone cell moved while rain dripped *plink-plink-plink* through the iron street-grating overhead.

Kneeling motionless in wet filthy straw with a dead baker's blood soaking warm through his newly won equestrian-rich wool, twenty-year-old Corvus heard seven words echo backward through silence until every hair along his nape stood straight up in terror:

*Went north through the rain carrying green wax... to buy four legions and an Emperor.*

**North-east, the Castra Praetoria!**

And four nights ago up in that warm Fish-Market loft while Sura went downstairs for barley-water and Corvus knelt with his back turned warming Caecilius's wax over red coals, **twelve silent heartbeats beside his unbuckled satchel before Lucida kissed the scar on his thumb like a woman burying the dead**!

Slowly Corvus laid old Habinnas Barba's white head gently down onto his own folded dry cloak, closed the solitary black eye with two trembling blood-stained fingers, stood up out of the straw, and walked up the dark steps into the gathering dusk of the Nones of March knowing—ten hours before Senator Priscus's courier ever broke the blank linden-wood seal at Brundisium, that the sky was already falling.
