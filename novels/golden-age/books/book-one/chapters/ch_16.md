# Chapter XVI — Weevils in the Hold

*Fourteenth Day Before the Kalends of October, AD 159 · Lucida*

When Lucida slit the belly-stitch of the ninth Sicilian sack with her bronze sampling-horn at the second watch of the night, the wheat inside moved by itself.

Under the red glare of Oven One's cresset, thousands of tiny snouted black *curculio* beetles boiled out through the parted hemp threads over her wrist, carrying the unmistakable sour, mushroomy stench of bilge-rotted grain that had lain three weeks wet under a leaky hatch at Messana and been topped off two palms deep at the mouth with clean dry kernels to cheat dockside buyers.

Habinnas Barba plunged both flour-scarred hands into Sack Ten, Sack Eleven, Sack Twelve—ripping twine open down the entire afternoon wagon-load delivered off **Gaius Baebius**'s river-lighter, and let out a strangled Cappadocian curse that ended in gray phlegm spat onto the basalt flags.

"Every single sack!" Barba wheezed, holding two handfuls of hollow, black-crawling husks toward the lamp. "Forty-eight *modii* of sprouted slime! Dump that filth between our Orvieto stones tonight and it gums every furrow solid with wet paste before the fourth watch—and our clean dry bin holds **sixty-two *modii***, thirty-eight short of Trajan's hundred!"

Beside the wall-slate fifteen-year-old **Sura** froze with chalk poised in her hand beneath yesterday's proud **DIES DLXV** (Day 565). Five hundred sixty-five unbroken days and nights, more than half of Lucida's thousand-and-ninety-five-day climb out of Junian Latin servitude—and if those four volcanic mills failed to grind and bake thirty-eight clean *modii* more before the midnight-to-midnight *annona* tally closed, **every single one of those five hundred sixty-five days dropped dead into the Tiber**, resetting her statutory count to Day One!

Lucida didn't scream at absent Baebius and didn't waste five breaths kicking spoiled sacks. Standing in her dark brown working tunic with grain-weevils crawling over her bare knuckles, she brushed the beetles cleanly into the ash-pit so none escaped into her clean dry bin, and read **Publius Vedius Pollio**'s handwriting straight through the rotten hemp.

Ever since April in the Green stables, when Vedius realized whose Caelian salve-jars had cost him Lucius Commodus's grooms, and especially since August, when **Corvus**'s building-commissioners started tearing the top two storeys off Vedius's straw-owned Subura tenements!—Hermippus had stopped playing gentlemanly games. Vedius didn't dare smash Ceionia Fabia's purveyor openly with clubs; instead he had paid Baebius thirty gold pieces down at Ostia to slip forty-eight *modii* of weevil-slime onto Lucida's twilight cart after the wholesale Porticus Minucia brokers bolted their shutters for the night!

"Keep all four mules harnessed on the clean sixty-two *modii* right now so the stones never stop turning," Lucida ordered Barba, already unbuckling her flour-apron and unlocking her hidden floor-box behind Oven Two to count **sixteen gold *aurei*** (1,600 HS, quadruple market price for fifty *modii*!) into a soft leather waist-belt. "Heat both domes white with dry oak. I'll have fifty clean *modii* rolling through that arch before the fourth watch horn blows."

"From where at midnight, girl?" Barba rasped, catching at her cloak-hem in genuine dread. "Every private broker from the Tiber to the Viminal locked iron grilles at dusk! Only the imperial **Horrea Galbana** down under Monte Testaccio keeps night-clerks awake for dawn barge-unloading—and Hermippus's cousin drinks dice-wine with half the bailiffs in Court Three!"

"Half isn't all," Lucida said, buckling her short five-inch bone-handled warehouse cord-knife inside her right boot-top and throwing a dark hooded woollen *paenula* over her braided hair. "Send two of the Marsian kneaders down behind me with our flatbed two-mule dray to wait outside the eastern *Galbana* postern, and don't take your eyes off those hoppers till you hear my whistle."

* * *

Built round three colossal colonnaded travertine courtyards covering twenty acres between the Aventine slope and Potsherd Hill, the **Horrea Galbana** at midnight felt less like a market than a fortified legionary fortress of bread.

Six-foot-thick ashlar walls without ground-floor windows shut out thieves and fire alike; narrow arched entry-gates bristled with triple Laconian iron locks guarded by sleepy imperial *vilici* with mastiffs on chain-leads; and inside Courtyard Two, a hundred and forty numbered brick storage-vaults (*cellae*) stretched away under dim hanging oil-lanterns, breathing out a dry, intoxicating, temple-deep warmth of **two million *modii* of sun-baked Egyptian, African, and Sicilian wheat** resting behind mortised Lebanese cedar planks.

Lucida knew every night-watch face in Courtyard Two from three winters of buying Emporium oil fifty yards west. Avoiding Gate One where Hermippus's drinking-crony Rufus sat snoring beside a mastiff, she slipped four silver denarii cleanly into the palm of an old Cilician night-porter at the eastern postern, left her two Marsian kneaders holding the mule-dray in the dark lane outside, and walked straight down the echoing travertine arcade to **Vault Twenty-Four**—the non-quota commercial surplus bin where merchant-shippers stored excess Sicilian flint-wheat (*triticum durum*) above state *annona* levy.

Inside the high brick doorway, beside an iron brazier kept carefully screened in brass wire-mesh ten feet clear of the dry grain-dust, sat **Decimus Valerius**.

Twenty-six years old, discharged two springs back after six years pulling marine oars and boarding-hooks in the Misene fleet, Decimus had broad, sun-darkened Campanian shoulders bursting the seams of a sleeveless scarlet tunic, cropped black curls oiled with cheap spikenard, a heavy silver ring stamped with Ceres's wheat-ear on his thumb, and the lazy, insolent dark eyes of a petty gatekeeper who grew rich charging rich men's factors midnight grease-money.

He looked up from trimming his fingernails with a bronze pocket-blade as eighteen-year-old Lucida stepped into the lantern-circle and pushed her dark wool hood back off her braided hair.

"Shut till the first horn, sweetheart," Decimus drawled automatically, boots propped comfortably on an empty olive-firkin, then lowered both boots flat onto the stone as he recognized her face from the Emporium quays. A slow, appreciative grin showed white teeth under dark moustache-stubble. "Wait—Habinnas Barba's pretty Tyrian partner from the Fish-Market ovens! Out walking the docks alone at midnight without your mute shadow?"

"Baebius dumped forty-eight *modii* of wet Messana weevil-sweepings at my gate at dusk," Lucida said without preface, stepping right up to his counting-desk and laying **eight gold *aurei*** (800 HS, sixteen sesterces the *modius* for fifty *modii* of four-sesterce wheat!) in a neat gleaming yellow line across his scarred pine board. "Vault Twenty-Four holds eleven hundred *modii* of dry Agrigentine surplus consigned to Numerius Flaccus, which you have authority to release against cash deposit plus shrinkage-margin. Measure fifty clean *modii* onto my mule-dray outside the east postern before the third watch ends: Flaccus gets his two hundred fifty sesterces book-price in the morning, and **five hundred fifty sesterces stays in your belt** for losing half an hour's sleep."

Decimus looked down at the eight gold pieces shining under the hanging lamp—more silver than the *Horrea* paid a deputy *vilicus* across four months, and whistled softly through his teeth.

Then he leaned back against the thick Lebanese cedar planks holding back forty wagon-loads of warm Sicilian wheat, scratching his bare collarbone with the flat of his bronze nail-knife, and didn't touch the gold.

"Five hundred fifty sesterces is sweet honey," Decimus said, dark eyes dropping unhurriedly from Lucida's wind-flushed cheeks down the open throat of her brown woollen *paenula* to the curve of her waist and hips underneath. "Trouble is, **Hermippus**'s market-clerk came round all three courtyards at sunset buying wine and whispering that anybody who let a single sack of private Sicilian grain roll toward the Fish-Market before morning would have Publius Vedius Pollio's harbour-factors blacklisting his cousins from Ostia to Puteoli. Eight gold pieces buys a man's blind eye on ordinary nights, Tyrian; risking Hermippus's teeth takes friendlier persuasion."

He stood up off the stool—two inches shorter than Corvus, thicker through the neck and forearms, smelling strongly of Campanian red wine, dry wheat-chaff, and hot male skin, stepping round the counting-table until barely two feet separated his scarlet chest from hers.

"Two winters I've watched you walk down Bollard Twelve with your chin in the air as if dock-men were mud under your calfskin soles," Decimus murmured, reaching out one broad, calloused thumb to brush the loose dark curl beside her ear—not grabbing, waiting with cocky, unmistakable hunger to see whether desperation made the proud eighteen-year-old Fish-Market *exercitor* beg, cry, or run. "Leave six of those yellow coins on the pine for Flaccus and my gate-boys, leave the knife in your right boot on that stool, and give me half an hour behind the cedar partition where it's warm, and my porters load **sixty** *modii* of cleanest Agrigentine amber onto your dray before the watch-horn blows."

Inside Vault Twenty-Four the hanging oil-flame hissed softly; behind six-inch cedar retaining-planks, mountains of dry sun-heated Sicilian grain ticked faint and warm in the quiet.

Lucida stood motionless with Decimus's thumb half an inch from her jaw, doing the coldest, cleanest ledger-reckoning of her eighteen years.

Walk out that brick arch with her pride untouched and eight gold pieces in her purse: at dawn Sura's chalk stayed blank beneath **DIES DLXV**, five hundred sixty-five mornings died, Trajan's three-year citizenship road collapsed to rubble, and **Publius Vedius Pollio** owned her labor, her ovens, and her corpse forever.

More than that—deeper down beneath numbers, down where five months of silent fury and starved eighteen-year-old blood had burned ever since she watched **Tullia Crispina**'s ringed white hand stroke Corvus's bare forearm under Pompey's plane-trees while Corvus climbed toward his gold ring, something dark, fierce, and wholly unsentimental uncoiled behind Lucida's breastbone.

Men in Rome—senators, shipping widows, equestrians, dock-bailiffs, treated a woman's body as either a locked strongbox whose single scratch ruined the owner, or a helpless rag thrown down in mud. **Lucida Tyria's daughter belonged to neither camp.** Corvus had not lost one ounce of his mind or iron taking twenty-five thousand sesterces out of Tullia Crispina's seaside bed at Laurentum; why the Hades should an eighteen-year-old woman let some cocky Misene marine think taking half an hour of warm skin put a collar round *her* neck, when sixty *modii* of prime wheat bought **five hundred sixty-six unbroken mornings toward Roman liberty**—and her own eighteen-year-old body was tired to death of sleeping cold on virtuous flour-sacks waiting for a boy who smelled of lemon-verbena?

Lucida did not tremble, did not drop her eyes, and did not touch the knife in her boot.

Instead she reached up with steady, stain-faded fingers, caught Decimus's wrist before his thumb touched her cheek, and looked straight into his surprised dark eyes from six inches away.

"Three rules, marine," Lucida said, her voice low, smoky, steady as weighed bronze. "First: you write and stamp the sixty-*modius* gate-release tablet with Numerius Flaccus's bin-seal *right now* while all eight gold pieces sit on this table, and hand the waxed wood into *my* hand before a hook unpins my cloak. Second: my boot-knife stays in my boot, your hands stay off my throat and hair, and you spill your seed on the cedar planks, never inside me—give me a bastard belly in Year Two and I'll pay Berenice's alley-boys four gold pieces to feed your balls to the Emporium eels. Third: sixty *modii* sit loaded on my mule-dray within twenty breaths after I buckle my belt. Agree, or keep your four-sesterce wages and watch me buy Gate One."

Decimus stared at her parted dark lips and blazing amber eyes, and whatever petty bully's triumph he had expected vanished instantly into hard, stunned physical arousal at dealing with a predator instead of a rabbit.

"Gods in Baiae," Decimus breathed, half-laughing under his breath as his hand went straight for the iron bin-seal and wax diptych on his desk. "Done—watch me write it!"

Within forty heartbeats the official two-leaved **Horrea Galbana** release-warrant for **sixty *modii* of Lot Nine Agrigentine hard wheat, paid in full**, sat stamped in warm red wax and tucked securely inside Lucida's leather money-belt beside two recovered gold *aurei* (she swept two of the eight back into her own purse the instant he said *six*!).

Lucida walked past him behind the tall cedar grain-partition where clean, folded linen grain-tarpaulins lay stacked waist-high against warm Lebanese timber, dropped the heavy iron privacy-latch across the inner wicket *herself*, unpinned her woollen *paenula* onto a clean sack, and unbuckled her belt, leaving her linen shift on, hiked loose above her bare olive thighs and hips while pulling ONLY the left shoulder-cord down far enough that Decimus's mouth found warm bare breast and the dark olive-pip mark under her collarbone as she pushed his scarlet tunic off his heavy muscled shoulders.

There were no gentle whispers and no romantic lies behind the cedar grain-wall. Decimus was twenty-six, clean-skinned, hard-muscled from fleet oars, smelling of salt sweat and crushed wheat rather than Caelian perfume; when he gripped both bare curves of her thighs to lift her hips back onto the waist-high stack of folded linen tarpaulins and knelt first for twenty fierce, greedy breaths with his hot mouth and rough tongue buried straight between her parted legs until her thighs clamped tight round his cropped black curls and a sharp, silent shudder arched her spine against the warm cedar wood, eighteen-year-old Lucida bit down hard on the knuckle of her own forefinger so not one sound escaped into Courtyard Two—eyes wide open on the rough brick vault overhead, letting pure animal heat wash five months of knotted tension clean out of her belly!

"Look at me, Tyrian," Decimus panted hoarsely, rising between her knees with his thick, heavy shaft slick against her hip, hands braced on the stacked linen on either side of her ribs as she guided him inside herself with her own steady hand, setting the angle and depth herself, wrapping both strong ankles tight above his muscled buttocks to control every hard, driving thrust against the creaking cedar grain-wall! Every slam of timber behind her shoulder-blades sent a tiny golden shower of dry Sicilian wheat-kernels trickling over the top plank down around their sweating shoulders; she dragged blunt fingernails hard across his broad sunburned back, refused his mouth when he tried to kiss her lips—pressing her hot cheek instead against his pounding neck-vein, driving her hips back into his hard rhythm until a fierce, hot spasm clenched straight up from the core of her groin through her ribs, and two breaths before Decimus groaned deep in his chest to spend, she unlocked her heels and shoved firmly against his lower ribs so he pulled out gasping to spill hot white across the dusty cedar floor-planks and empty hemp sacks just as agreed.

Ten breaths later—while Decimus still leaned with one hand braced on the timber catching his wind and staring at her in dazed admiration, Lucida was already wiping her inner thighs dry with clean linen tow, tying her shoulder-cord snug, buckling her leather money-belt with the waxed sixty-*modius* release warrant over her smoothed brown tunic, and shaking three golden Sicilian wheat-kernels out of her braids.

"Call the four night-porters in off the steps, Valerius," Lucida said evenly, voice steady and brisk as if they had just finished weighing salt-fish. "Sixty *modii* out of Bin Nine, bottom-probed by my own horn before they tie the necks."

Decimus dragged his scarlet tunic back over his damp head, shook himself with a crooked, rueful grin, and reached for his brass whistle. "Come back anytime Baebius wets your wheat, Fish-Market—next month I won't even charge Flaccus's six yellow chicks."

"Next month," Lucida replied, unlatching the cedar wicket and stepping out into the crisp September night air without looking back at the rumpled tarpaulin, "I won't be buying out of anybody else's bin."

* * *

At half an hour before dawn, with all sixty *modii* of flint-hard golden Agrigentine grain roaring clean through four freshly dressed Orvieto lava mills behind the Fish-Market and **ANNVS II · DIES DLXVI — MODII CXIV** chalked safe and white on Sura's slate!, Lucida stood naked behind the bolted stable-screen pouring bucket after bucket of cold well-water blended with sharp fuller's vinegar over her head, breasts, belly, and thighs.

She scrubbed with coarse pumice-meal until not one trace of cheap spikenard or another man's sweat clung to her skin, braided her wet blue-black hair tight in the red dawn light, and dressed in fresh-ironed white wool.

Looking down at her own steady hands lacing her boots, she waited to feel some crack of girlhood grief or dirtiness. Nothing came—only a strange, hard, weightless relief, as if a last childhood tether tying her body to an imaginary Sabine cottage with Corvus had finally been cut cleanly with her own knife, leaving both arms free to fight on equal ground.

By noon, carrying a heavy canvas bag containing **twelve thousand sesterces** out of her accumulated Caelian cosmetic and Green-stable reserves, she climbed the noisy Argiletum stairs first to check old **Berenice**'s drying-racks before meeting her guild straw-buyer.

Inside the dim herb-scented shop, sixty-three-year-old Berenice was pounding dried Pontic wormwood in a brass mortar while cursing the dust raining down through her ceiling joists from hammering two streets east.

"Hear those iron crowbars up on the Clivus Suburanus, little hawk?" Berenice spat into the hearth-ash, pointing her pestle toward the window. "Urban Prefect's building-commissioners again! Tore the sixth and seventh timber floors clean off Numerius Vettius's cousin's tenement at dawn because the eaves stood sixty-eight Roman feet above the curb instead of Trajan's sixty, threw thirty weavers' mattresses into the gutter and slapped forty thousand sesterces demolition liens across the ground-title! You know whose name every landlord from the Firewall to the Carinae is spitting into red wine this week when they curse the wrecking-hooks?"

Lucida kept her fingers still sorting dried rose-hips on the marble tombstone slab. "Senator Priscus's?"

"Priscus sits clean in the Senate eating fennel!" Berenice cackled grimly. "Out on the cobbles with brass surveyor's rods and ten *vigiles* hook-men behind him walks **your eighteen-year-old Sabine crow—Lucius Cassius Corvus**! Tall as a spear, cold as January sleet, measuring poor folks' roof-tiles to the quarter-inch for his Caelian masters! Keep your Fish-Market grain-loft low, girl, or your childhood sweetheart will take a crowbar to your shingles before winter, unless you go round to his Carinae bachelor door at dusk and beg him prettily to skip your street!"

Lucida dusted dried rose-chaff briskly off her palms and tied her canvas money-bag shut with two sharp jerks.

"The day I beg Lucius Cassius Corvus to skip my street," Lucida said quietly, "you can pour your green savin bottle into my own porridge, Beroe."

Half an hour later she stood two hundred yards uphill on the wide, roaring **Clivus Suburanus** itself, beside seventy-year-old **Marcus Ulpius Felix**—a deaf, granite-honest retired citizen baker from Salvius's burial guild who hated Hermippus like poison and had agreed for two hundred sesterces fee to hold legal Quiritary straw-title (*fiducia cum amico*) with secret simultaneous counter-bond in Lucida's strongbox until Day 1,095 made her a full Roman citizen!

Directly in front of them, wedged between a thriving coppersmith's forge and an oil-press, yawned a forty-by-ninety-foot fire-gutted commercial lot whose upper timber floors had burned down three winters back, leaving **indestructible three-foot-thick Republican peperino stone ground-piers** and, twenty feet underneath the char-ash, **two bone-dry vaulted tufa cellars capable of storing two thousand *modii* of Sicilian hard wheat** behind Lucida's own Laconian iron locks where neither Vedius Pollio's bribed skippers nor midnight *Horrea* gatekeepers could ever choke her hoppers again!

Better still: once cleared and roofed in fireproof brick arches under Trajan's precise fifty-eight-foot legal limit—two clear Roman feet below Corvus's sixty-foot wrecking-rod!, the street-front arches would rent to four honest Subura shops clearing thirty percent a year!

Across a portable folding table set on the curb, the bankrupt owner's auction-banker (*coactor argentarius*) tapped his wax hammer over the boundary-plan while sixty yards further up the steep basalt street—surrounded by four helmeted *vigiles* surveyors holding painted ten-foot measuring-poles against a tall fuller's cornice!, a tall, dark-haired eighteen-year-old in a crisp white citizen toga stood squinting upward along a brass plumb-bob.

Corvus did not look downhill through the market-wagons and never saw the dark-veiled young woman standing beside old Ulpius Felix at the auction table.

Lucida watched Corvus's distant white shoulder in the bright September sun for three slow heartbeats, touched the waxed sixty-*modius* *Horrea* tablet still tucked inside her belt as proof of what last night had cost, and leaned close to deaf old Felix's ear.

"Put down the twelve thousand cash silver right now, Uncle Felix," Lucida said clearly, "and tell the *coactor* our masons start laying dry brick over those wheat-vaults on Monday morning."
