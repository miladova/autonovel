# Chapter XXV — Saturn's Scales

*Third Day Before the Ides of December, AD 160 · Corvus*

Inside the subterranean travertine vaults beneath the **Temple of Saturn**, where Rome had locked its bullion, bronze laws, and fiscal contracts since the Kings were driven out, winter air never warmed past the temperature of river-bottom stone.

Twenty-year-old Corvus stood beside a lead-sheathed stone counting-pier thirty feet below the Forum pavement, warming stiff fingers briefly above a caged brass oil-lantern—open braziers were forbidden on pain of death inside the **Aerarium Saturni**, while two grey-haired public treasury slaves hauled Chest Nineteen of the *Praedia et Figlinae* contractor-rolls out of a recessed peperino niche.

Up above in the yellow December river-fog wrapping the eight colossal granite columns of Saturn's porch, Rome smelled of waiting.

Old Urban Prefect **Lollius Urbicus** lay five months ash inside his tomb; old Praetorian Prefect **Tattius Maximus** had died in autumn, leaving the Guard divided between upright **Furius Victorinus** and Lysistrata's greedy creature **Sextus Cornelius Repentinus**; out at Lorium seventy-four-year-old **Antoninus Pius** had canceled two straight December salutations on account of winter catarrh; and every money-changer along the Janus Medius charged half a percent higher interest on ninety-day silver because nobody knew whose face would sit stamped on April's coins.

Beside Corvus, sixty-year-old **Phileros** wiped watering eyes with a linen rag, holding a magnifying globe of clear water-filled rock crystal two inches from the unrolled parchment schedule of **Publius Vedius Pollio**'s *Figlinae Subortanae* imperial brick-supply contract from AD 155–156, the exact years **Domitia Lucilla**, mother of Marcus Caesar, lay dying while Vedius's factors stripped her clay-beds bare!

"My left eye sees nothing save grey cobwebs in lamp-light now, Sabine," Phileros murmured without self-pity, sliding the crystal globe across the stone pier into Corvus's scarred right hand. "Read the fiscal voucher totals certified by Procurator **Rufus** for Kilns One through Four in the consulship of Severus and Sabinianus, and compare them against the five pine tallies you carried out of the mud at fifteen."

Corvus didn't even need the crystal globe; at twenty his grey-green surveyor's eyes read faded four-year-old iron-gall ink in cellar gloom as sharply as noon chisel-cuts.

Out of his inner leather pouch Corvus drew two things he had carried since October 156: the heavy four-pound circular bronze **signaculum** die (*EX · PR · DOM · LVCILLAE · OP · DOL · L · NAEVI · RVFI*) and the five split pine kiln-tallies stolen off Rufus's counting-table while Mousa bled in the fog.

Line by line across cold stone, four and a half years of buried math locked shut with the clean, terrible ring of an iron portcullis dropping home.

"In AD 155 and 156," Corvus read aloud quietly, scarred thumb tracking the Treasury parchment column, "Publius Vedius Pollio's supply-company drew **seven hundred twenty thousand sesterces** out of the *fiscus* and the *opera publica* chest for delivering two hundred forty thousand first-grade two-foot *bipedales* bricks stamped from Kilns One through Four of *Figlinae Subortanae* for the repair of Trajan's Market vaults and the Ostian granaries. Look at the physical kiln-firing tallies Rufus notched for his own clay-gangs on those exact dates: Kilns One through Four fired **sixty thousand bricks total** across both seasons! One hundred eighty thousand *bipedales*—three-quarters of the entire imperial draw, **five hundred forty thousand sesterces of pure phantom clay**, never entered an oven! Rufus stamped sample top-courses with this bronze die using Domitia Lucilla's exempted tax-mark while shipping cheap unfired sun-dried rubble-fill behind the facing walls!"

Phileros smiled, thin, pale, corpse-cold in the yellow lantern-light.

"Under the *Lex Iulia de Residuis* and Hadrian's fiscal constitutions," Phileros whispered, "defrauding the public *opera* on falsified imperial estate stamps carries **quadruple restitution**—two million one hundred sixty thousand sesterces instant Treasury lien against every coin, warehouse, and tenement Publius Vedius Pollio owns! And under the statutory *praemium* schedule for private citizen *delatores* who prove hidden public embezzlement without state help, the prosecuting citizen recovers **one full fifth of collected principal, four hundred thirty-two thousand sesterces** paid straight across Saturn's bronze balance!"

Corvus's breath misted white and motionless above the parchment.

**Four hundred thirty-two thousand sesterces** in a single statutory stroke, vaulting over the **four-hundred-thousand-sesterce equestrian census** in one morning even without touching the **one hundred ninety-four thousand sesterces** already sitting clean inside his Castor box! Six hundred thousand sesterces total—the narrow purple stripe, the gold equestrian ring on his scarred hand, untouchable citizen immunity from the Esquiline cross forever!

"Wait," Corvus said, refusing to let gold dazzle one corner of his surveyor's eye as he unrolled **Sheet Seven** attached to Vedius's Treasury surety-file. "Hermippus isn't stupid. Two years ago, right after I broke Spurius Carvilius's Sandaliarius deed, Hermippus transferred legal Quiritary title over Vedius's Subura commercial leases, Ostian grain-sheds, and Aulus Lucidius's Puteoli patronal reversions into a separate **protective *fiducia cum amico* shield-trust** conveyed *per aes et libram* to Vedius's straw knight **Gaius Munatius**, specifically so that if a fiscal fine ever hit Vedius's brick-company, Vedius's Subura empire sat walled off behind Munatius's bronze-and-scales deed!"

Corvus pulled Sheet Seven, the certified archive copy of that protective *mancipatio* deed—under the brass lantern and ran his scarred thumb straight down the five statutory Roman citizen witnesses listed along the spine:

*1. P. Cornelius Scipio Salvidienus · 2. L. Vibius Rufus · 3. M. Pompeius Silo · 4. C. Terentius Varro · 5. **SEX · VIBIVS · M · F · FAL · BALBVS**.*

Corvus stopped dead on Witness Five.

*Sextus Vibius Balbus, son of Marcus, of the Falerna tribe*, a Campanian municipal knight from Capua!

"Phileros," Corvus said very softly, pulse slowing into that dead, lethal courtroom stillness that always preceded blood, "hand me the Praetorian Prefect's provincial *infamia* index roll from Chest Four, Campania, consulship of Bradua and Varus, four months *before* Vedius executed this shield-trust!"

Even half-blind Phileros sucked breath sharp through his teeth as Corvus dragged down the Campanian roll, flipped three stiff vellum sheets, and stabbed one ink-stained fingernail squarely onto Entry Nine under **CAPVA**:

*SEXTVS VIBIVS M · F · FAL · BALBVS — CALVMNIAE DAMNATVS IN IVDICIO PVBLICO ANTE III ID · MAI · BRADVA ET VARO COSS.*

*Condemned for malicious false accusation in a public criminal trial on the thirteenth of May, four months before standing as fifth citizen witness beside Publius Vedius Pollio's bronze scales!*

Under the Praetor's Perpetual Edict and the Law of the Twelve Tables, a man condemned of *calumnia* bore lifelong **infamia** and became **intestabilis**, just as dead in law beside the *mancipatio* balance as stage-player Decimus Atilius had been in Chapter Eleven's Sandaliarius rehearsal!

Vedius Pollio's entire two-million-sesterce protective shield-trust was **rotten wood from the hour the bronze struck the scale**—meaning the instant Corvus proved *both* the Subortanae brick-fraud AND the Caelian fifty-digit lead *fistulae* water-theft (still sitting corked red inside Priscus's cedar cupboard!), **every single asset Vedius thought sheltered, every Ostian lease, every Subura mill-lien, AND Vedius's patronal reversion and Castor Vault Forty-Two!**, collapsed straight into fiscal receivership!

"One problem left before we spring the trap," Phileros cautioned low, closing Chest Nineteen silently before the treasury slave returned. "Gaius Munatius's trust-deed omits Hermippus's private side-ledger proving Vedius kept beneficial enjoyment after the sham sale. To obtain an *instantaneous* emergency interdict and receivership transfer *per aes et libram* on court-day without giving Hermippus forty-eight hours to burn Castor Vault Forty-Two or spirit gold out of Rome, we need **one living patrician or equestrian insider whose own debt-papers carry Hermippus's cross-seal linking Munatius's trust directly back to Vedius's personal chest**—plus five unimpeachable Roman citizen witnesses standing shoulder-to-shoulder at the Praetor's spear!"

Phileros looked meaningfully across the cold lantern-flame at Corvus.

"And tonight at midnight," Phileros added dryly, "Hermippus forecloses **one hundred ten thousand sesterces** of compounded Blue-chariot dice notes against Senator Priscus's own nephew **Aulus Volusenus Niger**, whom Senator Priscus washed his hands of yesterday morning, swearing he would let the boy be posted *decoctor* on the Forum column rather than pay one copper to horse-thieves!"

Corvus slowly rolled the bronze *signaculum* die back into oiled leather.

*Hermippus's cross-seal linking Munatius's trust, sitting right on Aulus Volusenus Niger's defaulted gambling notes!*

* * *

At the fourth hour of a freezing December night, thick white river-fog had climbed all the way up onto the **Quirinal Hill**, muffling street-lamps to drowned yellow blurs and glazing frost across the silent bronze doors of the **Domus Volusenia**.

Nobody answered Corvus's knock at the main street-portal; every doorkeeper, Gaditane flute-girl, and silver candelabrum from June's drunken symposium had already been sold off to Saepta brokers over the past month to stave off minor creditors. Walking round to the narrow side service-alley, twenty-year-old Corvus pushed against the unbolted oak garden door, felt it swing inward on dry hinges with a mournful creak, and stepped alone into the dark, unheated marble *atrium*.

Moonlight filtering pale and ghostly through fog above the open ceiling *compluvium* showed stripped ancestor-cupboards, dark rectangular dust-scars on plastered walls where painted panels had been pried down for silver, and—dry leaves blown ankle-deep round the dry central rainwater *impluvium* basin.

Sitting slumped on the cold marble rim of that dry basin in a rumpled, wine-stained wide-striped senatorial tunic, barefoot in freezing forty-degree air, unshaved for four days, without a live coal burning anywhere in the cavernous house, sat twenty-three-year-old **Aulus Volusenus Niger**.

Between both puffy, trembling hands Volusenus held a broad-bladed twelve-inch Noric steel **boar-hunting spearhead-knife** (*culter venatorius*), its razor point resting pressed sideways deep into the crease of his own bare right groin directly over the pulsing femoral artery, where Roman patricians who lacked Cato's nerve to rip open their own bowels opened a two-inch slit that bled a man white to sleep inside three hundred breaths before tomorrow noon's *infamia* placard nailed his father's name to the bankruptcy column.

Hearing Corvus's calm hobnailed step cross the threshold mosaic, Volusenus jerked violently, nicking a quarter-inch bright red bead out of his inner thigh before squinting wild-eyed and tear-streaked through the fog-gloom.

"Hermippus's dogs come early?" Volusenus choked out with a ragged, hysterical laugh that broke in the middle. "Tell your filthy Smyrniot master he's six hours shy of the Kalends, and by dawn all he auctions off this basin is cold meat! Wait." Volusenus blinked hard as Corvus stepped out of pillar-shadow into the grey *compluvium* light. "**Cassius?** Did my uncle send his pet Sabine crow uphill to watch a Volusenus open his leg properly so Priscus can boast of Republican severity in the Curia?"

Twenty-year-old Corvus stood six feet away across dry blown plane-leaves, hands tucked warm inside his dark grey cloak, looking down at the boy who had split his fifteen-year-old lip open with an ivory stylus in Festus's rhetoric gallery while sixteen rich youths laughed, the boy who six months ago in the adjoining *triclinium* had tried to make nineteen-year-old Lucida kneel with ankle-oil before drunken advocates.

One quiet turn on his heel out that garden door, and inside two hundred breaths Aulus Volusenus Niger bled out into the dry rainwater basin, leaving **no rival left inside Priscus's house**.

Instead Corvus stepped smoothly forward, faster than drunken, shivering Volusenus could push steel, and **kicked the flat wood handle of the boar-knife clean out of Volusenus's numb fingers**, sending Noric steel skittering and clanging twenty feet across empty black-and-white mosaic into the corner!

Volusenus doubled over gasping, clutching his nicked thigh with both hands before collapsing into raw, ugly, snot-and-salt weeping against his bare knees, all twenty-three years of oiled Quirinal swagger gone to empty wet rags.

"Get your tunic down over your leg before you freeze your balls off on cold Luna marble, Aulus," Corvus said flatly, without a shred of either theatrical pity or gloating in his deep Sabine voice as he squatted two paces off on his heels and drew **three heavy, red-sealed wax *chirographa* tablets** out of his leather satchel. "And read the seals on these before you waste good steel."

Volusenus wiped his wet nose roughly on his purple *latus clavus* stripe, squinting down at the three tablets Corvus tossed onto the dry basin-rim beside him.

"My eighty-thousand-sesterce Circus note from March, rolled into **one hundred twelve thousand** payable to Gaius Munatius's trust at dawn!" Volusenus whispered, staring in stunned disbelief from Hermippus's emerald scarab seal up into Corvus's calm grey-green eyes. "Where in Hades did you get Hermippus's active collection-tablets two hours past midnight?!"

"Bought the paper *half an hour* ago through an Ostian shipping factor out of Munatius's greedy junior partner's hand for **forty-two thousand sesterces cash silver** out of my own Castor strongbox," Corvus said calmly, pulling a flint-striker, iron tinder-horn, and bundle of dry pine-shavings out of his pocket to light a small yellow flame right inside the cold bronze brazier-pan standing beside the basin. "Because a careless patrician who never read Julian's title on *usurae* didn't notice that Hermippus rolled thirty-two thousand sesterces of unpaid monthly interest directly into new interest-bearing principal: **illegal compound *anatocismus***, utterly void under every Senate decree since Lucullus, which meant Munatius's factor jumped at forty-two thousand hard silver tonight rather than risk having me plead statutory usury-voidance in court tomorrow."

Volusenus stared mesmerized at the small yellow pine-flame licking up inside the cold brazier, scarcely daring to breathe. "Forty-two thousand of *your* silver... so now *you* own my bankruptcy writ at dawn instead of Vedius Pollio?"

Corvus picked up the two compounding penalty-tablets, seventy thousand sesterces of crushing, career-killing *infamia* paper, and **held their waxed corners straight into the yellow pine-flame**!

Black beeswax hissed, bubbled, and dripped blazing into the cold ash; dry pine wood caught orange along the margins as seventy thousand sesterces of patrician ruin curled into black smoke between Corvus's scarred right thumb and forefinger, leaving **only the clean base forty-two-thousand-sesterce receipt bearing Hermippus's cross-seal to Munatius's trust** intact in Corvus's left hand.

Volusenus's mouth fell open in the orange fire-glow; tears cut clean tracks through the four-day stubble on his cheeks. "Why?" he whispered hoarsely. "I split your mouth open when you didn't even own shoes. I dragged your girl in front of Aufidius's couches in June."

"Touch her name a second time while we both breathe, Aulus, and I'll pick that boar-knife out of the corner and open your groin myself," Corvus said quietly, conversationally, watching the last wax seal turn to grey cinder in the pan. "You owe me forty-two thousand sesterces at honest four percent payable across five years out of your Sabine olive-rents, keeping your senatorial stripe whole and your name off tomorrow's *decoctor* column. In return, I want **two things** signed right now before this pine-shaving goes out."

"Name them!" Volusenus breathed, grabbing eagerly for Corvus's stylus with shaking ringed hands.

"First: your sworn affidavit authenticating Hermippus's own cross-endorsement on this base bond directing payment into **Gaius Munatius's *fiducia* chest for the personal account of Publius Vedius Pollio**, the missing link proving Munatius's trust is a sham front for Vedius's embezzled Treasury silver. Second: on the day old Antoninus dies, or the day Senator Priscus gives me the word, whichever strikes first, **Aulus Volusenus Niger stands beside the Praetor's bronze scales in clean white patrician wool as my fifth Roman citizen witness** when I break Publius Vedius Pollio's entire world in half. Break oath or whisper one word to Hermippus, and this forty-two-thousand base note goes onto the column at noon."

Volusenus did not hesitate half a breath; dropping onto both knees beside the small dying flame in his stripped ancestral hall, Senator Priscus's proud nephew wrote six clean lines of authentication across fresh wax, pressed his red carnelian *quadriga* ring deep into the seal-groove, and caught Corvus's scarred right wrist roughly with both cold hands before Corvus could rise.

"When you strike that fat oyster's shell open, Lucius Cassius," Volusenus whispered fiercely, patrician humiliation burned clean over into lethal convert's zeal against the usurer who had nearly driven steel into his groin, "call me at midnight or noon. I'll hold the bronze scale steady with my own teeth!"

* * *

Six evenings later on the first night of the **Saturnalia**, when the ancient woollen fetters were unbound from the feet of ivory Saturn down in the Forum and half a million Romans poured roaring through the streets in synthesis dining-robes shouting *"Io Saturnalia!"* while household slaves wore conical white felt **pilleus** liberty-caps for five brief carnival days of dice and unpunished insolence, twenty-year-old Corvus sat over roasted thrushes and warm Sabine wine inside **Tullia Crispina's** glazed Carinae winter-triclinium.

Four months after August's great Subura fire, municipal reconstruction orders for seasoned Apennine fir roof-beams and Campanian lime had tripled freight rates along the Tiber wharves; across the inlaid citrus-wood table Crispina's freedman steward counted Corvus's quarter-share dividend from their joint Ostian timber-barges: **thirty-six thousand sesterces** in clean Antonine silver denarii, lifting Corvus's personal liquid reserve back to **one hundred eighty-eight thousand sesterces** even after advancing Volusenus's rescue silver.

"Put two hundred and twelve thousand more beside it before summer, Lucius Cassius," thirty-one-year-old Crispina murmured, leaning one bare ivory-bracelet wrist on her crimson dining-cushion while outside her garden wall drunken Subura fullers beat brass pans and sang ribald verses about Caesar's doctors, "and you won't need Quintus Servilius Priscus's patronage to ask the Censor's clerk for the horse-and-ring. Or", her level dark eyes rested calm and unblushing on his scarred mouth across the lamp-cluster, "marry a Carinae equestrian widow whose timber-wharves already clear four hundred thousand on the Censor's books, and skip waiting for old emperors to cough."

It was the cleanest, safest staircase left in Rome: instant equestrian sufficiency, warm linen, intelligent alliance, no blood in flooded brick cellars, no teeth-marks left burning across his lower lip.

Outside in the frosty lane beyond Crispina's bronze garden-grille, three tipsy Cappadocian litter-slaves stumbled past carrying pine torches, knocking white felt carnival *pillei* askew over scarred ears and roaring laughter that would turn back into iron whip-scars on the morning of the twenty-fourth when the felt caps came off.

Half a mile down the dark hill behind the Forum Piscarium, Corvus knew, nobody was wearing carnival felt tonight; four black volcanic Orvieto millstones were turning blind and relentless through Saturnalia night just as they turned every other night of the year, white flour powdering dark braids and burned olive forearms while sixty-five-year-old Habinnas Barba coughed rusty paste into his rag so a real bronze plaque—not five days of drunken wool, would stand nailed above the Porticus Minucia in April.

"A man who marries his four hundred thousand across a supper-table never owns his own boots when storm-wind hits the porch, Tullia," Corvus said evenly, signing the timber-quittance with a steady hand and rising into the cold Saturnalia torch-glare. "Keep the barges loaded with seasoned fir; by March I bring my own four hundred thousand to the scales."
