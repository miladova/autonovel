# Chapter VII — Four Spears in the Marble

*Sixteenth Day Before the Kalends of June, AD 157 · Corvus*

Inside the **Basilica Julia**, four lawsuits were screaming at one another across four hundred feet of polished marble floor while thirty idle fishmongers on the outer steps played knucklebones over carved circles in the stone.

Corvus stood behind the third cedar bench of the Second *Hasta* with twenty-two pounds of bound boxwood tablets bruising his left collar-bone, sweating inside his clean grey woollen tunic, counting the hired clappers in the gallery overhead.

There were twenty-four of them today, arranged in two neat wedges along the upper travertine balustrade where their shouts would bounce hardest off the coffered timber roof: unshaved freedmen and out-of-work fuller's porters in borrowed chalk-whitened togas, watching neither the forty-five Centumviral judges sweating on their curved wooden tiers nor the frantic junior advocate down by the water-jar, watching only a fat Greek *mesochorus* in a yellow scarf standing behind the third fluted pilaster. Whenever the defence speaker **Gaius Sulpicius Rufus** paused to touch hisperfumed handkerchief to his upper lip and drop his voice a fourth into tragic vibrato, the fat Greek lifted two fingers; instantly all twenty-four *laudiceni* roared *"Bene! Pulchre! Belle!"* in ragged unison, earning three silver denarii apiece plus a basket of cold pork at sunset without understanding six words of Republican land-law between them.

Beside each of the four separate tribunals—screened clumsily from its neighbors by heavy purple-dyed sailcloth curtains hung between giallo antico columns, through which the bellowing of three rival advocates leaked like bulls fighting in adjacent stalls, stood an ancient, iron-butted ashwood spear wedged upright in a bronze floor-socket.

*Sub hasta.* Under the spear.

Senator Priscus had told Corvus why on their walk down the Clivus Scauri at dawn: because before Romans had praetors or wax tablets or Greek rhetoric-masters, a citizen took land with seven feet of iron-tipped ash, and the Centumviral Court kept the spear standing in the marble to remind soft men in perfumed wool what lay underneath every boundary-deed if the ink washed off.

Right now the spear of the Second *Hasta* was being twisted six inches deep into Senator Priscus's ribs.

Out in the open semi-circle before the judges' dais stood Priscus's junior pleader **Aufidius Bassus**, a thirty-year-old senator's son with weak lungs and wet pink ears, flanked on the bench by **Aulus Volusenus Niger**—who had prepared the boundary-schedule and spent the entire morning admiring the drape of his own wide purple *latus clavus* stripe in the polished African marble dado instead of checking the opposing *codex*.

Across the chalk line stood **Gaius Sulpicius Rufus**, retained at forty thousand sesterces, everyone in the Forum knew whose gold sat behind the straw litigant—by **Publius Vedius Pollio**'s land-company to swallow sixty *iugera* of prime irrigated market-gardens along the Via Salaria four miles north of the Colline Gate, belonging to an orphaned Sabine widow under Priscus's protection.

"Worthy judges of the Second Spear," Sulpicius Rufus purred, strolling three languid paces toward the front bench with one manicured hand tucked inside his *sinus* fold as Caesar Augustus stood on silver coins, "my learned young friend Aufidius has wept very prettily for half a clepsydra over his client's grandfather's pear-trees. Let us all dry our cheeks and read stone instead of poetry."

Up in the gallery two fingers rose; twenty-four hired throats chuckled dutifully on cue.

Sulpicius took a fresh, triple-sealed municipal extract out of his clerk's hand and held the dangling bronze signets toward the presiding *decemvir*'s nose.

"Certified yesterday afternoon at the fourth hour from the *tabularium* of the *curatores riparum et alvei Tiberis* and the rural census roll by **Marcus Tullius Dama**, sworn *scriba librarius* of the third decury! Let the court-herald read Line Nine: *From the old oak stump beside the Salarian ditch, running east four hundred twenty Roman paces to the terminal boundary-stone of red Fidenae tufa incised with the decumanus cross, acquired without force, stealth, or revocable leave since the consulship of Torquatus and Atticus!* Nine full harvests over the two-year *usucapio* period for Italian soil, judges! Not one foot of the widow's pear-orchard lies west of that red Fidenae stone!"

Aufidius Bassus went the color of boiled turnip, turning helplessly back toward Volusenus on the front bench. "Aulus, you told me the Salarian boundary ran from the *grey* stone at the willow-sluice! Where did he get a certified municipal extract naming red Fidenae tufa?"

Volusenus fumbled wildly through four unbound papyrus rolls on his knees, cinnamon-oiled curls bobbing, knocking an ink-horn sideways onto Phileros's sandal. "There wasn't any red tufa entry when my slave checked the index-tags on Tuesday! Somebody filed a supplemental extract!"

Behind the third bench, fifteen-year-old Corvus stopped counting clappers in the gallery.

*Four miles north of the Colline Gate, between the Salarian ditch and the river-meadow.*

Corvus shut his eyes for three heartbeats inside the deafening marble roar of four courts, a hundred shuffling litigants, and twenty-four hired clappers, until all of Rome dropped away behind his eyelids and he was twelve years old again, walking twenty paces ahead of his father Gaius Cassius Corvus in wet April grass with a red-and-white painted sighting-rod balanced plumb between two chapped hands while larks rose singing out of the Salarian clover.

*Red Fidenae tufa at four hundred twenty paces east of the ditch?*

Fidenae lay two miles *further north* up the Tiber ridge, where volcanic ash had cooled soft and rusty-red full of black scoria lumps. Down in the low alluvial flat at the fourth milestone, four hundred twenty paces east of the Salarian ditch—the river-silt overlay **grey *cappellaccio* ash-stone and blue potter's marl**, soft enough that no honest *agrimensor* since King Numa had ever planted soft volcanic tufa in wet river-meadow, because three winter frosts split porous red tufa into gravel! Rural surveyors planted **hard white Tiburtine travertine** or charred oak stakes ringed with broken amphora-bottoms (*testae*) underneath when marking wet Salarian bottom-land!

Even sharper: why would a thirteen-year-old municipal boundary-roll from the consulship of Torquatus and Atticus, AD 143, fourteen summers back—describe a stone in the exact geological vocabulary of a town clerk who had ridden past the red quarries at Fidenae once in a litter without ever digging a post-hole in flood-clay?

Up on the dais the presiding *decemvir* tapped his ivory wand against the curule chair-arm, looking down with open boredom as a bronze attendant plugged the clepsydra spout for the sixth-hour midday recess.

"The court rises until the eighth hour," the *decemvir* announced over the hubbub. "Unless Counsel for the petitor produces unimpeached counter-record impeaching the sworn municipal extract of Scriba Tullius Dama when the water runs again, I shall instruct the panel on completed *usucapio*."

Up in the gallery all twenty-four *laudiceni* erupted into thunderous, three-denarius cheering as Sulpicius Rufus swept his chalk-bright toga round one shoulder and strolled out toward the shady portico of the Vicus Tuscus surrounded by fawning juniors.

Behind the purple canvas curtain Senator Priscus stood slowly up from the *advocati* bench, lean jaw set like grey peperino, not even looking at stammering Aufidius, looking straight down at his nephew Volusenus.

"Sixty thousand sesterces of a dead equestrian's orchard, Aulus," Priscus said quietly, "and you sent a ten-sesterce bath-slave to read index-tags instead of opening the municipal *codex* with your own eyes."

"Uncle, Tullius Dama is a twenty-year sworn *scriba* of the third decury!" Volusenus protested, red to both ear-tips. "Nobody impeaches a decurial scribe's certified seal in ninety breaths—we'll have to withdraw before peremptory judgment and sue on the warranty!"

"Withdraw before judgment," Phileros murmured dryly while blotting Volusenus's spilled ink off his ankle with a rag, "and half Sabinum hears by market-day that Quintus Servilius Priscus's house cannot hold sixty *iugera* against Vedius Pollio's straw-buyers."

Corvus shifted the twenty-two-pound bundle of boxwood tablets onto his hip and stepped half a pace forward out of the junior clerks' shadow.

"Tullius Dama forged Line Nine after the third hour yesterday, Senator," Corvus said low, pitching his voice beneath the clatter of wooden benches being reset by court-slaves.

Volusenus whirled on him, furious at having a witness to his own dressing-down. "Shut your ditch-mouth, hod-boy, before I have Phileros dock a month's wool! You haven't even seen Dama's master-roll!"

"Let him finish, Aulus," Priscus said without raising his voice. "Speak, boy. How do you know when the line was written without seeing the book?"

"First, because red Fidenae tufa rots to porridge in three winters if you bury it in wet Salarian flood-clay four hundred paces off the ditch; every Sabine surveyor plants white Tiburtine travertine over charcoal and smashed tile on that flat," Corvus said cleanly. "Second, look at Sulpicius Rufus's certified wax copy lying on Aufidius's stool: Dama wrote *ad terminum tofi rubri Fidenatis*, fifteen Latin words crammed into a single marginal clause at the tail of the boundary-string without naming the *subruncivus* witness-marks underneath. In a bound municipal *codex* of waxed pine leaves or heavy linen-rag charta from fourteen years ago, there isn't room mid-column to insert fifteen new words unless the scribe scraped out a shorter closing formula—like *ad fossam veterem*, and wrote small over the hollow, or pasted a fresh marginal strip before sealing the extract."

Phileros's pale eyebrows lifted a quarter-inch. "The municipal *tabularium* annex behind Castor shuts its iron grilles for two hours at noon while the decurial clerks eat sausages and get shaved, patron. Even your senatorial ring won't open the master-chest before the eighth hour—when our clepsydra runs out."

Corvus looked past the towering marble piers toward the sunlit opening of the **Vicus Tuscus**, down which fat, round-shouldered men in scribes' tunics with iron key-rings at their belts were already waddling toward the shade-awnings and fried-fish stalls.

"We don't need the iron chest opened before the eighth hour," Corvus said. "Show me which back belongs to Marcus Tullius Dama, Phileros, and lend me Volusenus's silver pocket-mirror and four silver denarii."

* * *

The **Vicus Tuscus** smelled at midday of three thousand years of money, frankincense, and wet human hair.

Connecting the south-west corner of the Forum Romanum straight down toward the cattle-markets and river-wharves, the narrow basalt street squeezed Syrian silk-drapers, Etruscan bronze-statuette sellers, money-changers ringing silver denarii on touchstones, and open-fronted barbers' booths cheek by jowl beneath projecting wooden balconies that shut out all save a single blinding white ribbon of May sky overhead.

Forty paces past the high podium of the Temple of Castor, inside whose vaulted concrete basement basement bankers kept senators' strongboxes under armed watch—Phileros slowed beside a seller of boiled lupin-beans and touched two fingers lightly to his own chin toward a striped blue-and-white awning across the gutter.

Under a painted wooden sign showing Mercury holding bronze shears and a gilt comb sat **Marcus Tullius Dama**, fifty years old, triple-chinned, his gold-washed decurial scribe's ring gleaming plump on his left little finger while his heavy iron archive-keys rested right on his own lap beneath a coarse linen *involucre* tucked tight under his jaw.

**Glycon the Samian**—a wiry, pock-marked Greek barber with six spare iron razors (*novaculae*) warming in a brass pan of hot water and olive-oil emulsion at his elbow, because Romans shaved without tallow-soap, softening tough bristle with steamed towels and warm oil before scraping skin bare with curved Noric steel honed on oil-stone—had just tilted Dama's pudgy neck backward against the oak headrest and laid the gleaming three-inch crescent blade flat below Dama's right ear.

Beside Dama's stool on a three-legged tripod sat the scribe's leather *theca calamaria*, his personal pen-and-scraper case, which no corrupt record-clerk ever left unguarded back at his desk—next to a half-eaten fig pastry and a cup of watered Sabine wine.

"Sixty breaths before Glycon finishes the jaw-line," Phileros murmured into his sleeve, fading politely behind an incense-seller's rack so his famous Caelian face wouldn't spook the quarry prematurely. "Don't touch his person, striking an apparitor on court-day is *vis publica*. Make him open his own mouth."

Corvus walked straight across the sunny basalt cobbles into the striped blue shadow of Glycon's booth, sat down casually on the empty waiting-stool two feet from Tullius Dama's knees as though waiting his own turn for a two-as hair-trim, and flipped Volusenus's polished silver pocket-mirror once in his scarred right hand so a sharp white coin of noon sun flashed squarely across Dama's half-shut eyelids.

Dama grunted irritably under the steamed oil-cloth without daring to jerk his head while Glycon's curved iron razor hissed *scritch-scritch* down the soft roll of fat above his carotid artery.

"Careful round the windpipe, Glycon," Corvus said conversationally in crisp, slightly burred Sabine Latin, leaning both elbows on his knees and looking admiringly at the leather pen-case on the tripod beside Dama's wine-cup. "My uncle up at Fidenae used to say red volcanic tufa looks very firm in dry weather until a little spring water gets under the crust—then the whole stone crumbles right under your iron."

Under the linen cloth Tullius Dama's plump toes curled rigid inside his slit leather shoes. His watery brown eyes rolled sideways toward the lean fifteen-year-old boy on the waiting-stool.

"Hold your jaw still, worthy scribe, or I nick the mole!" Glycon clucked, lifting the oily steel half an inch to wipe grey stubble onto a piece of tow-rag.

Corvus didn't raise his voice above the sizzle of fried sprats next door; he reached out one bare finger, without touching Dama's person by a hair—and pointed half an inch above the split reed nibs sticking out of Dama's open leather pen-case on the tripod.

"Look at your third reed pen, Master Scribe," Corvus said pleasantly. "Freshly cut oblique nib, still stained purple-grey three fingers up the shaft with oak-gall and copperas *atramentum librarium*, the permanent acid record-ink your decury uses only on linen-charta master-rolls, never on carbon-soot everyday papyrus, because copperas eats weak reed unless you mix it thick. And look under the thumbnail of your right hand resting on those iron keys: two tiny curls of yellow beeswax mixed with old pine-dust from scraping an archive *tabula* seal before re-amping it, plus a grey smudge of pumice-flour round the quick where you rubbed ten-year-old linen-rag fibers smooth at dusk yesterday so fresh copperas wouldn't feather into the erased trough."

Every drop of pink drained out of Tullius Dama's jowls behind the gleaming oil.

Glycon paused with the curved iron *novacula* poised two inches off Dama's Adam's apple, glancing shrewdly from the boy to the sweating official, then tactfully stepped three paces outside his own awning to spit into the gutter and watch the sky—having shaved enough Forum clerks over twenty years to know when a four-denarius tip walked in to buy deaf ears.

Corvus dropped **four silver denarii** quietly onto Glycon's empty shelf, paying for the barber's deafness—then leaned six inches closer to Dama's ear while Dama sat pinned helpless in his wet linen bib like a trussed capon.

"Here is what happens at the eighth hour when the iron grilles behind Castor open, Marcus Tullius," Corvus murmured, grey-green eyes flat and quiet as winter cistern-water. "Senator Quintus Servilius Priscus, who sits on the senatorial commission auditing municipal archive rolls this June—walks straight to Chest Seven with two *decemviri* beside him and holds Sheet Fourteen of the Torquatus-and-Atticus Salarian roll up against afternoon sunlight, with Volusenus's silver mirror reflecting straight up through the back of the linen fibers. Thinned pumice-scraped rag shines bright white from behind like a greased lantern-horn; fresh oak-gall copperas stays greenish-brown for forty-eight hours before air turns it dead black like fourteen-year-old ink; and red Fidenae tufa doesn't exist within two miles of the Salarian river-flat. Under the *Lex Cornelia de Falsis*, altering a public municipal roll for silver carries deportation to Gyaros and confiscation of every sesterce in your strongbox, assuming you're wealthy enough to count as *honestior*. If the court rules a third-decury clerk *humilior*, they break your legs on the Esquiline."

Sweat burst out in fat beads through the olive oil on Dama's forehead, trickling salt into his mouth.

"Who—who the Hades are you?" Dama whispered hoarsely, terrified even to swallow against his tight collar. "Hermippus's broker swore Priscus's nephew hadn't opened the Salarian chest since March!"

*Hermippus's broker.* There sat the name, clean out of Dama's own trembling teeth without a thumbscrew laid on him.

"Priscus's nephew didn't," Corvus agreed calmly. "Gaius Cassius Corvus's son did, whose father drove every white travertine *terminus* along that Salarian ditch before you knew which end of a *groma* pointed north. Now listen carefully, because the seventh-hour sun just touched Castor's third column and you have thirty breaths left with both legs unbroken."

Phileros stepped smoothly out from behind the incense-seller's rack across the lane, already holding an opened two-leaved boxwood *testatio* and a dipped iron stylus like a priest arriving with extreme unction.

"You don't scream *forgery* against yourself in open court, Master Dama," Phileros said gently, laying the tablet right across Dama's knees on top of the linen barber-bib. "Self-accusation is messy and delays supper. Instead, like a conscientious twenty-year servant of the Roman People, you realized over your midday lupin-beans that yesterday's rush copyist accidentally transcribed a rider from the *upper Fidenae hill-lot* on Sheet Twelve onto the *lower Salarian meadow-lot* on Sheet Fourteen—leaving an uncertified blot on the working copy which you now formally correct under your own decurial ring before judgment! Sign five lines of clerical erratum right here while your right cheek is still unshaved, give back Hermippus's twenty gold pieces tonight if he wants them, and keep your house on the Aventine."

Dama's fat right hand shook so badly as he took Phileros's stylus that the first stroke skidded sideways across the black wax; he steadied his wrist against his iron key-ring, scratched the five-line *emendatio* naming the true **white Tiburtine stone at the old willow-sluice**, and pressed his gold-washed decurial ring deep into the warm sealing-grooves while three passing equestrian money-changers called in off the curb witnessed the cord for two sesterces apiece.

Corvus picked Volusenus's silver mirror off his knee, tucked it into his belt, and nodded politely to Glycon at the curb.

"Mind the mole under his right ear, master barber," Corvus said. "The worthy scribe has very delicate skin."

* * *

Back inside the roaring marble belly of the Basilica Julia at the eighth hour, Senator Priscus did not trust stammering Aufidius or preening Volusenus with the kill; he rose in his own short, unchalked Republican toga, walked three slow paces to the *cancelli* rail as the bronze attendant pulled the plug from the clepsydra, and laid Marcus Tullius Dama's wet-sealed clerical *emendatio* silently before the presiding *decemvir* alongside two grey chunks of Salarian river-marl Phileros had sent a mule-boy galloping four miles out the Colline Gate to dig fresh from the ditch.

He spoke for less than a quarter of a clepsydra, six dry, unornamented Latin periods that sounded like oak beams dropping into mortise-slots—never raising his voice once, forcing all forty-five Centumviral judges and even Sulpicius Rufus's twenty-four hired clappers to lean forward straining their ears in dead silence.

Across the aisle **Gaius Sulpicius Rufus** listened with a faint, connoisseur's smile, touched two fingers courteously to his brow in surrender when Dama's own seal was verified, and didn't bother wasting a breath defending a burned clerk, though as Sulpicius turned to gather his scrolls, his dark Bithynian eyes slid past red-faced Volusenus and rested for three long, measuring heartbeats squarely on the tall, black-haired fifteen-year-old standing behind the third bench with an iron stylus tucked behind his ear.

Forty-one wooden voting-tablets out of forty-five came out of the bronze urn marked **A** (*Absolvo / Action Approved*) for Priscus's widow before the ninth hour struck.

Out under the shady northern arcade afterward, while the weeping Sabine widow kissed Priscus's toga-hem, Priscus turned to his nephew.

"Aulus, go pay Aufidius his Viaticum and return your silver mirror to this boy—no, keep the mirror, boy, you used it better than my sister's son did. Phileros: **forty-eight sesterces a week** starting on the Kalends of June, and send him twice a week at the tenth hour to old Antonius Gnipho's freedman for vocal chest-breathing so he doesn't swallow Sabine vowels when he turns seventeen and argues *cognitor* suits for me."

Volusenus stood rigid between two fluted marble columns as his uncle walked ahead toward his litter. Before following, the nineteen-year-old patrician stepped close enough to Corvus that cinnamon beard-oil and sour sweat mingled six inches from Corvus's scarred lip.

"Enjoy the forty-eight sesterces, mud-lark," Volusenus whispered, twisting his red carnelian *quadriga* ring until his knuckle whitened. "My uncle collects clever street-dogs every five years until one bites the wrong ankle, then he drowns them in the Tiber himself."

Corvus looked calmly down two inches into Volusenus's flushed face—because between December's fever and May's sun Corvus had shot up another palm-breadth in bone and shoulder, already overtopping Priscus's plump nephew, and smiled solely with the white scar on his left lip.

"Watch your ink-horn on the steps, Aulus," Corvus said pleasantly. "Marble's slippery when you don't read the stone underneath."

An hour before sunset, carrying two fresh loaves of white *siligo* wheat-bread and a waxed copy of **Gaius's *Institutes*** lent overnight from Phileros's chest, Corvus ducked through the smoky, fish-smelling colonnade behind the **Forum Piscarium** and climbed the outside brick stair above **Habinnas Barba**'s half-idle *pistrinum*.

Down in the cavernous stone bakery courtyard below, only one of Barba's four hourglass-shaped dark grey volcanic lava mills (*molae asinariae*) was turning—dragged round and round in creaking circles by a single rib-sprung blindfolded mule while old one-eyed Cappadocian **Barba**, white from shaggy eyebrows to split knuckles with coarse bran-flour, coughed dry mill-dust into an empty sack and cursed the state *annona* inspectors in broken Greek.

Up in their dry, warm brick-vaulted loft directly over the cooling bread-arch, partitioned neatly in two with clean unbleached sailcloth so thirteen-year-old **Sura** had her own corner mat and chalk-slate—**Lucida** knelt barefoot in a sleeveless summer linen shift beside four fat Spanish oil-skins, balancing a miniature bronze steelyard (*statera*) on one steady forefinger while tallying numbers on a scraped wax board.

Heat rising up through the terracotta floor-tiles from Barba's afternoon baking had curled dark damp tendrils loose from her braids against the nape of her neck and brought a sheen of salt-glow across her bare olive shoulders. At fifteen and eight months she had lost the last pinched hollow of Subortanae starvation; her collarbones were long and clean above firm, high breasts bound snug under linen, her wrists supple and strong from lifting fifty-pound oil-measures every dawn at the river.

She glanced up as Corvus's shadow crossed the open shutter, caught the bright triumph still humming behind his grey-green eyes before he said a word, and set down her bronze steelyard.

"Priscus won his Salarian pear-trees," Lucida guessed at once, breaking off the warm heel of his *siligo* loaf and tossing half over the sailcloth screen to Sura, who caught it one-handed with a soft chirp.

"Forty-one tablets to four," Corvus said, sitting cross-legged on the warm tiles opposite her and dropping twelve silver denarii, his new forty-eight-sesterce weekly scale—ringing onto her counting-board. "Caught Hermippus's bribed scribe with wet copperas under his razor in a Vicus Tuscus barber-stall. Starting next month Priscus is putting me with a voice-master after hours so I can plead minor *cognitor* claims at seventeen."

Lucida whistled low between her teeth, eyes shining genuine fierce pride for him, then dropped immediately to the fat papyrus roll tucked under his belt. "What's the scroll? More aqueduct fines?"

"Better," Corvus said, unrolling the first fresh column of **Gaius's *Commentarii***—the brilliant provincial teacher whose crystal-clear four-book lectures on Roman civil status every Forum clerk was copying this spring, toward the open west window where red sunset slanted across the tiles. "Look at Book One, Column Nine, Vespera. Phileros let me bring the status-sheet home overnight because I told him I needed the *tutela* rules. Read from *Item Latino tribus modis*—on the ways a **Junian Latin** wins full Roman citizenship (*ius Quiritium*) *without* asking the old patron to walk before the Praetor's rod!"

Lucida went still as carved amber. Every muscle in her bare shoulders locked; she slid across the warm terracotta tiles until her bare knee pressed solid against Corvus's thigh, leaning over the papyrus so close her dark hair brushed his cheek, smelling of Spanish virgin olive oil, warm wheat-crust, and clean sweat.

Her stain-shadowed forefinger tracked slowly down the neat Latin capitals while she read aloud under her breath:

*"First, by imperial *beneficium* directly from Caesar... Second, by bearing three children after marrying before seven citizens under the *Lex Aelia Sentia*—too slow and puts a husband over your purse... Third, six years in the night-watch *vigiles*—men only... Fourth, building a sea-going ship of ten thousand *modii* to carry state wheat for six years... Fifth, spending half an estate over a hundred thousand sesterces building a new house in Rome..."*

Her finger stopped dead at Line Twenty-Six.

*"Item ex edicto divi Traiani: si Latinus in urbe triennio pistrinum exercuerit, in quo in singulos dies non minus quam centenos modios frumenti pinserit, ad ius Quiritium pervenit."*

Slowly Lucida translated the dense legal Latin out loud into plain street speech, testing every bolt in the iron lock:

*"Likewise by the Edict of the Deified Trajan: if a Junian Latin operates a mill-bakery in the City for three continuous years, in which he—or she, *Latinus* covers women in commercial edicts, doesn't it, Corvus?*"

"Ulpian and Gaius both say masculine commercial terms include *Latinae* wherever bodily military service isn't required, yes!" Corvus confirmed, heart quickening at the sudden blaze in her eyes.

*"—grinds and bakes not less than **one hundred modii of grain every single day** for three continuous years, that Latin arrives at full Quiritary Roman citizenship!"*

Lucida lifted her head from the scroll and stared straight down through the cracks of the floorboards—straight down into old **Habinnas Barba**'s smoky stone courtyard below, where three out of four massive volcanic lava mill-stones sat silent under cobwebs for want of capital and grain-credit, while one blindfolded mule crept round in the dusk!

One hundred *modii* a day, roughly sixteen hundred Roman pounds of wheat ground and baked every twenty-four hours—for **one thousand and ninety-five unbroken days**. Complete that statutory run registered on the Urban Prefect's mill-roll, and on the morning of Day One Thousand Ninety-Six **Publius Vedius Pollio's patronal reversion shattered to dust**, no eighty-sesterce monthly tribute (which Corvus still knew nothing about), no slave-shroud waiting at death, full citizen *ius Quiritium* won by flour and fire!

"Four lava mills downstairs," Lucida whispered, eyes narrowed to bright golden slits as her merchant brain began multiplying mule-shifts, firewood loads, and sack-tallies faster than an abacus. "One *mola asinaria* working double donkey-shifts grinds twenty-eight to thirty *modii* in twenty-four hours. Run all four of Barba's stones day and night, and this courtyard clears **a hundred ten *modii* a day** with ten *modii* margin for cracked axles!"

Corvus stared at her in half-awed alarm. "Vespera, buying grain, eight strong mill-asses, firewood, and kneaders to run a hundred *modii* a day takes eight or ten thousand sesterces working capital before the state *annona* grants subsidized wheat! All we have saved in the new box since March is four hundred twenty!"

"Four hundred twenty in May," Lucida said softly, laying her warm palm flat over Gaius's Latin lines as though claiming title by spear-point herself. Down below, Barba's iron oven-door clanged shut in the red gloom, sending a wave of dry oak-heat up around their bare ankles. "Give me until summer ends to turn Spanish oil into fuller's alum and Caelian salve, Sabine—and don't return this scroll to Phileros until I've copied every single word of Trajan's Edict onto my own wax."
