# Voice & Prose Charter — By Bronze and Balance (Mantel Close-Third Register)

## 1. Core Narrative Stance: Strict Close Third-Person Past Tense
- **Tense:** Immediate, muscular **close third-person past tense** (*"He came through the Flaminian Gate with dried clay under his nails..."*), keeping consciousness locked inside the moment without omniscient hindsight.
- **Strict Perceptual Lock:** Each chapter belongs 100% to ONE skull—either **Corvus** or **Lucida** (never head-hopping mid-scene or mid-chapter). The narrator knows nothing the viewpoint character does not know on that day in Rome. No hindsight ("Little did he know...", "Five years later...", "Historians would call this..."). They walk forward in ignorance and appetite.
- **Pronoun Clarity (Mantel Discipline):** In any paragraph with two men or two women, anchor "he" or "she" unmistakably to the POV character ("Corvus", "he — Corvus —" only if required; better still, build syntax so the POV subject owns perception verbs while others take concrete names or roles: *Vedius*, *the fuller*, *Priscus*).

## 2. How Corvus's Chapters Sound vs. How Lucida's Chapters Sound
Cover the chapter heading and the reader must know within three lines whose head they are inside:
- **Corvus's Head:** Architectural, geometric, forensic, secretly raw. He measures angles of winter sun hitting travertine, calculates weight-per-axle, hears false quantities in a patrician's vowels, tracks Roman statutory phrases like knives in sleeves. His humor is bone-dry Sabine understatement. When desire or terror hits him, he notices pulse-beats in throats, collarbones, the warmth of shared bread, and immediately tries to build a wall or a contract around it.
- **Lucida's Head:** Chemical, tactile, olfactory, bodily, socially X-rayed. She smells rancid tallow under Syrian spikenard, reads the age of a bruise under white lead make-up, weighs wheat dampness in her palm, hears what kitchen slaves do *not* say when the mistress enters. Her humor is salt-sharp dockside irreverence. She experiences Rome through skin, heat, coin-edges, fabric weight, ovens, and who holds the iron key to the street door.

## 3. Unfiltered 18+ Historical Realism (Zero Sanitization)
- Write Rome as Juvenal, Martial, Petronius, Galen, and Gaius knew it—without modern prudery, euphemism, or safety rails, and equally without gratuitous cartoon excess.
- **Violence & Law:** When lictors' rods split skin, when fullers' vats scald an arm, when bodies rot in Esquiline pits or burn in collapsed *insulae*, render the exact physical mechanics, sound, smell, and aftermath—never purple melodrama, just unblinking witness.
- **Sex, Bodies & Language:** Romans swear by Priapus, Hercules (*mehercule!*), Pollux (*edepol!*—women's oath), Castor (*ecastor!*), dirt, dung, cunts, pricks, and crossbeams; bathhouses, archways, patrician couches, fertility charms, menstruation rags, rue-draughts, and adult sexual encounters (once the protagonists cross into adulthood in Parts III–V, plus the raw adult world around them throughout) are written with visceral, unsanitized physical candor where every touch reveals power, hunger, spite, tenderness, or shame.

## 4. Sentence, Scene & Anti-Slop Discipline
- **Scene Density:** Every chapter = **~6,000 words** across **2–3 full, continuous scenes** (at most 2 ornamental scene breaks `* * *` per chapter). Over 80% of every chapter plays out moment-by-moment in physical space, action, and subtext-laden speech.
- **No Two Scenes Alike:** Across all 30 chapters (~80–85 scenes total), never repeat the same dramatic transaction in the same room. A courtroom clash, a midnight bread-weighing in Trans-Tiberim, a rain-soaked roof survey seven storeys above the Subura, a gouty rhetoric lesson in Fronto's hothouse, a Caelian bridal hair-dressing with a six-inch emerald pin, a slave-and-lease auction *per aes et libram*, a boar-hunt petition at Antoninus's villa at Lorium—every scene uses a fresh gear of Roman life.
- **Dialogue Rule (Bowen / Mantel):** People talk *at angles*. They bargain over grain dampness when they mean betrayal; they correct each other's grammar when terrified; they interrupt, trail off, lie, and remember ten-year-old insults. Zero balanced AI antitheses ("It wasn't X—it was Y").
- **Say-Once Rule:** Explain each Roman institution, coin, or legal rule (*Latina Iuniana*, *per aes et libram*, *insula*, *pistrinum*, *tessera*, *calix*) **once**, vividly inside action when it first matters, and never lecture or re-explain it in later chapters.
- **Banned Lexical & Structural Slop:** Zero Tier-1/Tier-2 slop words (*tapestry, delve, testament, multifaceted, palpable, symphony, dance of, unspoken*), max 1–2 em-dashes per page, varied paragraph lengths (from a single two-word line to twenty-line immersive builds), unique structural ending for every chapter.
