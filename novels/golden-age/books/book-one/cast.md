# Book One Cast Dossiers & C1–C12 Verification — By Bronze and Balance

Every principal below passes `shared/craft/CHARACTER.md` tests C1–C12.

---

## 1. Lucius Cassius Corvus (POV — 15 Chapters)
- **C1 Own Want:** To win the `400,000 HS` equestrian census and an untouchable magistracy-track post so no creditor, bailiff, or senator can ever lay a rod across his back or claim Lucida's bones.
- **C2 Contradiction Acted:** Privately tender—keeps his dead father's cracked brass plumb-bob, slips hot bread to street-boys, remembers every dead brick-worker's name—yet in court and contract rooms becomes surgical and pitiless, ruining lesser men with their own boundary-stones to climb one step higher (acted in Chs. 7, 15, 23, 29).
- **C3 Stress Maneuver (not tic):** When cornered by rank or terror, his blink-rate stops, his thumb rubs the crescent brick-stamp scar on his right knuckle, and he raises the stakes by offering to prove a harder legal point than anyone asked for—a compulsion that wins him Priscus's patronage in Ch. 5, traps him in Crispina's debt-web in Ch. 19, and triggers the catastrophic overreach of Ch. 29–30.
- **C4 Choice Ladder:**
  - *Part I (Age 15):* Hides the dead bailiff's stolen tally-tablet from Lucida to trade it to Priscus's clerk for a place at the rhetoric benches (first tiny secret between them).
  - *Part II (Age 16–17):* Wins his first boundary-fraud brief in Basilica Julia by exposing a small Subura fuller family alongside Scaeva's front-man—watching them evicted into winter mud so he earns `5,000 HS`.
  - *Part III (Age 18):* Accepts Senator Priscus's retainer and Crispina's bed/loan even when Priscus orders an inquiry into illegal grain-weights and water-taps in the Subura—telling himself he can steer the blade around Lucida's mill.
  - *Part IV (Age 19):* After the flooded-cellar night where he and Lucida nearly tear each other's clothes off and discover each is holding leverage on the other's patron, refuses her demand to burn Priscus's audit roll.
  - *Part V (Age 20, March 161):* On the night Antoninus Pius dies, strikes the *per aes et libram* receivership blow against Vedius in the Basilica Julia/Aerarium—saving Lucida from Vedius's treason frame while seizing custodial control of her patronal reversion and destroying Habinnas Barba's mill license, convinced she will have to accept safety from his hand.
- **C5 Hinterland:** Haunted by river-levels, geometry of arches, his father's drunken Virgil recitations in muddy fields, sudden aching hunger for figs warmed on hot kiln-tiles, humiliating physical awareness of Lucida's collarbone and throat whenever another man looks at her.
- **C6 Moral Need:** Confuses *protecting* Lucida with *owning the roof over her*; his terror of helplessness makes him become the very Roman instrument of law that crushes her independence.
- **C10 Forster Surprise:** In Ch. 25, when rival patrician Volusenus faces ruin and suicide over forged dice-bonds, cold-eyed Corvus secretly buys and burns the worst bond—not from mercy, but because he realizes a grateful live patrician owes deeper blood than a dead one, shocking even himself when genuine pity breaks through halfway.

## 2. Lucida (POV — 15 Chapters)
- **C1 Own Want:** Full Roman citizenship (*ius Quiritium*) *sui iuris*—won by her own ovens, vats, and silver, owing no patron, husband, or childhood boy a single thread of her skin.
- **C2 Contradiction Acted:** Fiercely unsentimental in bargains—waters wine for Praetorian drunks, blackmails hypocrite Caelian matrons over their abortion vials, squeezes sub-lessees to the *quadrans*—yet risks her entire fortune and neck to shield mute Sura, crippled baker Barba, and even Corvus when Scaeva's knives close in (acted in Chs. 4, 12, 20, 28, 30).
- **C3 Stress Maneuver:** Under threat, she grows dangerously hospitable—pouring Wine, straightening another person's collar, smiling with her teeth while counting every exit, lamp-flame, and heavy bronze object within arm's reach, and cutting pre-emptively before she can be pitied.
- **C4 Choice Ladder:**
  - *Part I (Age 15):* Mixes prohibited alum-and-lead cosmetic paste and carries secret letters between Caelian bedchambers for Beroe to pay off the medicine that saves Corvus from winter pleurisy—without ever telling him how she earned the silver.
  - *Part II (Age 16–17):* Enters the lion's mouth by signing a front-lease on a ruined Subura bakehouse (*pistrinum*) using dead men's funerary-club (*collegium*) silver, starting the three-year `100-modii-a-day` statutory clock for citizenship right under Vedius's nose.
  - *Part III (Age 18):* Becomes private perfumer and secret credit-courier to **Ceionia Fabia**, smuggling gold and intelligence for Lucius Commodus's circle while taking a calculated cold lover (an Ostian grain-trier) to secure wheat allocations—watching Corvus across Caelian courtyards with Crispina's glass bracelets on his sleeve.
  - *Part IV (Age 19):* Uses arson-damaged Subura property law to buy out three burned ground-leases over Corvus's own clients' heads after their near-consummation turns into a ferocious quarrel.
  - *Part V (Age 20, March 161):* Discovering Corvus's receivership stroke has shattered Barba's mill just forty days short of her three-year statutory citizenship mark—and believing Corvus betrayed her secret ledger to climb into Marcus's court—she rides before dawn to the Castra Praetoria with Ceionia Fabia, hands Lucius Verus the stolen senatorial conspiracy letters that ruin Priscus, wins an imperial rescript of full citizenship (*Lucida Libera*) and Ostian warehouse leases, and leaves Corvus alive but cut out of her heart by oath.
- **C5 Hinterland:** Memories of her Tyrian mother singing Phoenician counting-rhymes over boiling dye-pots; visceral pleasure in hot clean bathwater, honeyed dormice, and heavy Sidonian glass; grief over women's bodies torn in childbirth in airless sixth-floor garrets; unacknowledged terror that if she lets Corvus touch her as a man, she will vanish inside him the way her mother vanished inside a senator's back stair.
- **C6 Moral Need:** Treats every human tie except Sura as a potential slave-collar; strikes first whenever she fears someone can make her need them.
- **C10 Forster Surprise:** In Ch. 22, sitting alone beside sleeping, drugged Vedius Pollio with Ceionia Fabia's six-inch emerald pin in her fingers and a clean chance to stop his heart without a mark, she puts the pin back—realizing that if Vedius dies intestate tonight, his heir at law is a minor ward under Senator Priscus's guardianship, which would make *Corvus's* patron her master.

## 3. Historical Figures — Record vs. Gap (C12 Audit)
- **Antoninus Pius (Record):** Aged 70–74; mild, thrifty, meticulous over provincial accounts ("a penny-pincher / cumin-splitter," *cyminopristes* in street jokes); stays at Rome, Lanuvium, and Lorium; concubine **Galeria Lysistrate** (freedwoman of dead Empress Faustina Major) quietly sells equestrian posts behind the scenes; dies at Lorium 7 March 161 after eating Alpine cheese, feverish ravings about angry foreign kings, last watchword *Aequanimitas*, ordering the golden statue of Fortuna moved to Marcus's bedroom. **(Gap):** Seen once up close in Ch. 24 at a Lorium vintage petition—not a marble saint, an old tired widower with stained fingernails who smells of liniment and knows perfectly well his two adopted sons will split the Empire's seams when he shuts his eyes.
- **Marcus Aurelius Caesar (Record):** 35–40; *tribunicia potestas*; transition from Fronto's beloved rhetoric pupil to Junius Rusticus's austere Stoic convert; sickly chest, takes daily *theriaca* (opium-viper antidote compounded by imperial physicians); fiercely loyal to adoptive brother Lucius despite Antoninus's coldness toward Lucius. **(Gap):** His quiet moral vanity—wanting clean hands while men like Priscus and Corvus shovel the night-soil that keeps the grain barges moving.
- **Lucius Ceionius Commodus / Verus (Record):** 25–30; kept out of high command by Antoninus; passionate patron of the chariot horse *Volucer* and Green faction; generous, sensual, physically vigorous, adored by household freedmen and his sister **Ceionia Fabia**. **(Gap):** Far shrewder in human appetite than Marcus's Stoics realize; recognizes hungry Subura wolves like Lucida on sight because he too has spent seventeen years waiting in another man's anteroom.
- **Marcus Cornelius Fronto (Record):** ~61–66; rheumatic agony, obsessed with exact Latin *elocutio novella* ("unexpected right words"), sweet tooth, tender grandfatherly heart, despises Stoic hypocrisy. **(Gap):** Spots fifteen-year-old Corvus's terrifying ear for Latin cadence in Ch. 9 and gives him the one piece of advice Corvus never forgets: *"In Rome, boy, Caesar rules men, Dawn rules Caesar, and the man who names the thing owns the thing."*
