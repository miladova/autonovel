# Book One Design — By Bronze and Balance (156–161 CE)

Verified against `shared/craft/DESIGN.md` tests D1–D14.

## 1. One-Page Spine (D1)
- **The Central Question (D3):** Can two penniless fifteen-year-olds bound by blood and childhood survival climb out of Rome's *humiliores* gutter to legal freedom and the `400,000 HS` equestrian shield **without becoming the Roman masters who own and devour each other**? (Posed explicitly on the page in Ch. 2 by Beroe over their cracked clay brick-stamp: *"Rome has two doors up from the mud, children—the knife and the scale. Neither fits two abreast."*)
- **The Climax First (D2 — 6–8 March 161 CE, Chapters 28–30):** Seventy-four-year-old Antoninus Pius dies of fever at Lorium on 7 March 161. In the thirty-six-hour succession suspense before Marcus Aurelius forces the Senate and Praetorian Guard to accept **Lucius Verus** as co-Emperor:
  1. **Vedius Pollio** files a capital *delatio* naming Lucida and Beroe in a Caelian poisoning/magic case to bury Ceionia Fabia's debts and seize Lucida's thriving three-year bakehouse-and-tenement syndicate just forty days before her statutory *ius Quiritium* clock completes.
  2. **Corvus**—using the defective-witness flaw he discovered in Domitia Lucilla's sub-lease conveyances (Plant 1), the illegal lead water-pipe *calix* survey of Vedius's baths (Plant 2), and the secret funerary-club roll Lucida once trusted him to hide (Plant 3)—executes an emergency fiscal receivership *per aes et libram* at the Basilica Julia and Aerarium Saturni. He ruins Vedius, saves Lucida's neck from the Esquiline crossbeam, vaults himself over the `400,000 HS` equestrian threshold into the new imperial secretariat—and **shatters Habinnas Barba's mill license while placing Lucida's patronal reversion under his own receivership lock**, convinced he has finally made her safe behind his wall.
  3. **Lucida**—refusing to live one breath as Corvus's legal creature while Barba coughs blood in chains—rides through the rain before dawn with **Ceionia Fabia** to the **Castra Praetoria**, trades Senator Priscus's treasonous succession letters (stolen from Corvus's own travel-chest during his Ch. 27 visit) directly to newly hailed co-Emperor **Lucius Verus**, wins an **imperial rescript of full Roman citizenship (*ius Quiritium*) and Vedius's Ostian grain-warehouse leases**, topples Corvus's patron Priscus into disgrace, and leaves Corvus standing in clean equestrian purple across a chasm of dead loyalty.
- **The Cost:** Both win everything they entered Rome at fifteen to get—wealth, full untouchable status, seats inside the two rival imperial courts—and lose the only human being in the Empire who knew their real name.

## 2. The Opponent's Dated Plan (D5)
- **Primary Antagonist:** **Publius Vedius Pollio** (with street-blade **Scaeva**) vs. **Shadow Patron-Antagonist Senator Quintus Servilius Priscus**.
  - *Oct 156:* Clear squatter clay-workers off *Figlinae Subortanae* leasehold after Domitia Lucilla's death; recover stolen estate tally-tablets; reclaim escaped Junian Latin chattel (Lucida).
  - *Spring 157 – Autumn 158:* Corner Subura tenement ground-leases via summer arson and controlled *collegia* loans; bleed young Lucius Commodus with luxury credit so Vedius will control imperial supply contracts when Antoninus dies.
  - *159 – Summer 160:* Crush Priscus's Stoic water-and-grain inquiry; trap Lucida's *pistrinum* grain supply at Ostia before her three-year `100-modii` statutory citizenship term matures in April 161.
  - *March 161:* Purge liabilities on the night Antoninus dies so Lucius's elevation makes Vedius untouchable Prefect of the Grain Supply (*Praefectus Annonae*).
  - *Opponent's On-Page Wins:* Ch. 6 (seizes Beroe's shop lease and forces Lucida into direct tribute), Ch. 14 (humiliates Priscus's first witness in Basilica Julia), Ch. 21 (burns the Vicus Patricius *insula* block and frames Barba's ovens).

## 3. Plants, Tests & Payoffs Ledger (D6, D12)
1. **The Broken Brick-Stamp (*Signaculum* of Figlinae Subortanae):**
   - *Plant (Ch. 1):* Split terracotta master-impression and stolen bronze die showing pre-dated consular stamps on imperial brick loads.
   - *Test (Ch. 11):* Used partially by Corvus in court, revealing one witness on Vedius's *mancipatio* deed was an *infamis* arena contractor—invalidating a minor boundary claim.
   - *Payoff (Ch. 29):* Unlocks the defective *mancipatio per aes et libram* chain behind Vedius's entire Caelian-Subura trust.
2. **Illegal Bronze Water-Nozzles (*Calices* on the Aqua Claudia/Caelian Spur):**
   - *Plant (Ch. 5):* Fifteen-year-old Corvus crawls a Caelian distribution castellum and measures stamped fifty-digit pipes tapped behind twenty-digit licenses.
   - *Test (Ch. 17):* Corvus uses water-theft leverage to blackmail an aedile's clerk, accidentally drying out a Trans-Tiberim fuller trough Lucida relies on.
   - *Payoff (Ch. 29):* Provides instant *crimen publicum* confiscation jurisdiction during the interregnum when civil courts pause.
3. **Trajan's 100-Modii Mill Statute & the Funerary Collegium Chest:**
   - *Plant (Ch. 8):* Phileros lets slip Gaius's lecture on Trajan's *pistrinum* citizenship escape; Lucida inspects Habinnas Barba's half-dead Subura mill.
   - *Test (Ch. 16 & Ch. 21):* Grain-weevil sabotage at Ostia and August fire nearly break Lucida's daily `100-modii` tally; she saves the count by nocturnal hand-quern shifts.
   - *Payoff (Ch. 29–30):* Corvus's receivership halts the mill forty days before completion, triggering Lucida's irreversible strike at the Castra Praetoria.
4. **Priscus's Private Succession Letters to the Syrian Legate:**
   - *Plant (Ch. 19):* Corvus copies confidential Greek notes between Priscus and anti-Ceionian senators debating sidelining Lucius Commodus when Pius dies.
   - *Test (Ch. 23):* Lucida spots wax-shavings in Corvus's fingernail seams during their rain-cellar collision and deduces what Priscus is preparing.
   - *Payoff (Ch. 30):* Lucida lifts the duplicate wax leaf and lays it in Lucius Verus's hand before the Praetorian eagles.

## 4. Rehearsals (D7)
- **Rehearsal 1 (Ch. 11 — Centumviral Boundary Suit, Spring 158):** Corvus and Lucida secretly coordinate across court and alley—she bribes a fuller's slave for dye-stained boundary rags, he springs the trap in Basilica Julia. **Succeeds**, winning their first real silver, teaching both how lethal they are together—and planting the fatal habit of treating law as a shared blade.
- **Rehearsal 2 (Ch. 21–22 — The August Subura Fire Inquiry, Summer 160):** Each tries independently to protect the other from Vedius's arson trap without consulting first; their uncoordinated moves collide, getting a mute apprentice maimed and driving them into the catastrophic confrontation of Ch. 23. **Fails**, proving secret paternalism and secret counter-plotting destroy whatever stands between them.

## 5. Five-Part Escalation & Clocks (D9, D11)
- **Part I — Blood of the Right-Hand Horse (Oct 156 – Mar 157, Age 15, Chs. 1–6):** Survival clock—winter starvation, Corvus's dead father's `3,200 HS` bond, Scaeva's hunt through the Subura.
- **Part II — Chalk and Flour (Apr 157 – Nov 158, Ages 15–17, Chs. 7–12):** Entry clock—Corvus winning a voice in Fronto's/Priscus's orbit and his first Centumviral brief; Lucida launching the three-year `100-modii` bakehouse clock (`1,095 consecutive grinding days`).
- **Part III — The Narrow Stripe and the Stola (Feb 159 – Dec 159, Ages 17–18, Chs. 13–18):** Divergence clock—Ostian grain fleet arrival; adult patrons, separate beds (Crispina / Ostian grain-trier), Caelian vs. Ceionian court recruitment.
- **Part IV — Dog-Star Over Subura (Mar 160 – Nov 160, Ages 18–19, Chs. 19–24):** Rupture clock—Urban Prefect Lollius Urbicus's death, summer tenement fires, the flooded-cellar erotic collision & breach, Antoninus Pius's visibly failing strength at Lorium.
- **Part V — Aequanimitas (Dec 160 – Mar 161, Ages 19–20, Chs. 25–30):** Succession countdown—ninety days to Lucida's statutory freedom vs. Antoninus Pius's deathbed at Lorium on 7 March 161.

| *Chirographum* | Ch. 3 | Hand-signed debt bond sealed on wax tablets |
| *Calix* / *fistula* | Ch. 5 | Stamped bronze water-nozzle and lead pipe regulating how much aqueduct water a rich house may draw |
| *Centumviri* / *hasta* | Ch. 7 | Inheritance and property court in Basilica Julia where a spear stands planted in the marble floor |
| *Pistrinum* / *modius* | Ch. 8 | Commercial mill-bakery; a Roman peck of grain (~6.5 kg), 100 a day for 3 years buying a Junian Latin's citizenship |
| *Collegium tenuiorum* | Ch. 10 | Poor folk's monthly burial-and-supper club, the one association Rome lets small people form legally |
| *Humiliores* / *Honestiores* | Ch. 11 | The legal line dividing those who may be whipped and sent to the mines from knights and senators who may not |
| *Per aes et libram* | Ch. 11 (rehearsed), Ch. 29 (paid) | "By bronze and balance-scales": solemn sale or will struck with bronze against scales before five citizen witnesses |
| *Delatio* / *delator* | Ch. 26 | Paid capital accusation laid before the Prefect or Senate for a share of the condemned person's estate |
