# Book One Master 30-Chapter Outline — By Bronze and Balance (156–161 CE)

Target per chapter: **~6,000 words** | **2–3 fully dramatized scenes** (max 2 `* * *` breaks) | **Dual Close-Third Past Tense** (15 Corvus, 15 Lucida).
Strict diversity law: **No two scenes in the 30 chapters repeat the same setting + transaction.**

## PART I — THE BLOOD OF THE RIGHT-HAND HORSE (October 156 – March 157 CE | Age 15)

### Chapter 1 — *Ex Praediis* (POV: Corvus | 15 Oct 156 CE | ~6,000w)
- **Scene 1 (Dawn — Figlinae Subortanae clay-pits north of Milvian Bridge):** Scaeva and estate bailiffs burn the squatter shacks on the late Domitia Lucilla's sub-leased clayworks to wipe out disputed tallies and seize Lucida as unperfected slave-reversion property. Fifteen-year-old Corvus uses a red-hot kiln rake and surveyor's chain in river fog to break Scaeva's mule-driver, snatches the bronze *signaculum* die and wet wax tallies, and flees downstream with Lucida.
- **Scene 2 (Midday — Porta Flaminia & Campus Martius):** Entering Rome raw, barefoot, blood-caked, straight into the **Ides of October chariot race and sacrifice of the October Horse**. Orientation set-piece: Rome at noon of the Empire seen through a starving surveyor's son's eyes.
- **Scene 3 (Dusk — Via Sacra / Subura Firewall Arch):** Caught inside the ferocious annual street-brawl between the **Subura** and **Sacra Via** wards fighting for the bleeding horse's severed head; Lucida saves their stolen pouch with a shard of Spanish amphora, and they cross beneath the hundred-foot *lapis Gabinus* Firewall into the Subura as Suburamen roar around them.

### Chapter 2 — *Oil of Bitter Almonds* (POV: Lucida | 16–18 Oct 156 CE | ~6,000w)
- **Scene 1 (Morning — Beroe's sixth-floor garret above the Argiletum fulleries):** Lucida bargains with one-eyed Syrian cosmetic-chemist/midwife Beroe for six feet of floor-straw; plain revelation of Lucida's *Latina Iuniana* curse and Vedius Pollio's inherited patronal claim; they split the cracked terracotta tile-stamp in half.
- **Scene 2 (Afternoon — A Subura *fullonica* [urine-vats]):** Lucida and Corvus take day-labor treading cloth in stale ammoniac urine and fuller's earth; twelve-year-old mute girl **Sura** gets scalded at the sulfur-bleaching cages; Lucida talks down the overseer with razor arithmetic and steals lanolin grease for Sura's burn.
- **Scene 3 (Night — Rooftop tiles under autumn rain):** Counting their last eleven铜 *asses* while heavy supply wagons thunder below after sunset; Beroe warns them about Rome's two narrow doors upward.

### Chapter 3 — *The Dead Man's Wax* (POV: Corvus | Nov 156 CE | ~6,000w)
- **Scene 1 (Morning — Steps of the Temple of Saturn & Forum Argentarium):** Corvus verifies his own citizen birth-entry on the public whitewashed noticeboards while ducking money-changer clerks hunting his dead father's `3,200 HS` *chirographum* debt-bond; first glimpse of austere **Senator Quintus Servilius Priscus** and archive-freedman **Phileros**.
- **Scene 2 (Midday — A portico grammar-and-rhetoric school behind the Forum Transitorium):** Hired to scrub charcoal braziers and scrape spoilt wax tablets for rich boys, Corvus corrects patrician **Volusenus Niger**'s miscalculated land-inheritance declamation aloud—earning a split lip from Volusenus's stylus and a cold second look from Phileros.
- **Scene 3 (Night — A Subura *popina* dice-room):** Scaeva's runners sniff around the Argiletum; Corvus secretly trades one non-essential clay-yard boundary tablet to Phileros for `40 HS` and silence on his father's debt—keeping his first tiny secret from Lucida.

### Chapter 4 — *White Lead and Rue* (POV: Lucida | Dec 156 CE — Saturnalia | ~6,000w)
- **Scene 1 (Saturnalia afternoon — Beroe's compounding table):** Grinding cerussa (white lead), alkanet root, and pennyroyal abortifacients while Rome goes drunk in felt *pileus* caps outside; Corvus is burning up on the straw pallet with winter pleurisy.
- **Scene 2 (Evening — Back dressing-chambers of a Caelian *domus*):** Lucida makes her first delivery inside a patrician house during a raucous Saturnalia banquet—witnessing Senatorial hypocrisy raw (a drunken guest vomiting thrushes into a silver basin while a slave-boy is whipped silent in the pantry) and first laying eyes on **Ceionia Fabia** and her six-inch emerald hairpin as Fabia buys silence for a pregnant slave-girl.
- **Scene 3 (Fourth watch — The garret):** Buying Greek physician's cupping-horn and honeyed squill-vinegar for delirious Corvus; pressing her body against his shivering ribs all night until the fever breaks, swearing nobody will ever buy either of them.

### Chapter 5 — *Twenty Digits of Lead* (POV: Corvus | Jan 157 CE | ~6,000w)
- **Scene 1 (Dawn — Underground settling-tank [*castellum aquae*] on the Caelian spur):** Convalescent Corvus is sent by Phileros into freezing waist-deep aqueduct water with a brass gauge to prove a neighbor is stealing water; discovers stamped fifty-digit lead *fistulae* hidden behind licensed twenty-digit bronze *calices* feeding **Publius Vedius Pollio**'s private baths.
- **Scene 2 (Noon — Peristyle of Senator Priscus's Caelian house):** Dripping sewer-lime on mosaic stone, Corvus refuses to simply hand over numbers—he maps the entire fraudulent siphon geometry in charcoal on a broken roof-tile before **Priscus** and Stoic **Junius Rusticus** (C3 stress maneuver); wins a permanent clerk's stool and a clean tunic.

### Chapter 6 — *The Pear-Knife* (POV: Lucida | Mar 157 CE | ~6,000w)
- **Scene 1 (Morning — Emporium docks & Monte Testaccio):** Lucida corners bruised Spanish olive-oil dregs and cracked amphora tallies among river-haulers, discovering her eye for wholesale arbitrage.
- **Scene 2 (Afternoon — Vedius Pollio's sunlit *horti* pavilion):** **Opponent's Win:** Scaeva tracks Beroe's shop sub-lease back to Vedius's own holding company; Lucida is summoned face-to-face before her legal *dominus-patronus* **Vedius Pollio**, who peels a winter pear with a tiny silver knife, inspects her hands like livestock, spares her return to the clay-pits only because a clever Junian Latin girl earning gold in Rome will enrich *his* strongbox when she dies—and levies a monthly tribute while rescuing mute **Sura** costs Lucida every coin she saved.

## PART II — CHALK AND FLOUR (April 157 – November 158 CE | Ages 15–17)

### Chapter 7 — *Four Spears in the Marble* (POV: Corvus | May 157 CE | ~6,000w)
- **Scene 1 (Morning — Basilica Julia set-piece):** Four Centumviral courts roaring at once behind canvas curtains while hired *laudiceni* clap for three denarii on cue and dice click on the steps outside; sixteen-year-old Corvus carries tablet-bundles behind Volusenus and watches a sleek defense advocate shred Priscus's junior pleader.
- **Scene 2 (Late afternoon — Public latrines & barber's stall off the Vicus Tuscus):** Corvus corners a bribed court *scriba* mid-shave, proves the clerk altered a boundary-stone minute, and learns how Roman lawsuits are really won twelve hours before anyone puts on a chalked toga.

### Chapter 8 — *A Hundred Modii* (POV: Lucida | Aug 157 CE | ~6,000w)
- **Scene 1 (Pre-dawn — Habinnas Barba's smoky Subura *pistrinum* [mill-bakery]):** Blindfolded asses turning volcanic-stone *meta* and *catillus* querns in stifling heat; Lucida learns of Trajan's `100-modii-a-day` three-year statute from Gaius's published *edict* notes via Corvus, and pitches bitter, half-blind Cappadocian freedman **Habinnas Barba** a partnership to revive his dying ovens.
- **Scene 2 (Evening — Trans-Tiberim Aqua Traiana water-mills):** Walking the thundering Janiculum mill-race with Corvus; bathing bruised feet in cold tailrace foam; first unmistakable tremor of teenage bodily awareness as sixteen-year-old Corvus washes flour from the hollow behind Lucida's ear and pulls his hand back too fast.

### Chapter 9 — *Gout and Plautus* (POV: Corvus | Nov 157 CE | ~6,000w)
- **Scene 1 (Afternoon — Gardens of Maecenas / Esquiline hothouse of Marcus Cornelius Fronto):** Priscus sends Corvus with scrolls to gout-crippled master orator **Fronto**; **Marcus Aurelius Caesar** (thirty-six, pale, ink-stained) and radiant twenty-six-year-old **Lucius Ceionius Commodus** arrive in the same hour—one smelling of medicine and Stoic notebooks, the other of stable-leather and Syrian nard. Fronto tests Corvus aloud on a passage of Cato and sharpens his Sabine burr into steel.
- **Scene 2 (Night — Outside the Porta Esquilina execution ground):** Walking home past torches where night-watchmen nail a condemned slave-forger (*humilior*) to the crossbeam; Corvus stares at what happens to men below the `400,000 HS` line and swears aloud he will wear the equestrian gold ring before he turns twenty-one.

### Chapter 10 — *The Supper of the Dead* (POV: Lucida | Feb 158 CE | ~6,000w)
- **Scene 1 (Night — Vaulted underground hall of the *Collegium Aesculapii et Hygiae* burial club on the Via Appia):** To raise `8,000 HS` capital without Vedius tracing the loan, sixteen-year-old Lucida pitches forty grizzled fullers, bakers, and vinegar-sellers over funeral wine and roast pork to invest their idle burial-chest silver in cheap night-baked *panis secundus* contracts—starting Day 1 of her 1,095-day statutory citizenship clock.
- **Scene 2 (Dawn — Clivus Suburanus):** Scaeva intercepts Sura and Lucida at the bakery shutter, demanding security; Lucida bluffs him with a forged Caelian household purchase-seal and hides shaking hands inside warm dough.

### Chapter 11 — *Rehearsal in Chalk* (POV: Corvus | Jun 158 CE | ~6,000w)
- **Scene 1 (Morning — Subura dye-alley & boundary wall):** **Rehearsal 1:** Corvus and Lucida team up secretly to crack an encroaching warehouse case—she lures a rival bailiff into staining his boots in madder-sludge behind a false party-wall; theirbanter is electric, seamless, two halves of one brain.
- **Scene 2 (Midday — Basilica Julia, Second *Hasta*):** When Volusenus freezes under cross-questioning, seventeen-year-old Corvus speaks for the first time before the Centumviral judges as *cognitor*, demolishes Vedius's straw-owner using the *infamis* witness defect—and watches an innocent cobbler tenant-family dragged weeping into the street as collateral damage of his brilliant win (`5,000 HS` fee).
- **Scene 3 (Dusk — Tiber steps by the Pons Aemilius):** Lucida sees the evicted cobbler's wife on the bridge and asks Corvus how much his new wool toga cost; first hairline fracture in how she looks at him.

### Chapter 12 — *Saffron and Sweat* (POV: Lucida | Oct 158 CE | ~6,000w)
- **Scene 1 (Afternoon — Women's hot rooms [*caldarium*] of the Baths of Trajan):** Naked patrician and freedwoman politics in echoing marble steam, oil-scrapers (*strigiles*) rasping skin, wet hair, vicious gossip; seventeen-year-old Lucida rescues **Ceionia Fabia** from a blackmailing bath-attendant over a love-note from a young senator, earning entry into Fabia's inner boudoir trade.
- **Scene 2 (Night — Bakery loft above the warm ovens):**Celebrating two years in Rome with roasted chestnuts and stolen Caecuban wine; seventeen-year-old Corvus falls asleep with his cheek against Lucida's bare ankle; awake in the dark, Lucida touches the brick-stamp scar on his thumb and realizes with cold dread that his patron Priscus and her new patroness Fabia want opposite emperors on the throne.

## PART III — THE NARROW STRIPE AND THE STOLA (February 159 – December 159 CE | Ages 17–18)

### Chapter 13 — *The Hexagonal Basin* (POV: Corvus | Feb 159 CE | ~6,000w)
- **Scene 1 (Morning — Portus Augusti et Traiani, Ostia's giant hexagonal harbor):** Eighteen-year-old Corvus rides to Ostia inspecting maritime bottomry loans (*pecunia traiecticia*) for equestrian widow **Tullia Crispina**; Alexandrian grain-ships warping in, cranes creaking, grain-measurers (*mensores*) skimming wheat with rigged *modius* strikes.
- **Scene 2 (Night — Crispina's seaside villa at Laurentum):** Sea-wind against shuttered mica windows; thirty-two-year-old Crispina draws eighteen-year-old Corvus into her bed—explicit, unsentimental, adult initiation where pleasure and financial tuition are braided so tightly Corvus wakes with salt on his mouth and `25,000 HS` of shipping-audit share-contracts on the table beside the lamp.

### Chapter 14 — *The Green Stables* (POV: Lucida | Apr 159 CE — Ludi Cereales | ~6,000w)
- **Scene 1 (Morning — *Trigarium* & Barracks of the *Factio Prasina* [Greens] in Campus Martius):** Eighteen-year-old Lucida negotiates barley-bran and ointment contracts amid sweating Spanish stallions, charioteers wrapped in chest-thongs, and **Lucius Ceionius Commodus** feeding honey-cakes to his prized horse *Volucer* while Vedius Pollio whispers numbers in his ear.
- **Scene 2 (Afternoon — Circus Maximus upper wooden tiers vs. lower podium):** 150,000 Romans roaring; a three-chariot wreck at the bronze turning-posts (*metae*) sprays blood and splintered axle-wood; **Opponent's Win:** Vedius uses the race-day crowd to have Priscus's star grain-informant trampled in an engineered betting-riot.
- **Scene 3 (Evening — Colonnade of Pompey):** Lucida crosses paths with Corvus walking beside Crispina (whose green Campanian glass bracelets clink against Corvus's wrist); six sentences of polite ice between childhood best friends that draw more blood than knives.

### Chapter 15 — *Lex Aquilia* (POV: Corvus | Jul 159 CE | ~6,000w)
- **Scene 1 (Noon — Urban Prefect Lollius Urbicus's tribunal):** Corvus argues a brutal slave-maiming and warehouse-collapse damage suit under the *Lex Aquilia* before grim old **Lollius Urbicus**, learning the cold Roman arithmetic that prices a broken human hip strictly at market depreciation.
- **Scene 2 (Evening — Priscus's library on the Caelian):** **Junius Rusticus** and Priscus recruit Corvus into a confidential audit of illegal Subura *insula* heights (capped at 60 Roman feet by Trajan) and suspect *pistrina* grain allocations—handing him a scroll whose fourth column lists Barba's district.

### Chapter 16 — *Weevils in the Hold* (POV: Lucida | Sep 159 CE | ~6,000w)
- **Scene 1 (Midnight — Emporium grain-wharf *horrea*):** Vedius quietly chokes Lucida's private wheat deliveries with damp, weevil-rotted African sacks to break her `100-modii` daily statutory streak in Year Two; Lucida takes handsome, corrupt Ostian grain-trier **Decimus** against the cedar grain-bins—raw, transactional, eyes-open adult hunger where *she* sets the pace, keeps her clothes half-laced, and walks out at cockcrow with clean Sicilian hard-wheat tallies signed in his ink.
- **Scene 2 (Midday — Beroe's back room):** Beroe warns Lucida that Corvus's name is now spoken in fear among Subura tenement-landlords; Lucida refuses to ask him for mercy—instead buying a burned-out ground plot on the Clivus Suburanus through a straw freedman to build upward herself.

### Chapter 17 — *Dry Pipes* (POV: Corvus | Oct 159 CE | ~6,000w)
- **Scene 1 (Morning — Aqua Traiana distribution arch, Trans-Tiberim):** **Plant 2 Tested:** Corvus enforces an aqueduct-tap seizure against one of Vedius's front-companies to force a witness to flip—succeeding brilliantly, except the shut-off valve starves the public trough feeding three small dye-and-wash yards Lucida bankrolls.
- **Scene 2 (Dusk — Half-built brick *insula* scaffolding above the Subura):** Lucida climbs four swaying pine ladders in the wind to confront Corvus on naked scaffolding sixty feet above the street; roaring argument over who owns Rome's dirt, ending with Corvus catching her wrist over a forty-foot drop, both breathing hard, lime-dust on their mouths, pulling apart without speaking.

### Chapter 18 — *The Hairpin* (POV: Lucida | Dec 159 CE | ~6,000w)
- **Scene 1 (Evening — Ceionia Fabia's mirror-room on the Caelian Hill):** Fabia, dressing for an imperial family supper on the Palatine with Marcus Caesar and Faustina, lets Lucida pin her red-gold hair while dissecting every marriage, ulcer, and resentment in the imperial house—revealing that old Antoninus Pius's legs are swelling and the empire will soon crack between austere Marcus and golden Lucius.
- **Scene 2 (Midnight — Subura bakery courtyard under first snow):** Mute **Sura** sketches on slate that Scaeva's men have bought the tenement next to Barba's ovens; Corvus arrives uninvited at the courtyard gate carrying roasted Sabine figs in an expensive napkin—a fragile, aching truce over warm ash where for twenty minutes they are fifteen again, laughing until neither dares look down at the other's mouth.

## PART IV — DOG-STAR OVER SUBURA (March 160 – November 160 CE | Ages 18–19)

### Chapter 19 — *Letters to Syria* (POV: Corvus | Mar 160 CE | ~6,000w)
- **Scene 1 (Night — Priscus's locked *tablinum*):** Nineteen-year-old Corvus transcribes encrypted Greek letters from Priscus's faction to contacts near the Syrian legions—contingency plans to ensure Lucius Commodus receives zero military *imperium* when Antoninus dies (**Plant 4**).
- **Scene 2 (Late night — Crispina's town *domus*):** Crispina presses Corvus to invest his accumulated `160,000 HS` alongside Priscus's straw-men in foreclosed Subura commercial leases; Corvus realizes Crispina views him as a brilliant pet plebeian who will never sit at her equals' table, fucks her with cold, furious precision on the dining couch, and walks home alone through pre-dawn street-mud toward the Firewall.

### Chapter 20 — *The Vinegar-Men* (POV: Lucida | Jun 160 CE | ~6,000w)
- **Scene 1 (Afternoon — Station of the Fourth Cohort of *Vigiles* near the Viminal):** Nineteen-year-old Lucida bribes a *centurio vigilum* to inspect fire-hooks and water-ballistae on her street, learning how landlords pay *vigiles* captains to march slowly when unprofitable slums burn.
- **Scene 2 (Night — Volusenus Niger's drunken symposium on the Quirinal):** Heavily indebted patrician **Volusenus**—now secretly financed by Vedius—invites Lucida not as guest but as exotic businesswoman-courtesan to humiliate Corvus (who sits three couches up among junior advocates!). Lucida turns Volusenus's cruel toast inside out, walks out with two senators' wives' cosmetic accounts, and leaves Corvus white-knuckled on his couch having broken a Sidonian glass cup in his bare palm.

### Chapter 21 — *Red Wind in August* (POV: Corvus | Aug 160 CE | ~6,000w)
- **Scene 1 (Third watch of night — Great Subura Tenement Fire on the Vicus Patricius):** **Rehearsal 2 & Opponent's Win:** Dry Dog-Star wind; Scaeva's oil-soaked tow fires a seven-storey timber *insula* block to clear land and frame Habinnas Barba's bakery flues. Terrifying set-piece: burning roof-tiles exploding like artillery, tenants leaping onto cobbles, *vigiles* tearing down walls with iron hooks while Corvus fights upstream against fleeing crowds to reach Lucida's bakehouse.
- **Scene 2 (Dawn — Smoking rubble & Prefecture lockup):** Corvus tries to shield Lucida without telling her by diverting the *vigiles* tribune's inquest onto an adjacent fuller's yard—inadvertently exposing Lucida's secret straw-lease ownership and getting Barba's apprentice maimed in the arrest scramble.

### Chapter 22 — *Sleeping Almond* (POV: Lucida | Aug 160 CE — Three Nights Later | ~6,000w)
- **Scene 1 (Afternoon — Charred ground-plot on the Vicus Patricius):** Ash ankle-deep; furious at Corvus's unasked "rescue" that exposed her straw-man, Lucida outmaneuvers Corvus's own equestrian client at an emergency rubble-lease auction, buying the burned frontage for song-money under Trajan's rebuilding statute.
- **Scene 2 (Late night — Vedius Pollio's summer sleeping-cubiculum):** Summoned to rub poppy-and-almond cooling salve into Vedius's sun-blistered shoulders after he boasts of Barba's impending ruin, Lucida holds Fabia's six-inch emerald pin one finger above his carotid while he snores—and stays her hand (**C10 Surprise**) upon realizing intestate inheritance law would hand her reversion straight to Senator Priscus's wardship.

### Chapter 23 — *Under the Vault* (POV: Corvus | Sep 160 CE | ~6,000w)
- **Scene 1 (Evening — Autumn cloudburst flooding the Argiletum sewer-grates):** Corvus tracks Lucida into the vaulted brick cellar beneath her half-rebuilt *insula* as storm-water backs up from the Cloaca.
- **Scene 2 (Continuous — The lamp-lit brick vault):** **The Erotic & Emotional Fulcrum of Book I.** Drenched, nineteen, furious over the fire inquest and rubble auction, five years of swallowed hunger, jealousy over Crispina and Decimus, and childhood terror explode at once. He shoves her back against warm dry kiln-brick; she bites his lower lip until it bleeds, tears his tunic at the shoulder, drags his hands under her wet wool *tunica*; skin on skin, mouths, thighs, raw gasping profanity—right to the edge of consummation—until her fingers brush the Priscus audit seal in his belt-pouch and she sees her own bakery street marked in his handwriting (while Lucida simultaneously spots green wax-shavings of Priscus's Syrian letters under his nails). They Wrench apart like scalded wolves; clothes half-torn, chests heaving, speaking the cruellest truths each knows about the other's dead parents.

### Chapter 24 — *The Old Man's Vintage* (POV: Lucida | Oct 160 CE | ~6,000w)
- **Scene 1 (Afternoon — Imperial country villa at Lorium on the Via Aurelia):** Lucida travels in Ceionia Fabia's baggage-train to the imperial grape-pressing at Lorium; seventy-four-year-old **Emperor Antoninus Pius**—stooped in a wooden corset-splint so he can stand straight, smelling of camphor, splitting cumin-seed accounts—receives petitions beside the wine-vats while **Marcus Caesar**, pregnant **Faustina**, and restless **Lucius Commodus** circle each other in autumn sunlight.
- **Scene 2 (Night — Coach-inn on the Via Aurelia):** Fabia tells Lucida plainly that the old Emperor will not see next summer—and offers Lucida an imperial citizenship rescript the hour Lucius puts on purple, if Lucida can bring proof that Priscus and Rusticus mean to cheat Lucius of the throne.

## PART V — AEQUANIMITAS (December 160 – March 161 CE | Ages 19–20)

### Chapter 25 — *Saturn's Scales* (POV: Corvus | Dec 160 CE | ~6,000w)
- **Scene 1 (Morning — Aerarium Saturni [State Treasury] vaults):** Urban Prefect Lollius Urbicus is dead; Praetorian Prefect Tattius Maximus is dead, replaced by Furius Victorinus and Cornelius Repentinus; Rome smells succession in the winter fog. Corvus uncovers the final link between Vedius's illegal Caelian water-pipes, Domitia Lucilla's looted brick-stamps, and the five witnesses on Vedius's master trust.
- **Scene 2 (Night — Volusenus Niger's darkened atrium):** **C10 Surprise:** Cornered by dice-usurers about to break his senatorial career, arrogant rival Volusenus holds a hunting-blade to his groin-artery; Corvus buys and burns the worst forged bond on the brazier, binding the weeping patrician as his fifth citizen witness for what is coming.

### Chapter 26 — *Forty Days to April* (POV: Lucida | Jan–Feb 161 CE | ~6,000w)
- **Scene 1 (Pre-dawn — Barba's *pistrinum*, Day 1,050 of 1,095):** Forty-five days left until Lucida's three-year statutory citizenship matures! Old Barba is coughing grey blood into flour-sacks; mute Sura tallies the morning's hundredth *modius* while frost glazes the threshold.
- **Scene 2 (Afternoon — Temple of Castor safe-deposit strongrooms):** **The Trap Closes:** Phileros tips off Beroe for fifty denarii—Vedius Pollio has just lodged a sealed capital *delatio* accusing Beroe and Lucida of compounding *venena* (poison/love-philtres) for Caelian matrons, timed to unseal the week Antoninus dies when Vedius's ally Repentinus holds the Praetorian sword.

### Chapter 27 — *The Cracked Tile* (POV: Corvus | 4 Mar 161 CE | ~6,000w)
- **Scene 1 (Dusk — Couriers galloping in from Lorium):** Word hits Priscus's Caelian house: Antoninus Pius ate Alpine cheese at supper, vomited in the night, and lies burning with fever twelve miles out on the Via Aurelia.
- **Scene 2 (Midnight — Beroe's old garret stair, one last meeting before the storm):** Corvus comes to Lucida in the dark—twenty years old now, three days before the Empire changes hands—begging her to hand over her bakery leases into his safe-keeping and step behind his legal shield for ten days while he destroys Vedius. While Corvus tends a cut on Sura's hand by the brazier, Lucida—convinced his "shield" is a cage that will forfeit her forty remaining days of self-won freedom—quietly takes a wax impression and duplicate leaf of Priscus's Syrian succession letter from Corvus's oilskin satchel, kisses his scarred right thumb once like a funeral rite, and bolts the door behind him.

### Chapter 28 — *Alpine Cheese* (POV: Lucida | 6–7 Mar 161 CE | ~6,000w)
- **Scene 1 (Night of 6/7 March — Subura bakehouse raid):** As Antoninus Pius speaks *"Aequanimitas"* to the night-tribune at Lorium and dies before dawn on the Nones of March, Scaeva arrives with hired thugs and urban *stationarii* carrying Vedius's warrant to seize Lucida's ovens and strangle Beroe in the Smoke-house.
- **Scene 2 (Continuous — Subura alleys & fullery roofs in freezing March rain):** Brutal, breathless fight in dark wet brick-passages: Lucida blinds Scaeva's point-man with boiling lye-ash, Sura hamstrings the hound-handler, and old Habinnas Barba stays behind swinging an iron peel-shovel to hold the narrow mill-gate—getting his ribs crushed and dragged into custody while Lucida escapes over the wet pantiles with blood under her nails and forty days of her three-year statutory mill-count broken forever.

### Chapter 29 — *By Bronze and Balance* (POV: Corvus | 7 Mar 161 CE — Day of the Interregnum | ~6,000w)
- **Scene 1 (Morning to Midday — Curia Julia steps & Basilica Julia):** Rome holds its breath as the Senate convenes; Marcus Aurelius shocks Priscus and the Stoics by refusing the throne unless **Lucius Ceionius Commodus** is hailed co-Augustus (**Imperator Caesar Lucius Aurelius Verus Augustus**) beside him!
- **Scene 2 (Afternoon — Aerarium Saturni & Praetor's Tribunal):** Before Repentinus and Vedius can execute the *delatio*, twenty-year-old Corvus springs all three plants at once before the fiscal praetor and five citizen witnesses (including bound Volusenus): invalidating Vedius's *mancipatio* trust via the *Figlinae Subortanae* stamp fraud, triggering instant public confiscation over the stolen Caelian aqueduct water, crushing Vedius into ruinous exile, **earning `420,000 HS` statutory fiscal-informer & receivership share (crossing the equestrian census in a single stroke!)**—and sweeping Vedius's entire Subura mill-network AND Lucida's patronal reversion into his own sealed receivership chest, believing he has just rescued her life and bought their future.
- **Scene 3 (Dusk — Outside the Tullianum / Prefecture cells):** Corvus goes down with clean equestrian authorization to release Barba and hand Lucida her safety—only to find old Barba dying on wet straw from ruptured lungs, spitting flour-blood into Corvus's new narrow-striped tunic and whispering what Scaeva screamed as the mill-stones were smashed: *that Priscus's young surveyor signed the receivership roll that shut the ovens.*

### Chapter 30 — *Two Emperors* (POV: Lucida | Night of 7 Mar – Dawn of 8 Mar 161 CE | ~6,000w)
- **Scene 1 (Midnight — Ceionia Fabia's racing carriage to the Castra Praetoria):** Soaked, barefoot under a borrowed silk cloak, grieving crushed Barba and convinced Corvus broke her ovens forty days shy of freedom so he could own her reversion himself, twenty-year-old Lucida rides into the torchlit **Praetorian Camp** where twenty thousand guardsmen clatter shields for `20,000 HS` apiece donative from the **two new Emperors**, Marcus Aurelius and Lucius Verus.
- **Scene 2 (Fourth watch — The Principia tent of Emperor Lucius Verus):** Face-to-face with newly purpled **Lucius Verus** and **Ceionia Fabia**, Lucida lays Priscus's stolen Syrian letters on the camp table—saving Verus from an early senatorial ambush, destroying Corvus's patron Priscus overnight, and receiving wet-inked from Verus's own signet ring **full Roman citizenship (*ius Quiritium*, *sui iuris*), confiscation-immunity, and Vedius's prime Ostian grain-warehouse leases**—plus Scaeva's bound body in a wagon below, whom Lucida neither tortures nor pardons, ordering him shipped in irons to the Sardinian lead-mines where *humiliores* rot blind.
- **Scene 3 (Dawn, 8 March 161 — The Arch of the Firewall between the Forum of Augustus and the Subura):** Torch-smoke and cold white sun over wet marble. Corvus—wearing the narrow purple equestrian stripe and gold ring for the first morning of his life, carrying his dead patron's ruined briefs and Lucida's useless release-deed—meets Lucida stepping down from a Caelian litter in clean white citizen wool at the exact stone arch where they entered the Subura at fifteen on the afternoon of the October Horse. Neither raises a hand. She drops her half of their childhood terracotta brick-stamp onto the travertine drain-grate between their sandals, crushes its edge under her new red-dyed *calceus* sole, and tells him softly that Rome has two Emperors this morning—and room inside it for only one of them by thirty.
