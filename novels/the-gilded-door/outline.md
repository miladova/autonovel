# THE GILDED DOOR — working canon and outline

A novel of Rome, AD 112–113. Third person limited, past tense. 25 chapters,
2–3 scenes each, 5,000–6,000 words. Mature.

---

## The world

Rome, March AD 112. Trajan is in his fourteenth year; consul for the sixth time
with Titus Sextius Africanus. The Forum of Trajan is nearly finished; the Column
still rises. The Dacian wars ended six years ago; the triumph was five years ago;
the war fever now points east, at Parthia. Trajan's sister Marciana dies that
summer and is deified. In October 113 the emperor leaves for the east and never
returns to Rome.

Rome runs on status. A senator's census is a million sesterces; an equestrian's,
four hundred thousand. Freedmen can be richer than either and still sit below
both. A woman's legal capacity is thin ice over deep water. A slave is a body
with a price on it, and the law permits that body to be questioned under torture.

## The house

Antonia Cassia keeps a house on the Clivus Suburanus, where the Subura climbs
toward the Esquiline. The door is iron, sheathed in gilt; the threshold mosaic is
a door within a door. Behind it: a peristyle garden, a dining room of twelve
couches, a small bath, a room with a strongbox and a book. Banquets, music,
conversation, women. Senators come; merchants come; men come who believe
themselves unobserved. Nothing said in the house is forgotten, and much of it is
written down.

## Principal people

**Antonia Cassia**, 39. Born a slave in the household of the Cassii; her slave
name was Prima. Manumitted in late AD 93 by Cassia Severa, mother of Manius
Cassius Verus. In AD 94 she bore a daughter, who was taken from her by the
child's father. Two years in a brothel on the Aventine, held by debt, legally
free the whole time. Freed from the debt in 97 by Gaius Antonius Felix, a rich
freedman in marble and wine; his companion, then his heir, when he died in 101.
She took his name, kept her manumittor's name, and dropped the slave name. The
city calls her Antonia Cassia. No law made that name; she did.

She wants *restitutio natalium* — an imperial grant giving a freed person the
legal standing of the freeborn. It is a favour, not a right; it can be killed by
scandal.

**Manius Cassius Verus**, 45. Consul (suffect) AD 103; consular; of the ancient
Cassii; a candidate for the proconsulship of Asia and a voice on the emperor's
council. In AD 93, aged 26, he interrogated his own father's slaves to save his
family from a treason charge, and was present while the woman he had been
sleeping with was put to the question. He arranged her manumission afterward.
He took her daughter and had her raised as a slave — first in the country, then
in his own house, as maid to his legitimate daughter. He has told himself for
eighteen years that this was protection. He is not a monster in his own mind,
which is worse.

**Cassia Myrtale ("Myrta")**, 18. Slave in the Cassian house; maid to Publia.
Believes her mother died. Wears a split lead token on a cord, given her as a
child. Has no idea she was born free.

**Publia Cassia**, 16. Verus's daughter; being married to Titus Caesennius Rufus,
54, for money. Comes to Antonia's door asking for help.

**Manius Cassius Verus the Younger ("Verianus")**, 20. Verus's son by his first
wife. Just elected one of the decemviri for status cases. Hates his father. Was
on the terrace the night Decimus Iunius Gallus died.

**Acilia Marcella**, 33. Verus's second wife. Brought the money that saved the
Cassii. Has known about Myrtale for years. Not a villain; something more useful.

**Lucius Iunius Gallus**, 58. Consular; Antonia's friend and client. Father of
Decimus, who dies at the Floralia. A decent man; grief makes him dangerous.

**Oppius Nepos**, 43. Equestrian; informer; made his fortune from confiscations
under Domitian and has survived into the better age by changing his coat. His
father was a freedman auctioneer; Nepos's pedigree is a lie he has paid to keep.
He wants Antonia's book of debts, and Verus's ruin, and a prefecture.

**Lucius Valerius Crescens**, 34. Tribune of the seventh cohort of the vigiles.
Equestrian, in debt, Antonia's lover and her eyes in the fire service.

**Gaius Calpurnius Daphnus**, 50. Freedman shipper at Ostia and Portus; Antonia's
partner in a grain venture.

**Tullia Procula**, 47. Freedwoman; Antonia's steward; keeps the book. Loyal to
the house as an institution, which is not the same as loyal to its mistress.

**Kallisto**, 31. Greek; freedwoman; the house's finest voice; works for a share
and wants a house of her own.

**Dessa**, 20. Dacian, taken in 106 at fourteen. Antonia's slave. Learns to read
and keep accounts. Wants to go home to a country that no longer exists.

**Eutychus**, 52. Physician; freedman. **Beryllos**, 40. Cook. **Cerdon**, 30.
Doorkeeper. **Hilarus**, 25. Litter-bearer. **Sabina**, 15. Newly bought.

**Neratius Priscus**, jurist and consular. **Petronius Celer**, urban prefect.
**Plotina**, the empress. **Trajan**. **Marciana**, his sister. **Matidia**.
**Attianus**, praetorian prefect. **Haterius**, freedman clerk in the petitions
office. **Iusta**, midwife, at Ficulea. **Bassa**, dead dealer in children.
**Cominius Asiaticus**, dead brothel-keeper. **Cassia Severa**, dead mother of
Verus. **Gaius Antonius Felix**, dead patron of Antonia. **Didius Probus**, dead
informer of 93. **Chryseros**, dead slave accountant.

## Objects

**The token.** A lead token the size of a thumbnail, struck with a door on one
side and a wreath on the other; broken in half. Antonia kept the door half; the
wreath half went with the baby. Myrtale wears the wreath half on a cord.

**The book.** Antonia's ledger: names, gifts, loans, favours, dates. Not a
brothel's accounts; a city's. Kept in a locked room behind a bronze door. It is
the most dangerous object in Rome that no magistrate has ever seen.

**The scar.** Antonia's back and left shoulder, from the question. She does not
let anyone see it, and cannot lift her left arm above the shoulder.

**The ring.** Verus's signet, a cassia leaf. Antonia has kept it since 93.

## The law (as the characters use it)

- *Restitutio natalium*: imperial grant of freeborn standing to a freed person.
  A favour; petitioned through the petitions office; granted by rescript.
- *Causa liberalis*: a freedom suit. Someone claimed as a slave must have a
  free man as *adsertor libertatis* to assert the claim; the case is heard by
  the praetor with the decemviri. If the adsertor loses, he pays a penalty;
  the person is returned to the master.
- *Plagium*: knowingly holding a free person as a slave (lex Fabia). A public
  crime; a senator convicted of it loses rank and more.
- *Infamia*: the legal stain attached to certain trades and convictions. It
  closes courts, marriages, and doors.
- Children follow the mother, but a child conceived or carried while the mother
  was free is born free.
- A senator may not marry a freedwoman.
- Slaves' evidence is taken under torture, and only then is it good.

## Chapter map

### Part One — THE GILDED DOOR

1. **The House on the Clivus** — Rome, March 112. A banquet. The house, the
   guests, the rules. Decimus Iunius Gallus tells a story about a singing girl in
   the Cassian house "with the old man's eyes." Verianus goes white. Nepos
   mentions the petition. After midnight: Tullia reports the petition is stalled
   and a man has been asking about a slave child sold eighteen years ago. A
   veiled woman at the back door: Publia.

2. **A Daughter of the Cassii** — Publia wants the marriage broken. Antonia
   refuses, then smells a weapon. Myrtale waits in the passage with her mistress
   and is not looked at twice. Tullia brings word of the Cassian finances: the
   dowry is the price of the proconsulship. Antonia sets watchers.

3. **The Sixth Hour** — The Baths of Trajan. Antonia does business among bathers,
   keeps her back covered, is cut by Marcella's circle. At the petitions office
   she learns her rescript needs a consular to carry it. Publia tells her about
   the girl Myrtale, the token, and her father's refusal to sell her.

4. **What the Senator Wanted** — Verus comes to the house alone. The first duel.
   He forbids her the Cassian door; she refuses. Both say too much. Later
   Crescens warns her: Nepos has been asking the prefect's office about her.

5. **The Harbour Master's Ledger** — Ostia. Grain, ships, a purchase of a girl
   that Antonia makes without pleasure. Bassa's heir and Bassa's ledger. The
   entry: *girl, three months, to the house of C. Cassius Verus.* Antonia now
   knows where, not who.

6. **Floralia** — The festival; the house at its height; the aediles bought. On
   the terrace, Decimus and Dessa; Verianus on the stairs; the body in the
   fountain. Antonia chooses to lie. Dessa runs.

### Part Two — THE BOOK OF DEBTS

7. **The Vigiles** — The fire-watch, the aediles, the body. Crescens's dilemma.
   Gallus at dawn, and Antonia's lie, and her promise to find the killer.

8. **A Father's Grief** — The funeral; the masks; the pyre; the mother's silence.
   Gallus asks her to destroy the Cassii. Nepos offers condolences and mentions
   a missing slave girl.

9. **The Cassian House** — Verus summons her. The *fauces*, the atrium, the old
   marble, the cold. Myrtale pours wine; Antonia sees the token; the world tilts.
   In the study, Verus lies, and Antonia says the girl's name, and he goes still.

10. **The Informer** — Nepos's terms: the book, and a statement about Verus. He
    shows her the 93 file. Antonia begins her counter: Nepos's father, the
    auctioneer, and a date.

11. **The Token** — Myrtale's chapter. Below stairs in the Cassian house: the
    routines, Publia's kindness, Marcella's eye, the master's nightly visits in
    which he looks at her and says nothing. The token. The visitor who stared.

12. **What Verus Kept** — Verus's chapter. Marcella has known for years. He goes
    to Antonia at night and tells her the shape of it: the sale, the country
    nurse, the household, the eighteen years. She demands the girl. He refuses to
    give her up and calls it love.

### Part Three — THE WOUND

13. **Prima** — Rome, AD 92–94. The ornatrix, the young master, the first touch.
    The knock. The elder Verus. The lamp, the rope, the questions. The
    manumission. The birth. The taking of the child. The room above the fuller's
    shop, and the door that closes.

14. **The Girl Who Ran** — Dessa's chapter. Hiding, memory, the road out of Rome,
    the slave-catcher. Nepos's men take her.

15. **A Question of Status** — The jurist explains the suit, the adsertor, and
    the risk to Antonia's own name. Publia offers help. The Palatine refuses the
    petition unless a consular endorses it. Antonia understands what she will
    have to pay.

16. **The Wedding** — September. The flammeum, the torch, the walnuts, the songs.
    Antonia in the Cassian house as Marcella's guest, standing on the floor where
    she was a slave. Publia goes through with it. That night she comes to the
    gilded door in her wedding clothes.

17. **Fire in the Subura** — September. The vigiles, the pumps, the collapsing
    insula, the lane. Cerdon dies. The book is carried out and a copy is not.
    Antonia stands in the ash and does the arithmetic.

18. **The Price of a Silence** — October. Gallus learns the truth from Nepos.
    He comes to her with the demand: the truth, publicly, and he will stand for
    the girl. Verus offers the opposite bargain. Antonia chooses the truth and
    does not know yet what it will cost.

### Part Four — THE CAUSA

19. **The Decemviri** — November. The court in the Forum. The adsertor. Verianus
    among the ten. Antonia's testimony. The case is adjourned when the defence
    produces a bill of sale.

20. **The Empress's Garden** — November. Plotina. The rescript, the price, the
    question of what an empress wants. Antonia is offered her name and asked to
    buy it with someone else's ruin.

21. **The Raid** — December. The house closed; the women taken; the book seized.
    Verianus opens his veins in his father's bath and leaves a letter confessing
    to the death of Decimus. Antonia holds the letter.

22. **The Interrogation** — Dessa's chapter. The cell, the rope, the question.
    The physician's hands. What she says and what she does not. She is brought
    out with her right hand ruined. The letter arrives too late.

23. **The Testimony** — December. The Senate. Antonia tells the truth of 93 in
    her own voice: the lamp, the rope, the names, the child. Verus stands and
    confirms it. The house of the Cassii ends in a room full of men.

24. **The Verdict** — December. The freeing of Myrtale by the rod. The naming.
    The daughter does not call her mother. Verus's relegation. Nepos's reward.
    The book burned or kept. The house reordered.

25. **The Column of Trajan** — May–October 113. The dedication. Dessa looks for
    her country in the spiral of stone. Plotina's last word. Verus at the gate.
    Antonia at her door.

## Continuity notes

- Antonia does not know Myrtale is in the Cassian house until ch. 9. She suspects
  from ch. 3.
- Verus knows from the beginning. His silence is the engine of chs. 4–12.
- Verianus is guilty of Decimus's death (a push, not a plan). Only he, Dessa,
  and Antonia know. Verianus's letter in ch. 21 says so.
- Marcella knows about Myrtale from 104, when she found the purchase record.
  She has said nothing for eight years.
- Nepos's father, the auctioneer **Gaius Oppius Philargyrus**, was a slave at
  Nepos's birth and was freed two years later. Nepos's equestrian standing rests
  on a false declaration. Only Bassa's ledger and a freedman's memory can prove
  it.
- Antonia's manumission is valid and recorded. Her petition fails for political
  reasons, not legal ones. This matters: she is not unmasked; she is refused.
- Myrtale was born free because her mother was manumitted before the birth. The
  proof is the manumission record, the midwife, the token, and Bassa's ledger.
- No character is raped on the page. Coercion is shown by power, not by
  spectacle. The torture of slaves is shown plainly and briefly, without relish.
