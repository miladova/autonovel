# THE GILDED MYRTLE — working canon (private, not part of the manuscript)

## Place and time
Rome, spring–autumn AD 112. Trajan absent in the East (Asia, then Antioch; winter 112/113 at Antioch). The Forum of Trajan and its column recently dedicated. Senate debates luxury, the corn supply, the alimenta, and the cost of public building. Pliny the Younger is a senator and a guest; never a viewpoint character.

## Principal persons
- **Antonia Cassia**, 39. Born in the Subura. Daughter of Lucius Antonius Crispus, a grain factor who hanged himself in his shop when she was eleven, ruined in a lawsuit. Mother dead in childbirth. Sold into slavery at fourteen by her guardian; manumitted at twenty-seven. For six years (89–95) she was the *delicata* — purchased companion — of Appius Claudius Corvus in his house on the Palatine. In 95, aged twenty-three, she killed Corvus's father, the consul **Lucius Claudius Corvus**, in the domestic shrine of the Palatine house: he had just had her flogged and was raping her; on the altar was his pens. She took a bronze stylus from the account box and drove it into his throat. Half-brother Caius saw; he was twelve. Corvus (then twenty) came, sent her away and buried the matter as an accident, buying her silence with her freedom and a purse. She has since built the Gilded Myrtle on the Vicus Tuscus, a house of banquets and courtesans, fortune ~1.2 million sesterces, half in loans and property, half in the house and its people.
- **Antonia Cassia's house**: Tiro (freedman steward, 52, ex-gladiator, cut face, one dead son, a boy in a smithy at Tibur); Calybe (cook, Bithynian, 44); Leda (singer, 21, bought at fourteen from a slave dealer at Puteoli for 12,000 sesterces — in fact a freeborn Italian girl taken by pirates at eleven, from Surrentum; her real name is **Vibia Rufa**, daughter of Vibia Prima of Surrentum); Meroe (dancer, Egyptian); Prisca (housekeeper); Junilla (doorkeeper); Felix (lamp boy, 12, from the Subura, bought for 800); Sarpedon (physician, Greek, drunk).
- **Gaius Claudius Corvus**, 41, senator, praetorius, of the ancient *Claudii Corvi*. Proud, dry, cruel in the way of men who never had to ask. Widower. Two children: **Marcus Claudius Corvus**, 20 (military tribune in Dacia 110–111, returned), and **Caesia**, 15. Mother: **Claudia Junia**, 63. Sister: none living. Steward: **Faustus** (freedman).
- **Tiberius Claudius Eutychus**, 55, imperial freedman, *a rationibus* (secretary of accounts), immensely rich, collector of obligations. Not a caricature: courteous, patient, genuinely witty.
- **Gaius Vettius Proculus**, 61, senator, *consularis*, widower, one son **Publius Vettius Proculus** (30, joyless, in debt). Vettius was at Puteoli six years ago and believes he saw a girl he once knew; he wants to buy Leda. He dies at the Gilded Myrtle at the end of Chapter 1 — poisoned, apparently by a cup meant for someone else.
- **Decimus Terentius Blandus**, senator, ruined, one of Antonia's clients.
- **Vipsania Marcella**, 44, wife of a consular, Antonia's oldest patron-friend and her most dangerous acquaintance.
- **The poisoner**: not a person but an arrangement — Eutychus's man **Callistus** (a slave of Vettius's household, in the pay of Vettius's son Publius) put the powder in Vettius's cup. Publius wanted his father dead before he changed his will. Corvus knows this by Chapter 18 and conceals it, because Publius's evidence is what he needs against Antonia.

## Key objects
- **The bronze stylus**, Antonia's. Left in Corvus's father's throat; Corvus kept it.
- **Corvus's letter** of 95, in his own hand, sealed: *"Take the purse and go. Say nothing, and you will never see my face again. Say one word, and I will see you dead."* Kept in Antonia's strongbox in a myrtle-wood casket with the stylus.
- **The purchase document** of Leda, in Antonia's strongbox, naming the seller at Puteoli and the girl's stated origin (falsified).

## Timeline

## Rules of the world
No anachronism. Money in sesterces (HS). No gladiator spectacle at the house. Torture of slaves is legally admissible and used. *Infamia* attaches to the house's women and, by association, to Antonia. The urban prefect's court (the *cognitio*) can try a woman of the house for *lenocinium* without the Senate.

## Endgame as drafted (brings the canon current; do not contradict)
- **Trial (Part Four).** Marcus forces his way into the basilica and sits at the back. Antonia produces the matching pen against the stylus; Tiro testifies and confesses on the stand. Verdict: not guilty of the murder of Vettius; guilty of keeping the house and of corruption; 100,000 sesterces and five years out of Rome and Italy. The house is confiscated to the city.
- **The fire.** The night after the trial's first day the house burns: a crowd comes up from the Forum with a torch. Four dead. Tiro dies under a beam across the doorway with the papers saved; the money is gone from the box. The eleven women and three men come out holding their own manumission papers.
- **Leda.** Brought to the Palatine, she is killed when Claudia Junia seizes her wrist at a rotted gallery rail; she falls into the atrium and dies within the hour. Ruled an accident by the carpenter's evidence. Burned with the pink cowrie in her hand; ashes taken to Surrentum. Marcus had arranged a ship at Ostia; she refused it and told him she loved him, which was the cruellest thing she did that day.
- **Corvus.** Resigns the consulship before the Senate; takes hemlock in his father's shrine with Antonia present. The codicil leaves her the stylus and 11,000 sesterces to the bakers' guild in Felix's name. Marcus sails for Syria without writing.
- **After.** House sold; the women scatter with their papers. Antonia keeps a wine shop at Surrentum with Prisca (accounts), Meroe (dancing lessons), Junilla (the door) and Tiro's son (learning his letters). Vibia Prima and her son Gaius are in the town. The duumvir Vibidius's complaint is dismissed. Marcus writes once from the Euphrates; she answers once.
- **Fates.** Faustus dies in the mines at the end of the first year. Vettius exiled to an island. Maro killed by a falling tree on the Danube in year two. Eutychus opens his baths with the myrtle tree in the garden.
